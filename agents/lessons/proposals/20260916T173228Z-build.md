## A valid parts-of-one-object table can still leave the draft audit non-clean

What happened: T278 stores the four parts of one Hall example, with parameter
`part` taking `x`, `y`, `k` and `r`. The family instructions explicitly chose
the "parts of one object" exception, and the draft definition says that the
four entries with the same $x$ are the parts of one Hall example. The audit
still returns `clean: false`, because the structural one-quantity check reports
every draft whose small symbolic parameter repeats over the same grid. It does
not read the definition or suppress the finding after the exception is stated.

What the skill says now: a table may keep "parts of one object" together, and
the audit finding says to say so in the definition and leave them together.

What it should say: when the table is deliberately using the parts-of-one-object
exception, the one-quantity audit finding may remain as a reviewer-facing
question rather than a fixable error. Answer it in the definition, give the
value column a non-generic header, report the residual finding, and do not split
the table merely to make the audit clean.

Evidence: T278 on 2026-09-16. After the definition was changed to "the four
entries with the same $x$ are the parts of one Hall example" and the
`number-header` was changed from `value` to `$x$, $y$, $k$, or $r$,
`/api/table/T278/audit` still returned the single finding beginning
"parameter part takes 4 names (k, r, x, y)".
