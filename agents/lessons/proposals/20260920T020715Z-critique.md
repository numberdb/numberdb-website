## An exclusion clause in `complete-note` has to have excluded something

What happened: T350's `complete-note` renders as

> Table is complete: no (it holds the values with $\varphi=t\pi$ for
> $t\in\{1/24,\ldots,1/2\}$ and $m=a/b\in[0,1)$ in lowest terms with
> $b\leq12$; rows whose value is rational would be omitted)

and nothing was omitted. The table holds $12\times46=552$ entries, which is
the full grid the first half of the same sentence describes: twelve amplitudes
against every reduced $m=a/b\in[0,1)$ with $b\leq12$. The attached generator
has no such filter either — `enumerate` yields every pair and `value` returns
every one of them. The clause describes a rule the author had in mind and
never needed, written in the conditional mood in the one field whose job is to
say what is actually here.

The cost is to a reader, and it is the exact reader this field exists for:
somebody holding a number, checking whether it belongs in this table. They are
told some rows are missing and not told which, so they cannot tell whether
their number is absent because the family excludes it, because the range stops
short, or because of this rule. The answer is none of the three, and there is
no way to reach that answer from the page.

It is an easy clause to write. A generator that *could* produce an exact value
in a table of approximations, or a duplicate, or a degenerate case, usually
grows a guard while it is being written; the note is written at the same time,
from the same intention, and then the guard turns out never to fire — here
because $E(t\pi,m)$ is irrational for every pair in the grid, so the rule was
empty from the start. Nothing catches it: `audit_table` is clean on T350, and
it has no rule that reads a `complete-note` against the rows.

What the skill says now: section 4 says the range that is actually here
belongs under `Data properties`, that `complete-note` renders inside
"complete: no (...)", and that it should be "not that the table is
incomplete, which is true of almost all of them, but what it *does* cover". It
says nothing about a clause saying what the table leaves out.

What it should say: beside that sentence, one line --

> If the note says something was left out, check that something was. Count the
> rows the rest of the sentence describes and compare with the entries: a
> grid of 12 amplitudes and 46 parameters is 552 entries, and if the table
> holds 552 then the exclusion clause excluded nothing and belongs in the
> generator's comments, not in the note. State what is here, in the
> indicative; a rule in the conditional mood sends a reader looking for gaps
> that are not there.

Could a contributor with a laptop and Sage hit this? Yes, and it is two lines
to check: `len(rows)` against the product of the ranges the note describes.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T350 critique. `GET /api/table?id=T350`: `Numbers` has
12 groups of 46, 552 entries, no duplicate values, every value a decimal
string. `https://numberdb.org/files/T350/generate.py`: `ANGLE_MULTIPLES` is a
literal 12-tuple, `_m_values` yields the 46 reduced fractions, and neither
`enumerate` nor `value` filters on the result. The audit answers
`{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. Written up as finding 3 in
`agents/critiques/T350.md`.

## Write a parameter's constraint in the symbol the column is headed with

What happened: T350's amplitude parameter has `display: "$\varphi/\pi$"` and
`constraints: "$0<t\leq1/2$, where $\varphi=t\pi$"`. The site sets those two
fields on one line, so the parameter list renders as

    $\varphi/\pi$ —   angle multiple ($0<t\leq1/2$, where $\varphi=t\pi$)

which is three names for one quantity inside one set of parentheses. The
column is $\varphi/\pi$; the constraint is about $t$; and $t$ is then defined
by a trailing clause that introduces a third symbol. A reader checking whether
their own argument is in range has to do the substitution before they can
begin. Its twin table T349 writes the same constraint as
`$0<\varphi/\pi\leq 1/2$`, which needs no `where`, and the two tables are
otherwise the same table with a different integrand.

The general shape: a constraint is usually written while thinking about the
mathematics, where a short letter is convenient, and the `display` is chosen
later or from a sibling table. They are then rendered together, and nothing
rereads them as one line. `audit_table` does not compare the two fields.

What the skill says now: section 4's table gives `Parameters` as "each
parameter's type and the constraint the entries actually satisfy", and the
rule after it is about the constraint being the family rather than the run. On
`display` it says a value's display is a label and not a formula, and that
`param-latex` replaces its group's label. Nothing ties the constraint's
notation to the display's.

What it should say: with the parameter rules in section 4 --

> The constraint renders on the same line as the parameter's `display`, so
> write it in that symbol. A constraint that introduces a letter of its own
> and then defines it -- `$0<t\leq1/2$, where $\varphi=t\pi$` under a column
> headed `$\varphi/\pi$` -- makes a reader substitute before they can check
> their own argument. `$0<\varphi/\pi\leq1/2$` says the same thing and needs
> no `where`.

Could a contributor with a laptop and Sage hit this? Yes: it is two fields of
their own document, and it is only visible once the page renders them side by
side.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T350 critique. T350's `Parameters.phi` is
`{"type": "Q", "title": "angle multiple", "display": "$\\varphi/\\pi$",
"constraints": "$0<t\\leq1/2$, where $\\varphi=t\\pi$"}`; T349's is the same
field as `"$0<\\varphi/\\pi\\leq 1/2$"`. The rendered line is quoted above
from `/tmp/T350_page.html`, rebuilt by the sqlite draft-render recipe (552
records, status 200, 323,726 bytes). The audit is clean on T350. Written up
as finding 7 in `agents/critiques/T350.md`.
