## A parameter that looks unbounded may be finite: check the table's own formulas before writing a bound into the completeness note

What happened: T291 stores a monodromy characteristic polynomial for each
singularity and each number of variables $n$, and the parameter constraint on
$n$ reads "the number of variables, at least the corank" -- correctly the
family. The completeness note then says "with $n\leq3$", and the generator
loops `range(spec.corank, 4)`. But the table's own `suspension` formula is
$\Delta_{f+u^2}(t)=(-1)^\mu\Delta_f(-t)$, and $\mu$ does not change under
suspension, so applying it twice returns $(-1)^{2\mu}\Delta_f(t)=\Delta_f(t)$:
**the value depends on $n$ only through its parity.** Checked on all 41
labels for $n$ from the corank to corank$+5$, no exception; it is visible in
the stored data too, where $A_1$ holds `t + 1` at both $n=1$ and $n=3$.

So $n$ has exactly two values per row, not infinitely many. The table holds
both parities for the corank-1 and corank-2 labels and only one for the seven
of corank 3, where the loop runs once -- a ragged edge produced by a bound on
a parameter that did not need one. Seven entries turn "with $n\leq3$", which
is a fact about the afternoon, into "at every $n$", which is complete forever.

What the skill says now: it separates the family (`Parameters`) from the run
(`complete-note`) and says to write the clause that finishes "complete: no
(...)". It does not say to ask whether the run-bound is needed at all.

What it should say: before writing a bound on a parameter into the
completeness note, check whether an identity the table already carries makes
that parameter finite. A recurrence, a suspension, a functional equation or a
symmetry can collapse an unbounded index to a handful of classes, and then
the note says "every $n$" instead of a number. A generator's `range(...,
BOUND)` on an axis the table has a formula for is worth one minute's
suspicion.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, draft T291 and its generator
`monodromy-characteristic-polynomials-simple-unimodal-singularities`. The
check ran under `agents/sage.sh` against the generator's own
`monodromy_polynomial`: `period-2 in n fails for: nothing (41 labels, n up to
corank+5)`. The seven missing $n=4$ values are all new polynomials, e.g.
$S_{12}$ at $n=3$ is $\Phi_{13}(t)$ and at $n=4$ is $\Phi_{26}(t)$.

## Bound a family by the invariant its classification is stratified by, not by one sub-family's index

What happened: T291's range is "$A_k$ for $1\leq k\leq12$, $D_k$ for $4\leq
k\leq12$, the exceptional simple singularities, and the parabolic and
exceptional unimodal singularities". Three different rules, and the edges do
not line up: $\mu(A_k)=\mu(D_k)=k$, so the two infinite families stop at
Milnor number 12, while the sporadic labels run to $\mu=14$ ($E_{14}$).
Nothing says why 12.

Every singularity in this classification has a Milnor number, and for each
$\mu$ there are finitely many of them -- including the hyperbolic series
$T_{p,q,r}$, where $\mu=p+q+r-1$ bounds all three indices at once. So a single
bound, $\mu\leq14$, cuts the two infinite families, the sporadic list and the
series at the same place, and it is the classification's own number rather
than a chosen one: 14 is the smallest bound containing every sporadic label
the table already holds. The completeness note becomes one sentence, and a
reader can tell whether their own singularity is in range by computing one
integer.

What the skill says now: "Say which range you chose and why in the
completeness note. That sentence is the argument." It gives the test (does
anybody arrive holding this value) but not the shape of a good bound.

What it should say: when a family is a classification with several
sub-families of different shapes, bound it by the invariant the
classification is stratified by -- Milnor number, degree, conductor,
discriminant, crossing number -- rather than by each sub-family's own index.
One bound that cuts every sub-family at the same place makes the completeness
note a single checkable sentence, keeps the sporadic and the infinite parts
in step, and tells the next person extending the table exactly which number
to raise. A range with one rule per sub-family is usually a range nobody
chose.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, draft T291. Measured with the table's own generator and
the closed form for $T_{p,q,r}$: at $\mu\leq14$ the table is 82 labels, 178
entries, 27,223 bytes (soft limits 1200 entries, 320 KB); at $\mu\leq20$, 209
labels, 438 entries, 70,095 bytes. The longest entry is 190 characters at both,
because the bound is on $\mu$ and $\mu$ is the degree.

## Singular's `milnor` and its `spectrum` live in different libraries, and the error for the missing one reads like a broken Singular

What happened: a check of the monodromy of the hyperbolic singularities
loaded `gmssing.lib` -- which is where `spectrum` is, and what T291's
generator already loads -- and then called `milnor(F)`. Singular answered

    ? `milnor` is not defined
    ? error occurred in or before STDIN line 14: `milnor(F);`

which through Sage arrives as `SingularError` and reads like Singular is
missing or the ring is wrong. `milnor` is in `sing.lib`; loading `gmssing.lib`
does not bring it. Adding `LIB "sing.lib";` alongside fixed it, and nothing
else about the run changed. The whole first pass of seven checks was lost to
it, because the `milnor` call came before the `spectrum` call in each one.

What the skill says now: it warns that a Sage import does not bring what you
expect with it, and names `sage.interfaces.singular` as a way to check a value
independently. It does not say that Singular's own libraries are separate
namespaces.

What it should say: Singular's functions come from libraries loaded by name,
and a library does not load its neighbours. `LIB "sing.lib";` gives `milnor`,
`tjurina`, `deform`; `LIB "gmssing.lib";` gives `spectrum`, `sppairs`,
`vfilt`; `LIB "dmod.lib";` gives `bfct`. A "`f` is not defined" from Singular
means a missing `LIB`, not a missing Singular. Load every library the check
uses at the top, and put the cheap call last so a missing one does not cost
the expensive computation.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, `agents/sage.sh` against the builder image. The second
run with both libraries loaded returned `milnor` and `spectrum` for ten
triples $T_{p,q,r}$ in under a minute; `dmod.lib`'s `bfct` answered the same
germs in 0.2 to 7.5 seconds.
