## `HREF{}` takes a T-number as well as a slug, and bare it prints the T-number

What happened: the T384 critique had to decide whether `HREF{T381}[Discriminants
of the general polynomial of degree $n$]`, which T382, T383 and T384 all use in
`Similar tables`, is a link that works or a guessed name that silently points
at nothing. It works. Rendered through `/preview`, the four forms come out as:

    HREF{T381}                                -> <a href="T381">T381</a>
    HREF{Elementary_symmetric_polynomials}    -> <a href="Elementary_symmetric_polynomials">Elementary_symmetric_polynomials</a>
    HREF{T381}[the general polynomial]        -> <a href="T381">the general polynomial</a>
    HREF{T381#5}[degree five]                 -> <a href="T381?entry=5">degree five</a>

So a T-number resolves exactly as a slug does, the `#entry` suffix works on
either, and **neither form looks the title up**: a bare `HREF{...}` prints the
address itself, which is why every link in the corpus carries a caption.

This matters because of the failure the skill already warns about. A slug is
not the title with underscores -- mathematics is dropped from it, it is
truncated, and a clash appends a number, so
`Discriminants of the general polynomial of degree $n$` is at
`Discriminants_of_the_general_polynomial_of_degree`, which nobody would guess.
A T-number cannot be mis-guessed: it is on the table's own page, in the address
bar, and in the search result. For a contributor writing `Similar tables` from
a search result they already have in front of them, `HREF{T381}[caption]` is
the form that cannot be wrong.

What the skill says now: "`HREF{...}` takes the slug, and the slug is not the
title with underscores", followed by the three ways a guessed slug goes wrong,
and "Read both off tables that already have them". Every example it gives --
`HREF{Bernoulli_numbers}[the Bernoulli numbers]`, `HREF{slug#entry}`,
`HREF{Feigenbaum_constants#delta}[$\delta$]` -- is a slug. It never says a
T-number is accepted, so a contributor who has one still has to go and find the
slug.

What it should say: `HREF{}` resolves a T-number as readily as a slug, and the
T-number is the safer of the two because it is not derived from the title and
cannot be truncated or clash-numbered. Both forms need a caption -- bare, the
link prints its own address, `T381` or
`Elementary_symmetric_polynomials`, neither of which is a thing to read. Use
`HREF{T381}[the general polynomial of degree $n$]` when the T-number is what
you have, `HREF{slug}[caption]` when you read the slug off a search result, and
`HREF{T381#5}[caption]` for a single entry either way.

Evidence: 2026-09-21, T384 critique. `/tmp/href_test.py` posted the four forms
through `GET https://numberdb.org/preview?table=<json>` and read the anchors
out of the response; `https://numberdb.org/T381` and
`https://numberdb.org/Elementary_symmetric_polynomials` both answer 200, and
T382, T383 and T384 use the T-number form in `Similar tables` today.
