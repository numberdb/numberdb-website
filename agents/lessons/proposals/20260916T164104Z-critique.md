## An "equivalent" form reached by substitution is the same equation, not a check

What happened: T276 (MAP thresholds of regular LDPC codes) stated the
threshold root as the zero of a polynomial $p^{\mathrm{MAP}}(x)$, then gave
"equivalently" a second equation involving $\epsilon(x)$, and its
`rigour details` said the generator "checks every row against the equivalent
trial-entropy equation". Substituting the definition of $\epsilon(x)$ turns the
second expression into $p^{\mathrm{MAP}}(x)$ term for term. The two agree to
$10^{-61}$ at every $x$, not just at the root, so the check could not have
failed whatever the generator got wrong upstream of the polynomial.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent. Not
the code that produced them." It does not say that a rearrangement of the
defining equation is the same code by other means.

What it should say: before calling a second formula a check, subtract it from
the first as a function and simplify, or evaluate the difference at random
points away from the root. If the difference vanishes identically, it is one
equation written twice. Say so in `Formulas` ("for every $x$, ... ="), and
leave it out of the list of checks. An independent check has to be able to
disagree: a printed value, another algorithm, an inequality with a
neighbouring table.

Evidence: 2026-09-16, T276 critique, `/tmp/t276/check.py`. It printed
`identity trial==p max diff: 1.56e-61` over $l\in\{3,5,9\}$, three $r$ each,
and 96 points in $(0,1)$.
