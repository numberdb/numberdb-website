The batch is `agents/table-ideas/BATCH-2026-09-22T0131.md`, the spectra of the
one-dimensional anharmonic oscillators: the quartic coupling series, the pure

## Two truncations agreeing is not an error bound when the error is one-sided

What happened: the eigenvalues of $-y''+V(x)y$ were computed by Rayleigh-Ritz
in a truncated harmonic basis, at two basis sizes and two basis frequencies,
and the two runs agreed to $1.3\times10^{-11}$. The natural reading is eleven
digits. It is not: by the min-max theorem every eigenvalue of the truncated
matrix is an *upper* bound for the true one, so both runs err in the same
direction and their difference bounds the improvement, not the error. A basis
that is too small in the same way twice agrees with itself.

The same argument runs the other way and gives something for nothing: that
upper bound is a theorem, so half of a proven enclosure costs nothing beyond
doing the arithmetic in balls. The other half is a Temple-Lehmann lower bound,
which needs a rough lower bound on the next eigenvalue and is the only real
work in making such a table `proven`.

What the skill says now: section 7 defines `heuristic (agreement-checked)` as
"computed at two or more precisions, keeping the digits they agree on", and
`numberdb.agreeing(..., at=(150, 200))` takes *decimal digits*. That is sound
for arithmetic precision, where the two computations approach the answer from
whichever side rounding puts them on.

What it should say: agreement between two runs measures the error only when
the two errors are independent. Where the parameter varied is a discretisation
(a basis size, a truncation order, a grid spacing, a cut-off radius) the error
is usually one-signed, and agreement understates it. Vary the discretisation
*and* something structural about it: here, the basis frequency $\omega$ as
well as the size $N$, since the two sizes converge from the same side but two
frequencies do not converge along the same path. And say in `rigour details`
which quantities were varied, not just that two runs agreed.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, numpy in the deployed Sage image via `agents/sage.sh`.
$-y''+(x^2+x^4)y$, $N=400$ at $\omega=2$ against $N=600$ at $\omega=2.6$,
agreeing to $1.3\times10^{-11}$; the same two sizes at one $\omega$ agree
better and mean less.

## A near-degenerate pair loses the quantity somebody came for, and reports success

What happened: the quartic double well $-y''+(x^2-a^2)^2y$ has its lowest
levels in pairs, and the splitting inside a pair is the tunnelling rate, which
is the number the instanton literature actually quotes. Computed in double
precision:

    a = 1   E_0 = 1.13778584818822   E_1 = 2.71302789777057   split 1.6e+00
    a = 2   E_0 = 3.86366927930654   E_1 = 3.86518565472700   split 1.5e-03
    a = 3   E_0 = 5.94323028235233   E_1 = 5.94323028235256   split 2.2e-13

At $a = 3$ both eigenvalues are converged, agree with a larger basis to
$5\times10^{-13}$, and would pass every check a table runs. Their difference
is entirely rounding: $2.2\times10^{-13}$ is one unit in the last place of
numbers of size 6, and the true splitting is smaller still. Nothing in the
output says so, because the stored values are right and only the thing you
would compute from them is wrong.

What the skill says now: "A value belongs in a row, not in a comment", and the
`equals`/`Similar tables` machinery for relations between tables. Nothing
about a quantity that is a *difference* of stored values.

What it should say: before storing a family whose interesting content is a
difference or a ratio of neighbouring entries, check that the precision
exceeds that difference. A table of energies at 20 digits is a table of
tunnelling splittings at zero digits once the splitting is $10^{-25}$, and the
entries give no sign of it. Either carry enough digits that the derived
quantity survives, or say in the comment that it does not, or store the
derived quantity as its own family computed directly rather than by
subtraction.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, as above, $N = 600$ at $\omega = 2a$ against $N = 800$
at $\omega = 2.5a$.

## Compare signed values against a published series, never magnitudes

What happened: an independent Rayleigh-Schrödinger recursion was written to
check the Bender-Wu perturbation coefficients of the quartic oscillator
against the published $3/4$, $-21/8$, $333/16$, $-30885/128$, $916731/256$.
The first run returned

    E^(1) = +0.750      E^(2) = +2.625      E^(3) = +20.8125
    E^(4) = +241.289    E^(5) = +3580.98

Every magnitude exact to fourteen digits, the odd orders right, the even
orders sign-flipped. The cause was one character: the first-order wavefunction
was written `new[m] = -rhs[m] / gap[m]` where the recursion
$(H_0-E_0)\psi_k = \text{rhs}$ gives `+rhs[m] / gap[m]`, so every $\psi_k$ of
odd $k$ had the wrong sign and every $E^{(k)}$ of even $k$ inherited it.

A comparison written as `abs(computed - abs(published))`, or a check that
prints magnitudes because the source table prints "$21/8$" with the sign in
the prose, confirms this code completely.

What the skill says now: "Do not discard what the source certified" (T174 and
$|\lambda_n|$), which is about *storing* a magnitude where the sign is known.

What it should say: the same rule applies to *checking*. An alternating
sequence is checked against its signed values, because a sign error in a
recursion is the commonest way to produce exactly-right magnitudes, and it is
invisible to any comparison that takes an absolute value first. Where a family
alternates, the alternation is itself a check worth asserting: assert
`sign(E[k]) == (-1)**(k+1)` and a run that has lost a sign says so.

Evidence: 2026-09-22; after the fix all six published coefficients match to
$10^{-12}$ relative, the sixth being $-65518401/1024$.

## A non-Hermitian eigensolve returns converged and worthless values in one output

What happened: the imaginary cubic oscillator $-y''+ix^3y$ is not
self-adjoint but its spectrum is real. Diagonalising it in a real harmonic
basis, $N = 400$:

    E_0 = 1.1562670719880   imaginary part 3.9e-13   agreement 2.6e-12
    ...
    E_4 = 15.2915537504069  imaginary part 3.9e-11   agreement 7.8e-10
    E_5 = 17.07 + 12000 i   imaginary part 1.2e+04   agreement 1.2e+04

The command exits zero. The first five values are right to eleven digits and
the sixth is not a number the operator has. Nothing distinguishes the run that
produced them.

What the skill says now: "An enclosure that contains everything agrees with
everything", which is the same shape of trap for ball arithmetic, and "Check
the claim the digits make, not the value at their midpoint".

What it should say: a per-run verdict is not a per-value verdict. Where one
call returns a whole family at once, the check runs on each value: here, that
the imaginary part is below tolerance and that the value agrees between two
bases, value by value. The range of the table is then set by the last index
that passes, not by the size of the array the solver returned. This is
general, and the shape it takes in the more common case is: `verify(sample=N)`
sampling a family whose failures are all at one end of the index will report
success from the end that works.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, $N=400$ at $\omega=1.5$ against $N=600$ at $\omega=2$.

## Search OEIS for the constants too, and a negative result is worth writing down

What happened: three real constants were about to be proposed. Asked of OEIS
in both the forms it accepts, the decimal and the digit sequence:

    1.0603620904                  null     pure quartic oscillator, E_0
    1,0,6,0,3,6,2,0,9,0,4,8,4     null
    1.3923516415                  null     quartic anharmonic, E_0
    1,3,9,2,3,5,1,6,4,1,5,3,0     null
    1,1,5,6,2,6,7,0,7,1,9         null     imaginary cubic, E_0
    3,21,333,30885,916731         null     Bender-Wu numerators
    keyword:cons anharmonic       null

That OEIS does not hold the three most-quoted constants of a whole subject is
the strongest single argument the batch has, and it took four minutes. It is
also the argument the prompt asks for and nothing else supplies: the prompt
says where this database is stronger than OEIS in general, and this says it
about these particular numbers.

Two practical notes for anybody repeating it. OEIS stores a constant as its
digit sequence, so search both forms; a constant present under `keyword:cons`
will usually be found by the digits with the decimal point and sometimes only
without it. And `https://oeis.org/search?fmt=json&q=...` answers a search with
no hits with the literal JSON `null`, not with an empty `results` list, so
`json.load(...)` returns `None` and `d['results']` raises `TypeError` while
`d.get('results')` raises `AttributeError`. A screening script that treats
that exception as "could not reach OEIS" reports the opposite of what
happened. Write `d = json.load(...) or {}`.

What the skill says now: section 10 says to check new values against something
independent, and names OEIS as a link target; a previous lesson in this
directory adds that a polynomial family is usually in OEIS as a coefficient
triangle.

What it should say: the same for constants, at the proposal stage rather than
the checking stage, and in both search forms. Record the negative in the
proposal. "OEIS does not have this" is a fact about whether the table is worth
making, and unlike most such facts it can be established in one call.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, through `urllib` on the runner (a previous lesson in
this directory records that `curl` gets a 403 from OEIS where `urllib` does
not); the control `1,1,2,3,5,8,13` returned 10 hits headed by A000045, so the
nulls are answers and not failures.
