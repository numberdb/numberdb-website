## Walking the T-numbers to see the corpus spends the whole rate limit, and the duplicate screen is what pays

What happened: the skill says there is no call that lists the corpus and that
to see everything you walk the T-numbers, so this run did exactly that --
`numberdb.table('T%d')` for `T1` to `T400`, eight threads, keeping only
`Title` and `Tags`. It worked and took about two minutes: 374 tables, the full
picture of what the corpus covers, which is genuinely what an ideation run
needs.

Every call after it failed:

    numberdb._errors.RateLimitError: too many requests; retry in 789s

and it stayed failing for the next half hour, because the window is rolling and
each retry is itself a request. The calls that were blocked were the ones that
matter: `already_here(name)`, the check that the family is not in the corpus
under another name, which is the single thing a proposal cannot be written
without.

The failure is safe -- `already_here` returns
`(could not ask the corpus: RateLimitError: ...)` rather than an empty list,
which an earlier lesson already made it do -- but the cost is an hour of a run
that has nothing else to do but wait.

What the skill says now: "There is no call that lists the corpus. To see
everything you walk the T-numbers." Nothing about what that costs.

What it should say: the walk is one request per table and the server's limit is
well below the size of the corpus, so it can be done once and not twice, and it
must be done *after* the searches the run actually depends on, not before.
`search_text` on a broad word returns up to twenty tables for one request, so
five or six searches see most of a subject for a fiftieth of the price. Keep
the titles in a file if a later run might want them.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `/tmp/list.py` (374 tables listed), then
`already_here('Arithmetic factors in the moments of the Riemann zeta
function')` refused four times over twenty-five minutes with the countdown
789s, 593s, 398s, 203s.

## An Euler product of a rational function of $p$ is one PARI call, and that decides whether a proposal is cheap or a project

What happened: sizing a proposal for the arithmetic factor

$$a_k=\prod_p\Bigl[(1-1/p)^{k^2}\sum_m\bigl(\Gamma(m+k)/(m!\Gamma(k))\bigr)^2p^{-m}\Bigr]$$

the direct product in `RealBallField(200)` to $p<10^6$ gave seven correct
digits, which made the proposal look like a page of acceleration work per
entry. It is not, for *integer* $k$: the local factor is then a rational
function of $p$, and

    prodeulerrat((p^2-1)/p^2)

returns $6/\pi^2$ to every one of the 120 digits PARI was asked for -- the
whole product, in one call, with the truncation handled inside. The same call
with $((p-1)^4(p^2+4p+1))/p^6$ and $((p-1)^9(p^3+9p^2+9p+1))/p^{12}$ gives
$a_3$ and $a_4$ to 120 digits.

The distinction that matters for a proposal is **rational in $p$ or not**. At
half-integer $k$ the same family's local factor is
$(1-1/p)^{k^2}{}_2F_1(k,k;1;1/p)$, `prodeulerrat` does not apply at all, and
the acceleration does have to be written by hand. Two halves of one table, two
methods, and it is the sort of thing a proposal should say rather than a build
discover.

The corpus already knew: T163, *Hardy--Littlewood singular series of prime
tuples*, says in its `rigour details` that its generic tail comes from
`prodeulerrat` at 140 digits with 100 written, and T166 uses the
Belabas--Cohen algorithm for the same shape of constant. Both claim
`heuristic (agreement-checked)` and neither claims `proven`, which is also the
answer to how strong a claim this route supports without a written tail bound.

What the skill says now: nothing about Euler products, in any section of it I
read. PARI appears as one of the systems a `Programs` section should carry an
incantation for.

What it should say: before proposing or building a constant defined by an
Euler product, ask whether the local factor is a rational function of $p$. If
it is, `prodeulerrat` gives 100 digits in one line and the honest rigour is
`heuristic (agreement-checked)` unless somebody writes the tail bound. If it
is not, the acceleration is real work and belongs in the size estimate.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `agents/sage.sh /tmp/chk7.py` through `sage.libs.pari`;
`prodeulerrat((p^2-1)/p^2)` and `6/Pi^2` printed identical 120-digit strings.
T163 and T166 read from the live corpus with `numberdb.table`.

## Anything that pulls in the symbolic ring is unavailable after `import numberdb.sage`, and that includes `PowerSeriesRing`

What happened: checking an identity about power series, the obvious imports

    from sage.functions.other import factorial, binomial

raised

    AttributeError: cannot access submodule 'function' of module
    'sage.symbolic' (most likely due to a circular import)

Replacing them with `sage.arith.misc.binomial` and `ZZ(n).factorial()` fixed
that line and the next one failed the same way, from a place with nothing
symbolic about it:

    P = PowerSeriesRing(QQ, 'x'); P.gen()**3

`PowerSeriesRing._element_constructor_` imports `sage.rings.lazy_series`, which
imports `sage.data_structures.stream`, which imports `sage.functions.other`,
which is the module that raises. So the failure is not in the import line a
reader would suspect; it is three levels down and only on the first coercion,
which means the ring constructs fine and the first arithmetic on it explodes.

What works: truncated polynomial arithmetic in `PolynomialRing(QQ, 'x')`,
expanding $1/(1-x)^{n}$ as $\sum\binom{m+n-1}{m}x^m$ by hand and cutting to a
fixed order. That verified the identity wanted here -- the local factor of the
moment-conjecture Euler product -- to order $x^{39}$ for seven values of the
parameter, in one call.

What the skill says now: it lists what the named imports do not bring -- root
finding, `RealBall.str`, `QQ('1.25')`. All three are *missing* things. This is a
different failure: the module is there and raises on import.

What it should say: name the rings you use, and expect anything that reaches
the symbolic ring to raise a circular-import `AttributeError` rather than an
`ImportError`. `sage.functions.*` is out, and so is `PowerSeriesRing`, whose
dependency on it is invisible until the first multiplication. Series work is
truncated polynomials.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `agents/sage.sh /tmp/chk.py`, two tracebacks both ending
at `sage/symbolic/expression.pyx", line 14220, in init
sage.symbolic.expression`.

## `barnes_g` is on `ComplexBall` and not on `RealBall`, which is the difference between `proven` and `heuristic`

What happened: a proposed table needs $G(1+k)^2/G(1+2k)$ for rational $k$, and
`RealBallField(200)(3.5)` has no `barnes_g` -- `dir()` on it shows nothing
matching "barnes" at all. The natural next move is mpmath's `barnesg`, which
works and is not interval arithmetic, so the table would have had to claim
`heuristic` for a quantity that is a perfectly ordinary special function.

`ComplexBallField` has both `barnes_g` and `log_barnes_g`:

    ComplexBallField(200)(3.5).barnes_g()
    [1.2596482574951921440863079510960698255629857900462586518557 +/- 3.05e-59]

so the value is `proven` after the same two lines the skill's own
`elliptic_k` skeleton uses: take the complex ball, assert the imaginary part
contains zero, return `.real()`.

What the skill says now: the generator skeleton reaches for `ComplexBallField`
to compute a *real* elliptic integral and does not say why, so it reads as an
accident of that one function.

What it should say: arb implements many special functions only on the complex
ball, and Sage exposes them only there. Before settling for `heuristic` because
`RealBallField` has no method for the function, check `ComplexBallField`; the
skeleton's `assert value.imag().contains_zero()` is the pattern, not a quirk of
`elliptic_k`.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `agents/sage.sh /tmp/chk4.py`. `[m for m in
dir(ComplexBallField(200)(1)) if 'barnes' in m.lower()]` gives `['barnes_g',
'log_barnes_g']`; the same on `RealBall` gives `[]`.

## An arXiv abstract page is not evidence that the paper names your family

What happened: every proposal has to cite a source that actually names the
family, and the natural citation for four proposals here is one paper. Against
its abstract page the check fails:

    source_names_it('Arithmetic factors in the moments of the Riemann zeta
                     function', 'https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206018')
    -> the source does not mention factors, riemann

which is true. The abstract of *Integral moments of $L$-functions* says
"moments", "characteristic polynomials", "unitary, orthogonal, or symplectic",
and never says "Riemann" or "factor"; those words are in the body, where the
quantity is defined. The same four names pass against the full-text rendering
at `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0206018`.

The trap is that the abstract page is the URL anybody has to hand, and the
check reads as "this family is not called that" when it means "the abstract is
280 words".

What the skill says now: it names Wikipedia, LMFDB, OEIS, MathWorld, mpmath or
a paper as acceptable sources, without distinguishing a paper from the page
that advertises it.

What it should say: when the source is a paper, check the name against the full
text -- `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/<id>` or `arxiv.org/e-print/<id>` -- and
cite the paper in `References` by `arxiv` and `doi`, not by the rendering URL.
An encyclopedia article is the whole source; an abstract page is not.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `/tmp/src2.py` (four failures against `/abs/`) and
`/tmp/src3.py` (the same four names, `None` against the ar5iv rendering).
