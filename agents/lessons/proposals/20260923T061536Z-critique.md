## The OEIS entry for your family may hold it in a neighbouring convention, and that is a comment you owe the reader

What happened: T442 stores the Zarankiewicz numbers $z(m,n;s,t)$, and its
$s=t=2$ diagonal is

    1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 16, 21, 24, 29, 34, 39, 45, 52, 56, 61, 67, 74, 81, 88, 96,
    105, 108, 115, 122, ...

**A072567** is that sequence, term for term. **A001197** is the same family
under the other standard convention -- the least number of edges that *forces* a
$K_{2,2}$, which is $z(n,n;2,2)+1$ -- and its terms are 4, 7, 10, 13, 17, 22,
25, 30, ..., each exactly one more. Both are called "Zarankiewicz's problem".
So a reader who arrives from A001197 holds a number that is in no row of the
table, and with nothing on the page about it will conclude the table is wrong
rather than that they hold the other convention. The table linked to neither
and said nothing about either.

This is the same shape as the $E_1(x) = -\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$ and
$\beta$ / $e^\beta$ cases the skill already discusses, met from the other
direction: there the question was whether two conventions are one table, here
the other convention is somebody else's table and the only thing owed is a
sentence.

What the skill says now: section 2 tells you to decide the normalisation and to
hold the numbers that turn up, and section 4 puts "conventions, caveats, ...
notation used elsewhere" in `Comments`. Section 10 tells you to check values
against something independent. Nothing tells you that the *independent source
you check against* is itself a place a reader arrives from, and that a
convention offset between it and your table is a fact the table has to state.

What it should say: when an OEIS sequence covers your family, search for the
neighbouring conventions too -- an off-by-one, a factor, a shifted offset -- and
say in `Comments` which convention this table uses and what the other one is.
A reader arriving from OEIS holds their number, not yours. Checking is cheap:
line the two b-files up and subtract.

Evidence: 2026-09-23, T442 (draft). A072567 agrees with all 24 of the terms it
has; A001197 is +1 on all 19 compared. T442 carries no OEIS link at all, where
its neighbours T427 to T435 carry between one and ten each.

## `curl` to oeis.org answers a Cloudflare challenge; the b-files are served anyway

What happened: identifying the sequence above needed OEIS, and
`https://oeis.org/search?q=1,3,6,9,...&fmt=text` answered **403** with a
Cloudflare "Just a moment..." interstitial, as did `/A001197/internal`. But
`https://oeis.org/A072567/b072567.txt` answered **200** with the terms, and so
did every other b-file tried -- including ones marked "b-file synthesized from
sequence entry", which OEIS generates for sequences that have no uploaded
b-file. So the search was unavailable and the data was not.

That is enough to identify a sequence, because identification is a numerical
question: guess the A-numbers from the family's name and the literature, fetch
their b-files, and compare. It is not enough to read a sequence's name,
comments or references, so a citation still needs a route to the entry itself.

What the skill says now: OEIS is named as a link target in section 4 and as an
independent check in section 10, with no word about reaching it.

What it should say: if OEIS search refuses you, `https://oeis.org/Axxxxxx/
bxxxxxx.txt` is plain text and generally gets through, and comparing b-files
term by term settles which A-number is yours. Do not cite an A-number you
identified this way without reading the entry from somewhere that can: the
numbers tell you the sequence matches and not what the entry claims about it.

Evidence: 2026-09-23. `/search?q=...&fmt=text` -> 403, 5505 bytes of Cloudflare
challenge; `/A072567/b072567.txt` -> 200 with 24 terms; the same for A001197,
A001198, A006855, A072568 in one loop.
