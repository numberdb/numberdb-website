# Lessons proposed by the T338 critique, 2026-09-19

## A `Similar tables` relation says which table holds what, and "here" is the easy word to get backwards

What happened: T338 stores the Segre classes $s_n$ as polynomials in the Chern
classes -- its rows are `-c1`, `c1^2 - c2`, `-c1^3 + 2*c1*c2 - c3`. Its
`Similar tables` entry for `Complete_homogeneous_symmetric_polynomials` reads

    give the $h_n$ whose signed Chern-root expressions are stored here

and that is backwards. Nothing is stored here in the Chern *roots*; the linked
table is the one whose rows are root expressions (`x1 + x2`,
`x1^2 + x1*x2 + x2^2`, ...). The sentence describes the page a reader is about
to click through to, while the word "here" promises it is describing the page
they are on.

The shape is what makes it easy. A relation gloss necessarily mentions two
tables' contents in one sentence, and it is rendered with the linked table's
name in front of it -- "Complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials -- give the
..." -- so the author is writing from the other table's point of view at the
moment they reach the word "here", which points back at their own. Neither the
audit nor any renderer can check it: both tables exist, the slug resolves, the
sentence parses, and the mathematics named in it is true of *somebody*.

The corpus has the correct shape written down two tables away.
`Chern_character_polynomials` glosses the same kind of relation as "give the
power sums $p_n$ whose **Chern-class expressions** determine these
polynomials" -- same frame, and right about which table holds the
root-or-class form.

What the skill says now: that `Similar tables` "states the *relation*, in
words", with an example, and that the sentence "is the valuable part". Under
"Name the thing, do not point at it" it rules out "the former", "the latter",
"as above" and "the second one", but all of those are pointers *within a
formula on the same page*. It says nothing about the deictics a relation gloss
runs on -- "here", "these", "this table" -- which point across a link.

What it should say: a relation is a claim about both tables, so read it back
with each name substituted in. "Here", "these" and "this table" mean the table
you are writing, never the one you are linking to; if the sentence describes
what the *other* table holds, name it or say "there". The cheap check is to
open the linked table and look at its rows: if the form your sentence attributes
to "here" is what is printed *there*, the sentence is inverted.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, T338 critique. `GET /api/table?id=T338` (rows above);
`curl https://numberdb.org/Complete_homogeneous_symmetric_polynomials` (rows
`x1`, `x1 + x2`, `x1^2 + x1*x2 + x2^2`, ... in $x_1,\dots,x_n$);
`GET /api/table?id=Chern_character_polynomials` for the correct frame.
`GET /api/table/T338/audit` returns `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`.

## A stored value copied into `Formulas` is the same fault as one copied into a comment

What happened: T338's Formulas section ends with

    (4) $s_1=-c_1$, $s_2=c_1^2-c_2$, and $s_3=-c_1^3+2c_1c_2-c_3$.

which is rows 1, 2 and 3 of the same page, restated about four inches below
where they are printed, in a second notation. It is not an identity, a closed
form, a generating function or a recurrence; Formula (1)'s recurrence already
produces all three in one line. Nothing keeps the two copies in step, so a
correction to a row would leave the page contradicting itself within one
screen. Neither of the two published tables T338 is modelled on
(`Todd_polynomials`, `Chern_character_polynomials`) carries an analogue.

What the skill says now: "**A value belongs in a row, not in a comment.** ... A
number written into an entry comment answers no search, carries no type, and
cannot be cited". That rule is written for one destination, `Comments`, and the
reasons given are about entry comments specifically. The section table's
`Formulas` row says the section holds "identities, closed forms, generating
functions, recurrences" and not "prose about them", which excludes the wrong
thing here: the fault is not prose, it is data.

What it should say: extend the existing sentence past comments. A value that is
already a row does not belong anywhere else in the document -- not in a
comment, not as a `Formulas` entry. `Formulas` is for what generates the rows,
not for a sample of them, and a formula that is a list of particular entries is
a second copy nobody maintains. Where the intent is to show the typeset form of
entries that render in the corpus's ASCII polynomial notation, that is a job
for one comment saying `c1` means $c_1$, not for reprinting rows.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, T338 critique. T338 `Numbers` keys `1`, `2`, `3` are
`-c1`, `c1^2 - c2`, `-c1^3 + 2*c1*c2 - c3`; `Formulas[formula-first]` restates
the three. The rendered page (draft rebuild, 6 records, status 200, 29,326
bytes) prints both within one screen. `Todd_polynomials` and
`Chern_character_polynomials`, both published and both of this family, have no
such formula.
