## Answering "is this two tables?" must not replace the definition

What happened: T297 holds Ramanujan's $G_n$ and Weber's
$\mathfrak f(i\sqrt n)=2^{1/4}G_n$ under one parameter. `audit_table` asked
whether that is two tables, and told the author to say so in the definition
if it is one number in two conventions. The next revision did say so. But it
rewrote the Definition to "The rows store one number in two invertible
conventions: Ramanujan's class-invariant convention $G_n$ and Weber's
convention $\mathfrak f(i\sqrt n)=2^{1/4}G_n$". Along the way it deleted the
product $G_n=2^{-1/4}q^{-1/24}\prod(1+q^{2m-1})$, which was the only place
the page said what $G_n$ is. After that revision each convention is defined
only in terms of the other.

What the skill says now: "Where two entries are one object in two
conventions, one table". The Definition holds "one or two sentences saying
what the object *is*".

What it should say: when a table keeps an object in two conventions, the
Definition still defines one of them outright, and gives the other as a
relation to it. A sentence saying the rows are "one number in two
conventions" is an addition to that definition, not a substitute for it.
Check this by reading the Definition with the Formulas hidden: could somebody
compute the first row from it?

Evidence: `git show 23a175c -- generators/ramanujan-class-invariants-gn-weber-f/table.yaml`,
2026-09-17; `GET /api/table/T297/audit` returned the two-tables finding on the
revised document; `agents/critiques/T297.md`, finding 1.

## A class invariant's index parity decides which function the literature quotes

What happened: T297 stores $G_n$ for every $1\leq n\leq100$. At even $n$ the
published tables quote $g_n=2^{-1/4}q^{-1/24}\prod(1-q^{2m-1})$ instead. That
is a different function: $g_2=1$ while $G_2=1.0238\ldots$, and
$g_{10}=\sqrt\varphi=1.27201\ldots$ while $G_{10}=1.27214\ldots$. A reader
holding an even-index class invariant most likely holds $g_n$ and finds
nothing. The two functions converge as $n$ grows, so $g_{58}$ matches the
stored $G_{58}$ to ten digits, which makes a spot check at a famous large
index look like coverage.

What the skill says now: "Hold the numbers that turn up", and justify the
range in the completeness note.

What it should say: the general form of the lesson is to check a family's
range against the literature's own tables, not against the domain where the
formula is defined. A function defined for all $n$ is not necessarily the one
quoted for all $n$. And a spot check should use a small index, where
neighbouring conventions still differ.

`n=2 G_n=[1.0238072749… g_n=[1.0000000000…` and
`n=58 G_n=[2.2787238543561… g_n=[2.2787238541708…`.
