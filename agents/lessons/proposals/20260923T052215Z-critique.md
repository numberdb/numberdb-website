# Lessons proposed by the T437 critique, 2026-09-23

Run `20260923T052215Z`, stage three, table T437 (values of the even Mathieu
functions). Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in
force.

## State the grid in the unit you chose it in, not in the one you store

What happened: T437 stores the argument $z$ as the parameter `z_over_pi`,
because $z/\pi$ is a rational and $z$ is not. Its completeness note therefore
reads "it holds every entry with … $z/\pi\in\{0,1/36,2/36,\ldots,1/2\}$",
which is true and complete and tells a reader nothing about why those
arguments. The grid was not chosen in thirty-sixths of $\pi$: the generator
computes `degrees = 5.0 * step`, the table's own `Programs` block works in
degrees, and one of its two comments warns that software taking degrees must
convert. The grid is $z$ in steps of $5^\circ$ from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ --
which is how Mathieu functions are tabulated, and the one fact that lets a
reader holding $\mathrm{ce}_2(35^\circ,2)$ see at a glance that it is here.
The transform ate the argument, and the note is the only place the argument
could have survived.

What the skill says now: "let the arguments choose it rather than the count",
and "Say which range you chose and why in the completeness note. That sentence
is the argument, and it is what a reader checking whether their own number
belongs here actually reads." Both are about *choosing* the range. Neither says
what happens when the parameter you store is a transform of the one you chose
in.

What it should say: where the stored parameter is a transform of the grid you
actually chose -- $z/\pi$ for an angle, $1/s$ for a rate, $\log x$ for a
length -- say the grid in both units in the completeness note. The stored form
is what makes the values rational and the note true; the chosen form is what
makes it an argument. A list of denominators that nobody picked reads as
counting, which is the thing the rule already forbids, and the reader cannot
tell the difference from outside.

Evidence: T437 `complete-note`, "$z/\pi\in\{0,1/36,2/36,\ldots,1/2\}$", against
`generators/values-even-mathieu-functions/generate.py` line 253,
`degrees = 5.0 * step`, and its `program-scipy`, `z_degrees = 30.0  # z = pi/6`.
The same table's `comment-argument` already tells the reader that degrees are
the unit they are likely holding.

## A `CITE{}` to a formula key resolves, and is usually better than an external link

What happened: T437's definition ends "with the normalization of
CITE{DLMF28.4}", sending the reader to DLMF for
$\int_0^{2\pi}\mathrm{ce}_n(z,q)^2\,dz=\pi$ -- which the table states itself,
as `formula-normalisation`. `CITE{formula-normalisation}` would have rendered
as a link reading "(2)", pointing at the table's own statement of it. The same
table's `rigour details` does exactly this twice, so the author knew the
renderer accepts it there and did not use it in the definition.

What the skill says now: "**Link the first mention of a thing to what explains
it.** A table if the corpus holds one; otherwise a reference the table already
declares." It names two targets, a table and a reference, and does not mention
that a table's own `Formulas` and `Comments` keys are the third and nearest.

What it should say: `CITE{}` resolves a formula or comment key as well as a
reference key, and renders as the numbered link to that block. When the thing
being cited is something this table already states, cite the block rather than
the source: the reader stays on the page, and the two copies cannot drift
apart. Cite outward for what the table does *not* state.

Evidence: T437 `rigour details`, `CITE{formula-zero-q}` and
`CITE{formula-normalisation}`, render as `(3)` and `(2)` linked to
`#formula-zero-q` and `#formula-normalisation`; checked in `/preview` on
2026-09-23. The same behaviour was noticed and checked independently by the
T435 critique the same day, which suggests it is not discoverable from the
skill.
