## Lead `rigour details` with the check: a one-paragraph note folds after its first *sentence*

What happened: T336's `rigour details` is one paragraph of 435 characters,
written in the order the work was done -- the generator first, the checks
after. The site folds it, and what a reader sees with the disclosure shut is

    The generator computes exact rational coefficients in
    $\mathbb Q[p_1,\dots,p_6]$ from Formula (1).

which says the table was computed from a formula the table itself states, and
is evidence of nothing. Behind **more** is the whole case: three checks, one
of them independent (substituting the Pontryagin classes of $\mathbb{CP}^{2m}$
and getting signature 1). The threshold is `FOLD_ABOVE = 400` in
`numberdb_app/prose.py` and it counts *rendered* characters, so the note is
435 as typed and 600 as rendered: each `CITE{}` becomes a 45-character anchor,
and four of them account for about 180. With no blank line in the note there
is no second paragraph to fold, so the site cuts at the last sentence break
that fits, which here is the only one, 175 characters in.

Five tables in one family have now shipped the argument behind the fold --
T331, T332, T334, T335 and T336 -- and T335 is published with it today. Each
critique found it separately, which means the note is being written in the
order of the work every time and no rule is stopping it.

What the skill says now: in the table of where things go, "`rigour details` |
how the digits were obtained, at whatever length that needs: blank lines make
paragraphs, `- ` makes a list, backticks mark code, and a long note folds
after its first paragraph". So the fold is mentioned, and what it says is
wrong for the commonest case: a note with no blank line in it has no second
paragraph, and it folds after its first *sentence*.

What it should say: the fold decides what your evidence is, because the
opening sentence is the only part most readers see. Lead with the check that
does not depend on the generator -- an outside source, an independent
identity, a known value reproduced -- and put "the generator computes ..."
last, where folding it costs nothing. Two numbers make it concrete: the
threshold is 400 rendered characters, and every `CITE{}` costs about 45 of
them, so a note with four citations folds at roughly 250 characters of your
own prose.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, T336 critique. `/preview?table=` with T336's
`Data properties` gives `<p class="prose-paragraph">` of 175 rendered
characters and `<details class="prose-more">` of 425. The same note with its
two sentences swapped, sent to the same route, renders with the check visible.
The live pages of T334 (`https://numberdb.org/T334`, check first) and T335
(`https://numberdb.org/T335`, generator first) are the two orders side by
side in the published corpus.

## Two Wikipedia links can be one article: check for a redirect before declaring both

What happened: T336 declares two of its three `Links` as

    LGenusWiki:  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-genus
    GenusWiki:   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_of_a_multiplicative_sequence

and the page prints them as `[1]` and `[2]`, two numbered sources. They are
one page: `/wiki/L-genus` is a redirect to `Genus of a multiplicative
sequence#L genus`. The table's Definition cites `[1]` and its Formula (2)
cites `[2]` for statements on the same article, and `rigour details` cites
`[1]` for a fact that `[2]`'s article carries, so a reader has no way to see
that following either lands in the same place.

A status check does not find this. MediaWiki resolves a redirect server-side,
so `curl -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}'` on the redirect answers `200` and
`-L` reports the same URL as final; the redirect is invisible to HTTP. What
finds it is the raw wikitext:

    $ curl -s "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-genus?action=raw"
    #redirect[[Genus of a multiplicative sequence#L genus]]

55 bytes, and the whole article is 14,709.

What the skill says now: `Links` holds "sources outside: Wikipedia, LMFDB,
OEIS, MathWorld", and "Link outward only to sources that will still exist".
Nothing about two links being the same source.

What it should say: before declaring two links to one site, check they are
distinct pages. On Wikipedia, `?action=raw` on each URL; a body beginning
`#redirect` or `#REDIRECT` names the page you are actually citing. Citing a
redirect is not wrong, but two numbered references to one article present it
as two independent sources, and a table whose evidence reads "checked against
[1] and [2]" is then claiming more than it has. If a section is what you mean,
cite the article once with the `#anchor` on the URL and say which section in
the title.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, T336 critique. `curl
"https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-genus?action=raw"` returned the redirect
above, 55 bytes, while the same call on
`Genus_of_a_multiplicative_sequence` returned 14,709 bytes containing the
`==L genus==` section that the table's Definition, Formula (2) and
`rigour details` all draw on. T334 cites the same pair.
