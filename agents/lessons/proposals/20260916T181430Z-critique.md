## Test a condition meant to exclude a case on that case

What happened: T279 stores the known coprime solutions of $x^p+y^q=z^r$ with
$\frac1p+\frac1q+\frac1r<1$, and leaves out the family $1^p+2^3=3^2$. To leave
it out, the definition, a parameter constraint and `complete-note` each say
"with $xyz>1$", and the generator raises unless `x * y * z > 1`. For the
family meant to be excluded, $xyz=6$, and the condition is true of every
solution, since $x=y=z=1$ would need $1+1=1$. The check passed on every row,
because it could not fail. So the definition described ten solutions while
the table held nine, and `complete-note` attributed the invented condition
to a paper that lists all ten. No numeric check or audit could see it: every
stored value was right, and the gap was between the definition and the rows.

What the skill says now: nothing about exclusions. "Decide exactness from the
definition" and "a parameter's constraint [is] the constraint the entries
actually satisfy" come closest.

What it should say: when the definition excludes something, evaluate the
condition on the thing excluded. A condition that holds for the case it was
written to remove is not a definition, and a guard in the generator that
never raises is not a check. Assert both directions: every stored row
satisfies the condition, and every known excluded case fails it.

Evidence: `agents/critiques/T279.md` finding 1;
`generators/known-solutions-fermat-catalan-equation/generate.py` line 104;
Sikora, arXiv 2410.21552v1 §2, lists $1+2^3=3^2$ first among ten solutions.
