## The definition on Wikipedia is a hypothesis, and the family's own orthogonality is the test

What happened: a batch of six proposals for the discrete orthogonal
polynomials was screened, and the English Wikipedia article was taken as the
source for each. Three of the five formulas it gives are not the polynomial
the corpus would want to store, and none of the three is flagged on the page:

* **Hahn.** The article gives
  $Q_n(x;\alpha,\beta,N)={}_3F_2(-n,-x,n+\alpha+\beta+1;\alpha+1,-N+1;1)$. With
  $-N+1$ the series divides by zero for $n\geq N$, and of the 40 pairs
  $(m,n)$ it can evaluate at all, **all 40** fail orthogonality on
  $\{0,\dots,N\}$ against the weight the same article names. With $-N$, as in
  Koekoek–Lesky–Swarttouw chapter 9.5 and DLMF §18.20.2, all 57 pairs are
  orthogonal.
* **Meixner.** The article's sum is $(\beta)_n$ times the KLS ${}_2F_1$ — a
  different polynomial, not a different way of writing one. KLS has
  $M_2(x;1,\tfrac12)=\tfrac12x^2-\tfrac52x+1$ and the article has $x^2-5x+2$.
  Checked over 216 parameter triples; the ratio is $(\beta)_n$ every time.
* **Charlier.** The article gives
  $C_n(x;\mu)=(-1)^nn!L_n^{(-1-x)}(-1/\mu)$ beside its own ${}_2F_0$
  definition, and the two disagree at $n=1$. What holds is
  $C_n(x;a)=n!(-a)^{-n}L_n^{(x-n)}(a)$.

A fourth is not an error but is a trap of the same kind: the article writes
the Wilson polynomial as $p_n(t^2)$ where KLS writes $W_n(x^2)$, with
$t=ix$, so $p_n(u)=W_n(-u)$ and the coefficients alternate in sign. For a
table of polynomials that is the difference between a search matching and a
search not matching.

What the skill says now: "Verify a claim before writing it into a table,
including one somebody suggested. A suggestion is a hypothesis." That is about
a suggestion from a person. It also says to check values against something
independent, which is about the numbers rather than the definition.

What it should say: the same rule applies to the *definition*, and the source
it most often applies to is the encyclopedia article a proposal cites. For a
family defined by orthogonality, the defining property is itself the test and
it is cheap: compute the sum or integral the source names, exactly, over the
family's own weight, for a handful of parameters, and check that distinct
degrees pair to zero and equal degrees do not. That one computation caught
all three errors above. Where two sources disagree by a scalar, decide which
to store on whether the factor is *guessable from a number in hand* — a power
of a parameter the reader already has, yes; a $\binom{N}{k}(q-1)^k$, no, and
then it is two tables as T103 and T104 already are.

Evidence: 2026-09-16, `/tmp/askey_check2.py` and `/tmp/askey_check4.py` under
`agents/sage.sh`. "alpha=0 beta=0 N=6: with -N, 0 of 21 pairs non-orthogonal
(0 undefined); with -N+1, 15 of 15 (6 undefined)", and the same at $(1,2,6)$
and $(\tfrac12,\tfrac32,5)$. "Wikipedia M_n = (beta)_n * KLS M_n : 0 failures
of 216". "a=1: KLS 9.14.11 ... True ; Wikipedia ... False", and likewise at
$a=2$ and $a=\tfrac12$. "(a,b,c,d)=(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2): equal? False
W(-u) = p(u)? True".

## When OEIS refuses a search, its b-files still answer

What happened: an accepted lesson in `PROPOSALS.md` says to search OEIS for a
polynomial family's coefficient triangle before writing the checks, and cites
`https://oeis.org/search?q=hilbert+class+polynomial` working on 2026-09-01.
On 2026-09-16 every HTML endpoint of oeis.org answered **403** with a
Cloudflare interstitial from this machine: `/search` with `fmt=text`, with
`fmt=json` and with a browser `User-Agent`, an A-number's own page
(`/A046716/internal`), and the bulk `names.gz`. Concluding "no OEIS check is
possible" would have been wrong. The static b-file,
`https://oeis.org/A046716/b046716.txt`, answered **200** with 40 KB, and 91
terms of it match the computed Charlier coefficients exactly.

What the skill says now: nothing about OEIS being unreachable. The accepted
lesson above assumes search works.

What it should say: the b-file is served from a different path than the pages
and survives a challenge the search interface does not, so a check against a
*known* A-number is still available when the site will not be searched. What
is lost is finding the A-number from the terms, and what is lost with it is
the *name*: a sequence whose b-file matches is evidence that the numbers
agree and not that the sequence is the one you think, so cite it in `Links`
only after somebody has read its name.

Evidence: 2026-09-16. `curl -o /tmp/names.gz https://oeis.org/names.gz` ->
`403 5252`, the body an HTML challenge page despite the `.gz` name.
`https://oeis.org/A046716/b046716.txt` -> `200 40724`. `/tmp/askey_check.py`
comparison: "rows 0..12 match A046716 b-file: True (91 terms)".

## A Sage power series will not exponentiate under named-ring imports

What happened: a check wrote $e^t(1-t/a)^x$ as `t.exp() * (1 - t/a)**x` in a
`PowerSeriesRing(QQ, 't')`, in a script that imports the rings by name as the
skill's generator template does. It raised

    AttributeError: cannot access submodule 'function' of module
    'sage.symbolic' (most likely due to a circular import)

from `PowerSeries.exp` -> `solve_linear_de` -> `sage.rings.lazy_series` ->
`sage.data_structures.stream` -> `sage.functions.other` ->
`sage.functions.trig` -> `sage.symbolic.function`. The value was never
symbolic; `exp` merely reaches a module that needs `sage.all` to have run.
Writing the series out by hand — coefficients $1/n!$, multiplied by a
truncated polynomial power — took four lines and ran.

What the skill says now: "Name the rings you use, rather than importing
`sage.all`", with a list of what the named imports do not bring: root finding,
`RealBall.str`, and `QQ('1.25')`.

What it should say: add `PowerSeries.exp` and `PowerSeries.log` to that list,
and the general shape of it — anything reaching `sage.functions` or
`sage.symbolic` from inside a ring method fails the same way, and the failure
names a circular import rather than a missing import, so it does not look like
the same problem. A generating-function check is the common case; write the
series by hand rather than reaching for `exp`.

Evidence: 2026-09-16, `/tmp/askey_check3.py` under `agents/sage.sh`, first
run. The same script with `exp_t = [QQ(1)/QQ(fact(n)) for n in range(TERMS)]`
and a four-line truncated multiply: "Charlier sum_n C_n(x;a) t^n/n! =
e^t (1-t/a)^x : 0 failures of 36".
