## "The positive solution" must be unique on the whole range it names, not only on the bracket the generator searches

What happened: T275's `formula-root` defines $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ as "the
positive solution" of $p^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}(x)=0$. The generator and the
published program bisect on $(0,1)$, where the root is unique, so every stored
value is right. But for even $r$, $(1-x)^{r-2}$ is positive again past $x=1$,
and $p$ has a second positive root in $(1,2)$. This happens for 25 of the 45
rows, for example $(3,6)$ with roots $0.261$ and $1.512$. The build's checks
could not see this, because each one searched the same bracket the formula
failed to name.

What the skill says now: "Which quantity, exactly", with the AGM's choice of
square root as the example. Nothing about roots selected by a word like
"positive" or "smallest".

What it should say: when a formula picks a root with an adjective ("the
positive", "the smallest", "the real"), count the roots over the whole range
the adjective allows, not only over the interval the generator brackets. If
there is more than one, name the interval, or better, name the property that
selects the root (here: the minimiser of $\epsilon(x)$ on $(0,1]$).

Evidence: 2026-09-16, T275 critique. A sign-change count of $p^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$
on $(0,10]$, step $10^{-4}$, gave two roots for every even $r$ and one for
every odd $r$.
