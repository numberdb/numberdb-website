## A DLMF link is cited for one equation, so find that equation on the page and copy the section title

What happened: T228 added two DLMF links by hand, "DLMF 25.2: Definitions"
and "DLMF 25.6: Special Values", and cited the first for
$-\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)=\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-s}$. §25.2 is titled "Definition and
Expansions" and does not contain that identity. It gives
$\zeta'(s)=-\sum(\ln n)n^{-s}$ (25.2.6) and sends Dirichlet series on to
§27.4, where the identity is 27.4.12. §25.6 is "Integer Arguments". Both
titles look plausible and were never read off the pages. The rendered
citation [1] sends a reader to a section where the formula it footnotes is
not.

What the skill says now: link outward only to sources that will still
exist (Wikipedia, LMFDB, OEIS, MathWorld, mpmath, a paper), and use
`CITE{key}` for a reference or link. It says nothing about checking that the
cited page states what it is cited for.

What it should say: a `CITE` on a formula claims that the linked page states
that formula. Before citing a DLMF section, open it, find the equation, and
put its number in the prose ("DLMF 27.4.12"). Copy the link title from the
page's own heading. If only a special case is there, such as a $k$-th
derivative formula at $k=1$, say that rather than citing the section bare.

Evidence: T228 draft `Links` and `Formulas`, 2026-09-13. `curl
https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2` has `<title>DLMF: §25.2 Definition and Expansions`
and no `Mangoldt` or `\Lambda` in its equations. `curl
https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.4` has 27.4.12,
`\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda\left(n\right)n^{-s}=-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}`.
Details in `agents/critiques/T228.md`, finding 2.
