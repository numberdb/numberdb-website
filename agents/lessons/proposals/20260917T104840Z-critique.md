## A nome has two conventions, and the CM point a comment names must be the one the formulas use

What happened: T298 holds $R(e^{-\pi\sqrt r})$. Its comment said "the CM point
is $\tau=i\sqrt r$, so the nome is $q=e^{\pi i\tau}$", following the
elliptic-modulus tables. Its $j$-formula, correctly, took $q=e^{2\pi iz}$ with
$z=i\sqrt r/2$, which is the variable in which $R$ is modular. Both sentences
are true. But a reader who follows the comment to the table of singular moduli
expects $j(i)=1728$ at $r=1$ and finds 287496 ($j(2i)$) there. The row with
1728 is $r=4$. The order the comment named, $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-r}]$, is the
order of $\tau$, not of $z$.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing" is to be
decided and written down, but nothing about the same page using two of them.

What it should say: where a family is indexed by a nome, write the argument in
the one convention the page's formulas use: $q=e^{2\pi iz}$ for modular forms
and functions, and $q=e^{\pi i\tau}$ for theta and elliptic functions. Where
another table uses the other convention, say "this is the nome $e^{\pi i\tau}$
of $\tau=\dots$" once, as a translation. Check any claim about a CM point (its
order, its discriminant, the $j$-value it gives) at one small row against a
table of known values before writing it.

Evidence: T298 draft, 2026-09-17. Formula (2) evaluated in Sage on the stored
values gives 287496 at $r=1$, 8000 at $r=2$, and 1728 at $r=4$. Comment (1)
read "The CM point is $\tau=i\sqrt r$ ... The order is
$\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-r}]$". See `agents/critiques/T298.md`, finding 1.
