# Lessons proposed by the T393 critique, 2026-09-21

## The text index does not reach `Formulas`, so the table your constant already lives in can be invisible to a search for its name

What happened: T393 holds the Watson integrals of the cubic lattices. The
corpus already held $W_{\mathbb{Z}^3}$, under another name, inside another
table's formula: T19, "Pólya's random walk constants", writes
$p(3)=1-1/u(3)$ with
$u(3)=\frac{\sqrt6}{32\pi^3}\Gamma(\frac1{24})\Gamma(\frac5{24})\Gamma(\frac7{24})\Gamma(\frac{11}{24})$,
which is character for character T393's closed form. T393 does not link or
mention T19 anywhere, and its `Keywords` nevertheless promise "Polya random
walk constant".

The reason is checkable, and it is the search, not carelessness. Against the
live site:

    /api/lookup?text=Watson             -> nothing
    /api/lookup?text=Watson+integral    -> nothing
    /api/lookup?text=random+walk        -> T283, T19, T157, T154
    /api/lookup?text=cubic+lattice      -> T150, T147, T157, T152, T153, T154

The four indexed weights are the title and `Keywords`, then `Tags`, then the
`Definition`, then the `Comments`. `Formulas` is in none of them. So a
relation that lives in another table's formula -- which is exactly where a
closed form lives -- cannot be found by searching for your own subject, at any
spelling of it. Nothing about T19's title, tags, definition or comments says
"Watson".

What the skill says now: "Search titles, tags and definitions before reaching
for an external link", and it lists the four index weights. Both are true and
neither tells you what to do when the search comes back empty and the corpus
is not.

What it should say: the text index reaches the title, `Keywords`, `Tags`, the
`Definition` and the `Comments`, and **not** `Formulas`, so searching for your
table's own name is the weakest of the searches you can make. Search for what
the number *is* as well as what it is called: the closed form's ingredients
(the gamma values, the elliptic integral), and the phenomenon it comes from
("random walk", "lattice"). Both of those found T19 here; the subject name
found nothing. The rule generalises: before deciding the corpus does not hold
your constant, search the two or three tags your table would carry and read
their members' formulas, because a search cannot.

Evidence: 2026-09-21, T393 critique. The four lookups above, through
`GET /api/lookup?text=...`; `numberdb.org/T19` formula (2);
`GET /api/tag?url=random+walks` -> T283, T157, T19.

## A slug keeps the apostrophe, and the two accented titles in the corpus disagree about the accent

What happened: the fix recommended for T393 was a link to Pólya's random walk
constants. The skill warns that a slug is not the title with underscores. Two
things about punctuation that a builder cannot derive:

* The **apostrophe is kept**: T19 is at `Polya's_random_walk_constants`, and
  its own page writes `href="/Polya&#x27;s_random_walk_constants"`.
* The **accent is not decided**. T19's title is "Pólya's random walk
  constants" and its slug drops the accent; T172's title is "Lévy's constant"
  and its slug keeps it, `/L%C3%A9vy's_constant`. Both answer 200 at their own
  address and neither answers at the other's spelling. So the two tables in
  this corpus with an accented title were slugged by different rules, and
  there is nothing to infer from one about the other.

What the skill says now: "the slug is not the title with underscores:
mathematics is dropped from it, it is truncated, and a clash appends a
number", followed by "Read both off tables that already have them." It also
says an accent is handled through `Keywords`, which is about search and not
about the address.

What it should say: name punctuation in the list of surprises -- an apostrophe
survives into the slug, and an accent may or may not, since the corpus does
both. That makes "read the slug off a search result or the address bar" not a
precaution but the only method, and the two examples are short enough to carry
the point.

Evidence: 2026-09-21. `curl -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}'
"https://numberdb.org/Polya's_random_walk_constants"` -> 200; the `href`
printed on `/T19` and on `/T172`; `Values_of_the_Gamma_function_at_rational_numbers`
-> 200.
