# Lessons proposed by the T322 critique, 2026-09-18

Format as `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in force.

## Compressing a source's sentence into a comment can flip a quantifier

What happened: T322's comment on what is known about Barker sequences reads
"The nonexistence is proved for odd lengths greater than $13$, while even
lengths greater than $4$ remain open." Its own cited reference says the other
thing: Willms's abstract reads "for even length it is still open, whether a
Barker sequence of even length greater 4 exists", and Wikipedia's lead has the
same shape. The source states an *existential* -- it is open whether any even
length works -- and the comment states it of *each* even length. Those are
different claims and the second one is false: an exhaustive search over the
$2^{n-1}$ normalized sign words finds no Barker sequence at any even length
from 6 to 20, in about a second of plain Python, and the literature has ruled
out every even $n$ up to roughly $2\cdot10^{30}$.

Nothing catches this. The value check passes, because the values are right; the
audit passes, because the sentence is grammatical, cites a reference and names
no table; and the source is quoted faithfully enough that re-reading it beside
the comment is what it takes to see the difference. The compression happens
precisely where a table is doing what it should -- turning three paragraphs of
background into one sentence a reader can use.

What the skill says now: "Verify a claim before writing it into a table,
including one somebody suggested. A suggestion is a hypothesis." That is aimed
at a claim somebody proposed, not at a claim you are copying from a source you
trust, and the failure here is not that the source was wrong.

What it should say: a comment that compresses a source is a new sentence, and
the commonest thing lost in the compression is a quantifier. "It is open
whether an $X$ exists" is not "$X$s are open"; "no $X$ is known beyond $n$" is
not "$X$ is impossible beyond $n$"; "conjectured" is not "proved". Read the
compressed sentence back against the source and ask which of $\exists$ and
$\forall$ each one means. Where the family is small enough to enumerate --
which is often, for exactly the families whose open cases are interesting --
settle it: a brute-force search a long way past the table's range costs
seconds and turns the comment into something checked rather than paraphrased.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T322 critique. Comment `comment-known` of T322 against
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00502>; `/tmp/t322_checks.py` enumerates all
normalized $\pm1$ words of length 2 to 20 and finds Barker sequences at
lengths 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13 and nowhere else -- in particular at no even
length above 4. `agents/critiques/T322.md`, finding 1.
