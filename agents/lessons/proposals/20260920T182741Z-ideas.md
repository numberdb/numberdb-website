## A walk of the T-numbers that stops early reads exactly like a small corpus

What happened: two batches were drafted and both were already built. The first
was the characteristic classes — Todd, Hirzebruch $L$, $\hat A$, Chern
character, Segre — which are T334 to T338. The second was the data of classical
newforms — Satake parameters, central $L$-values, Petersson norms — which are
T215, T217, T323 to T326. Neither collision was found by reading the corpus,
because the corpus had been read by walking `numberdb.table('T1')` through
`numberdb.table('T199')` and stopping there, on the strength of the prompt's
sentence that 126 tables exist. The walk printed 198 titles, it looked
exhaustive, and everything above T199 was invisible. Both families were found
only by `already_here`, which searches rather than walks.

What the skill says now: "there is no call that lists the corpus, so to see
everything you walk the T-numbers". It does not say when to stop walking, and a
truncated walk is indistinguishable from a complete one.

What it should say: walk until a run of consecutive misses — twenty is plenty —
and never take a corpus size from a range you chose yourself, or from a count
written down when the corpus was smaller. A missing number in the middle is
normal (T75 is absent) so the first failure is not the end. The cost of getting
this wrong is not a wasted walk; it is proposing a family that exists, which
the screen will catch only if the name matches.

Evidence: the walk to T199 reported "198 tables"; a second walk with a
25-miss stopping rule reported T1 to T375. `already_here('Segre classes of the
total Chern class')` returned "Segre classes $s_n(c_1,\dots,c_n)$ in terms of
Chern classes" and `already_asked` returned "#163 [closed] Family:
characteristic classes", 2026-09-20.

## `discriminant()` and `is_irreducible()` are not in what the named ring imports bring

What happened: the skill's generator template imports `numberdb.sage` and then
names the rings it needs, rather than `sage.all`, and that is what a generator
attached to a table looks like. Under `sage -python` with those imports only:

* `Polynomial.discriminant()` works over `ZZ['x']` — it returned $-108$ for
  $x^3-2$ — and raises `AttributeError: cannot access submodule 'function' of
  module 'sage.symbolic' (most likely due to a circular import)` when the base
  ring is a multivariate polynomial ring, which is the case for a universal
  discriminant in the coefficients.
* `Polynomial.is_irreducible()` over `ZZ['x']` raises `ImportError: cannot
  import name PolynomialSequence_generic`, from `sage.libs.singular` by way of
  `Factorization`. So does anything else that factors.

Both go away if `import sage.all` is added, which a modular passagemath does not
have.

What the skill says now: it lists what the named imports do not bring — root
finding, `RealBall.str`, `QQ('1.25')` — and says bisection and a rational built
from its digits are usually the way past all three.

What it should say: add two to that list. The discriminant of a polynomial over
a multivariate base is
`(-1)^(n*(n-1)//2) * f.resultant(f.derivative()) // f.leading_coefficient()`,
computed entirely in the coefficient ring, which is also the definition the
table should state. Factorisation and irreducibility have no such workaround;
if a generator needs them, it needs `sage.all` and should say so at the top of
the file, next to the commands for running it.

Evidence: `agents/sage.sh` on a script building the universal discriminant of
degree 2 to 6; both tracebacks above, and the same script completing after one
added line, 2026-09-20.

## Measure the largest entry against readability, and the variable cap separately

What happened: sizing the discriminant tables of this batch, two different
ceilings turned out to bind, and they bind at different degrees. The search key
that matches polynomials up to renaming tries permutations of at most six
variables, so the discriminant of the general polynomial of degree $n$, which
lives in $n+1$ coefficients, cannot be stored past $n=5$. Length binds
elsewhere: the depressed polynomial of degree 7 has six variables and so is
allowed, but it is 320 terms and 8099 characters, which no reader reads. The
two ceilings gave $n\le5$ for one table and a recommendation of $n\le6$ for
another whose cap was $n\le7$.

What the skill says now: it gives the character counts of Chebyshev and Hermite
polynomials by degree and says to measure the largest entry before choosing a
range. The six-variable cap is in `screen.py` and in the API's refusal, and the
skill does not mention it at all.

What it should say: for a multivariate family, two independent limits set the
range — the six-variable cap on the renaming-invariant key, which is a hard
refusal and is about the *number of parameters of the object*, and the length of
the largest entry, which is a judgement. Compute both before proposing a range,
and say in the completeness note which one you hit, because a reader who sees a
table stop at degree 5 cannot tell a refusal from a choice.

Evidence: measured with `agents/sage.sh`, 2026-09-20. General polynomial,
terms and characters: $n=2$, 2 and 14; $n=3$, 5 and 65; $n=4$, 16 and 291;
$n=5$, 59 and 1338; $n=6$, 246 and 6602 but seven variables. Depressed
polynomial: $n=5$, 19 and 330; $n=6$, 76 and 1615; $n=7$, 320 and 8099 in six
variables.

## A pair index is worth proposing for its own sake, and it also deduplicates

What happened: the resultant of two monic polynomials of degrees $(m,n)$ is
indexed by a pair, and $\operatorname{Res}(g,f)=(-1)^{mn}\operatorname{Res}(f,g)$
with the two variable blocks exchanged. Because the search key already treats a
renaming as the same polynomial, storing both $(m,n)$ and $(n,m)$ would be
storing one entry twice under two addresses. The same reasoning removed the
$m=1$ column, whose entries are $g(-a_0)$ expanded and match any search for a
short sum of monomials.

What the skill says now: the key "that matches polynomials differing only in
variable names is found by trying permutations", in the context of the
six-variable refusal. It does not say that this makes two entries of a
symmetric family the same entry.

What it should say: when a family is indexed by an unordered pair, or is
symmetric in two blocks of variables, the renaming-invariant key makes the
transposed entries one entry. Store the half you can name, say in `Comments`
which half and what the sign is, and do not fill the other half — those rows
answer no search that the stored ones do not answer already.

Evidence: measured entries of the monic resultant, 2026-09-20: $(2,2)$ 7 terms,
$(2,3)$ 13, $(2,4)$ 22, $(3,3)$ 34; the $(1,n)$ entries are $n+1$ terms and are
the expansion of $g(-a_0)$.
