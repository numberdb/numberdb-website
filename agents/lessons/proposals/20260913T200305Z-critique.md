## A keyword promises the reader something the page must then say

What happened: T234, the hypersimplex Ehrhart table, carries the keywords
"uniform matroid", "Grassmannian", "Veronese type", "dice sums" and "delta
polynomial". Each names a true connection. None of the five words appears
anywhere else on the page. Keywords are searched at the title's weight, so a
reader who types "Grassmannian" gets this table first, and then reads a
definition, three comments and four formulas without learning why.

What the skill says now: `Keywords` is the place for a word the title lacks
("what would somebody type who is holding one of these numbers"), and for
unaccented spellings. It says nothing about what the page owes a reader who
arrives by one.

What it should say: a keyword that is a synonym (unaccented, or a variant
spelling) needs nothing more. A keyword that names a *connection*, such as
another object this one is, or a field where these values arise, needs one
sentence on the page that makes the connection. Otherwise it is a search hit
that explains nothing. Write the sentence in `Comments`, or drop the keyword.

Evidence: `GET /api/table?id=T234`, 2026-09-13. The five keywords appear
nowhere in `Definition`, `Comments`, `Formulas` or `Data properties`.
`audit_table`'s checks do not look at keywords.

## `rigour details` outlives the draft: do not date it by the run

What happened: three consecutive drafts, T232, T233 and T234, have
`rigour details` saying "Before the draft was filled, the rows were
checked…". The field is shown on the published page, where "the draft" is a
stage no reader saw. One sentence records the build's timeline instead of
the method.

What the skill says now: `rigour details` holds "how the digits were
obtained", and a check you ran belongs there "once". It does not warn
against dating the check by the publishing workflow.

What it should say: write `rigour details` as it should read after
publication. Say what was checked and against what ("the rows were checked
against direct coefficient counts"). Do not say when in the run it
happened ("before the draft was filled", "after the first fill").

Evidence: the `rigour details` of T232, T233 and T234, read 2026-09-13. The
T232, T233 and T234 critiques each noted it.
