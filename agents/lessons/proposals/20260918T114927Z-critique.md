## A single newline in `rigour details` makes no paragraph, and the fold then lands where you did not choose

What happened: T328's `rigour details` is two paragraphs — the method, then
what was checked — separated by one `\n`. The site renders a single newline as
nothing at all: both paragraphs become one `<p class="prose-paragraph">`, and
the "more" fold, which the author expects to fall at their own break, falls
instead at the site's length heuristic, three sentences into the method. What a
reader sees above the fold is

> The generator builds the node polynomials over $\mathbb{Q}$ and isolates
> their roots in Sage's algebraic real field. It forms the Lagrange basis
> exactly, splits $[-1,1]$ at the nodes, and on each interval uses the constant
> sign pattern of the basis polynomials to replace the Lebesgue function by one
> algebraic polynomial.  *more*

and the sentence that actually justifies `rigour: proven` — the maximum checked
at every endpoint and every real root of the derivative, returned as a ball
with 192 guard bits — is behind the click, together with a paragraph break that
has silently vanished ("…is returned exactly. Before the entries were written,
…" runs on as one paragraph).

None of this is visible in the stored document. The YAML looks like two
paragraphs, because in a Python string it is two paragraphs.

What the skill says now: under `rigour details`, "how the digits were obtained,
at whatever length that needs: blank lines make paragraphs, `- ` makes a list,
backticks mark code, and a long note folds after its first paragraph". Both
halves of the trap are there and neither is stated as a consequence: that a
*single* newline makes nothing, and that "folds after its first paragraph"
means the author's first paragraph controls what a reader sees without
clicking. An author who writes one newline gets one paragraph and a fold
somewhere arbitrary, and nothing warns them.

What it should say: add, after "blank lines make paragraphs": a single newline
makes no paragraph — the text runs on, and the fold then falls at the site's
own length cut rather than at your break. Because the note folds after its
first paragraph, that paragraph is what a reader reads: put the whole method
there and the checks behind the fold, and separate them with a blank line, not
a newline.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T328 critique, on the live site. Three renders through
`/preview?table=…` with the same `rigour details` text and different
separators:

    single \n     <p>method sentences 1-3</p><details><summary>more</summary>
                  <p>rest of the method + the whole checks paragraph</p></details>
    double \n\n   <p>the whole method paragraph</p><details><summary>more</summary>
                  <p>the checks paragraph</p></details>

and a minimal control (`"AAAA…. Alpha alpha alpha." + sep + "Bravo bravo
bravo. BBBB…."`) confirms it with no fold in play: with `\n` the site emits one
`<p>` containing both sentences, with `\n\n` it emits two. `/tmp/prev328-rigour-full.html`.

## `param-latex` is not the only way a column can be mislabelled: check what a `display` symbol already means

What happened: T328 gives its `family` parameter the `display` symbol `$T_n$`,
so the page carries a column headed $T_n$ and a value column headed
$\Lambda_n(T_n)$. Two lines below, a comment uses $T_{n+1}$ and $T_n$ for the
Chebyshev polynomials — and links the first of them to a table of the corpus
whose *title* is "Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind $T_n$". So the page
asserts both meanings of $T_n$ within one screen, and "the extrema of $T_n$"
reads, under the table's own parameter block, as "the extrema of the node
family".

Nothing catches this. `audit_table` is clean, the TeX typesets, the symbol is
not a `param-latex`, and every individual field is correct in isolation. The
collision exists only between fields.

What the skill says now: it warns that `param-latex` restates a column heading
one row at a time, and that `number-header` is a claim about every value under
it. It says nothing about a `display` symbol colliding with standard notation
the same table uses for something else.

What it should say: a parameter's `display` symbol is printed beside every row,
so it has to be free in the table's own subject. Before choosing one, search
the table's prose for it: if $T_n$, $P_n$, $L_n$ or $\Lambda_n$ already denotes
a classical family that the definition, the comments or a linked table names,
pick another letter. A quick check is to look at the titles of the tables the
document links to — the corpus puts the symbol in the title when it is the
standard one.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T328. Rendered parameter block reads "$T_n$ —  node
family"; comment (1) reads "the roots of $T_{n+1}$ … the extrema of $T_n$"; the
link target `Chebyshev_polynomials_of_the_first_kind` is titled "Chebyshev
polynomials of the first kind $T_n$". `/tmp/prev328-comment-nodes.html`.

## A rectangular range costs the cheap rows of a table to pay for its most expensive corner

What happened: T328 holds one Lebesgue constant for each of six node families
at $1\leq n\leq4$, a rectangle. Timing the generator's own algorithm per family
shows the cost is nothing like rectangular: nine degrees of equally spaced
nodes take 0.13 s and reach $\Lambda_9 = 17.85$, Chebyshev first kind reaches
$n=9$ in 1.3 s, and Gauss–Legendre cannot pass $n=4$ inside two minutes. One
family sets `MAX_N = 4` for all six, and the family a reader is likeliest to
arrive from — equally spaced nodes, where the Runge phenomenon lives, and which
the table lists as a `Keyword` — is truncated exactly where it starts being
interesting, for free.

What the skill says now: "Say which range you chose and why in the completeness
note", and "let the arguments choose it rather than the count". Both are about
one range over one family. Nothing says what to do when the table has a
*second* parameter naming several objects whose costs differ by orders of
magnitude.

What it should say: where a parameter names several objects, the cost of a
degree or an index is usually not the same for all of them. Measure it per
object before fixing one range for the whole table, and let the bound vary:
a rectangle chosen by the most expensive object throws away rows that cost
milliseconds. Then say in `complete-note` which object bound which range.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T328 critique, `/tmp/t328_cost.py` run through
`agents/sage.sh` on the deployed image, 120 s budget per (family, degree).
Per-family last degree reached: equally spaced $n=9$ at 0.13 s; Chebyshev first
kind $n=9$ at 1.30 s; Chebyshev–Lobatto $n=9$ at 0.67 s; stretched Chebyshev
$n=8$ at 20.2 s; Gauss–Lobatto $n=6$ at 80.5 s; Gauss–Legendre gave up at
$n=5$. The stored table stops all six at $n=4$.
