## Distribution entropies need a density-level control, not only a named formula

What happened: the differential-entropy proposal named two closed forms that
looked plausible and were wrong for the table's stated standard forms. It said
the unit-variance hyperbolic secant distribution had entropy $4G/\pi$. The
stated density was $\frac12\operatorname{sech}(\pi x/2)$, whose variance is
1 and whose entropy is instead $\log 4$, confirmed both by scaling SciPy's
`hypsecant` density and by direct quadrature of $-f\log f$. The generator was
changed before any entries were written.

The same independent check caught a second normalization error: the Wigner
semicircle row for radius 1 was first written as
$\log(\pi/2)+1/2$, but SciPy's `semicircular` distribution and direct
quadrature of $(2/\pi)\sqrt{1-x^2}$ on $[-1,1]$ give $\log\pi-1/2$.

What the skill says now: settle the convention, check every identity before
writing it down, and chase disagreements to a cause. It does not call out
probability distributions as a place where the same name often hides several
scale conventions and where the entropy shifts by a logarithm of the scale.

What it should say: for differential entropies, write the density or
location-scale convention first, then run a control that integrates
$-f\log f$ for representative rows, especially any distribution whose name has
multiple standardizations. A closed form found by name is not enough until it
has been checked against the density the table says it stores.

Evidence: T241 build, 2026-09-14. `/tmp/hypsecant_check.py` printed
`log(4)=1.3862943611198906`, `4G/pi=1.1662436161232751`, and direct
quadrature of $\frac12\operatorname{sech}(\pi x/2)$ gave `log(4)` with
variance `1.0`. `/tmp/semicircle_check.py` printed
`0.64472988584940017` for direct quadrature, matching `log(pi)-1/2` and
SciPy's `semicircular.entropy()`.
