Nothing here is in force. Format as `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md` asks.

## `lfun` at a pole returns the polar part as a series, and the constant term is not in it

What happened: sizing a table of Euler–Kronecker constants
$\gamma_K = c_0/c_{-1}$ of the Laurent expansion
$\zeta_K(s) = c_{-1}(s-1)^{-1} + c_0 + \cdots$, the obvious thing in PARI is

    L = lfun(lfuncreate(x^4 - x^3 - x^2 + x + 1), 1)

and it works: it returns `0.33063066328223158676...*x^-1 + O(x^0)`, a `t_SER`
whose $x^{-1}$ coefficient is the residue. So `polcoef(L, -1)` is a clean,
independent way to get the residue of a Dedekind zeta function, which is worth
knowing on its own — it is the check that caught nothing and confirmed
everything on 456 fields here.

But `polcoef(L, 0)` does not give $c_0$. It raises

    PariError: polcoef: domain error in polcoef: degree > -1

because the series really is $O(x^0)$: PARI computed the polar part and
nothing beyond it. A run that expects a Laurent expansion gets an exception,
not a wrong number, which is the good failure — but the recipe it suggests
(ask for more terms) does not work either.

What does work is to divide the pole out first and use the entire function:

    L = lfundiv(lfuncreate(f), lfuncreate(1));   \\ zeta_K / zeta
    Euler + lfun(L, 1, 1)/lfun(L, 1)

which is the same shape as the formula T230 uses for quadratic fields,
$\gamma + L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)$, but with the Artin $L$-function
$\zeta_K/\zeta$ in place of the Dirichlet one — so it works for any number
field, abelian or not. Checked against T230's stored *proven* values: it
reproduces $D = -4$ ($0.822825249678847032995328716261\ldots$) and $D = 5$
($1.404895141617037748597559079759\ldots$) to all 30 digits computed.

What the skill says now: nothing about `lfun`; its PARI examples are
`bnfinit`, `lfun(L, 1)` for a residue and `bestappr(lfun(L, -1), ...)`.

What it should say: if a proposal or a table needs a Laurent coefficient other
than the residue, divide the pole out with `lfundiv` and work with the entire
function; `lfun` at a pole gives the polar part only. And note that nothing
here is interval arithmetic, so a value obtained this way is at best
`heuristic (agreement-checked)` however many digits it prints.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `agents/sage.sh` on PARI 2.17.1 through `cypari2`;
T230's values read from the live table with the client.

## The sign of the discriminant tells you less in degree four than in degree three

What happened: the corpus's cubic tables (T158, T159, T162) can and do use
$D > 0 \Leftrightarrow$ totally real, because a cubic field has signature
$(3,0)$ or $(1,1)$ and $\operatorname{sign}(D) = (-1)^{r_2}$. Carrying that
sentence into degree four is wrong: quartic fields have three signatures,
$\operatorname{sign}(D) = (-1)^{r_2}$ still holds, so $D < 0$ means $(2,1)$
but $D > 0$ covers both $(4,0)$ and $(0,2)$. The two smallest quartic
discriminants, $117$ and $125$, are both positive and both *totally
imaginary*; the smallest totally real one is $725$.

The same degree bump moves two other things a table would otherwise inherit
silently: the unit rank is $1$, $2$ or $3$ rather than always $1$ or $2$, and
the number of roots of unity is no longer always $2$ — over the 456 quartic
fields with $|D| \le 5000$, $w_K = 2$ for 338 of them, $4$ for 44, $6$ for 71,
and there is one field each with $w_K = 8, 10, 12$. $w_K$ is a factor of the
class number formula, so a cubic formula copied with its $2$ in place is
wrong on 118 of 456 fields.

What the skill says now: "what the parameters are and the constraint entries
actually satisfy", and the example of a table claiming $k \equiv 1 \bmod p$
while 702 of its 856 entries were not.

What it should say: the same warning in the other direction — a table built by
analogy with an existing one inherits its *constraints* as well as its shape,
and a constraint that is a theorem in the smaller case may be false in the
larger. Check every "so $D>0$ means" sentence against the new range before
copying it.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `nflist` enumerations and `nfrootsof1` over all 456
quartic fields with $|D| \le 5000$.

## `nflist` enumerates number fields, but only one Galois group at a time

What happened: PARI 2.17 has `nflist`, which makes "the quartic fields of
discriminant at most $B$" a one-line enumeration where it used to be Hunter's
theorem written out by hand (T158's generator does it the hard way for cubic
fields). Two things about it are worth a contributor's time:

* It takes a *group*, not a degree. `nflist(4, [1,5000])` is an error
  ("unsupported group"); the quartic fields are the union of
  `nflist("C4", ...)`, `"V4"`, `"D4"`, `"A4"`, `"S4"`. Forgetting one silently
  produces a smaller table that looks complete — dropping `S4` here would lose
  222 of 456 fields, or half the table.
* A bare integer argument means "discriminant exactly $N$", not "up to $N$":
  `nflist("C4", 2000)` returned one field and `nflist("C4", [1,2000])`
  returned three. The first reads like a complete enumeration and is not one.

It is fast: all 456 quartic fields with $|D| \le 5000$ in under two seconds,
and `bnfinit` plus `bnfcertify` on every one of them in sixteen.

What the skill says now: nothing about enumerating number fields.

What it should say: if a family is indexed by a number field, `nflist` is the
enumeration, with those two traps named; and the count should be asserted in
the generator against an independent source, as T161 does, because both traps
produce a short list rather than an error.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `agents/sage.sh`, PARI 2.17.1.

## A value family can be distinctive at one parameter and not at another

What happened: $\zeta_K(1-2m)$ for totally real quartic $K$ is rational and
exact, which is the strongest kind of entry. At $m = 1$ the values are small
rationals and sometimes small integers — $2/15$, $2/3$, $4/15$, and exactly
$1$ at $D = 2304$ — so at that one argument the table behaves like the class
number table the skill warns against, where every small value matches many
fields. At $m = 2$ and $m = 3$ the same fields give $541/15$,
$118338039978739652/315$: unmistakable.

So the "is the value distinctive" test is not a property of the family. It is
a property of the family *at a parameter*, and the answer can be the reason to
hold three arguments rather than one.

What the skill says now: the class-number argument, as a test on a family
("values are mostly 1, 2, 3 — put it in a comment instead").

What it should say: apply the test per parameter value. A family whose values
are indistinctive at the first index and distinctive later is a good table
that should say so in `Comments`, not a bad table and not a table that starts
at the second index.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, `bestappr(lfun(L, -1|-3|-5))` over all 204 totally real
quartic fields with $D \le 30000$.
