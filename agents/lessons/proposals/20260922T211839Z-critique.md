# Lessons proposed by the T420 critique, 2026-09-22

Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in force.

## A title that begins with "The" files under T, and the address keeps it

What happened: T420 is titled "The Falkner–Skan separation parameter". No
other title in the corpus begins with an article -- its five siblings begin
"Displacement", "Momentum", "Wall" -- and the table listing sorts on
`title.lower().replace('$','')` with nothing stripped, so this one files under
**T**, between "Teichmüller representatives" and "Todd polynomials". A reader
browsing for a boundary-layer constant walks past it. The slug takes the
article too: the draft's address is `/The_Falkner_Skan_separation_parameter`,
and a slug is frozen at publication because citations resolve on it, so the
article is permanent from that moment.

Search is unaffected -- `Keywords` carried the bare phrase -- which is why
this survives every check: the audit does not look at the first word, and the
value search never sees a title.

What the skill says now: "A title is what a search reaches", and the rules
about how finely to divide a subject. Nothing about the listing, about
sorting, or about the first word.

What it should say: begin a title with the name of the thing. An article
sorts the table under "The" in the listing and is baked into the slug, which
cannot be changed after publication. The house style is visible in the corpus:
of the tables here, none begins with "The", "A" or "An".

Evidence: 2026-09-22, T420 critique. `numberdb_app/views.py:345`,
`tables.order_by('title_lowercase')`; `numberdb_app/editing.py:488`,
`title_lowercase = title.lower().replace('$', '')`. The listing at
`/tables?page=1..8` holds no title beginning with an article. The draft's slug
is read off its own page, not guessed.

## A new tag on a batch of drafts always fails the audit, and that is not a verdict

What happened: `GET /api/table/T420/audit` returned one finding, `Tags: "fluid
dynamics" reaches only this table; a tag that leads nowhere else leads
nowhere`. Five other tables in the same batch carry that tag -- wall shear,
wall heat transfer, displacement thickness, momentum thickness of the same
Falkner-Skan flows -- so the skill's rule, three or more tables *counting the
others in the same batch*, is met twice over. The check reads
`Tag.table_count`, which is recounted over `tag.public_tables` only
(`editing.py:733`, `recount_tags.py:31`), so every table in a batch of drafts
that introduces a tag together will report this finding, and every one of them
will stop reporting it as soon as one sibling is published.

The finding is worth keeping -- a tag with one table on it really is useless,
and most tables that trip this are alone. But a build that treats the audit as
a checklist deletes a good tag to get a clean run, which is the opposite of
what the rule wants.

What the skill says now: "propose a new one when three or more tables would
carry it, counting the others in the same batch, and say which they are", and
separately "Let `manage.py audit_table` confirm afterwards rather than
instead". It does not say the two can disagree, or that this particular
disagreement is expected.

What it should say: the tag check counts published tables, so a tag shared
across a batch of unpublished drafts reads as reaching one table. Where the
batch is the argument for the tag, say which tables in the offer for review
and keep the tag; the finding clears when the first sibling publishes.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, T420 critique. Audit of T420: one finding, the tag.
T415, T417, T418 and T419 carry `fluid dynamics` and were all drafts at the
time; `/tags` lists no `fluid dynamics` at all, because that page filters on
`table_count__gt=0`.
