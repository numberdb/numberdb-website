# Proposed by the T349 critique, 2026-09-20

Two lessons, both met while reading T349 (values of the incomplete elliptic
integral $F(\varphi,m)$) against the tables it overlaps. Both are true for a
contributor with a laptop: one is about what the corpus search does with their
digits, the other about what their table has to say for a reader to use it.

---

## Two tables at the same precision need not write the same digits, so `repeats` folds less than it looks

What happened: T349 stores $F(t\pi,m)$, and its $t=1/2$ row is $K(m)$ by
definition. All forty-six parameters in that row are also in T25, the complete
first-kind table, and both tables computed them in arb at a hundred digits.
**Seventeen of the forty-six are written character for character the same;
twenty-nine differ in the last place**, because the two ball midpoints round
the other way:

    m = 0     T25   1.570796...4499314017412671058533
              T349  1.570796...4499314017412671058534
    m = 3/4   T25   2.156515...7634967875781445902
              T349  2.156515...7634967875781445903

Nothing is wrong with either: the last digit of a stored decimal is the one
the convention says may be off by one, so the two strings denote overlapping
intervals and both contain $K(m)$. But `fold_repeats`
(`numberdb_app/search.py`) compares the **stored text**, so a
`repeats:` declaration folds the seventeen and leaves the twenty-nine
answering twice. Whether a reader who types the shared digits gets one answer
or two is decided by a rounding direction neither table can see.

The same thing in a second shape, against T29 (rational multiples of $\pi$)
on the $m=0$ column: six amplitudes in common, two written identically, and
the rest differing either in the last place or in the number of decimals --
T29 writes 99 decimals for a value below 1 where T349 writes 100, both aiming
at a hundred significant digits.

What the skill says now: it introduces `repeats` as "where these two tables
hold the same number, that one states it first", and says of a near-miss only
that "two tables holding one number to different *precision* do not collide
there and will not fold here either" (the audit's `_shared_values`
docstring is where that is spelled out). Same precision is presented as the
case that does fold.

What it should say: declare `repeats` on the relation between the two
tables, not on the arithmetic -- it is right even when nothing folds. And do
not expect it to deduplicate a search: two independent ball computations of
the same constant at the same precision often disagree in the last place --
here 29 of 46 did -- so a shared value may still answer twice. If a fold
actually matters for a particular value, `equals` on the entry is the field
that says "these two are the same number" without depending on the digits
agreeing.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T349 critique. `GET /api/table?id=T25` against
`GET /api/table?id=T349`: 46 of 46 parameters shared, 17 identical strings,
29 differing in the hundredth digit; `numberdb_app/search.py:164`
(`fold_repeats`, "the stored text is compared, which never merges two numbers
that differ").

---

## A table that stores one fundamental domain should give the identity that reduces into it

What happened: T349 stores amplitudes $0<\varphi\leq\pi/2$, one quarter
period, and says so in the parameter constraint. That is the right range --
$F$ is quasi-periodic and the rest of the real line is a reflection and a
multiple of $K(m)$ away -- but the page nowhere says it. A reader holding
$F(3\pi/4,1/2)$, which is an ordinary thing to be holding, finds a table that
looks as though it simply stops, and no way in.

The identity that lets them in is one line, is in the DLMF section the table
already links (19.2.10, $F(n\pi\pm\varphi,k)=2nK(k)\pm F(\varphi,k)$), and
needs the companion table for $K$, which the table already points at. Written
down, it turns the range from a cut into a normalisation, and it is most of
the "why" that `complete-note` is supposed to carry.

What the skill says now: a great deal about *which* values turn up and about
saying why the range was chosen, and nothing about periodicity. The nearest
sentence is the one about derivable values -- "a reader tries a half, a two, a
pi, and stops" -- which is the same problem seen from the other side.

What it should say: where the family is periodic, quasi-periodic, or symmetric
about a point, store one fundamental domain and put the reduction identity in
`Formulas`, citing a reference the table already declares. It is the sentence
that tells a reader outside the stored range that the table is still about
their number. Two things to check when writing it: that the letters do not
collide (DLMF writes the integer in 19.2.10 as $m$, which in the parameter
convention $m=k^2$ is already taken), and that whatever the reduction needs --
here $K(m)$ -- is linked where it is named.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T349 critique. The stored range
`$0<\varphi/\pi\leq 1/2$`; DLMF 19.2.10, fetched from
<https://dlmf.nist.gov/19.2>, which is the table's own `DLMFDefinitions`
link; T25 as the table holding $K(m)$.
