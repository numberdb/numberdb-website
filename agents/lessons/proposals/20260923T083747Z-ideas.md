## `repr` of a ball prints a bound on $|x|$, not a centred interval, so a ball that excludes zero can look as though it straddles it

What happened: a check compared five published critical-exponent sets against
the scaling relations, each value a ball `centre +/- published uncertainty`,
and printed the residual with `repr` and a verdict from `contains_zero()`. Two
rows printed

    Josephson a=2-3n    residual [+/- 6.94e-3]    FAILS
    Fisher g=n(2-eta)   residual [+/- 0.517]      FAILS

which reads as a contradiction: a ball printed as `[+/- r]` looks centred on
zero, so `FAILS` looks like a bug in the verdict. It is not. When arb cannot
determine a single significant digit of the midpoint it prints `[+/- b]` where
`b` bounds $|x|$, that is `|mid| + rad`, and says nothing about where inside
$[-b,b]$ the ball lies. Asked directly, the Josephson residual was
`mid=0.003570, rad=0.003360`: a ball that excludes zero by a fifth of its own
width, printed in a form that suggests the opposite. The Fisher residual was
`mid=0.44367, rad=0.07329`, off by six times its radius, printed as
`[+/- 0.517]`.

What the skill says now: it warns that a wide ball agrees with everything
("one of radius 1.4e18 contains zero, and every other answer") and says to
check that the radius is small enough to mean anything. It says nothing about
how a ball is printed, and the printed form is what a person reads when
deciding whether a check passed.

What it should say: never read a verdict off `repr` of a ball. `[+/- b]` is a
bound on the absolute value, not a centre and a radius, so it appears both for
a residual that is genuinely zero and for one that is six standard errors away.
Print `x.mid()`, `x.rad()` and `x.contains_zero()` when the point of the line
is whether a quantity vanishes. (Note that `RealBall.str`, which would format
it properly, is one of the things the named imports do not bring, so `repr` is
what a generator reaches for by default.)

Evidence: Sage 10.9 through `agents/sage.sh`. `/tmp/check3d.py` printed
`residual [+/- 6.94e-3] FAILS` and `/tmp/perc.py`, on the same balls, printed
`Josephson mid=0.0035700000000000731 rad=0.0033600000 contains_zero=False` and
`Fisher mid=0.44366739999999990 rad=0.073289600 contains_zero=False`.

## The relations a family satisfies are a check on the *source*, and a well-known source fails one

What happened: before proposing a table of three-dimensional critical
exponents, the five scaling relations that tie the exponents of one
universality class together — Rushbrooke $\alpha+2\beta+\gamma=2$, Widom
$\gamma=\beta(\delta-1)$, Fisher $\gamma=\nu(2-\eta)$, Josephson
$\alpha=2-d\nu$, and $\beta=\nu(d-2+\eta)/2$ — were run on the published
values themselves, each as a ball with the published uncertainty as its
radius. Four classes passed all five. The percolation row of Wikipedia's
*Universality class* article failed three: it prints $\eta=0.46(8)$, while its
own $\beta=0.4181(8)$ and $\nu=0.87619(12)$ give $2\beta/\nu-1 =
-0.0456\pm0.0063$ and its own $\gamma=1.793(3)$ gives $2-\gamma/\nu =
-0.0458\pm0.0074$. Two independent routes within the same table agree on a
value that is not the one printed. Had the table been built first and checked
after, one entry in it would have been wrong by half a unit with a published
source behind it.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent. Not
the code that produced them", and "Verify a claim before writing it into a
table, including one somebody suggested." Both are about values you computed
or were told; neither is about values you are about to transcribe.

What it should say: where a table's family satisfies identities, run them on
the source before transcribing, not on the finished table. A published table of
related quantities is over-determined, so its own identities test it for free,
and they catch the error that no recomputation can — a typo in the source. When
a source fails, say which value failed and what the other entries imply it
should be, in the entry's comment.

Evidence: `agents/sage.sh /tmp/check3d.py`, arb at 200 bits. Ising, XY
$O(2)$, Heisenberg $O(3)$ and the self-avoiding walk: residual balls contain
zero on all five relations. Percolation: Fisher `mid=0.44367 rad=0.07329`,
$\beta$-relation `mid=-0.22152 rad=0.03594`, Josephson `mid=0.00357
rad=0.00336`, none containing zero.

## An arXiv abstract page is a poor source for `source_names_it`, and the screen must be run on the exact title

What happened: `source_names_it` was run on six candidate names against the
arXiv abstract pages of the defining papers. Names that are entirely standard
came back as complaints: "Scaling dimensions of the 3D Ising CFT" against
arXiv:1603.04436 fails on `cft`, because titles and abstracts spell the phrase
out; "Universal amplitude ratios of the three-dimensional Ising universality
class" against the review that is *about* them fails on `dimensional`, because
the physics literature writes "3D". Separately, "Central charge**s** $c_T$ of
the 3D CFTs" failed against a page that says "central charge", and the
singular form passed.

What the skill says now: nothing about `screen.py`; the screen lives in
`agents/`, and this is a note for the stage rather than for the published
skill — but the first half generalises, because it is really about what a
source page contains.

What it should say: cite the encyclopedia article rather than the paper when
screening a name, because `source_names_it` asks whether the page contains
every distinguishing word of the name, and an abstract is a hundred words of
prose that uses the field's abbreviations. Both Wikipedia's *Universality
class* and *Conformal bootstrap* pass names that every arXiv page refuses. And
screen the name exactly as the table will be titled: a plural, an accent or a
dropped hyphen changes the answer.

Evidence: `source_names_it('Scaling dimensions of the 3D Ising CFT',
'https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08471')` -> "the source does not mention scaling";
the same name against `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_bootstrap` ->
`None`. `'Central charges $c_T$ of the 3D CFTs'` against arXiv:1307.6856 ->
"does not mention charges"; `'Central charge $c_T$ of the 3D CFTs'` -> `None`.
