# Lessons proposed by the T353 critique, 2026-09-20

Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in force.

---

## An identity a generator computes half its range with is not a check on that half

What happened: T353 holds $B(x;a,b)$ on a grid symmetric under
$x\mapsto 1-x$, $(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)$. Its generator computes the rows with
$x\leq1/2$ from the hypergeometric series and the rows with $x>1/2$ as
$B(a,b)-B(1-x;b,a)$, which is the complement identity
$B(x;a,b)+B(1-x;b,a)=B(a,b)$ rearranged. Its `rigour details` then says "The
615 entries were checked against the complement identity". They cannot fail
it. The stored value at $x>1/2$ is the stored value at $1-x$ subtracted from
$B(a,b)$, by construction, so summing the pair recovers $B(a,b)$ whatever
either value is -- on 594 of the 615 rows. The 21 rows at $x=1/2$, where both
orders are computed directly, are the only ones the identity tests.

This is the ball-arithmetic trap in a different costume. There the check
reports `overlaps = True` because one side is `nan` or a ball of radius
$1.4\cdot10^{18}$; here it reports agreement because the two sides are the same
arithmetic written twice. Both look like confirmation in the log.

It is easy to walk into, because using a reflection or complement formula for
half a range is the *right* thing to do numerically -- the series converges
badly at the far end -- and the identity is then the most obvious check to
reach for, precisely because it is already in the file.

The test that catches it: ask whether the check would still pass if the
generator's arithmetic were wrong. If every quantity in the identity comes out
of the same `value()` call tree, the answer is yes and the check is empty.
What does work on a table like this is an independent closed form (T353's
values are all $\alpha\arcsin\sqrt x$ plus a rational polynomial in $\sqrt x$
and $\sqrt{1-x}$, which a checker can evaluate in `decimal` without Sage), or
quadrature at higher precision with a control -- which T353 also did, on five
entries, and that sentence is the one its `rigour details` should have led
with.

What the skill says now: section 10 says "Check new values against something
independent. Not the code that produced them", and "An enclosure that contains
everything agrees with everything". Neither names the case where the *identity*
rather than the enclosure is the thing that agrees with everything, and the
first sentence reads as being about reusing the same function, not about
reusing the same theorem.

What it should say: an identity used to compute part of a table is not a check
on that part. When a generator splits its range and derives one half from the
other, say which half each check reached, and count the rows that were really
tested -- `rigour details` is read as a claim about the number of entries it
names.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T353. `generators/values-incomplete-beta-function/
generate.py`, `incomplete_beta()`. 297 rows have $x>1/2$ and all 297 have their
complement row stored. The independent check that does work: 168 rows against
six elementary closed forms and 31 x-values against
$B(x;3/2,1/2)=\arcsin\sqrt x-\sqrt{x(1-x)}$, all agreeing to the last stored
place, in `/tmp/check353.py` -- 130-digit `decimal`, no Sage.

## `Keywords` is a silent claim: it is indexed at title weight and renders nowhere

What happened: T353's `Keywords` are `incomplete beta function`, `regularised
incomplete beta`, `regularized incomplete beta`, `beta distribution CDF` and
`Student t distribution`. The table holds unregularised $B(x;a,b)$ and nothing
else, and the rendered page does not contain the string "Student", or
"distribution", anywhere -- not in the definition, not in a comment, not in a
link. So the table answers a search for a quantity that no reader of the page
can find any trace of, and somebody who searched for it has no way to tell
whether they have found what they meant.

The asymmetry is what makes this easy to miss: every other field is checked by
reading the page, and `Keywords` is the one field a reader never sees. The
audit does not read it either -- `_prose_faults` takes Definition, Comments,
Formulas, Similar tables, `rigour details`, `complete-note` and entry comments.

What the skill says now: "If the title does not contain that word, put it in
`Keywords`, which is the same weight -- that is also how an accent is handled".
Both examples given are about *spelling* the thing the table holds (Lévy /
Levy). Nothing says a keyword is a claim about the contents.

What it should say: a keyword is read by the search index at the weight of the
title, and by nobody else, so it must name what the table holds. An alternative
spelling, an accent dropped, a synonym for the same object: yes. A related
quantity the table does not store: no, unless the page earns it -- and where
the relation is real, the fix that costs least is a comment naming it, which
makes the keyword true and tells the reader who arrives why.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T353 document (`GET /api/table?id=T353`) against the
rendered page (`/tmp/T353_page.html`, 416,529 bytes, 615 records).
`grep -ci student /tmp/T353_page.html` is 0.

## A one-paragraph `rigour details` folds at a sentence, not at the paragraph

What happened: T353's `rigour details` is a single paragraph of seven
sentences. On the page it renders as the first two sentences, then a `more`
disclosure holding the rest. The skill says a long note "folds after its first
paragraph", which suggests a note with no blank line in it is not folded at
all. `numberdb_app/prose.py` falls back to `_split_sentence` exactly for that
case: "One long paragraph, which is what all 139 existing notes are ... Cut
after a sentence instead."

The consequence is editorial rather than technical. Whatever a contributor
writes first is what a reviewer reads; everything after the cut needs a click.
In T353 the visible half is how the values were computed and the folded half is
how they were checked, which is the right way round -- but it is the right way
round by accident.

What the skill says now: "`rigour details` ... at whatever length that needs:
blank lines make paragraphs, `- ` makes a list, backticks mark code, and a long
note folds after its first paragraph".

What it should say: add that a note written as one paragraph is cut after a
sentence instead, so the first two or three sentences are what most readers
see. Put the level and the method there, and the checks after.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T353. `/tmp/T353_page.html` renders "How they were
obtained" as two sentences followed by `<details class="prose-more">
<summary>more</summary>`; `numberdb_app/prose.py:71-76`.
