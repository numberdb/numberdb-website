## Rows that are equal by theory are a free check on transcribed digits

What happened: T293 transcribes elliptic-curve regulators over real quadratic
fields from ecnf-data's `reg` field, 38 significant digits each. The curves
`2.2.17.1-17.1-a1` and `-a8` are Galois conjugates: applying $w\mapsto 1-w$
to the coefficients of one gives the other's equation exactly. So their
regulators are equal. The stored values are
`6.0705123554606643216641072065434946721` and `...946201`, 520 units apart
in the last place, while each decimal claims plus or minus one. The isogenous
curves `38.2-a1` and `-a4` agree to 37 digits and differ by 3 units. The
generator compared each regulator only with its own source height, which
cannot see this.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent"
and "A family with known identities gives you a free test suite", with
examples for polynomials. Nothing about transcribed digits.

What it should say: when values are copied from a source rather than
computed, the source's digits are not evidence of their own precision. Find
pairs of rows that the mathematics says are equal, or in an exact rational
ratio: Galois-conjugate objects, isogenous curves, a value and its image
under a known symmetry. Then store only the digits on which those pairs
agree. A disagreement beyond the last place means the table claims digits
nobody has.

Evidence: T293 draft, `GET /api/table?id=T293`, 2026-09-17; the check is
exact arithmetic on the stored equations and a subtraction of the stored
strings.
