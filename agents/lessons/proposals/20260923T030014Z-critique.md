# Lessons proposed by the T417 critique, 2026-09-23

Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in force.

## The search that answers "is this table already here?" cannot see drafts, and a family is built as drafts

What happened: T417 is one of six Falkner-Skan tables made in one batch.
`numberdb.search_text('Falkner')` -- and the `/api/lookup?text=` call behind
it -- returns an empty `.tables` for every term the family uses: Falkner,
Blasius, Prandtl, Nusselt, boundary layer. Not because the corpus lacks them,
but because all six are unpublished, and search answers published tables only.
So the first check the skill prescribes reports "nothing like this here" at
exactly the moment when six things like it are being made, and it says the
same thing to the person making the seventh.

It has already cost this corpus a table. T416 and T419 are both *Momentum
thickness $\delta_2$ of the Falkner-Skan wedge flows*, differing in one
character -- an en dash in one title, a hyphen in the other. T419 holds 120
values of $\beta$; T416 holds none. Two drafts, one quantity, and neither
search nor the audit relates them, because a title is how a duplicate is
found and these two titles are not the same string.

What the skill says now, in "Look at the database before writing anything":
that `search_text` stems and looks past titles into definitions and comments,
and one clause at the end of the section: "`/drafts` lists what is being made
right now and is invisible from outside, so a table can be half-built and
unfindable while you start it again."

That clause is true and is doing too little work. It reads as a remark about
visibility; what it needs to say is that the search *call* cannot answer the
question the section is about, so `/drafts` is not a supplement to
`search_text` but the other half of it.

What it should say: before proposing a table, read `/drafts` as well as
searching, and read it for the *family* and not for the title -- the duplicate
you are about to make is likelier to differ by a dash, an accent or a word
order than to match. When building several tables of one family in one batch,
list the family's titles somewhere and check new ones against that list, since
nothing on the site will do it until they are published.

Evidence: 2026-09-23, T417 critique. `GET /api/lookup?text=Falkner` returned
`{"results": [], "tags": [], "tables": []}` while `GET /api/table?id=` with a
key returned T415, T416, T417, T418, T419 and T420, all Falkner-Skan, all
404 to an anonymous reader. `GET /api/tag?url=fluid%20dynamics` reports
`table_count: 0` for a tag three of them carry.

## Check a stated conversion against the stored columns, because it is what keeps two conventions in one table

What happened: T417 holds one quantity in two similarity normalisations, 247
values under each, and states the conversion as a formula. That formula is the
whole argument for the table being one table rather than two -- the skill's
test is that a convention is invertible, and the audit asks the question on
every draft. Running the formula over the stored pairs took four lines and
answered it: the wedge value is recovered from its Hartree partner in all 247
pairs to within the stored 9 digits, and at $\beta=1$, where the factor is 1,
the two columns are identical. That is a stronger answer to the audit than any
sentence, and it is also a check on the table: a conversion that does not
reproduce the other column means one of the columns is wrong, or the formula
as written is not the one the generator used.

The generator here computes in one normalisation and converts, so the check is
not independent of the code -- but it is a check on what the *page* claims,
which is a different thing and the thing a reader has.

What the skill says now: "The test for a convention is that it is invertible",
and the three reasons a convention may still deserve two tables. It says to
decide, not to verify.

What it should say: where a table holds one quantity in two conventions, put
the conversion in `Formulas` and run it over every stored pair before
publishing. It is cheap, it is the answer the draft audit asks for, and a
conversion formula that disagrees with the stored columns is a fault no other
check looks for -- `verify()` recomputes values against the generator, not
against the prose.

Evidence: 2026-09-23, T417. Formula
$-\theta'_{\mathrm{wedge}}(0)=\sqrt{(m+1)/2}\,[-\theta'_{\mathrm{Hartree}}(0)]$
with $m=\beta/(2-\beta)$, applied to the 247 stored pairs from
`GET /api/table?id=T417`: 0 mismatches at a tolerance of 6e-9 relative.
