## `/tags/<name>` lists drafts, costs no API quota, and is the duplicate check that works

What happened: this run screened a batch of sharp constants of the classical
inequalities -- Khintchine, Babenko-Beckner, Young's convolution,
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev -- and verified all of it in ball arithmetic,
including the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev/Sobolev duality against T92's closed
form, before finding that every one of those tables already existed as a
draft. Nothing in the screening had shown it. `numberdb.table('T1')` through
`T279` had answered, `T280` onward had not, and `search_text` on the family
names returned noise. What found it was the tag page:

    https://numberdb.org/tags        -- 70 tags, each with its table count
    https://numberdb.org/tags/inequality
      T355 Sharp constants in the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
      T357 Sharp constants in Young's convolution inequality
      T92  Best Sobolev constant for W^{1,p}(R^n)
      T354 Sharp constants in the fractional Sobolev inequality
      T358 Sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg constants of Del Pino and Dolbeault
      T356 Babenko-Beckner constants of the Hausdorff-Young inequality
      T359 Sharp constant in Nash's inequality

The tag listing had said `inequality (7 tables, 4299 entries)` while the
published corpus showed one. That discrepancy is the whole signal, and it is
visible on one page.

Walking every tag page and collecting `href="/T\d+"` gave a **complete
391-table inventory, drafts included**, as ordinary web pages with no API key
and no rate limit. Grepping that inventory is a far better "is this area
already taken" than any number of `search_text` calls: search returns what
shares a *word*, and a draft in your area under another name shares none.

What the skill says now: "Is the table already here under another name?",
answered with `search_text` and `numberdb.table`, plus a note that `/drafts`
lists work in progress. `/drafts` requires a login and shows only your own.

What it should say: before proposing or building in an area, fetch
`https://numberdb.org/tags` and the tag pages nearest your subject. They list
drafts as well as published tables, they are the only listing of the corpus
there is, and a tag whose table count exceeds what you can find published is
telling you somebody is already working there.

Evidence: the seven tables above, against a published corpus in which
`numberdb.table` answers for T92 and for no other table with that tag.

## The rate limit is global, and a corpus walk spends all of it

What happened: walking `numberdb.table('T1')` .. `('T279')` to inventory the
corpus succeeded, and every API call for the next twenty minutes did not:

    RateLimitError: too many requests; retry in 999s

The limit is shared across `table`, `search_text` and `tag`, so an inventory
walk costs the searches you were about to run. Worse, the second walk (T280
onward, to find the drafts) was written as

    try:    d = numberdb.table(t)
    except: d = None

which turned *rate limited* into *this table does not exist*, and the walk
reported that the corpus ends at T279. It ends at T396. The screen module's
own docstring warns about exactly this shape for `search_text` -- "a failed
question and an empty answer must not look the same" -- and the same trap is
one line away in any loop over `table`.

What the skill says now: nothing about the rate limit, and nothing about
`table` failing.

What it should say: the API rate-limits all reads together and answers
`RateLimitError` with the seconds to wait; do not walk the corpus with
`table`. Catch `RateLimitError` separately from a missing table and stop,
because swallowing it makes a throttled read indistinguishable from an empty
corpus. The tag pages above are the walk you wanted anyway.

Evidence: `RateLimitError: too many requests; retry in 999s` from
`search_text`, from `table('T92')` and from `tag('inequality')` in the same
minute, after 279 successful `table` calls.

## Derive the recurrence; a wrong one does not fail, it answers

What happened: Leaver's three-term recurrence for the Schwarzschild
quasinormal modes, written from memory, converged smoothly to thirty digits
on

    M*omega = 0.354300008904891230 - 0.089836165618570902 i

where the literature says `0.3736716844 - 0.0889623157 i`. Nothing in the run
looked wrong: the continued fraction converged, the root-finder converged, the
imaginary part was right to two digits. Deriving the recurrence from the
Regge-Wheeler equation instead -- substituting `psi = P u` with
`P = x^{-iw}(1-x)^{-iw} exp(iw/(1-x))`, `x = (r-1)/r`, and clearing
`x(x-1)^2` -- gave

    alpha_n = (n+1)(n + 1 - 2 i w)
    beta_n  = -( 2n^2 + (2 - 8 i w) n + l(l+1) - s^2 - 8 w^2 - 4 i w + 1 )
    gamma_n = (n - 2 i w)^2 - s^2

against a recalled `gamma_n` carrying a spurious `+ 1`. With the derived
coefficients the fundamental mode came out
`0.373671684418041835793492 - 0.088962315688935698280461 i`: every published
digit.

The same run then found that root-finding on the *uninverted* continued
fraction, with a guess walked down the imaginary axis, returns
`0.4254 - 0.2445i` for the first overtone -- a number that is not a
quasinormal frequency at all. Leaver's `n`-th inversion (root-find on
`beta_n - head_n - tail_n` with the finite head running down to 0) is what
selects the `n`-th overtone, and with it all six `l = 2` modes agreed with
the published table.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent" and
"use known-answer control checks". It does not say that the *definition* you
are checking against can be the thing that is wrong.

What it should say: where a table rests on a recurrence, a series or a
continued fraction taken from a paper, derive it in the generator's own terms
and check the derivation against the recalled form. A recurrence that is
wrong in one coefficient converges, to a number with the right order of
magnitude, and the agreement check you planned will confirm it against your
own wrong formula. Check against a published *value*, not a published
*method*.

Evidence: `0.3543...` and `0.3737...` from the same code with one term
changed; six overtones matching the literature only after the inversion.

## What a Sage import does not bring, continued: `RealBall.str` and Maxima

What happened: two things the skill's list of "what the named imports do not
bring" does not mention, both met in one run.

`RealBall` has no `.str()`. The skill already says this in passing; what it
does not give is the way round, which is

    x.mid().str(digits=25)        # RealNumber.str does take digits

`RealNumber` has it, `RealBall` does not, and `repr(x)` gives you the ball
with its radius and a precision you did not choose.

`Expression.simplify_full()` raises, because it loads Maxima and Maxima's
Sage bridge fails to import:

    AttributeError: module 'sage.functions' has no attribute 'other'
      in sage/interfaces/maxima_lib.py, line 1209

so no symbolic simplification is available through the symbolic ring at all.
What worked was doing the algebra as rational functions instead -- write the
coefficients as ratios of polynomials, `cancel`, take the `lcm` of the
denominators, divide out the `gcd` -- which needs no simplifier and is faster.
`sympy` is present and works, but `sympy.simplify` on an expression of this
size did not return in ten minutes; `cancel`, `expand` and `Poly` did, in
seconds.

What the skill says now: "What the named imports do not bring: root finding
(`sage.numerical.optimize`), `RealBall.str`, and `QQ('1.25')`."

What it should say: add that `simplify_full` and everything else routed
through Maxima is unavailable, so symbolic work must be done as explicit
polynomial or rational-function algebra; and give `x.mid().str(digits=n)` as
the replacement for `RealBall.str`, since a reader who hits the gap needs the
substitute, not the name of the gap. Also: reach for `cancel`/`expand`/`Poly`
rather than `simplify`, which on a modest rational function did not finish.

Evidence: the Maxima traceback above; a `sympy.simplify` call killed after
ten minutes and the same derivation completing in seconds without it.

## A closed form can be exactly right and still return NaN at the endpoint

What happened: the Bardeen-Press-Teukolsky expressions for the innermost
stable circular orbit of a Kerr black hole reproduce every exact check --
`r = 6`, `E = sqrt(8/9)`, `L = 2 sqrt(3)` at zero spin, `r = 1` and
`E = 1/sqrt(3)` at extremal spin -- and return `NaN` for the energy and the
angular momentum *at* extremal spin, where the expression is `0/0`. Ball
arithmetic does not rescue it: the balls contain the indeterminacy honestly.

What the skill says now: "State the constraint entries meet"; nothing about
the endpoint of a parameter range being a removable singularity of the
formula you are enumerating over.

What it should say: when the generator enumerates a parameter to the edge of
its range, evaluate the formula *at* the edge before choosing the range. A
closed form that is right on the interior can be `0/0` at the endpoint, and
the endpoint is often the row a reader most wants (the extremal black hole,
the critical exponent, the degenerate case). Either exclude it with a
`complete-note` saying so, or store the limit and say in the entry comment
that it is a limit.

Evidence: `a=1  r=1.000000  E=NaN  L=NaN  Omega=0.500000` from
`RealBallField(300)`, beside six exact checks that passed.
