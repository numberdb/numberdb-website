## A stored value may be written `1836.15267343(11)`, and it is indexed at *one* sigma

What happened: T12 and T76 hold the same measurement. T12 writes
`1836.152673430 +/- 5.51e-7`, which is CODATA 2018's value widened to five
standard uncertainties by hand. T76 writes the concise form
`1836.15267343(11)` straight from NIST, and marks the entry
`equals: HREF{Proton-to-electron_mass_ratio#mu}` — the two tables assert they
hold the same number.

They are not searchable through the same window. `build_number_table` in
`data_pipeline/build.py:401` — which is not only the old data-repository
importer but the function `numberdb_app/editing.py:427` calls on **every**
save and publish — falls through to
`number_with_uncertainty_to_real_ball(number, standard_deviations=1)`. So
`(11)` is indexed as a ball of radius 1.1e-7 while T12's `+/-` form is indexed
at 5.51e-7, a factor of five apart on entries that declare themselves equal.
The file says so itself, in a comment above the call: "we interpret numbers
with uncertainty as real balls, whose radius is 1 standard uncertainty. That
is, that the actual number lies within it has probability 68%, **which is not
good**."

Measured, not inferred. A query 3e-7 from the centre, comfortably inside T12's
interval:

    numberdb.search('1836.15267373')
    -> [Result('1836.152673430 +/- 0.000000551', table='Proton-to-electron mass ratio')]

    numberdb.search('1836.15267343')
    -> [Result('1836.15267343(11)', table='Mass ratios'),
        Result('1836.152673430 +/- 0.000000551', table='Proton-to-electron mass ratio')]

T76 drops out. A reader holding a measurement that differs from the central
value by two standard uncertainties — an ordinary thing for a measurement to
do — finds one of the two tables that hold their number and not the other.

What the skill says now: nothing. §5 lists **four** forms a real may be
written in (`3.14`, `[2, 2.3728596]`, `3.14 +/- 2e-2`, `1p31415`) and says
"the first is the one the corpus is written in". The `(nn)` form is not among
them, so a contributor reading the skill would not know it is accepted at all,
let alone what radius it becomes.

What it should say: two sentences in the table of real forms.

- `1836.15267343(11)` is accepted: the parenthesis is the uncertainty in the
  last digits written, NIST's and CODATA's own notation, and it is what you
  should write when transcribing a published measurement rather than
  re-deriving its interval.
- **It is indexed at one standard uncertainty**, where the `+/-` form is
  indexed at exactly what you wrote. If a table's convention is to hold the
  measurement to five sigma — which is what every `rigour: measured` table in
  this corpus says in its `reliability` note — then writing `(11)` does not
  express that, and the two forms are not interchangeable even though they
  print the same number. Pick one convention per subject and say which in
  `reliability`.

The deeper version of this is a question for a person rather than a lesson:
whether `(nn)` ought to be read at five sigma to match what the `reliability`
notes claim, which would change the indexed radius of every entry in T76 and
T78. The TODO in `build.py` is already asking it. This proposal only asks that
the skill stop being silent about a form the corpus uses in two tables.

Evidence: `numberdb.table('T12')`, `numberdb.table('T76')`; the four searches
above through `clients/python`; `data_pipeline/build.py:401` and its comment;
`numberdb_app/editing.py:427`. 2026-09-21, T12 growth review.

## An audit that says `clean` on a table with two entries has not run the overlap check

What happened: `GET /api/table/T12/audit` returned
`{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. Every value in T12 is also in T76, which is
the exact thing `_values_also_in_another_table` exists to report, and its message is the
finding the review ended up writing by hand: *"if these are the same numbers,
one of the two tables should not exist, and if they are not, say how they
differ in Similar tables"*.

It never ran. The check opens with

    rows = list(Number.objects.filter(table=table)...)
    if len(rows) < self.ENOUGH:      # ENOUGH = 3
        return

and T12 has two indexed values. Had it run it would have fired at the ceiling:
2 of 2 distinctive values found elsewhere, against a threshold of three and of
half.

The floor is not wrong — three is there so the check does not shout about a
small table whose one shared value proves nothing — but the consequence is
worth a sentence, because `clean: true` reads like a verdict and here it is
the absence of evidence.

What the skill says now: "Let `manage.py audit_table` confirm afterwards
rather than instead" (§1), which is the right instinct and not specific enough
to cover this.

What it should say: the audit's checks have sample floors, and on a table of
one or two entries several of them return before looking at anything — the
overlap-with-another-table check needs three indexed values, and the
parameter-grid check needs two entries under each of two parameter values. So
a **small table's `clean` is much weaker than a large one's**, and the one
question it cannot answer for you is whether your handful of numbers already
sits in somebody else's table. Search each of them yourself before publishing;
on a two-entry table that is two calls.

Evidence: `numberdb_app/management/commands/audit_table.py`, `SAMPLE = 8`
(:476), `ENOUGH = 3` (:482), `COMMON = 4` (:499), and the guard at the head of
`_values_also_in_another_table` (:688, guard at :709-711).
`GET /api/table/T12/audit` clean while
`numberdb.search` finds both of T12's values in T76. 2026-09-21.

## `HREF{}` works inside `complete-note`, and that is where a range's cross-reference belongs

What happened: the review wanted to recommend a `complete-note` that points at
the neighbouring table, and had to check whether the link would render or
print raw — `numberdb_app/views.py:1177` folds the note into the `complete`
line with `escape_maths` and no visible call to the prose renderer, which
reads as though it would not.

It does. T296's note is

    it holds $K(m_r)$ for every integer $1\leq r\leq100$, matching
    HREF{T295}[the singular moduli $k_r$]; over this range the order
    $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-r}]$ has class number at most $12$

and the page renders `<a class="HREF" href="T295">the singular moduli
$k_r$</a>` inside the parentheses after "Table is complete: no".

What the skill says now: "It renders inside 'complete: no (...)', so write a
clause that finishes that sentence", with an example that has no link in it.

What it should say: the note takes `HREF{}` and mathematics like any other
prose field, and a link is often the clearest way to finish the sentence —
"it holds every $r\leq100$, matching HREF{T295}[the singular moduli]", "the
rest of the family is in HREF{T76}[Mass ratios]". A reader deciding whether
their number is in range meets that line before `Similar tables`, so the
cross-reference that answers "then where is it?" earns its place there as well
as lower down.

Evidence: `numberdb.table('T296')['Data properties']['complete-note']` and the
rendered `https://numberdb.org/T296`, 2026-09-21.
