## Name the condition that picks out the notable rows, not the rows

What happened: T333 holds the Euler characteristics of 999 smooth complete
intersections. Four entry comments name Calabi-Yau threefolds -- the quintic,
$(2,4)$, $(3,3)$, and the four quadrics -- and the fifth complete-intersection
Calabi-Yau threefold, $(2,2,3)$ in $\mathbb P^6$, has no comment. Both
$(3,3)$ in $\mathbb P^5$ and $(2,2,3)$ in $\mathbb P^6$ store $-144$, so the
page shows the same number twice, once named a Calabi-Yau threefold and once
bare. The condition is closed and short: a complete intersection is Calabi-Yau
exactly when $\sum_i d_i = n+1$, and 53 of the 999 rows satisfy it. The sibling
table T332 states that condition in one comment instead, and names every one of
the 53 by doing so.

What the skill says now: it says an entry's comment is in no search index, and
that a comment on every entry is worth leaving out "when it repeats what a
formula in the document already says for every row at once". Both are about
cost and about search. Neither says that a roll-call of notable rows is
incomplete by construction, and neither points at the repair.

What it should say: where a property that makes rows notable is given by a
condition on the parameters, put the condition in a table comment. Naming the
rows one at a time in their own comments cannot be finished, goes out of date
when the range is extended, and leaves two rows holding the same value labelled
differently. Entry comments are then for what is true of that row alone -- a
name the condition does not supply, a caveat about one value.

Evidence: T333, 2026-09-18. Its `Numbers` has 53 rows with $\sum_i d_i=n+1$ and
four entry comments naming Calabi-Yau threefolds; `Numbers['6']['2,2,3']` is
the plain string `-144` and `Numbers['5']['3,3']` is `-144` with the comment
"Calabi-Yau threefold of type $(3,3)$". T332's `comment-c1` states the
condition once.
