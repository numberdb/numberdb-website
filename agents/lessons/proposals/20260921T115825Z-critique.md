## An exact title can rank second: the search's ordering is dominated by the common word

What happened: the T381 critique of 2026-09-21 established that the text
search ranks rather than filters, so a word the table does not carry costs it
position rather than the hit, and concluded that the test for `Keywords` is
"would they find it *first*". This run found that the test can fail for a
table whose title the reader types **exactly**.

`Fine-structure constant` is the title of T10, and it is also the whole of the
query:

    q=Fine-structure constant   -> 20 results: Lévy's constant T172,
                                   Fine-structure constant T10,
                                   Eigenvalues of the Gauss-Kuzmin-Wirsing
                                   operator T174, Bendersky constants T227, ...
    q=Sommerfeld constant       -> the same ordering (Sommerfeld constant is
                                   T10's only keyword)
    q=Sommerfeld                -> Fine-structure constant T10, first and alone

It is not about T10. The same block of tables, led by T172, comes back for
every query ending in "constant", with the queried table somewhere inside it:

    q=Lochs's constant          -> T172, T227, T174, T249, Lochs's constant T173, ...
    q=Glaisher-Kinkelin constant-> T172, T227, T174, Glaisher-Kinkelin constant T249, ...
    q=Bendersky constants       -> T172, Bendersky constants T227, T174, T249, ...

The distinguishing word is in exactly one title each time, and it does not
lift that title to the top; the ordering looks the same whichever
distinguishing word is used, i.e. it is being decided by the common lexeme.
Queries with no such word behave as one would expect
(`q=Proton-to-electron mass ratio` returns T12 then T76).

What the skill says now: that `Keywords` share the title's weight and that the
test is "what would somebody type who is holding one of these numbers". With
the T381 lesson pending, "would they find it first".

What it should say: that a table whose title ends in a word many titles share
— "constant", "constants", "numbers", "polynomials" — will not necessarily be
first even for a reader who types its title in full, and that the word worth
putting in `Keywords` is therefore the *distinguishing* one, not the generic
one. `Sommerfeld` alone finds T10 and nothing else; `Sommerfeld constant`
does not put it first. A contributor choosing keywords should pick words that
are rare in the corpus, and can check by typing them.

Evidence: live queries against `https://numberdb.org/?q=...`, 2026-09-21,
quoted above; T10's `Keywords: [Sommerfeld constant]` from
`numberdb.table('T10')`.

## When the measurements disagree, a measured table's interval should be their union, and narrowing it loses readers

What happened: T10 (`Fine-structure constant`) and T12
(`Proton-to-electron mass ratio`) are the same kind of table — one measured
constant, in two invertible conventions — and they use two different
conventions for the stored interval.

T12, repaired on 2026-09-21, centres each interval on the current CODATA value
with radius five standard uncertainties. T10 takes the five measurements
published between 2014 and 2020, widens **each** to five standard
uncertainties, and stores the **union**; its recorded `generate.sage` does
exactly that, and the arithmetic reproduces the stored entries to the last
place.

For $\alpha$ the two conventions are not interchangeable, because the
measurements are in tension: Parker et al. 2018 give $\alpha^{-1} =
137.035999046(27)$ and Morel et al. 2020 give $137.035999206(11)$, five
combined sigma apart, and CODATA 2022 sits between them at
$137.035999177(21)$. Parker's central value lies **outside** five sigma of
CODATA 2022 — $137.035999046 \notin [137.035999072,\,137.035999282]$ — so
adopting T12's convention would remove a published measurement of this
constant from the table's interval.

That is not a cosmetic difference, because **search matches by overlap with
the stored interval**. Live, against the current union
$[137.035998910,\,137.035999316]$:

    q=137.035999046   (Parker 2018)   -> 1 result, Fine-structure constant #alpha-inv
    q=137.035999300   (inside, high)  -> 1 result
    q=137.035999500   (outside)       -> nothing
    q=0.0072973525900 (outside)       -> nothing

Under the narrower convention the first of those would answer nothing, for a
reader holding one of the two numbers the discrepancy in this constant is
about.

The union convention also has a property worth stating on its own: it is
monotone, so a new measurement changes the table only if it falls outside what
is stored. CODATA 2022 at five sigma lies strictly inside T10's two intervals,
so re-running its generator with the newest adjustment returns exactly the
entries already there. A table built this way can be *checked* cheaply and
rarely needs editing, which is the opposite of a centred interval that is
stale the day a new adjustment appears.

What the skill says now: `rigour: measured` is "not computed at all — an
experimental value. Not on the scale", and §5 says a real is stored as an
interval containing it. Nothing about which interval to choose when several
experiments disagree, and nothing about the `(nn)` concise form at all (the
subject of a separate proposal from the T12 review).

What it should say: for a measured quantity, the stored interval is a
catchment as much as a claim — search matches numbers that overlap it. So when
the published measurements disagree by more than their uncertainties, store
the union of them widened to a stated number of standard uncertainties, say in
`reliability` which measurements and how many sigma, and do not narrow it onto
the latest adjustment: a reader arrives holding an experiment's number, not a
committee's. When they agree, centring on the current recommended value is
fine and the two conventions nearly coincide.

Evidence: `https://numberdb.org/files/T10/generate.sage?raw=1`;
`utils/utils.py:762`, where `number_with_uncertainty_to_real_ball` defaults to
`standard_deviations = 5`; NIST `physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?alphinv`
and `scipy.constants` 1.17.1, which agree on CODATA 2022; the five live
queries above, 2026-09-21; `agents/critiques/T10-growth.md` §3 and §4 for the
arithmetic.
