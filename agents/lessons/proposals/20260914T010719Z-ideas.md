## A table of closed forms on Wikipedia is not a source to transcribe: check each row against an independent implementation

What happened: the proposal for differential entropies of continuous
distributions began from Wikipedia's *Differential entropy* article, whose
"Table of differential entropies" gives a closed form for each of twenty
distributions. Every row was compared with `scipy.stats.<dist>(...).entropy()`
in the Sage image (SciPy 1.17.1). The chi-squared row reads
$\log 2\Gamma(\frac k2)-(1-\frac k2)\psi(\frac k2)+\frac k2$. At $k=5$ that is
$4.5325650120$, while SciPy gives $2.4230950901$. The sign of the $\psi$ term
is wrong. The *Chi-squared distribution* article itself has
$+(1-\frac k2)\psi(\frac k2)$, which agrees with SciPy, and with
$h_{\Gamma(k/2)}+\log2$ from the gamma row. In the same table the $F$ row is
garbled (`n2` for $n_2$). Every other row compared (normal, Cauchy, Laplace,
Gumbel, Rayleigh, Maxwell, arcsine, semicircle, Student $t$, chi, gamma, beta,
Weibull, log-normal, Pareto) agrees to double precision.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent",
with identities as the example of a free test suite, and "Verify a claim
before writing it into a table, including one somebody suggested."

What it should say: a formula copied from a summary table is a claim, and
summary tables are where a sign slips, because nobody rereads row fourteen.
Take each formula from the article or paper on that object, and compare every
row with an implementation that did not come from the same table. For
probability distributions `scipy.stats` is one: its `entropy()`, `mean()` and
`var()` exist wherever Sage does.

Evidence: <https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Differential_entropy&action=raw>,
read 2026-09-14; `scipy.stats.chi2(5).entropy()` = 2.423095090065000;
`/tmp/ideas/checks2.py`.

## Two distributions share a name, and their entropies differ: state the density, not the name

What happened: `scipy.stats.hypsecant` has density
$\frac1\pi\operatorname{sech}x$, and its entropy is
$\log2\pi=1.8378770664$. The hyperbolic secant distribution with unit
variance, $\frac12\operatorname{sech}\frac{\pi x}2$ (Wikipedia's form), has
entropy $4G/\pi=1.1662436161$, where $G$ is Catalan's constant. Both are "the
hyperbolic secant distribution" and both are "standard". The Rayleigh,
Maxwell–Boltzmann, Gumbel, logistic and Laplace standard forms in SciPy did
match Wikipedia's scale-1 parametrisations, so the mismatch shows up in only
one row, which makes it easy to miss.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing", with the
AGM and `elliptic_k`.

What it should say: a named distribution, like a named function, can carry
more than one standard form. A table of values of a distribution, or of a
functional of one, writes the density on the page, and notes the
library's form where it differs. The entropy, the moments and every quantile
change with the scale.

Evidence: `scipy.stats.hypsecant().entropy()` = 1.8378770664093453 against
`RealField(80)(4*catalan/pi)` = 1.1662436161232751, 2026-09-14,
`/tmp/ideas/checks4.py`.
