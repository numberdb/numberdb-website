## A mixed-parameter family must state each proposal's finite grid

What happened: the Mathieu family fixed the equation, the positive $q$ grid,
the precision, and the tags, and said "$n$ runs to $10$." That settles the
two characteristic-value tables $a_n(q)$ and $b_n(q)$, but the proposal
"Characteristic exponents $\nu(a,q)$ of the Mathieu equation" has no $n$
parameter. Its missing finite choice is the grid for $a$. Several plausible
choices would give different tables, for example integer $a$, decimal $a$,
or the unperturbed values $a=n^2$. None is forced by DLMF's definition of the
characteristic exponent, which defines $\nu$ for arbitrary $a$ and $q$.

What the skill says now: stop rather than guessing if a proposal leaves a
convention open and no source settles it.

What it should say: for a family whose proposed tables have different
parameter sets, the family conventions must settle the finite computed range
for each table, not only the range for the highest-ranked or most familiar
tables. A sentence like "$n$ runs to $10$" does not settle a table whose title
has parameters $a$ and $q$ and no $n$; the build should skip it until the
proposal says which $a$ values are intended.

Evidence: `python3 agents/queue.py show 194`, 2026-09-23, gives the $q$ grid
and "$n$ runs to $10$"; DLMF 28.2.16 defines the characteristic exponent by
`cos(pi nu) = w_I(pi; a, q)` for arbitrary $a$ and $q$, but does not choose a
finite $a$ grid.
