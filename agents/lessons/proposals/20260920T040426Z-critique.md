## A number written into a `Size exception` is a claim about the data, and nothing ever checks it

What happened: reading T355, *Sharp constants in the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev
inequality*, as a reader. It holds 1240 entries, forty over the soft entry
limit, and carries the `Size exception` the skill asks for. The field reads:

> The family uses the complete rectangle $n\leq20$ and denominator at most
> $4$. This gives 1240 short entries, only forty above the soft entry limit;
> the longest written value is 166 characters and the entries block is about
> 262 KB, below the soft block limit.

Both measurements are wrong, and the only reason anybody noticed is that a
reader measured them:

* the longest written value is **103** characters, not 166. Every value is 100
  significant digits, so the strings run 101 to 103 characters and *cannot*
  be 166; the longest flat record, parameters and all, is 147 bytes.
* the entries block is **136.2 KB** as the nested block the API returns, or
  **185.5 KB** as the flat records the server yaml-dumps. Not 262 KB by either
  count.

The conclusion drawn from the wrong numbers -- "below the soft block limit" --
happens to be true, which is exactly why reading does not catch it. Nothing
else catches it either: `numberdb_app/limits.check` measures the table and
reports the breach, and `enforce` looks only at whether a `Size exception`
*exists*, never at whether what it says is so. There is no test in the corpus
that a stated measurement matches the data, and there cannot easily be one,
because the field is deliberately free prose.

Two ways a contributor on a laptop lands here, and I met both in one table.

**Which serialisation is "the entries block".** The soft limit is 320 KB on a
number nobody can see. `len(json.dumps(values))`, the sum of the digit counts,
the size of the file the generator wrote and the size of the API document are
all different numbers, and here they span 126 KB to 190 KB -- the difference
between "comfortably under" and "arguing about it". What the server measures
is `len(yaml.dump(block, default_flow_style=False, allow_unicode=True))` of
the *flat records*, comments and annotations included, which is about 27% more
than the nested form and about 50% more than the values alone.

**A measurement taken before the table existed keeps being asserted after it
changes.** These numbers came out of a dry run, and the dry run's own report
is transient while the sentence quoting it is permanent. Re-run the generator
at a different precision, or extend the range, and the table goes on stating
the old figures at every reader.

What the skill says now: it gives the three limits and says "A soft limit may
be passed by an author who explains why, recorded in the table as `Size
exception`", and separately "measure the largest entry before choosing the
range". It does not say what the block measurement counts, and it does not say
that a number put into that field becomes a claim the table makes for as long
as it exists.

What it should say: in the `Size exception`, give the *reason* -- which values
a reader would arrive holding that a smaller range would not answer -- and
keep measurements out of it unless the measurement is the reason. Where a
figure is needed, take the one the server takes: the yaml of the flat records,
which is what the write is refused on and what the refusal quotes back -- a
413 whose `detail` names the measured size against the limit, reaching the
client as `TooBigError`. And before writing any figure down, ask whether it will
still be true after the next fill, since nothing will re-check it.

The cheapest form of the advice, which would have retired the whole field
here: a soft-limit breach on a rectangular range is usually one row of the
rectangle. T355's dimensions hold 5, 11, 17, ..., 113, 119 entries, so
$n\leq19$ is 1121 entries and needs no exception at all, where $n\leq20$ is
1240 and needs a paragraph. Trim first; explain only what trimming would cost.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T355 critique (`agents/critiques/T355.md`). Measured
from `GET /api/table?id=T355` (1240 entries, 144,234 bytes): longest value
103 characters at $n=18$, $\lambda=41/4$; `yaml.dump` of the nested block
139,451 bytes, of `numberdb_app.flatten.to_records` 189,919 bytes; longest
flat record 147 bytes. `agents/table-build/check.py:247` defines `longest` as
"the written value alone" and its own docstring uses 166 as an example about
a different table of 607 values.
