## A one-paragraph `rigour details` folds too, mid-paragraph, and markup counts against the budget

What happened: T352's `rigour details` is a single paragraph of 740 characters
with no newline in it. On the page it renders as

> Values are computed as real parts of Sage complex balls with arb at
> `numberdb.bits(digits, losing=64)` bits from exact rational parameters and
> arguments, using the complement formula (1). The imaginary part is required
> to contain zero. The widest returned ball had radius less than $10^{-117}$
> for 100 requested digits.  **more**

with the rest — "The entries are checked against the recurrence (2), against
the error-function identity (3), against the elementary integer formula (4),
and against the Kummer relation (5)." — behind the disclosure. That sentence
is the whole argument for the table's `proven` label, and it is the half a
reader does not see.

The author had no reason to expect a fold: there is only one paragraph, and
the skill says a note folds "after its first paragraph". Two things are true
of the renderer that the skill does not say.

**It cuts a single paragraph mid-paragraph.** `numberdb_app/prose.py` sets
`FOLD_ABOVE = 400`; when there is no second block, `_split_sentence` cuts at
the *last* full stop followed by a capital or `$` that fits in 400, and if no
break fits it takes the first break anywhere. The code comments say why —
every one of the 139 existing notes is a single paragraph, so a
paragraph-only fold would never fire on the notes it was built for. So a note
over roughly 400 characters always folds, however it is punctuated, and the
author chooses what shows by choosing which sentence comes first.

**The 400 is measured on rendered HTML, not on what a reader sees.** The head
above is 396 characters of HTML and **317 characters of visible text**: the
`<code class="prose-code">` span around `numberdb.bits(...)` and the
`<a class="CITE" href="#formula-complement">(1)</a>` spend 79 characters of
the budget on markup nobody reads. A note with several `CITE{}`s or backticks
folds noticeably earlier than its length suggests, and each one added moves
the cut backwards by about fifty characters.

What the skill says now, in the section table:

> **`rigour details`** — how the digits were obtained, at whatever length that
> needs: blank lines make paragraphs, `- ` makes a list, backticks mark code,
> and a long note folds after its first paragraph

What it should say: that a note longer than about four hundred characters
folds whether or not it has a second paragraph — a single paragraph is cut
after a sentence — and that backticks and `CITE{}` count against that length
even though a reader does not see them. And therefore the practical rule:
**put the sentence you most want read first.** In a `rigour details` note that
is usually what was checked against something independent, not the working
precision: a reader consulting the field is deciding whether to believe the
rigour label, and the guard and the ball radius are the supporting detail.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T352 critique. `numberdb_app/prose.py`, `FOLD_ABOVE =
400` and `_split_sentence`. On the rendered draft page at
`/tmp/T352_page.html`, the `<p class="prose-paragraph">` before
`<details class="prose-more">` has 396 characters of inner HTML and 317 of
visible text, and the stored note contains no newline
(`json.load(...)['Data properties']['rigour details'].count('\n') == 0`).
