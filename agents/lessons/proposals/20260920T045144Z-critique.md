## A printed decimal is truncated or rounded, and a check written for one source rejects correct rows from the other

What happened: T284's generator checks each value against the decimal its
source prints, by demanding that the isolated root lie inside
$[d,\ d+10^{-k})$ — the interval a *truncated* decimal denotes. That is right
for its source: Mossinghoff's page ends "Note: Salem numbers are truncated,
not rounded." The obvious way to extend that table is Sac-Épée's published
list of 25 Salem numbers between 1.3 and 49/37, and his table rounds. Of his
25 rows, all 25 agree with a recomputation in interval arithmetic as
roundings and only 14 as truncations. Transcribed into the generator
unchanged, 11 of the 25 correct rows would raise `ArithmeticError` saying the
root is not inside the source interval — which reads exactly like a wrong
value, and the natural response to it, widening the check, throws away the
only thing the source decimal was there to catch.

What the skill says now: §5 says a written real "is an interval", the one its
last place denotes, and §10 says to check the claim the digits make rather
than the value at their midpoint. Both are about values this corpus stores.
Nothing says that a *source* printing a decimal may have used either
convention, or that the convention is a property of the source and not of
decimals.

What it should say: when a generator checks its values against a printed
table, establish which convention that table uses before writing the check,
and say which in a comment beside it. A source that says so is rare; a source
that can be tested is not. Recompute two or three rows to more places than
the source prints and see whether the printed digits are the leading digits
(truncation) or the nearest (rounding). A table assembled from two sources
needs two checks, one per source, because a truncation check silently refuses
about half the rows of a rounded list.

Evidence: T284, `generators/salem-numbers-less-than-1.3/generate.py`,
`check_source_truncation`; Sac-Épée, *Salem numbers less than 49/37*,
arXiv:2409.11159v3 §3.2, 25 rows; `/tmp/t284growth3.py` run through
`agents/sage.sh` on 2026-09-20 prints, per row, whether the printed decimal
is a truncation and whether it is a rounding of the interval-arithmetic root:
"of 25 rows: 14 are truncations of the computed value, 25 are roundings".

## `roots(RealIntervalField)` can raise AssertionError; Sturm counting and bisection do the same job

What happened: a check on T284 needed the number of real roots of high-degree
integer polynomials, in a sweep over the polynomials $x^kp(x)\pm p^*(x)$ of
Salem's construction, degrees up to about 90. `polynomial.roots(field)` with
`field = RealIntervalField(400)` — which the table's own generator uses, and
which works on every polynomial the table stores, to degree 46 — died on one
of them with a bare `AssertionError` from
`sage.rings.polynomial.real_roots.interval_bernstein_polynomial_integer.de_casteljau`,
no message and nothing naming the input, and the failure is inside the
isolation algorithm rather than a shortage of precision to report.

What the skill says now: §5 and §7 recommend ball and interval arithmetic and
name `RealIntervalField` as fine when the whole computation is interval
arithmetic. Nothing says it can fail outright, or what to do then.

What it should say: `roots(RealIntervalField(...))` is the right tool for
isolating a root you are going to store, and it is not a reliable way to
*count* roots over a family of polynomials, because Sage's real-root isolation
can raise `AssertionError` on high-degree inputs. Where a count is what you
need, PARI's Sturm sequences answer exactly and cannot fail this way:
`pari(str(f)).polsturm([a, b])` is the number of real roots in $[a,b]$, in
exact arithmetic. Where a value is what you need and the isolation fails,
bisecting on sign changes in `QQ` costs a few lines and gives an interval as
honest as any other. A rigorous count plus a rigorous bracket is often all a
verification wants, and neither step needs a root finder.

Evidence: `/tmp/t284growth.py` through `agents/sage.sh`, 2026-09-20, traceback
ending `File "sage/rings/polynomial/real_roots.pyx", line 740, in
interval_bernstein_polynomial_integer.de_casteljau / AssertionError`;
`/tmp/t284growth2.py` is the same sweep with `polsturm` and `QQ` bisection and
completes in under a minute, finding 14 Salem numbers between 1.3 and 49/37.
The Salem test itself is worth the note too: for a reciprocal $P$ of degree
$2d$ there is a $Q$ of degree $d$ with $P(x)=x^dQ(x+1/x)$, built from the
Dickson recurrence $D_0=2$, $D_1=y$, $D_k=yD_{k-1}-D_{k-2}$, and the condition
"exactly one root outside the unit circle" becomes "every root of $Q$ is real,
one above 2 and the rest inside $(-2,2)$", which is three `polsturm` calls.
