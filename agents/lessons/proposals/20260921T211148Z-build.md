## Check algebraic identities in ball arithmetic when exactification becomes the computation
What happened: the Daubechies filter generator builds every coefficient as an
exact algebraic number in `QQbar`. Directly checking the normalisation,
vanishing moments and even-shift orthogonality by exact `QQbar` sums was still
running after more than a minute on the intended range, because each zero test
forced algebraic exactification of long sums of conjugates. The generator now
keeps the exact `QQbar` construction for the values, then converts the exact
coefficients to high-precision real balls and checks that each identity
contains zero with a small radius. That is still a certified check, and it
keeps the dry run fast enough to iterate.

What the skill says now: check every identity before writing it down, and use
balls for numerical enclosures. It does not say what to do when the values are
exact algebraic but the identity checks themselves are what becomes
intractable.

What it should say: for a family of exact algebraic numbers, it is acceptable
to construct the values exactly and verify identities using interval
arithmetic, provided the interval calculation proves the required zero or
equality with a radius bound that is much smaller than the stored precision.
Do not wait for `QQbar` to exactify every large sum if a ball enclosure proves
the same statement.

Evidence: T391 build on 2026-09-21. The original exact
`run_integrity_checks()` did not finish within the wrapper timeout on
$1\leq N\leq 25$; the checked generator verifies the same identities in
160-digit real balls with zero enclosures of radius below $10^{-120}$, and
dry-run reports 650 exact entries.
