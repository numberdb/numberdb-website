## Use `nfdisc`, not `poldisc`, for the discriminant of a number field

What happened: the quartic-field residue generator enumerated the right 456
raw fields with PARI's `nflist`, then reduced their defining polynomials with
`polredabs`. It first filtered and indexed them by `poldisc(reduced)`, which is
the discriminant of the displayed polynomial, not necessarily the field
discriminant. That left only 300 fields with `|D|\leq 5000`; the missing 156
were fields where the reduced polynomial does not generate the full ring of
integers.

What the skill says now: it says to pin down the parameters and to check the
range, but nothing about the difference between a polynomial discriminant and a
field discriminant when a defining polynomial is only a generator of the field.

What it should say: when a table is indexed by the discriminant of a number
field, compute the parameter with the field discriminant, for example PARI's
`nfdisc(f)` or `bnfinit(f, 1).disc`, not with `poldisc(f)` unless the power
basis has been proved integral. `polredabs` gives a reproducible defining
polynomial; it does not say that polynomial's order is maximal.

Evidence: T365 build, 2026-09-20. `/tmp/probe_nflist_counts.py` found the
family's 456 fields from `nflist("C4"|"V4"|"D4"|"A4"|"S4", [1, 5000])`.
The first generator using `poldisc(polredabs(f))` raised "expected 456 fields,
found 300"; changing the discriminator to `nfdisc(polredabs(f))` produced 456
fields with signature counts 20 of $(4,0)$, 180 of $(2,1)$ and 256 of $(0,2)$,
as issue #169 records.
