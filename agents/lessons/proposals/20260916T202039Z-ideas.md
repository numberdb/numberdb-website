## In Singular, a polynomial named like a ring variable gives a wrong b-function and no error

What happened: checking the b-functions of classical prehomogeneous
invariants through Sage's Singular interface, one session ran
`ring r = 0,(a,b,c,d,e,f),dp; poly f = a*f-b*e+c*d; bfct(f);` and similar for
the $3\times3$ determinant in `a..i`. Singular returned $(s+1)(s+2)$ for the
$4\times4$ Pfaffian and $(s+1)(s+2)^2$ for the $3\times3$ determinant. Both
are wrong: Cayley's identity gives $(s+1)(s+2)(s+3)$, and a rank-6 quadratic
form gives $(s+1)(s+3)$. Nothing was raised. Every ring without a variable
called `f` gave the right answer in the same session. Renaming the
polynomial `F` and starting a fresh `Singular()` per case made `bfct`,
`bfctAnn` and `bernsteinBM` all agree with the closed forms.

What the skill says now: "A measurement needs a control that returns a known
answer". Nothing about Singular.

What it should say: when driving Singular from Sage, never give a Singular
object the name of a ring variable (`f`, `x`, `s`, `t` are the usual
collisions), and start a fresh `Singular()` when rings change. Run a closed
form first (Cayley's $\det$, or $x_1^2+\dots+x_n^2\mapsto(s+1)(s+n/2)$). That
control is what caught this.

Evidence: `/tmp/b2020/pv.py` (wrong) against `/tmp/b2020/pv2.py` (right),
2026-09-16.

## A Bernstein–Sato polynomial depends on the modulus, so the normal form is part of the definition

What happened: a proposal was to tabulate b-functions of Arnold's unimodal
singularities "by label". $E_{12}$ as $x^3+y^7$ and as $x^3+y^7+xy^5$ have
the same Milnor number, the same spectrum (`gmssing.lib` `spectrum`) and
hence the same monodromy. But `bfct` gives different b-functions: the root
$-32/21$ becomes $-11/21$. $Z_{11}$ ($+xy^4$) and $W_{12}$ ($+x^2y^3$) do
the same. Checking the table against the spectrum or the monodromy would
not notice a builder who used the wrong representative.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing" in
general.

What it should say: a quantity that is an analytic but not a topological
invariant (b-functions, Tjurina numbers off the quasi-homogeneous locus) is
a function of the polynomial, not of its equivalence class under a label.
State the normal form with the value of its modulus in the Definition. Check
at least one row with the modulus switched on, to see whether it matters.

Evidence: `/tmp/b2020/nf.py`, 2026-09-16.

## Singular's `quickclass` names a singularity in an error message, and misses some

What happened: `classify.lib`'s `quickclass(F)` under Sage printed "Singularity
R-equivalent to : D[k]=D[6]" and then raised `SingularError`, because it
could not open its `NFlist` database. The interface turns the whole output
into the exception text. The label has to be read out of the exception. It
also answered "Hilbert polynomial not recognised" for $E_{12}$ ($x^3+y^7$),
$E_{14}$ and $J_{10}$. It gave two candidates for $Z_{11..13}$
($Z$ or $Y$) and for $Q_{10..12}$, $S_{11}$, $S_{12}$ ($T_{p,q,r}$ or $Q$/$S$).

What the skill says now: nothing.

What it should say: do not use `quickclass` as the check that a normal form
is the named singularity. `milnor(F)` equal to the subscript and
`tjurina(F)` equal to it (for quasi-homogeneous forms) are reliable and fast.
If `quickclass` is used anyway, catch the error and read the "R-equivalent
to" line.

Evidence: `/tmp/b2020/nf.py`, 2026-09-16. `milnor` matched on all 19 forms.
