## Two tables a reader uses together need the same range, not just a `Similar tables` line

What happened: T293 holds the regulator $R_{E/K}$ for elliptic curves over
real quadratic fields, and its `Similar tables` names *Special $L$-values of
elliptic curves over real quadratic fields* as "the $L^*(E/K,1)$ side of the
Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer formula". Formula (1) on T293's page is that
formula, with both quantities in it, so a reader who wants to use the formula
needs one row from each table. The two ranges were chosen independently: T293
covers six fields at conductor norm $\leq250$, one row per curve; the
$L$-values table covers five fields at conductor norm $\leq150$, one row per
isogeny class. **309 of T293's 1171 rows have a counterpart.** Every row over
$D=21$, and every row over the other five fields at norm between 151 and 250,
is unpairable. Nothing on either page says so; the relation sentence reads as
though the two cover the same curves, and one of the two even said "for the
same ecnf-data curves" until it was edited.

Neither table is wrong on its own, and both completeness notes state their own
range honestly. The fault only exists between them, which is why no check on
one table can see it.

What the skill says now: it is good on *linking* related tables --
`Similar tables` states the relation in words, `HREF{}` links first mentions,
`equals` says two entries are the same number. It says nothing about the
ranges of two tables that are two halves of one formula. The nearest thing is
the Ehrhart observation, that "a range that is ragged for this reason is a
sign of two tables rather than of one unfinished" -- which is about one table
holding two forms, not two tables holding two sides.

What it should say: when a table's `Similar tables` entry says another table
holds *the other factor in a formula this table states*, the two ranges are
part of the product and should be chosen together. Count the overlap before
publishing the second one, and say the overlap in the completeness note if it
is not the whole of either table. A reader who can pair a quarter of the rows
has a quarter of a reference. The cheap case is the common one: extend the
table with room rather than trimming the one without it -- here the
$L$-values table holds 572 entries in 111 KB, and six fields at $N\leq200$
(1058 classes) or five fields at $N\leq250$ (1141 classes) would bring it into
step inside every limit.

Evidence: T293 and T292 on 2026-09-20, read through `GET /api/table?id=...`.
Isogeny classes and positive-rank curves counted from
`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JohnCremona/ecnf-data/10b28418e80392032b106ea00e6c5aa109d28e7b/RQF/{isoclass,mwdata}.2.2.{5,8,12,13,17,21}.1`;
the count reproduces the $L$-values table's 572 rows exactly at five fields
and $N\leq150$, so the rule read off its note is the rule it was built on.
Positive-rank curves over the five shared fields at $N\leq150$: 309.

## A range set by a bound on one quantity gives uneven depth in the family it indexes, and that unevenness is a fact the table has to state

What happened: T293's range is a single bound, "conductor norm at most 250",
applied across six base fields. The six fields get 17, 63, 253, 195, 295 and
348 rows. The bound is uniform; the result is not, because the smallest
conductor norm carrying a positive-rank curve is 199 over $D=5$ and 17 over
$D=21$. So the table's thinnest field by a factor of twenty is
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ -- the first real quadratic field anybody meets, and the
one a reader is likeliest to arrive from. The page gives that reader seventeen
rows and no sentence explaining why, although the fact that explains it is
sitting in a comment two screens down, stated as two numbers without its
consequence.

The same effect closes a sub-family off entirely. 1163 of the 1171 rows are
rank 1 and 8 are rank 2, because under a conductor-norm bound the first rank-2
curve arrives at norm 235 over $D=21$ but not until norm 1831 over $D=5$; a
range reaching rank 2 in all six fields would carry 17,643 rows. Selecting on
rank instead of on conductor norm gives 1330 curves, one table's worth, and
that is what the corpus already does over $\mathbb{Q}$, where rank 1, 2 and 3
are three tables.

What the skill says now: it is good on *which* range to choose -- "hold the
numbers that turn up", "let the arguments choose it rather than the count" --
and good on saying which range you chose and why in the completeness note. It
assumes throughout that a range is one bound over one index. It does not say
that a bound on one quantity, applied to a family indexed by another, is a
different depth in each part of the family.

What it should say: two sentences. When the range is a bound on quantity $A$
and the family is indexed by $B$, count the entries per value of $B$ before
publishing, and if they are uneven, say in a comment what the unevenness is
and what causes it -- a reader who finds their part of the family thin cannot
otherwise tell whether the table stops short or whether there is nothing
there. And when a whole sub-family turns out to be unreachable under that
bound, that is a sign the sub-family wants its own table selected on its own
axis, not a longer range: the second table is small and complete where the
extended first one would be enormous and still lopsided.

Evidence: T293 on 2026-09-20, 1171 live entries, per field 17 / 63 / 253 /
195 / 295 / 348 for $D=5,8,12,13,17,21$, by rank 1163 / 8. From the pinned
`ecnf-data` commit above, first rank-2 conductor norm by field: 235 ($D=21$),
478 ($D=17$), 637 ($D=13$), 814 ($D=12$), 1031 ($D=8$), 1831 ($D=5$);
positive-rank rows at $N\leq1831$ over the six fields, 17,643; curves of rank
exactly 2 over the six fields at any norm, 1330, and none of rank 3 or more.
The three $\mathbb{Q}$-side tables T293 links hold 1007, 955 and 1061 entries.

## The API refuses the 61st anonymous request in an hour, and a retry loop spends the budget on nothing

What happened: reading a table through `GET /api/table?id=T293` while the
network was flaky, I retried in a loop. The loop's failures were transport
failures, but each attempt that reached the server counted, and the table read
that followed came back as
`{"error": "Rate limit exceeded (60 requests per 60 minutes). ...",
"retry_after": 793}` -- thirteen minutes of waiting bought by a dozen retries
that returned nothing. Sending the key in an `X-API-Key` header answered
immediately: the limit with a key is 1000 an hour, and only `/api/*` is
limited at all, so the rendered page at `/T293` was reachable throughout.

What the skill says now: nothing about rate limits. It says publishing needs
the owner's API key, which reads as though a key is only for writing.

What it should say: the API allows 60 requests an hour without a key and 1000
with one, counted per IP or per key over a rolling hour, and it applies to
reads as well as writes. Send `X-API-Key` on reads too if you have a key.
Table *pages* are not limited, so a page fetch is the fallback when a read is
refused. And do not wrap an API call in a bare retry loop: a transport failure
and a refusal look the same to the caller and only one of them is worth
retrying.

Evidence: `numberdb_app/throttle.py`, `ANONYMOUS_LIMIT = 60`,
`IDENTIFIED_LIMIT = 1000`, `WINDOW_SECONDS = 3600`, and the docstring "Only
``/api/*`` is limited. The site's own pages, including advanced search" are
not. Observed on 2026-09-20 against numberdb.org.
