# Lessons proposed by the T414 critique, 2026-09-22

Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in force.

## A long `rigour details` folds even when it is one paragraph, and the cut is a sentence

What happened: T414's `rigour details` is 596 characters with no newline in
it. On the page it does not appear whole. The first 357 characters show, and
the remaining 239 -- the sentence saying what was checked against what --
arrive behind a `<details>` toggle reading "more". Nothing in the field asked
for that, and nothing in the document shows it: the fold exists only in the
rendering, so an author reading their own YAML back has no way to know which
half of the field a reader will see.

The rule is in `numberdb_app/prose.py`. Above 400 characters of *rendered
HTML* the note folds. Where there are several paragraphs the first one shows,
which is what the skill describes. Where there is only one -- which is what
every note written before the field had any structure is, and what an author
who does not know the field has structure will write today -- it cuts at "the
last full stop followed by a capital that fits the window", falling back to
the first such break anywhere if none fits. So the visible part is a whole
number of sentences, chosen by arithmetic on their lengths, and 400 characters
of HTML is rather less than 400 characters of author text once `<p>` tags and
escaped mathematics are counted: T414's threshold fell after its third
sentence.

This is not a bug and the folding is worth having. It is a fact an author
needs before writing the field, because it decides what is read.

What the skill says now, in the "Where each thing goes" table:

> **`rigour details`** | how the digits were obtained, at whatever length that
> needs: blank lines make paragraphs, `- ` makes a list, backticks mark code,
> and a long note folds after its first paragraph

"A long note folds after its first paragraph" is true of a note with
paragraphs and misleading about the commoner case, where the author wrote one
block of prose and will be surprised by where it is cut.

What it should say: keep the sentence, and add the single-paragraph case and
the consequence for the author. Something like:

> and a long note folds: above about four hundred characters the tail waits
> behind a "more" toggle, after the first paragraph if there is more than one
> and otherwise at the last sentence end that fits. So put the sentence a
> reader most needs -- the precision, the method, the bound -- first, and keep
> the opening two or three sentences inside four hundred characters; what
> follows them is read only by somebody who clicks.

A test in `numberdb_app/test_skill.py` asserting the phrase "at the last
sentence end that fits" would hold it in place. `numberdb_app/prose.py`
already has `test_rigour_prose.py` beside it, so the behaviour is tested; only
the sentence telling an author about it is not.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, T414 critique. The stored field is one string of 596
characters with `count('\n') == 0`; rendered through `/preview?table=` it
comes back as `<p class="prose-paragraph">...modulo $p$.</p><details
class="prose-more"><summary>more</summary><p class="prose-paragraph">Before
the draft was filled, ...</p></details>`, the split falling at source offset
357. `numberdb_app/prose.py`, `FOLD_ABOVE = 400` and `_split_sentence`.
