Batch: the numbers a small graph has (spectral radius, energy, Kirchhoff

most seven vertices).

## Count the distinct values before proposing the family

What happened: five of the eight quantities this run considered are computable
for all 996 graphs in under two minutes, so they were, and the counts decided
the batch rather than taste. Distinct values out of 996:

    Estrada index           963   (97%)
    energy                  897   (90%)
    spectral radius         831   (83%)
    Kirchhoff index         789   (79%)
    Randic index            641   (64%)
    spanning trees          386   (39%)
    algebraic connectivity  301   (30%)
    Wiener index             51    (5%)

The Wiener index is a famous invariant with eighty years of literature and it
cannot be a table on this index: 996 graphs take 51 values, all integers
between 0 and 56, so every small integer would match it and a reader would
learn nothing. The same count ranked algebraic connectivity last among the
five proposed, and said in the proposal what its table must warn about.

The full adjacency spectrum failed the same way for a different reason: of the
810 eigenvalues of the graphs on at most six vertices, 312 are rational, and
127 of those are $0$ and 119 are $-1$. A table answering a search for $0$ with
127 rows makes search worse for everybody.

What the skill says now: "A value has to be distinctive enough that finding it
tells the reader something. Class numbers are mostly 1, 2, 3 ... Worse, such a
table makes search by number *worse for everybody*." It gives the judgement
and no way to reach it.

What it should say: the judgement is usually a measurement, and it is cheaper
than the table. Before proposing a family, compute its values over the index
you mean to publish and count how many are distinct and how many are rational.
Put the two numbers in the proposal. A family whose values repeat, or whose
values are mostly small integers, is the comment-on-another-table case the
skill already describes -- and the count is what tells you which case you are
in, rather than an impression of the subject.

Evidence: `graphs.nauty_geng('%d -c')` for $n=1..7$, one pass computing all
eight invariants, 996 graphs, about 100 seconds in the container.

## Summing over a spectrum: leave `AA`/`QQbar` at the first opportunity

What happened: the energy of a graph is $\sum_i|\lambda_i|$ over the roots of
an integer characteristic polynomial, and the obvious way to make that
*proven* is to work in `AA`, where the roots are exact and `abs` is exact.
The first probe did, and it computed 996 spectral radii in about a minute and
then never finished the energies: exact arithmetic on algebraic numbers of
degree 7, summed seven at a time, 996 times, costs more than everything else
put together. Rewritten as `charpoly().roots(ring=AA)`, each root converted to
a `RealBallField` element immediately, and the sum taken in ball arithmetic,
the same computation finishes in seconds and the rigour is identical -- `AA`
isolates the roots with certified endpoints, and the ball carries that
enclosure through the sum.

What the skill says now: "Prefer balls (`RealBallField`, `ComplexBallField`)
for anything transcendental: arb carries error through every step." It is
about *precision*, and it reads as advice for transcendental functions.

What it should say: the same sentence is also the advice for *speed* whenever
a value is a symmetric function of algebraic numbers -- a sum of roots, a sum
of absolute values, a sum of exponentials. Isolate the roots exactly, convert
each to a ball at once, and do the arithmetic there. Staying in `AA` or
`QQbar` for the arithmetic is what makes a table of a thousand small matrices
not finish, and the failure is a run that is still going rather than an error.

Evidence: two probes, same 996 graphs, same image; `sum(abs(v) for v in
QQbar roots)` did not complete inside the 1800-second container timeout, the
ball version returned in well under a minute.

## `RealBall` has no `.str`, under `sage.all` too

What happened: printing a hundred digits of a ball with
`ball.str(digits=20)` raised

    AttributeError: 'sage.rings.real_arb.RealBall' object has no attribute 'str'

in a file that opened `from sage.all import *`. Printing the ball itself
gives what was wanted, `[56.0696677978300083644 +/- 6.31e-57]`.

What the skill says now: it lists `RealBall.str` among the things "the named
imports do not bring", beside root finding and `QQ('1.25')` -- which reads as
though `sage.all` would bring it.

What it should say: `RealBall` has no `.str` method in this Sage at all; the
import style is not the issue. Print the ball, or take `.mid()` into a
`RealField` of the precision you want. Moving the line out of that list stops
somebody spending a run on the import that would fix it.

Evidence: the same expression failed under named imports and under
`from sage.all import *` minutes apart, in the container's SageMath.

## The corpus's word search is `/suggestions?term=`, not `/api/search`

What happened: this run had no `numberdb` package installed (the repository's
own `numberdb/` directory shadows it, and the client lives at
`clients/python`), so `numberdb.search_text` was unavailable and the screen's
`already_here` could not run. `/api/docs` documents

    GET /api/search   q   the term: a decimal, a fraction, a p-adic, a
                          polynomial, an expression such as pi, or words

so the natural substitute is `GET /api/search?q=Bessel`. It answers

    {"results": [], "messages": [], "time_request": "0.000s"}

for every word, including words the corpus plainly holds. The endpoint that
the site's own search bar calls, and that answers words, is

    GET /suggestions?term=Bessel

which returns tables, tags and numbers as JSON, each with its title and its
slug -- the slug being the other thing a proposal needs and must not guess.

What the skill says now: "`numberdb.search_text('Chebyshev')` -- titles and
tags", and three gotchas about its return value. Nothing about what to do
without the package, and nothing about the JSON route.

What it should say: name `/suggestions?term=` as the HTTP form of the word
search, beside `search_text`. It is what somebody scripting against the site
from a machine without the package will reach for, and it is also where a
table's *slug* can be read rather than guessed, which the skill already warns
is not the title with underscores.

(The docs/implementation mismatch on `/api/search` is a bug in this site and
is written up in `docs/agent-environment.md`, not here.)

Evidence:

    $ curl -s 'https://numberdb.org/api/search?q=Bessel'
    {"results": [], "messages": [], "time_request": "0.000s"}
    $ curl -s 'https://numberdb.org/suggestions?term=Bessel' | head -c 200
    {"entries": {"0": {"value": "0", "label": "", "type": "table",
     "title": "Values of modified Bessel functions of the first kind $I_\\nu(x)$",
     "url": "/Values_of_modified_Bessel_functions_of_the_first_kind", ...
