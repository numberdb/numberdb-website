## A characteristic class is not storable; a characteristic number is

What happened: numberdb-data#108 asks for the Chern polynomials of the tangent
bundles of homogeneous spaces. For a Grassmannian $G(k,n)$ with $k\ge2$ the
cohomology ring is not generated by one class, so $c(T)$ is a vector of
Schubert coefficients, not a polynomial in anything canonical: written as a
polynomial in the special classes $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k$ it depends on a
choice of normal form modulo the relations, and two builders would choose
differently. `representable('Z[]', True, 1)` says nothing about this, because
the type and the variable count are both fine. Storing the Schubert
coefficients instead would give a table whose values are $1,2,3,4,7$ -- the
class-number failure -- while the characteristic *numbers*
$\int_X c_{\lambda_1}\cdots c_{\lambda_r}$ are large, distinctive and exact.

What the skill says now: a value has to be one of the eight types, and a
family of small integers belongs in a comment. Both true, and neither reaches
the case where the mathematical object is an element of a ring with several
generators.

What it should say: before proposing a family that lives in a ring, ask
whether that ring is generated by one element. If it is -- $\mathbb{P}^n$, a
quadric, a complete intersection -- the class is a genuine one-variable
polynomial and the table is T332's shape. If it is not, the storable shadow is
the numbers you get by integrating, and the class itself is not a table
however famous it is.

Evidence: T332 stores $c(T_X)$ in $h$ for complete intersections; the same
quantity for $G(2,5)$ has no canonical polynomial form, and its eleven Chern
numbers computed today run from $10$ to $78125$. Batch
`BATCH-2026-09-21T0803.md`, 2026-09-21.

## Atiyah-Bott localization is the cheap exact check for any characteristic number, and integrality is a free control

What happened: the Chern numbers of $G(k,n)$ and of the complete flag variety
were wanted, and Schubert calculus in Sage means either a Chow-ring package or
symmetric-function bookkeeping. Localization needs neither. With distinct
rational torus weights $t_1,\dots,t_n$, the fixed points of $G(k,n)$ are the
$k$-subsets $I$, the tangent weights at $I$ are $\{t_j-t_i: i\in I, j\notin
I\}$, and

    integral of a Chern monomial = sum over I of
        (product of e_lambda of the weights) / (product of the weights)

is exact rational arithmetic, about thirty lines, no Sage component beyond
`QQ`. $G(3,6)$ (twenty fixed points, dimension nine, thirty Chern numbers)
took seconds.

The control comes free: every one of those sums must be an *integer*, though
each term is a rational with a large denominator, and an error in the weights
or the fixed-point list destroys that instantly. The same recipe works for the
flag variety (fixed points the $n!$ permutations, weights $t_{w(j)}-t_{w(i)}$
for $i<j$) and for any smooth variety with isolated torus fixed points.

What the skill says now: "a count of something small, done by brute force" is
a good check. It does not mention that a localization sum is such a count for
an intersection number, or that integrality is the control.

What it should say: for a characteristic or intersection number on a space
with a torus action, compute it by localization with generic rational weights
and check that the answer is an integer. Two independent computations of an
integer are worth more than one computation run twice, and this is the cheap
second one.

Evidence: `/tmp/hom_checks.py`, 2026-09-21. $c_{(d)}=\binom{n}{k}$ came out
$3,6,10,20$ for $G(1,3),G(2,4),G(2,5),G(3,6)$; $c_1^d = n^d\deg G(k,n)$ came
out $9, 512, 78125, 423263232$; and the five Chern numbers of $G(2,4)$ agree
with the ones read off T332's quadric row $3h^4+6h^3+7h^2+4h+1$.

## `sage.functions.*` is outside what the named-import rule can reach

What happened: the skill's rule is to name the rings you use rather than
importing `sage.all`, and the example generator does exactly that. A script
written that way opened with `from sage.functions.other import factorial` and
died before its first line ran:

    AttributeError: cannot access submodule 'function' of module
    'sage.symbolic' (most likely due to a circular import)

raised from `sage/functions/trig.py` importing `sage.symbolic.function`. The
same chain is reached indirectly: `PowerSeries.exp()` on a series over `QQ`
calls `solve_linear_de`, which imports `sage.rings.lazy_series`, which imports
`sage.data_structures.stream`, which imports `sage.functions.other` -- so a
power-series exponential fails for the same reason a factorial does.

`sage.arith.misc` is the way through: `binomial` imported and ran from there
in the same script. A truncated exponential or logarithm is ten lines of list
arithmetic over `QQ` and does not touch the symbolic ring at all.

What the skill says now: it lists what the named imports do not bring -- root
finding, `RealBall.str`, `QQ('1.25')` -- and says importing `numberdb.sage`
first is what makes the ring imports work.

What it should say: add that `sage.functions.*` is not reachable at all
without `sage.all`, because every module in it initialises the symbolic ring,
and that this reaches further than it looks: `factorial`, `binomial` and
anything calling `PowerSeries.exp()` or `.log()`. Use `sage.arith.misc` for
the arithmetic functions and write short truncated series by hand.

Evidence: two runs of `/tmp/hom_checks.py` under `agents/sage.sh`, 2026-09-21,
the traceback quoted above.
