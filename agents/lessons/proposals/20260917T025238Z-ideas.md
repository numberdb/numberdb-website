## MathWorld's formulas are in the images' `alt` text, so its closed forms can be checked after all

What happened: an accepted-for-review lesson in `PROPOSALS.md` ("`source_names_it`
confirms a name on MathWorld and never a value") says MathWorld renders formulas
as images, so "a value cannot be quoted from it". Stripping the tags does
remove every formula, and the prose that is left says only "(3) (4) (5)".
But every equation image carries its formula in the `alt` attribute, as
plain Mathematica-style input. On *Elliptic Integral Singular Value* this
includes `K(k_7) || (Gamma(1/7)Gamma(2/7)Gamma(4/7))/(7^(1/4)·4pi)`, and on
*Elliptic Alpha Function* it includes
`alpha(10) || -103+72sqrt(2)-46sqrt(5)+33sqrt(10)`. Read that way, the
page gives closed forms that a ball computation can test row by row. I
transcribed eight rows of $K(k_r)$ and ten of $\alpha(r)$, and all eighteen
agree with an independent theta-function computation to $10^{-100}$.

What the skill says now: nothing about MathWorld beyond naming it as a link.
The pending lesson says its values cannot be read.

What it should say: MathWorld gives closed forms, not decimals, and they
are in the `alt` attribute of each equation image:
`re.findall(r'alt="([^"]+)"', html)`. Pair each label with the formula
after it, evaluate both sides in ball arithmetic, and require the difference
to contain zero with a small radius. A closed form has one advantage over
digits: a transcription error in it shows up as a disagreement, never as
a lost last place.

Evidence: 2026-09-17. `python3` with `urllib` on
`https://mathworld.wolfram.com/EllipticIntegralSingularValue.html` and
`.../EllipticAlphaFunction.html`: the stripped text has no formula, and the
`alt` list has all of them. `/tmp/ideas0252/sv2.py` under `agents/sage.sh`
printed `agrees: True` for $K(k_2),K(k_4),\dots,K(k_{10})$ and
$\alpha(5),\dots,\alpha(10)$. `/tmp/ideas0252/sv.py` did the same for
$\alpha(1),\dots,\alpha(4)$.

## A closed form recalled from memory failed where every published one passed

What happened: while checking Wikipedia's closed forms for the
Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction, I added one for $R(-e^{-\pi})$ that
I remembered rather than read. It was the only row of eighteen that
disagreed. Wikipedia gives no value at a negative nome. So the
negative-nome question was left out of the proposal rather than settled on
a false check.

What the skill says now: "Verify a claim before writing it into a table,
including one somebody suggested."

What it should say: the claim most in need of that rule is your own. A
formula written from memory into a check script has no source, so when it
agrees it proves nothing, and when it disagrees it can look like a fault in
the table. Every closed form a check compares against should carry the page
it was read from.

Evidence: `/tmp/ideas0252/sv2.py`, `R(-e^-pi) ... agrees: False`, against
`/tmp/ideas0252/rr.py`, where all seven of Wikipedia's rows agree, 2026-09-17.
