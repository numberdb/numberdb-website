## A convention is stated on the page, not delegated to a link, and the fact it depends on is stated too

What happened: T235, the permutohedron Ehrhart table, defines its second
column as "the numerator $h^*_{\Pi_n}(z)$ using the Ehrhart-series convention
that CITE{WikiEhrhart} describes". The linked convention puts
$(1-z)^{d+1}$ in the denominator, with $d$ the dimension. The page never says
that $\Pi_n$ is $(n-1)$-dimensional. It places $\Pi_n$ "in $\mathbb{R}^n$" and
titles the parameter $n$ "rank", which is $n-1$ for $A_{n-1}$. A reader who
follows the link with $d=n$ expects a different numerator from the one stored.
The right exponent is in a Formula that the Definition does not point to.

What the skill says now: the Definition should say "which normalisation,
branch, and indexing", and "a symbol that appears in a formula or a comment
must be defined in the same table". It does not say that a link is not a
definition, or that a convention whose exponent depends on a dimension needs
that dimension stated.

What it should say: the Definition states the convention in symbols, for
example "defined by $\sum_t L(t)z^t=h^*(z)/(1-z)^n$". It does not say "the
convention that [1] describes": the link is where a reader checks the
convention, not where they learn it. Where the convention depends on a
quantity such as the dimension, the degree or the rank, the page states that
quantity for this family. A parameter's `title` names what the parameter is,
using the word the literature uses ("order" for the permutohedron), because
a word like "rank" that is off by one misleads more than a neutral one.

Evidence: `GET /api/table?id=T235`, 2026-09-13. The Definition, and the
parameter title "rank". The Wikipedia raw text for "Permutohedron" says "of
order $n$ is an $(n-1)$-dimensional polytope". `agents/critiques/T235.md`,
findings 1 and 2.

## A spelling variant belongs in `Keywords` as surely as an accent does

What happened: T235 is titled with "permutohedra", and its keywords carry
"permutohedron" and "permutohedra". Wikipedia gives "permutahedron" as the
other spelling, and Sage's constructor is `polytopes.permutahedron`. So a
contributor's own Sage session types the spelling that the table does not
hold. Search stems words but does not match one spelling to another.

What the skill says now: `Keywords` has the title's weight, and it is how an
accent is handled ("Lévy's constant" carries "Levy constant"). It does not
mention spellings.

What it should say: add the other spellings of the title's word to
`Keywords`, including the one the usual software uses: permutahedron,
Tchebychev and Chebyshev, labeled and labelled.

Evidence: `polytopes.permutahedron(6)` under `agents/sage.sh`, 2026-09-13,
and the first sentence of the Wikipedia article. `agents/critiques/T235.md`,
finding 3.
