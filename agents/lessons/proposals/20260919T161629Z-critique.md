## Choose a range whose *next* value also fits, not the one that just fits

What happened: reading T293 to ask whether it could grow, I found it holds
1171 entries against a soft limit of 1200 -- 29 to spare. Its range is "the
six real quadratic fields of smallest discriminant, conductor norm at most
250", read off a pinned `ecnf-data` enumeration. The bound is the only knob,
and the counts it produces are lumpy: $N\leq250$ gives 1171, $N\leq254$ gives
1195, $N\leq255$ gives 1213, and $N\leq300$ gives 1639. So the entire
remaining headroom buys a bound of 254 -- twenty-four rows, and a number
nobody would choose or defend. There is no natural bound between "fits" and
"40% over". The table is not extensible by anybody, including its own author.

What the skill says now: it says to aim at about half the soft *block* limit,
"about 160 KB -- not at the limit", because "a table that only just fits
cannot be extended by the next person without breaching it". That reasoning is
exactly right and is stated only for bytes. Nothing says it applies to the
entry count, and nothing says to check what the next value of the range
parameter costs.

What it should say: the same margin argument applies to entries, and the check
is concrete. When a range is set by a bound on an enumerated source -- a
conductor norm, a discriminant, a degree -- count the entries at the bound you
want *and at the next few values above it* before choosing. If the next
natural value of the bound is far over the limit, you are choosing between a
table with no room and a table that needs a `Size exception`; pick the bound
one step lower, so the next person can take the step you did not. A count that
lands at 97% of a limit is a range chosen by the limit rather than by the
mathematics, and the completeness note cannot honestly say why that number.

Evidence: T293 on 2026-09-19. `limits.measure()` on the live document gives
1171 entries and 279,665 bytes against soft limits of 1200 and 320 KB.
Counting positive integer ranks in
`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JohnCremona/ecnf-data/10b28418e80392032b106ea00e6c5aa109d28e7b/RQF/mwdata.2.2.{5,8,12,13,17,21}.1`
by conductor norm bound: 250 → 1171, 251 → 1177, 252 → 1189, 253 → 1189,
254 → 1195, 255 → 1213, 260 → 1305, 300 → 1639, 500 → 3577, unbounded →
37,573. Adding the next field by discriminant ($D=24$, $N\leq250$) costs 577
more rows; the cheapest seventh field ($D=29$) costs 285. The source holds 130
real quadratic fields at that commit, against the table's six.

## A soft limit refuses a script outright; it only warns a person

What happened: working out whether T293 *could* be extended, I expected a soft
breach to be a warning that a publish would carry through with a note. It is
not, for anything publishing over the API. `limits.enforce(tree, strict=True)`
raises `TooBig` on a soft breach and writes nothing; `strict` is set for API
writes and bulk machine proposals precisely because "a script has no judgement
to exercise". A person editing on the site is warned and their edit is saved.
The escape is the same either way -- a `Size exception` key, in the document,
with free prose saying why -- but it has to be in the document *before* the
write, not offered afterwards.

What the skill says now: "A soft limit may be passed by an author who explains
why, recorded in the table as `Size exception`." True, and it reads as though
the explanation can follow the refusal, which for a generator it cannot.

What it should say: a generator crossing a soft limit is refused and writes
nothing. If you mean to go over, put `Size exception` into the table document
and publish that first; then publish the numbers. And say plainly that the
site and the API differ here: on the site a soft limit is a warning, from a
script it is a refusal.

Evidence: `numberdb_app/limits.py`, `enforce()` -- `if soft and
stated_reason(tree): return []` then `if soft and strict: raise TooBig(soft)`.
T293 at 1171/1200 entries carries no `Size exception` in `Data properties` or
at top level, so its own generator would fail rather than warn at the 30th
additional row.
