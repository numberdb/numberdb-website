## A side condition that picks a root has to separate the conjugates, and "positive" almost never does

What happened: T431 stores Freiman's constant and gives its minimal polynomial
as a second formula, with a clause meant to say which of the two roots the
table holds:

    $491993569c_F^2-4443128192c_F+10031248672=0$, with $c_F>4$.

The polynomial is right -- the coefficients are primitive, the constant term
is exactly $(p^2-462q^2)/q'$ for the closed form in the first formula, and the
generator checks that its computed ball satisfies it. The clause is what does
not work. The conjugate root is

    (2221564096 - 283748*sqrt(462)) / 491993569 = 4.5030368352655996674...

against the stored $4.5278295661608791408\ldots$, so both roots satisfy
$x>4$, and both satisfy $x>4.5$ as well. A reader who takes the page at its
word has a quadratic and an inequality that admits two numbers, differing from
the second decimal place -- close enough that picking the wrong one looks like
a rounding disagreement rather than a different number.

This is easy to write and hard to see. The author knows which root is meant
and reads the clause as a reminder rather than as a definition, and every
mechanical check passes: the polynomial is correct, the stored value is a root
of it, and the digits agree with the closed form. `audit_table` is clean on
the table. Nothing in the pipeline evaluates the *other* root.

The same slip has a wider form. "the positive root", "the root in $(0,1)$",
"with $x>0$" and "the larger in absolute value" are all fine when the
conjugates straddle the fence and all silent when they do not, and for a real
quadratic irrational $(p+q\sqrt d)/r$ with $p$ large the conjugates are close
together and on the same side of every round number a person reaches for.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing" must be
decided before computing, and "The nth zero needs a statement of where
counting starts". Both are about the family. Nothing says that a condition
written to disambiguate must be *checked* against the alternative it is
supposed to exclude, and nothing warns that a closed form and its conjugate
can agree to two figures.

What it should say: when a formula selects one root of an equation, compute
the roots it rejects and confirm the condition rejects them. One line does it:

    sage: [r for r, _ in (491993569*x^2 - 4443128192*x + 10031248672).roots(AA)
    ....:    if r > 4]
    [4.503036835265600?, 4.527829566160879?]     # two, so "x > 4" says nothing

Where the conjugates are close, an inequality is the wrong instrument
altogether: name the root instead ("with $c_F$ the larger root"), which stays
true however the coefficients are later rewritten. An inequality that is
merely *true* of the intended value is not a selection, and a table whose
Formulas section holds both a closed form and a minimal polynomial is exactly
where a reader will try to check one against the other.
