# Proposed lessons from the T377 critique, 2026-09-21

Three, all of which a contributor with Python and Sage on their own laptop
would hit: two are about how a document reaches the page, one is about what a
table may assert. The deployment-only findings from the same reading are in
`docs/agent-environment.md` instead.

---

## A `CITE` straight after a bare operator name in maths renders as a function application

What happened: T377's `rigour details` reads, in the stored document,

    computes the middle prime range from the exact power-series coefficients of
    $\log$ CITE{formula-ak}, and computes the remaining prime tail from ...

which is unremarkable prose: the logarithm is meant generically, and the
citation points at the Euler product whose logarithm is being expanded. The
HTML it renders to is

    coefficients of $\log$ <a class="CITE" href="#formula-ak">(2)</a>, and

MathJax sets `$\log$` as the upright word "log". The citation sets as "(2)".
What a reader gets on the page is

    coefficients of log (2), and computes the remaining prime tail

and the first reading of that is $\log 2$ -- a specific number, plausible
enough in a note about Euler products that a reader may never notice they have
misread it. The fault is invisible in the document, invisible to
`audit_table`, and invisible to anybody who checks their table by reading the
YAML they wrote. It exists only in the rendering, and only because the
citation's parentheses land where a function's argument goes.

The same shape will fire on `$\exp$`, `$\sin$`, `$\zeta$`, `$\Gamma$`, `$\log$`
-- any maths fragment ending in a function name immediately followed by
`CITE{}`, which is also exactly where a citation naturally wants to go.

What the skill says now: nothing about this. It has a whole family of rules
about rendering that the stored document hides -- a `<` in maths eating a
section, `--` reaching the page as two hyphens, `param-latex` restating a
column header -- and this belongs beside them. It also says "Link the first
mention", which actively encourages putting a citation right after the thing
it explains.

What it should say: a citation renders as `(n)` or `[n]`, so never end the
maths immediately before one with a function name. Put a noun between them --
"the logarithm of the local factor in CITE{formula-ak}" -- or move the
citation to the end of the clause. Worth one sentence in the section on where
each thing goes, beside the `--` rule, and worth a regex in `audit_table`:
`\\(log|ln|exp|sin|cos|tan|zeta|Gamma|Re|Im)\$\s*CITE\{`.

Evidence: 2026-09-21, T377 critique. The stored string is in
`Data properties['rigour details']`; the HTML above is from the whole-page
rebuild (`/tmp/t377_page.html`, 34,460 bytes, status 200). The clause is in
the paragraph *above* the `<details>` fold, so it is the second thing a reader
of that section sees. `GET /api/table/T377/audit` returns `"clean": true`.

## A conjecture is not an asymptotic: say which of a table's rows are under a theorem

What happened: T377 stores the arithmetic factors $a_k$ of the moments of the
Riemann zeta function, and its `Formulas` section states

    $\frac1T\int_0^T |\zeta(1/2+it)|^{2k}\,dt\sim c_k(\log T)^{k^2}$,
    with $c_k=a_k f_U(k)$.

flat, with no hedge, and its `Definition` says $a_k$ "is the arithmetic factor
in" that. The asymptotic is a theorem at $k=1$ (Hardy-Littlewood) and at $k=2$
(Ingham). For every other $k$ it is the Keating-Snaith / CFKRS **conjecture** --
including in the paper the table itself cites for its integer-$k$ formula,
*Integral moments of $L$-functions*, where it is labelled as a conjecture.
Sixteen of the table's eighteen rows sit under the conjecture and two under a
theorem, and the page does not distinguish them.

Nothing is wrong with the values. $a_k$ is an Euler product that converges for
every $k>0$; it is a number whatever becomes of the asymptotic. What is wrong
is the one sentence that tells a reader *why the number exists*, which is
precisely the sentence somebody who arrived holding $7.0\times10^{-84}$ reads
first.

This is not a rare situation in this corpus. Constants attached to conjectural
asymptotics are a whole genre -- Hardy-Littlewood singular series (T163),
Bateman-Horn constants (T166), Artin's constant, and now this -- and the
constant is always perfectly well defined while the asymptotic it is the
constant *of* is not proven. The two facts pull in opposite directions and a
table has to state both.

What the skill says now: "Verify a claim before writing it into a table,
including one somebody suggested", and "A formula states a relation, not who
checked it". Neither covers the case where the relation is a conjecture: the
first is about checking, and this claim cannot be checked, only attributed.

What it should say: where a formula is conjectural, the formula says so, in
the formula. Write "conjecturally $\sim$", and add a comment naming the cases
that are theorems and citing the conjecture. The constant itself is usually
unconditional and the table should say that too, so a reader knows the digits
do not depend on the conjecture. Two sentences, in the section on where each
thing goes, beside "A formula states a relation, not who checked it".

Evidence: 2026-09-21, T377 critique. `Formulas['formula-moment']` and
`Definition` as quoted; `References['CFKRS']` is
arXiv `math/0206018`, whose moment formula is Conjecture 1.5.4. The two
theorems are Hardy-Littlewood 1918 ($k=1$) and Ingham 1926 ($k=2$). Checked
against the table's own numbers: $c_2=a_2f_U(2)=(6/\pi^2)/12=1/(2\pi^2)$,
which is Ingham's constant, so the stored $a_2$ and the formula agree where
the formula is a theorem.

## A `Programs` snippet valid on part of a table's range must say which part

What happened: T377's `Programs` section holds one PARI/GP incantation, and
the page prints it with no title and no commentary:

    default(realprecision, 140)
    k = 3
    prodeulerrat((1 - 1/p)^((k - 1)^2) *
      sum(j = 0, k - 1, binomial(k - 1, j)^2/p^j), 1, 2)

That is the table's integer-$k$ formula, whose stated hypothesis is "if $k$ is
a positive integer". Twelve of the table's eighteen rows have $k$ a
half-integer. A reader who arrived holding $a_{5/2}$ and did the obvious thing
-- change `k = 3`, which is what a program with a literal parameter on line two
invites -- gets

    k = 3    -> 0.0493216735794000917619759100869799891531929217006036853364
    k = 4    -> 0.0002146814097756221813396428504209017832659459921380567761
    k = 5/2  -> PariError: _^_: domain error in sqrtn: valuation != Mod(0, 4)
    k = 7/2  -> PariError: _^_: domain error in sqrtn: valuation != Mod(0, 4)

The failure is loud rather than silently wrong, which is the better of the two
ways it could have gone. But the error names `sqrtn` and a valuation, says
nothing about $k$, and gives a reader no way to learn from the page that the
snippet was never for their row. The page renders `Programs` as bare code with
no field beside it, so the only place that can say so is the code.

What the skill says now: "`Programs` is the standard incantation in Sage, PARI
or mpmath for a reader who wants one more value", and "`Programs` and
`generate.py` answer different questions". It says nothing about a snippet
that covers less than the table.

What it should say: a `Programs` snippet must either cover every row the table
holds, or say in a comment in its own code which rows it covers, because the
rendered section is code and nothing else -- there is no title, no caption and
no prose beside it. One line, `\\ integer k only`, or `# valid for n <= 50`.
And prefer a snippet that computes the general case even when a special case
is shorter: a reader picks the parameter, and the special case is the one they
will not pick.

Evidence: 2026-09-21, T377 critique. `Programs['program-pari-integers']` as
quoted; the runs above are `sage.libs.pari` at the snippet's own
`realprecision, 140`, under `agents/sage.sh`, `/tmp/t377_pari.py`. The two
integer results agree with the stored entries digit for digit, so the snippet
is right and only its scope is unstated.
