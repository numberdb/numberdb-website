## `CITE{}` also cross-references a formula or comment, and a key it cannot
## resolve renders as the bare key

What happened: T443's `rigour details` says "using the left Hastings-McLeod
asymptotic CITE{formula-left}", where `formula-left` is a key of `Formulas`,
not of `References` or `Links`. Reading it against the skill I took that for a
rendering fault of the kind the critique prompt warns about -- a broken
identifier printed in the middle of a sentence -- and it is not: it renders as
**(2)**, the number the page gives that formula. The Airy table does the same
thing in a `Similar tables` relation ("their Wronskian is CITE{formula-wronskian}").

Two further facts fell out of testing it, and each of them is a trap:

* `Formulas` and `Comments` share **one** numbering sequence. Two formulas and
  one comment number (1), (2), (3), so a `CITE` of a formula renders as a bare
  parenthesised number that a reader has to find by scanning both sections. It
  is not "Formula (2)" unless you write the word yourself.
* A key that resolves to nothing renders as **the key itself**, as plain text,
  with HTTP 200 and no warning anywhere. `CITE{no-such-key}` reaches the page
  as `no-such-key`. There is no error to notice, so a typo in a `CITE` is
  invisible to everything except reading the rendered page -- which is exactly
  the fault a contributor is least likely to look for, because they believe
  `CITE` either works or fails.

What the skill says now: "`CITE{key}` for a reference or link", and, of
`References`, "papers and books, cited from the prose with `CITE{}`". Nothing
about citing a formula or a comment, nothing about the shared numbering, and
nothing about what an unresolved key does.

What it should say: `CITE{key}` resolves against `References`, `Links`,
`Formulas` and `Comments` alike -- a reference or link renders as `[n]`, a
formula or comment as `(n)`, and `Formulas` and `Comments` are numbered in one
sequence. Prefer it to "the formula above", which the page may not put above.
And a key that matches nothing renders as the bare key in the running text,
silently: check a `CITE` by looking at the rendered page, because no refusal
will tell you.

Evidence: 2026-09-23, T443 critique. A `/preview?table=` render of

    Formulas: {formula-a: "$a=1$.", formula-b: "$b=2$."}
    Comments: {c: "Formula CITE{formula-b} is the second,
                   CITE{no-such-key} is nothing, CITE{LinkX} is a link."}
    Links:    {LinkX: {title: "Wikipedia: Test", url: "..."}}

came back, HTTP 200, as

    Formulas (1) $a=1$. (2) $b=2$.
    Comments (3) Formula (2) is the second, no-such-key is nothing,
                 [1] is a link.
    Links [1] Wikipedia: Test

and T443's own rigour note renders `CITE{formula-left}` as `(2)` when its
`Formulas` block is present and as `formula-left` when it is not.
