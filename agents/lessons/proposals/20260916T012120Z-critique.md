Not in force. Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`.

## A `Programs` snippet must run for somebody who has not installed `numberdb`

What happened: T254's `program-sage` opens with `import numberdb.sage as
numberdb` and never uses the name again — the nine lines that follow need only
`ZZ` and `PolynomialRing`. Run under `agents/sage.sh`, where the client *is*
installed, it works and prints $P_{51}$. On a contributor's own SageMath,
without the `numberdb` package, it raises `ModuleNotFoundError` on line 1,
before any of the mathematics. The line is the only one in the snippet that
can fail and the only one that does nothing.

It is not one table's slip. Of the 129 `table.yaml` files in `generators/`
with a Sage program, 48 import `numberdb`; 35 of those go on to use it (they
are generator-shaped, calling `numberdb.agreeing` or a `Generator` subclass),
and **13 never mention it again**: `bateman-polynomials`,
`birkhoff-polytope-ehrhart-polynomials`, `boole-polynomials`,
`faulhaber-polynomials`, `heat-caloric-polynomials`, `mahler-polynomials`,
`mott-polynomials`, `permutohedron-ehrhart-polynomials`,
`stirling-polynomials`, `sums-of-powers-polynomials`, `zernike-polynomials`,
`zeta-derivative-rational-values`, `zeta-logderivative-rational-values`. The
import is being copied
from `generate.py`, where it belongs, into `Programs`, where it does not.

What the skill says now: "`Programs` and `generate.py` answer different
questions. `Programs` is the standard incantation in Sage, PARI or mpmath for
a reader who wants one more value. `generate.py` is the program that
reproduces and extends *this* table, attached to it." It says the two are
different and does not say what follows for the imports.

What it should say: the two differ in their *dependencies* as well as their
purpose. `generate.py` imports `numberdb` because sending values is what it is
for. A `Programs` snippet computes a value and sends nothing, so it must run
under plain `sage -python` with nothing installed beyond Sage — if `numberdb`
appears in it and is never called, delete the line. The test to run before
pasting a snippet in is the snippet itself, in an interpreter that does not
have the client.

Evidence: `agents/sage.sh /tmp/prog254.py`, 2026-09-16, printing $P_{51}$ and
`len(str(P_50)) = 1016`; the count of 13 from a `yaml.safe_load` sweep of
`generators/*/table.yaml` checking whether `numberdb` occurs anywhere in the
snippet after its import line.

## `Keywords` that repeat the title buy nothing: the index stems, and the title is in the same weight class

What happened: T254's title is "Heat (caloric) polynomials $P_m(x,t)$" and its
`Keywords` are `caloric polynomial`, `caloric polynomials`, `heat polynomial`.
All three are already words of the title, and the plural pair is one keyword
written twice. `reindex_for_search` builds `weight_A_text` as `'%s %s' %
(table.title, joined(document.get('Keywords')))` — title and keywords share
weight A — and the vector is stemmed English, which the comment in
`numberdb_app/search.py` states outright: "the vector holds `polynomi` for
`polynomials`". So the three keywords contribute the lexemes `calor`, `heat`
and `polynomi`, every one of which the title had already contributed, and the
field as written adds no term a reader could search for.

This is easy to get wrong in the direction T254 got it wrong — listing the
singular and the plural, and the words of the title — because the field looks
like a list of literal strings to match.

What the skill says now: `Keywords` appears in the section list of
`sections_form.py` as "Words a reader might search for", and the skill's
section 4 does not mention the field at all.

What it should say: keywords are indexed beside the title, at the same weight,
and stemmed. So a keyword that repeats a word of the title is dead, and a
plural beside its own singular is dead. Spend the field on the names a reader
would type that the title does not contain: the other name for the family, an
author's surname, the object the values come from. For T254 that is "heat
equation", "Rosenbloom", "Widder", "Appell", not "caloric polynomials".

Evidence: `numberdb_app/editing.py:662` for the shared weight-A text;
`numberdb_app/search.py:590-609` for the stemming, in the code's own words;
the T254 document, 2026-09-16.

## Search normalises a polynomial's variable names, so a table of polynomials answers whatever letters the reader used

What happened: this was checked rather than assumed, because T254 stores
bivariate polynomials in $x$ and $t$ while the corpus's other bivariate
polynomial table, T126, stores them in $x$ and $y$, and a reader who computed
a heat polynomial might call the variables anything. Through the Python
client, `search('x^2 + x + y')`, `search('y + x + x^2')`, `search('x^2+x+y')`,
`search('x**2 + x + y')` and `search('a^2 + a + b')` all return the same T126
row. Term order, spacing, `**` for `^`, and the variable names themselves are
all normalised away; what survives is the polynomial up to renaming the
variables in order of appearance.

What the skill says now: section 5 gives `Z[]` as a type and how to write a
value; nothing says how a polynomial is matched when somebody looks one up.

What it should say: a polynomial is looked up up to variable naming and term
order, so the letters a table chooses are a display decision and not a
findability one — pick the letters the literature uses ($x$ and $t$ for a heat
polynomial, $x$ and $y$ for a Tutte polynomial) without worrying that a reader
who used other letters will miss the table. The corollary is the one to watch:
two *different* families whose polynomials coincide after renaming will answer
the same search, and that is correct behaviour, not a collision to design
around.

Evidence: five `numberdb.search` calls against T126, 2026-09-16, each
returning `('T126', 'x^2 + x + y')`.
