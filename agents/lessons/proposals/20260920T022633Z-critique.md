# Lessons proposed by the T286 growth critique, 2026-09-20

Both of these sharpen a lesson already proposed by the T288 growth critique
(`20260919T185812Z-critique.md`) rather than raising a new one. A second
instance in a different family is worth recording, because the two disagree
about *where* the ceiling is, and T286 shows why.

## When the limit of a family is itself a table, the range ends before the entries start answering searches for it

What happened: T286 holds the Pisot numbers in $(1,\varphi)$ by rank. The
family is infinite and accumulates at the golden ratio, which the corpus holds
as T32, and it converges geometrically: $\varphi-\theta_r$ loses a decimal
digit every 9.6 ranks. At rank 141 an entry agrees with $\varphi$ in its first
fifteen decimals; at rank 150, in sixteen. A lookup for the golden ratio at
fifteen digits,

    {"kind":"RIF","lower":"1.61803398874989","upper":"1.61803398874990"}

today returns five results and all five *are* the golden ratio (T32, T35,
T153, T222, T297). Taken to rank 200 this table would put sixty more rows in
that interval, none of which is the golden ratio. The ceiling that the T288
lesson names -- sixteen digits, where nobody computing in double precision can
arrive holding the number -- is the same arithmetic, but the consequence here
is worse and easier to state: the rows do not merely go unfound, they crowd a
lookup that another table answers correctly.

What the skill says now: "every hit in the corpus is worth a little less for
it", under "Hold the numbers that turn up", as a general cost of rows nobody
looks for. It does not connect that cost to a family's limit point, and it
gives the author no test to run.

What it should say: when a family converges to a constant, look the constant up
before choosing the range. If the corpus holds it -- and a named constant
usually is held -- then the range must end while the entries are still outside
the interval a reader would type for that constant. One `lookup` with a
fifteen-digit interval around the limit, before and after the range you are
considering, is the whole check: if the answer changes from "these are all the
constant" to "these are mostly your new rows", the range is too long.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T286 growth critique. Roots of $P_n(x)=x^n(x^2-x-1)+1$
and $Q_n(x)=x^n(x^2-x-1)+x^2-1$ in $(1,\varphi)$ computed at 100 digits for
$n\leq250$ under `agents/sage.sh` (499 roots, 2 m 15 s). First rank agreeing
with $\varphi$ in its first $k$ decimals: $k=6$ at rank 55, $k=10$ at 95,
$k=15$ at 141, $k=17$ at 161, $k=25$ at 241, $k=50$ at 481. The five-result
lookup above, `GET /api/lookup`, on the live corpus.

## The resolution that binds is the gap between neighbours, and it can fall twice as fast as the distance to the limit

What happened: T286's entries arrive in pairs. The root of $P_n$ and the root
of $Q_{n+1}$ are separated by the *square* of their distance below $\varphi$ --
measured, the gap column is twice the distance column digit for digit at every
rank from 50 to 450. So two rows become one answer at rank 72, where
neighbours are $10^{-15.4}$ apart, while the distance to $\varphi$ does not
reach $10^{-15}$ until rank 141; and two rows store *identical* 100-digit
strings at rank 478, while no row equals $\varphi$ to 100 digits until about
rank 955. An author who measured only the distance to the limit, as the T288 lesson
says to, would have put the ceiling twice as far out as it is.

Two rows that are one answer is not necessarily a fault -- both are genuinely
that close, and each row's comment carries the minimal polynomial that tells
them apart. Two rows that store the same digits is a fault in any table.

What the skill says now: nothing about within-family resolution. The
`20260919T185812Z` proposal says to measure "how many leading digits an entry
shares with that limit", which is the right measurement for a family that
approaches its limit monotonically one entry at a time, and the wrong one for
a family whose entries cluster.

What it should say: measure both -- the distance to the limit, and the smallest
gap between consecutive stored values. Take the ceiling from whichever fails
first. The second measurement is one sorted difference over the values you have
already computed, and where entries pair up it is the one that binds.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T286 growth critique, same run. $\log_{10}$ of
(distance below $\varphi$, gap to the previous entry) at ranks 50, 100, 200,
300, 450: $(-5.57,-11.0)$, $(-10.8,-21.5)$, $(-21.2,-42.4)$, $(-31.7,-63.3)$,
$(-47.4,-94.6)$. The 100-digit strings of ranks 477/478 and 479/480 are equal;
the 320 KB block limit falls at about rank 488, so the two ceilings coincide.

## A repair re-attached to the table can leave the *author's own* copy behind

What happened: this is the T288 drift lesson with the direction reversed, and
it is worth recording because the reversed case is silent in a different way.
T286 was repaired on 2026-09-17, and the repair was done properly on the
server: the generator attached to the table carries all eight fixes, including
a completeness guard the original did not have. What was not updated is the
copy the author works from -- in this repository,
`generators/pisot-numbers-less-than-golden-ratio/generate.py` is still the
pre-repair file, and `table.yaml` beside it still has the pre-repair
`complete-note`. Nothing detects this: `verify()` passes, because the attached
generator and the table agree; the audit is clean; the page renders. The
failure arrives later, when somebody grows the table by editing the obvious
file -- the one in front of them -- and publishing, which would silently revert
five of the eight repairs.

What the skill says now: "`generate.py` is the program that reproduces and
extends *this* table, attached to it." That sentence assumes one copy.

What it should say: the attachment is the generator; a copy anywhere else is a
cache of it. Before extending a table you did not just build, fetch
`/files/<tid>/generate.py?raw=1` and diff it against whatever you have locally,
and start from the attachment when they differ. `verify()` cannot see this
drift, because the attachment is what verifies.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T286 growth critique.
`curl 'https://numberdb.org/files/T286/generate.py?raw=1'` is 6,831 bytes; the
repository copy is 5,859 and differs in `entry_comment` (semicolon fragments,
no $\Delta(2,n+1,\infty)$ links) and lacks the `guard_roots` completeness
check. The repair commit e43b274 touched only
`agents/critiques/T286-repaired.md`.
