## A value written to fewer than about six digits answers no search at all

What happened: reading T451 (Tracy–Widom densities), the rendered rows carried a
dagger linking to `/help#search-precision` — "This number will not be found in
the search" — on 272 of 903 entries, nearly a third of the table. The rule is a
threshold on *relative* width: a stored real is held out of search by number
when the interval its last written place denotes is wider than $10^{-5}$ of its
own size (`findable_by_number` / `exact_relative_width`, threshold
`NUMBERDB_MAX_RELATIVE_WIDTH = 1e-5`). Since `4.1e-13` means
$[4.0,4.2]\times10^{-13}$, that is a rule about significant digits: five digits
never passes, six passes only when the mantissa is 2 or more, seven always does.

Two consequences a contributor would not predict. A table whose precision varies
across its range — honest, and the right thing to do when the far tail is harder
— silently divides into rows that answer searches and rows that only decorate
the page. And the boundary depends on the leading digit, so neighbouring rows
written to the same six digits can fall on opposite sides of it: in T451,
$f_1(-8.50)=1.54808\text{e-}13$ is excluded (relative width $1.3\times10^{-5}$)
while $f_1(-8.55)$ and $f_1(-8.45)$, also six digits, are not.

What the skill says now: nothing. Section 5 explains that a written real *is* an
interval, which is the whole mechanism, and section 3 says "Expensive digits.
Few numbers known to great precision is as legitimate as many known to a hundred
digits" — but nothing says that below a threshold the value stops being findable
at all, which is the one thing the database exists to do. A contributor reads
"few digits is legitimate" and does not learn that six is the floor.

What it should say: somewhere near the interval convention in section 5 — search
by number matches stored intervals, and the site excludes a value whose relative
width exceeds $10^{-5}$, marking it on the page with a dagger. In practice that
is about six significant digits, and seven to be safe of the leading-digit
boundary. So when precision has to vary across a table, the digit count is not
only a statement of honesty, it decides which rows answer a search; and a range
whose values all fall below the threshold is a range worth reconsidering rather
than storing.

Evidence: 2026-09-23, T451 critique. `numberdb_app/models.py:616-655`;
`numberdb_app/views.py:1397` sets `not_findable` and the template renders the
dagger, confirmed in a `/preview` rendering of five T451 rows. Counted with the
site's own `utils.numbers.parse_real` over the 903 stored entries: 272 excluded,
80 at $\beta=1$, 83 at $\beta=2$, 109 at $\beta=4$, all of them in the tails.
