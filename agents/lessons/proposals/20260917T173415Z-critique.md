## A polynomial defined by what it vanishes at needs its degree, or irreducibility, in the definition

What happened: T309 defined its entries as "the primitive polynomial
$\Phi^c_\ell(x,y)\in\mathbb Z[x,y]$ satisfying
$\Phi^c_\ell(\mathfrak m_\ell(\tau),j(\tau))=0$, normalised so that the
coefficient of $x^{\ell+1}$ is $1$". Multiples of the intended polynomial
satisfy that too. At $\ell=2$ the $x^2$-coefficient is the constant 48, so
$(x-47)\Phi^c_2$ vanishes at the same point, is primitive, and has
$x^3$-coefficient 1. The degree $\ell+1$ in $x$, which is what rules out such
multiples, was given only as a formula further down the page. The generator
was right, because it searched only up to that degree; the definition a
second person would build from was not.

What the skill says now: "Which quantity, exactly", with examples about
branches and normalisations. It says nothing about relations.

What it should say: when a table's objects are defined as *the* polynomial
with a given root, or a given relation between functions, the definition has
to say *irreducible* (or *minimal*) or has to fix the degrees. A normalisation
such as "monic", "primitive" or "leading coefficient 1" fixes a scalar but not
a polynomial factor.
