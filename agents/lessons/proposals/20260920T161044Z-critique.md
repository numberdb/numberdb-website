# Lessons proposed by the T281 growth critique, 2026-09-20

Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in force.

## For a `Z[]`/`Q[]` table of a geometric family, the range is the rational part of the family, not the family

What happened: T281 holds Davenport-Stothers triples — 15 entries, 5,473 bytes,
1.3% of the entry limit — one representative per equivalence class for $M\leq4$
and one of the four classes at $M=5$. Every metric says it can grow. The
mathematics says it can grow by exactly one class, because the table's type is
`Q[]` and almost no class is definable over $\mathbb Q$:

- the classes of type $M$ are in bijection with plane trees with $M+1$ leaves
  and $M-1$ trivalent internal vertices, so there are
  $D(M)=1,1,1,1,4,6,19,49,150,442,\dots$ of them;
- a class whose tree is not isomorphic to its own mirror image is carried to
  the mirror class by complex conjugation, so it has no representative with
  *real* coefficients. That cuts $D(M)$ to the self-mirror count
  $1,1,1,1,2,2,5,5,14,14,42,\dots$ before any arithmetic;
- the literature knows six rational classes in total, all with $M\leq5$, and
  whether a single one exists with $M>5$ is an open question (Elkies, ANTS-IV
  2000, §4.1, restating Birch-Chowla-Hall-Schinzel 1965). Two independent
  computations agree with that: one source reports nothing over $\mathbb Z$ for
  $6\leq M\leq11$, and another computed the classes for $M=6,7,8,11,14,17$ and
  found them in fields of degree 6, 14, 47, 42, 14, 42.

So "can it grow" was a question about fields of definition, and the size limits
were never in it.

What the skill says now: pick a range by what turns up, then check the three
limits, and "measure the largest entry before choosing the range". Nothing about
a family whose members are only sometimes definable over the field the table's
`type` names.

What it should say: when the family is indexed by something geometric or
combinatorial — dessins, covers, curves, lattices — the count of the family and
the count of members definable over the table's type are two different
sequences, and the second one is the range. Settle that before treating the
index as a range, and put the reason in `complete-note`: "every class known to
have a representative over $\mathbb Q$; whether one exists with $M>5$ is open"
is a sentence a reader can act on, where "$M\leq5$" looks like somebody's
choice. Two cheap moves did most of the work here: a conjugation-symmetry
argument (a chiral object has no real model), and a one-line scaling that turned
a source's claim over $\mathbb Z$ into a claim over $\mathbb Q$ ($u=1/N$,
$w=N^M$ clears every denominator when the transformation is
$f\mapsto w^2f(ut+v)$).

Evidence: T281; Montanus, NAW 5/7 nr. 3 (2006), 172-176; Elkies,
arXiv:math/0005139 §4.1; Elkies-Watkins,
`magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/~watkins/papers/hall.ps`, Table 1;
`agents/critiques/T281-growth.md`.

## Look for the paper that lists the whole family, not only a paper with values in it

What happened: T281's build transcribed the five triples Montanus prints and
cited Montanus and Sijsling-Voight. It did not find Elkies's ANTS-IV paper,
which is on arXiv, is cited by both of those, and contains (a) a sixth rational
triple the table does not hold, (b) the catalogue of every rational one known,
and (c) the sentence that says the family has no known member beyond them. One
paper, and it answers the completeness question, the range question and one
missing row. Nor did the build use the *prose* of the source it did read: the
same five pages of Montanus hold the theorem counting all classes, the remark
that two of the four $M=5$ classes are complex conjugates of each other, and the
remark that nothing of type 6 to 11 is integral.

What the skill says now: check the corpus first, check new values against
something independent, cite what you used. The literature search it describes is
aimed at "is this table already here" and "is this value right".

What it should say: also search for the paper that says **how many there are**,
and read the prose of the ones you transcribe from. A transcription's range is
its source's range, so the source's own remarks about what is missing are the
table's `complete-note`, and the references of a source that counts a family
usually lead to the one that lists it. The cheapest route to both here was
arXiv: the LaTeX source of a 2000 paper, `grep`ped for the coefficient that
appeared in a preprint's table.

Evidence: the same three papers; T281's `complete-note` and
`comment-class-count` before this critique; `agents/critiques/T281-growth.md`
§2-§4.

## Transcribe the minimum and derive the rest, so the transcription checks itself

What happened: the sources for T281 print only $x$ (the first polynomial of
each triple) once the degree passes 4, because the second is forced: $y$ is the
polynomial part of the Laurent expansion of $x^{3/2}$ at infinity, and
$k=x^3-y^2$. Deriving $y$ and $k$ that way from a 101-character $x$ transcribed
out of a PostScript file, on a machine with no PDF reader, gave $\deg k=M+1$ and
integer coefficients throughout — and a single wrong digit anywhere in $x$ would
have given $\deg k=3M-1$ instead. The transcription validated itself, with no
second source and no eyeballing.

What the skill says now: it says to check values against something independent,
and (for this kind of family) that a generator should recompute rather than
store. It does not say to choose *which part* of an entry to transcribe.

What it should say: when a family has a defining identity, transcribe the least
data the identity needs and derive the rest inside the generator, choosing the
derivation whose failure mode is loud — a degree that comes out wrong, a
denominator that appears, a determinant that stops vanishing. That is a stronger
check than comparing a printed value against a printed value, and it is often
the only check available when the source is a scan. T281's generator does the
weaker version of this: it transcribes $f$ *and* $h$ and derives $g$ as
$\sqrt{f^3-h}$, so there is twice as much transcribed data to get wrong, and the
alarm is "$f^3-h$ is not a square" rather than a degree that drops.

Evidence: `agents/critiques/T281-growth.md` §3, which prints the derived $g$ and
$h$ and the checks; `generators/davenport-stothers-polynomial-triples/generate.py`.
