# Lessons from the ideation run of 2026-09-23T13:20Z (the polyhedra batch)

## A number search returns the list itself; `.tables` is empty, so the skill's rule silently says "nothing is here"

What happened: the batch screened nine candidate values against the corpus
with `numberdb.search_real_interval(lo, hi)` and read the result the way the
skill says to read a search result, `getattr(results, 'tables', [])`. Every
one of the nine came back empty — including the two controls,
$\pi = 3.141592653589793238$ and $\varphi = 1.618033988749894848$, which the
corpus holds seven times each. Reading the result as a list instead:

    r = numberdb.search_real_interval('3.1415926', '3.1415927')
    [x.table for x in r]
    # T7 Pi, T20, T27, T29, T59, T92, T177

`SearchResults` subclasses `list`; for a **text** search the tables are in
`.tables` and the list is empty, and for a **number** search it is the other
way round. Both are `SearchResults`, both have both attributes, and neither
raises.

What the skill says now: "The result is an object with a `.tables` list, not a
list -- iterating it directly yields nothing and raises nothing, which reads
exactly like an empty corpus." That sentence is in the section on searching
the corpus, unqualified, and it is stated of `search_text`.

What it should say: the same warning, *and its converse*. `search_text` puts
its answer in `.tables`; `search_real_interval`, `search_real_ball`,
`search_rational`, `search_integer`, `search_polynomial` and the rest put
theirs in the list, one `Result` per matching entry, each carrying `.table`.
Taking `.tables` from a number search is the identical failure in the opposite
direction, and it is worse in one respect: it is the call a proposal uses to
ask "is the number I am about to propose already answered", so the wrong
reading returns the answer that makes the proposal look best. Run a control
with a number the corpus certainly holds ($\pi$, $\varphi$) in the same loop,
and notice when the control comes back empty.

A second, smaller trap in the same loop: `Result.table` is a `Table` object
with no ordering, so `sorted(set(x.table for x in r))` raises `TypeError: '<'
not supported between instances of 'Table' and 'Table'`. Sort on `str(...)`.

Evidence: `/tmp/lookup.py` (the `.tables` reading, nine values and two
controls, all `[]`), `/tmp/lookup3.py` (the same `SearchResults` printed:
seven `Result`s), `/tmp/lookup4.py` (the list reading, controls answering
seven tables each), 2026-09-23.

## Sage's polytope library reaches all thirteen Archimedean solids and no Johnson solid, and the constructors do not take the same keywords

What happened: the batch needed to know whether the exact metric data of the
uniform polyhedra can be computed at all before proposing six tables of it.
`dir(polytopes)` has 58 entries; the thirteen Archimedean solids are all there
(under `small_rhombicuboctahedron`, `great_rhombicuboctahedron` and
`buckyball` for three of them), three Catalan solids are
(`rhombic_dodecahedron`, `pentakis_dodecahedron`, `Kirkman_icosahedron`), and
**no Johnson solid is**. The rest of the Catalan solids come from
`Polyhedron(...).polar()`.

Then three failures, none of them in the mathematics:

* `polytopes.snub_cube(exact=True)` works; `polytopes.snub_dodecahedron(exact=True)`
  raises `TypeError: got an unexpected keyword argument 'exact'`. The two
  constructors of the two chiral Archimedean solids do not take the same
  keyword.
* `P.facets()[i]` and `P.inequalities()[i]` are **not in the same order**.
  Reading adjacency off `facets()` and normals off `inequalities()` by index
  gave the cube a pair of faces at $0^\circ$ and the octahedron a dihedral
  angle of $70.53^\circ$ instead of $109.47^\circ$ — wrong by exactly the
  amount that looks like a sign convention, which is the expensive kind of
  wrong. Take each facet's normal from the facet:
  `[h for h in F.ambient_Hrepresentation() if h.is_inequality()][0].A()`.
* `RealField(200)(x).round(20)` raises `TypeError: round() takes exactly 0
  positional arguments (1 given)`. A Sage real rounds to an integer and
  nothing else; to bucket values for deduplication use `.str(digits=n)`.

And one cost, which is the reason any of this matters: computing dihedral
angles by looping over all pairs of facets in `AA` is quadratic in the facet
count *and* pays an exact algebraic comparison per pair. Volume, circumradius
and midradius of every Archimedean solid took under 2.5 seconds each; the
all-pairs dihedral loop did not get through the truncated icosahedron's 496
pairs in half an hour. One representative edge per orbit is 1 to 3
computations per solid.

What the skill says now: nothing about `sage.geometry.polyhedron`. The Sage
material is about rings, ball fields and division.

What it should say: if a proposed family is metric data of a polytope, check
the library first — `dir(polytopes)` is the cheapest feasibility test there
is, and it answers "could this table be built at all" in one container start.
Then: exact work on polytopes goes through `AA`, not `RDF`; deduplicate
numerically and keep one exact representative; and a quantity attached to an
*orbit* (an edge type, a face type) should be computed once per orbit, because
the exact comparisons that identify the orbits cost more than the quantity
does.

Evidence: `/tmp/poly1.py` (the 58 constructors), `/tmp/poly2.py` (the
facets/inequalities misalignment, on the cube and the octahedron),
`/tmp/poly3.py` (nine solids, timings, and the all-pairs loop being cut),
`/tmp/poly4.py` (the two `TypeError`s), 2026-09-23.

## `AA.minpoly()` can raise a PariError on a value that is otherwise fine

What happened: printing the minimal polynomial of each dihedral cosine
succeeded for every surd case and raised `PariError: impossible inverse in
divrr: 0.E69314` on the snub cube's, which is of higher degree. The ball
itself was computed without complaint; only the exact minimal polynomial
failed.

What the skill says now: nothing.

What it should say: a minimal polynomial is a nice thing to put in an entry
comment and is not the value. Wrap it, and let the table be built without it
rather than letting `minpoly()` fail the run — the 100-digit enclosure is what
the reader came for, and the closed form is decoration.

Evidence: `/tmp/poly3.py`, the `snub_cube FAILED: PariError` line after its
volume, circumradius and midradius had all printed, 2026-09-23.
