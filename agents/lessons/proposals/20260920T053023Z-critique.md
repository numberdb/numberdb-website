## When a family gets *cheaper* as the index grows, nothing stops the table but you

What happened: T283 holds $\mu_n=m(1+x_1+\cdots+x_{n-1})$, the Mahler measures
of the short-random-walk polynomials. It was built with four entries, $n=3$ to
$6$ -- the cases the literature evaluates in closed form -- and then grown to
$n=200$ from the Bessel integral
$\mu_n=\log2-\gamma-\int_0^1\frac{J_0(x)^n-1}{x}dx-\int_1^\infty\frac{J_0(x)^n}{x}dx$,
which holds for every $n\geq3$.

Every quantity the skill tells an author to measure before choosing a range
gets *better* as $n$ grows here, not worse:

    n        seconds per value       characters per value
    7            48.2                        51
    200           3.4                        51
    1202          0.1                        51

$J_0(x)^n$ decays like $x^{-n/2}$, so the oscillatory tail shortens and the
integral gets easier; and the value is a fixed-width decimal, so the entries
block grows linearly at 65 bytes a row and is 78 KB at the 1200-entry soft
limit against a 320 KB block limit. Precision does not degrade either: at
$n=1202$ two working precisions still agree to 56 digits, the same as at
$n=201$.

So the author reaches the end of the skill's checklist with every limit
further away than when they started, and the only thing left is one sentence
of §3 -- "the question is not what fits but what anybody is looking up". It is
the sentence doing all the work and it is the easiest to walk past. The table
went to $n=200$; it could go to $n=1202$ for about ten minutes of compute, and
the entry-count soft limit would be the first thing to say no.

What the skill says now: §3 names exactly two things that "move that number
down, and they are the usual case" -- *expensive digits* and *values that
grow*, with the Chebyshev table measured at $O(n^3)$ characters as the worked
example. Both are brakes. There is no worked example of a family with no
brake, and the phrase "and they are the usual case" reads as though the
remaining case needs no advice.

What it should say: name the third case, because it is the one that
over-grows. Something like --

> *Values that get cheaper.* Some families run the other way: the work per
> value falls with the index and the value stays the same width. A Bessel
> integral $\int J_0(x)^n dx/x$ costs less at $n=1000$ than at $n=10$, because
> the integrand decays faster. Nothing in the cost or the size will stop you,
> and the entry limit is not a reason -- it is a ceiling, not a target. These
> are the tables that quietly become listings. Fix the stopping point from the
> reader instead, write it in `complete-note` as a number rather than as "as
> far as the table goes", and say there why you stopped: a range with no
> external brake is the one a reader most needs the argument for.

And the check that makes it concrete, which is worth stating because it takes
a minute and settles the question: **if an asymptotic formula you can put on
the page already identifies the large entries to more digits than a reader
would be holding, those entries are not what the table is for.** For T283,
$\tfrac12(\log n-\gamma)+\frac{1}{8n}+\frac{5}{288n^2}-\frac{1}{192n^3}$ gives
11.6 significant digits at $n=200$ and a two-term-longer version gives 21 at
$n=1202$. The stored 49 digits are still genuine -- the series is asymptotic
and saturates near 9 digits at $n=20$, so it cannot replace the rows -- but
nobody arrives holding 49 digits of $\mu_{700}$.

Evidence: T283 on 2026-09-20, 198 entries, $n=3\ldots200$, 20,199 bytes as the
client returns it. Timings and the $n=1202$ value from three runs of the
table's own attached `generate.py` method through `agents/sage.sh`;
$\mu_{1202}=3.2573672304544718146422550908538580705990904970308$, computed at
working precisions 55 and 63, which agree to 56 digits. The asymptotic
coefficients $1/8$, $5/288$, $-1/192$, $-1711/172800$, $-101/34560$ were
identified numerically from the stored rows, matching to $3\times10^{-22}$,
$9\times10^{-21}$, $1.8\times10^{-20}$, $7\times10^{-20}$ and
$1\times10^{-16}$. Full report in `agents/critiques/T283-growth.md`.
