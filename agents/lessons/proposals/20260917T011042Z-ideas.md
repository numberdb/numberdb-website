## Over a number field, PARI's heights and Tamagawa product are not LMFDB's: fix the convention on a curve with a known answer

What happened: proposing BSD invariants of elliptic curves over real quadratic
fields, the stored LMFDB values (read from Cremona's ecnf-data files) were
compared with PARI 2.17.1 on six curves. Three conventions differ between the
two, and none of them is visible from either side alone:

* `ellheight` over a number field returns $[K:\mathbb Q]$ times the absolute
  Néron–Tate height. LMFDB's regulator uses the absolute height, so
  `matdet(ellheightmatrix(E,P))` is $2^r$ times LMFDB's `reg` over a quadratic
  field. The absolute one is what makes a base-change regulator *equal* a
  regulator over $\mathbb Q$: `reg` of 2.2.5.1-245.1-a2 is T64's entry for
  175b1 to 37 digits, and PARI's number is twice it.
* `elltamagawa` over a number field includes the number of connected
  components of $E^\sigma(\mathbb R)$ at the real places. For 2.2.5.1-31.1-a1
  it returns 2, while `elllocalred` gives $c_{\mathfrak p}=1$ at every bad
  prime. LMFDB's Ω instead carries the components inside the period.
* LMFDB's `Lvalue` is $L^{(r)}(E/K,1)/r!$. T70 and T71 over $\mathbb Q$ store
  $L^{(r)}(E,1)$ without the $r!$: T70's 389a1 is $1.518633…=2\times0.759316…$.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing" as a
general rule, with the elliptic integral's $m$ versus $k$ as the example.

What it should say: for arithmetic invariants computed by a library, the
normalisation is fixed by comparison, not by reading. Take one curve per rank
from a published source, compute each quantity with the library, and require
the BSD identity to close to full precision with Ш a perfect square. The
factor that fails to close is the convention that differs. For number fields,
the three conventions above are the ones that differ between PARI and LMFDB.

Evidence: `/tmp/ideas0917/bsd.py`, `omega.py` and `tam.py` under
`agents/sage.sh`. The BSD quotient with $2^r$, full-locus Ω and `elllocalred`
Tamagawa numbers is $1.000…$ to 38 digits for 31.1-a1, 199.1-c1, 199.1-c3,
245.1-a2 and 1831.1-c1 over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$.

## LMFDB answers scripts with a captcha; its elliptic-curve data over number fields is on GitHub

What happened: `curl https://www.lmfdb.org/api/ec_nfcurves/?...&_format=json`
and ordinary curve pages returned a Google reCAPTCHA challenge page. The
status gave no sign of it, so a screen reading that page reports "the source
does not mention …", which reads like a wrong citation rather than a blocked
one. The data behind `ec_nfcurves` is in <https://github.com/JohnCremona/ecnf-data>.
`RQF/mwdata.2.2.D.1` has rank, generators, `reg`, `omega`, `Lvalue` and `sha`
to 38 digits for real quadratic fields, `RQF/curves.2.2.D.1` has the
$a$-invariants, and `ecnf-format.txt` documents the columns. There is no
`mwdata` for imaginary quadratic fields.

What the skill says now: LMFDB is named as a link target and an example
source. Nothing about reaching it from a program.

What it should say: LMFDB is for linking, not for fetching from a script.
For bulk values fetch the upload files from the LMFDB data repositories on
GitHub (for curves over number fields, `JohnCremona/ecnf-data`). Treat a
source check that fails on an LMFDB URL as unread, not as a wrong source.

Evidence: `curl -s -m 40 "https://www.lmfdb.org/api/ec_nfcurves/?field_label=2.2.5.1&_format=json&_limit=2"`
returned `<base href="https://www.google.com/recaptcha/challengepage/">`,
2026-09-17. `gh api -H "Accept: application/vnd.github.raw" repos/JohnCremona/ecnf-data/contents/RQF/mwdata.2.2.5.1`
returned 9713 lines.
