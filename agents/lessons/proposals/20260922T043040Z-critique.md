## A `Links` entry nobody cites is furniture too, and the page numbers it beside the references

What happened: T398 declares three Links -- the Wikipedia article on
Gromov-Witten invariants, the Wikipedia article on enumerative geometry, and
OEIS A013587. Only the first is reached by a `CITE{}`. The rendered page numbers
References and Links in **one sequence**, so a reader meets `[1]` and `[2]` under
References, `[3]` cited from the definition, and then `[4]` and `[5]` -- two
numbered items that no sentence on the page points at. Worse, the sentence that
wants `[5]` is on the same screen and writes the name in plain text instead:
"How they were obtained: ... The values were compared with OEIS A013587 for
every entry", four lines above an unlinked `[5] OEIS A013587`.

Nothing catches this. `audit_table` checks citations in one direction only --
`unresolved_citations` requires every `CITE{}` to resolve to a Link, a Reference
or a same-page label -- and never asks whether a declared Link is cited by
anything. `GET /api/table/T398/audit` is `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`.

What the skill says now: the section table has, on the `References` row,
"papers and books, cited from the prose with `CITE{}`" against "uncited
decoration", and the sentence below it, "A reference is for citing, not for
listing. `CITE{Koshy}` in a comment earns its place; a bibliography nobody
points at is furniture." The `Links` row says only "sources outside: Wikipedia,
LMFDB, OEIS, MathWorld" against "anything the database holds itself". So a
contributor reading the skill is told to cite their references and told nothing
about citing their links, while the page treats the two as one numbered list.

What it should say: the rule covers `Links` as well as `References`. A link
declared and never cited prints a number a reader cannot get to from any
sentence. And the direction that goes wrong in practice is the one the audit
does not check: not a `CITE{}` with no target, but a target with no `CITE{}`.
Where the prose already names the source in words -- "compared with OEIS
A013587", "as in the Wikipedia article on X" -- that name is where the `CITE{}`
belongs, which is the same first-mention rule the skill already states for
`HREF{}`.

A suggested sentence, for the `Links` row and beside the `References` one:
"Every Link is cited from somewhere, for the same reason every Reference is:
the page numbers them in one sequence, so one nobody points at is a number the
reader cannot reach. Where the prose names the source in words, put the
`CITE{}` on the name."

Evidence: T398 draft, 2026-09-22. Rendered page (sqlite draft-render recipe,
status 200, 30,020 bytes): `[4] Wikipedia: Enumerative geometry` and
`[5] OEIS A013587` appear in the Links block with no anchor pointing at either,
while `rigour details` renders "compared with OEIS A013587" as plain text.
`numberdb_app/management/commands/audit_table.py` -- `unresolved_citations` and
the `HREF{}` loop are both target-side; there is no check on an unreferenced
`Links` key. The audit is clean on this table.
