## Prose copied from a sibling table keeps its words and loses its referents

What happened: T365, "Residues of Dedekind zeta functions of quartic fields",
is the third of a series, built after the quadratic and cubic tables and
largely by rewriting the cubic one, which is the right way to make a family of
tables consistent. Two sentences came across intact and stopped meaning
anything.

Its `complete-note` renders as

    Table is complete: no (it holds the same 456 quartic fields with
    $|D|\leq 5000$ under the same $D$ and $k$)

"the same" as what? In the cubic table that clause lives in `Similar tables`,
glossing the link to the regulators table: "the other factor of the class
number formula, for the same 515 fields under the same $D$ and $k$". There the
referent is the table at the other end of the link. In `complete-note` it
renders alone inside a parenthesis and there is no other table in the
sentence; the quartic series has no regulators table at all. The reader loses
more than the reference: the note no longer claims the table is *exhaustive*
up to the bound, which is the one thing somebody checking whether their field
is present came to read. The cubic note, written for its own slot, does say
it: "it holds every cubic field with $|D|\leq 3000$, the 419 complex and the
96 totally real ones".

The second: the comment that defines the index $k$ says the fields are sorted
"lexicographically by coefficient vector", where the cubic table it was
condensed from names the tuple, "$(a_2,a_1,a_0)$ of their reduced defining
polynomials $x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0$". Dropping the tuple made the rule
ambiguous; see the next lesson.

What the skill says now: it tells an author where a clause goes and how it
renders -- "It renders inside 'complete: no (...)', so write a clause that
finishes that sentence" -- and it has a rule about pointing, "Name the thing,
do not point at it", whose examples are all *within* one field ("the former",
"the first factor", "as above"). Nothing says that a sentence moved between
fields, or between tables, carries pointing words that had referents where it
came from and have none where it lands.

What it should say: when a table is written by adapting a sibling's prose --
which is the normal and right way to build a family of tables -- reread every
sentence in the slot it now occupies, with the sibling closed. Deictic words
are what breaks: "the same", "the other", "both", "as there". Each renders in
a different place, and `complete-note` is the worst of them, because it
renders inside a parenthesis with nothing before it. The test is to read the
rendered line alone: **if a phrase in it refers to something that is not on
this page, it has to be named or dropped.** And specifically for
`complete-note`, the clause has to say what the table *covers*, not what it
matches: "every quartic field with $|D|\leq 5000$" is the sentence, and "the
same 456 fields" is not, however true.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T365 critique (`agents/critiques/T365.md`). The rendered
`Data properties` slice via `GET /preview?table=` shows the note inside "Table
is complete: no (...)". The cubic table, T159, has the source sentences in
`Similar tables` and `comment-index`; `Regulators_of_quartic_fields` answers
404.

## An index defined by a sorting rule has to name the tuple: "lexicographic by coefficient vector" is two different orders

What happened: T365 indexes the several fields of one discriminant by
$k=1,2,\ldots$, and defines the numbering as sorting the reduced polynomials
"lexicographically by coefficient vector". PARI hands out two coefficient
vectors: `Vec` gives $(1,a_3,a_2,a_1,a_0)$, highest degree first, and `Vecrev`
gives $(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,1)$, lowest first. Both are "the coefficient vector"
in ordinary speech, and they are different orders.

They disagree on this table. 45 of its 405 discriminants carry more than one
field, and on 18 of those 45 the two readings number the fields differently:
at $D=1040$, $k=1$ is $a^4-3a^2-2a+5$ under the stored order (highest degree
first) and $a^4+a^2-4a+4$ under the other. So a reader cannot tell which field
a row is, and a second person reproducing the table has a coin flip on 18
discriminants. The generator's own key is unambiguous -- `tuple(poly[i] for i
in range(poly.degree() - 1, -1, -1))` -- and it simply did not reach the page.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing", with the
example "'The nth zero' needs a statement of where counting starts". That
covers where an index begins, not what order it runs in when the order is a
choice.

What it should say: the same rule, extended. **Where entries are numbered by
sorting them, the sort key belongs in the table, written out as a tuple, not
as the name of a vector.** "Lexicographic by coefficient vector",
"sorted by size", "in the usual order" each name a family of orders. Write
$(a_{n-1},\ldots,a_1,a_0)$ of $x^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0$, or say "by
increasing $|D|$, positive $D$ first", as this table's own `rigour details`
does for the rows and gets right. A cheap check on a draft, worth running once:
sort the multi-entry groups under the other plausible reading and count how
many change. If the answer is not zero, the sentence is not a definition.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T365 critique. Parsing the defining polynomial out of
all 456 entry comments and sorting each discriminant's fields both ways: the
high-to-low order reproduces the stored $k$ on all 45 multi-field
discriminants, the low-to-high order fails on 18 of them. The generator is
`generators/dedekind-zeta-residues-quartic-fields/generate.py:65`.
