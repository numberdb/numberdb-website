## A quantity a formula names must mean what the linked source means by that name

What happened: T306 (Adams–Moulton coefficients) writes "If $\gamma^*_m$
denotes the Gregory coefficient, then $\gamma^*_m=(-1)^m\psi_m(0)/m!$ …, and
$\beta^*_{s,0}=(-1)^s\gamma^*_s$". It links OEIS A002206 as "Gregory
coefficients" and puts that phrase in `Keywords`. A002206's $G_m$ is
$1,\tfrac12,-\tfrac1{12},\tfrac1{24},\ldots$, so the $\gamma^*_m$ defined
here is $(-1)^mG_m$. That is Hairer–Nørsett–Wanner's sign, and it is
opposite to the source's for odd $m$. The two signs cancel, and
$\beta^*_{s,0}=G_s$ exactly. A reader holding $3/160$ is told that the
Gregory coefficient is $-3/160$, and the linked source says $+3/160$. Every
statement is true under its own definition, and the audit passes it. This is
the sign version of the index-shift lesson proposed from T305.

What the skill says now: "Define notation where you use it", and link the
first mention. Nothing about a named quantity whose sign or normalisation
differs between the formula and the source the table links under the same
name.

What it should say: when a formula calls something by a name that a linked
source (a table here, OEIS, Wikipedia) also uses, use that source's
convention, sign and normalisation included, or say in the same sentence how
the two differ. Before writing an identity through an intermediate quantity,
check whether the intermediate signs cancel. If they do, state the direct
relation, because the detour is where the convention mismatch hides.

Evidence: T306 draft `Formulas: formula-gregory`; $G_s$ computed from
$x/\log(1+x)$ equals the stored $\beta^*_{s,0}$ for all $0\leq s\leq12$;
<https://numberdb.org/Bernoulli_polynomials_of_the_second_kind> formula (2),
$\psi_n(0)=1,\tfrac12,-\tfrac16,\tfrac14,\ldots$; critique
`agents/critiques/T306.md`, finding 1.
