Not in force. Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`.

## When a table's index is a sort, `Programs` has to show the sort

What happened: T323 indexes the eigenforms of each weight by increasing $a_2$,
a sort the table performs itself because no library agrees on an order. Its
`Programs` block is two snippets at weight 12, where $\dim S_{12}=1$ and the
index cannot be got wrong. Both return the stored entry to all thirty digits.
But PARI's `lfunmf(mfinit([1,k],0))[1]` lists a Hecke orbit's embeddings in an
order that is not increasing $a_2$ at $k=24$, $34$ and $38$ — three of the
eight weights in the table with more than one form — so a reader who changes
the `12` and keeps the `[1][1]`, which is the whole point of a `Programs`
block, gets the row filed under $i=2$ and has no way to notice. Sage's
`K.embeddings(RR)` order was increasing at all eight, which is luck rather
than a guarantee, and the two libraries therefore disagree with each other at
those three weights.

The table's `Comments` even say "Library orderings are not used as table
orderings", so the author knew; the snippet four inches away hands the reader
a library ordering anyway.

What the skill says now: "`Programs` is the standard incantation in Sage,
PARI or mpmath for a reader who wants one more value", and separately that a
parameter's key is an address that citations resolve on. Nothing says the
snippet has to reproduce the *index*, and nothing warns that an index defined
by sorting conjugates is one a library will not reproduce.

What it should say: when a parameter is defined by sorting — embeddings of a
Hecke orbit, roots of a polynomial, conjugates of an algebraic number — the
`Programs` snippet must perform that sort, not select by position. A snippet
tested at the one value of the parameter where the sort is trivial proves
nothing about the rest of the table. Where two libraries are named, check that
they agree on the order before writing a snippet in each; here they do not.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T323 critique, through `agents/sage.sh`.
`lfunan(lfunmf(mfinit([1,24],0))[1][j], 2)[2]` for $j=1,2$ gives
`5096.351…`, `-4016.351…`; at $k=34$, `49679.437…`, `-171359.437…`; at
$k=38$, `286011.754…`, `-480411.754…`. Increasing at $k=28,30,32,36,40$.
`Newforms(1,k,names='a')[0].hecke_eigenvalue_field().embeddings(RR)` applied
to $a_2$ was increasing at all eight. The table's $i=1$ at $k=24$ is the
$-4016.351$ form.

## A Dirichlet series is not a definition outside its half-plane of convergence

What happened: T323's Definition reads "this table gives the critical value
$L(f_{k,i},s)=\sum_{n\geq1}a_nn^{-s}$", and its parameter $s$ runs over
$1\leq s\leq k-1$. That series converges only for
$\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$ — which the table's own `Formulas` section states,
about the Euler product, four sections further down. At $k=12$ the Definition's
equation is false at six of the eleven rows, and across the table at roughly
half of the 681 entries. Two people building the table from the Definition
alone could not: at most rows the right-hand side is not a number. The
`audit_table` rules do not look at this, and the draft audits clean.

What the skill says now: "**Which quantity, exactly**", and that the Definition
should say what the object *is*, precisely enough that two people would build
the same table. Nothing names the commonest way that fails for an $L$-function
or a zeta value: writing the series that names the function and then indexing
outside where it converges.

What it should say: where a table's values are a function outside the region
its defining series or product converges, the Definition must name the
continuation. "…which for $\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$ is $\sum a_nn^{-s}$ and
continues to an entire function" is the whole fix, and it is also where the
range of the argument earns its constraint. The tell is mechanical and worth
stating: if the `Definition` carries a $\sum$ or a $\prod$ and a `Parameters`
constraint names arguments outside its convergence region, one of the two is
wrong.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T323. Deligne's bound gives absolute convergence of
$\sum a_nn^{-s}$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$; T323's own formula (2)
states the same bound for the Euler product; the parameter is
$1\leq s\leq k-1$.

## Named Sage imports do not bring `NumberField.embeddings` either

What happened: checking T323's ordering meant embedding a Hecke eigenvalue
field into the reals. Under named imports only —
`from sage.modular.modform.constructor import Newforms` and
`from sage.rings.real_mpfr import RR` — `K.embeddings(RR)` raises

    AttributeError: module 'sage.rings.polynomial' has no attribute
    'laurent_polynomial_ring'

through a chain that starts at
`'Matrix_rational_dense' object has no attribute '_change_ring'`, which names
neither the ring nor the call that wanted it. Swapping the order of the two
imports changes nothing. The same call succeeds in a script that has already
run `f.lseries(...)` on a modular form, because Dokchitser drags the missing
module in — so the failure is intermittent across scripts that look
equivalent, and a run that saw it work once will not expect it.

One explicit import fixes it, and so does the route round it:

    import sage.rings.polynomial.laurent_polynomial_ring
    K.embeddings(RR)                                     # works
    a2.minpoly().roots(RR, multiplicities=False)         # also works

What the skill says now: "**Name the rings you use, rather than importing
`sage.all`**", with a list of what the named imports do not bring — root
finding, `RealBall.str`, and `QQ('1.25')`.

What it should say: add `NumberField.embeddings` to that list, and say that
the error it raises names a polynomial ring rather than the call, so it does
not look like a missing import at all. The general shape is worth a sentence
too: a named-import failure that surfaces as an `AttributeError` about a lazy
import is a missing module, and importing the module the message names is the
fix.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T323 critique, through `agents/sage.sh` on
`numberdb/builder:latest`. `/tmp/crit323/impA.py` (pari imported first) and
`/tmp/crit323/impB.py` (modular forms first) both failed with the message
above; `/tmp/crit323/impC.py`, identical but for the one extra import,
printed `[-4016.351171716245, 5096.3511717162455]` from both `embeddings` and
`minpoly().roots`.
