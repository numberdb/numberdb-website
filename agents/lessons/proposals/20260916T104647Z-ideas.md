## An arXiv identifier is a claim: check the title it resolves to

What happened: the proposal for the random $k$-SAT thresholds cited
Mertens–Mézard–Zecchina as arXiv `cs/0504070`, written from memory. The
abstract page answers 200, so nothing looked wrong, but the paper at that
identifier is *The Combined Technique for Detection of Artifacts in Clinical
Electroencephalograms of Sleeping Newborns*. The paper wanted is `cs/0309020`.
The name check caught it only because none of "satisfiability", "random" or
"threshold" occurs on an EEG abstract; a wrong identifier that lands on a
paper in a neighbouring subject would pass.

What the skill says now: `References` take `bib` plus `arxiv`, `doi`, `zbl`,
`mr` fields; nothing says to confirm that the identifier resolves to the paper
the `bib` names.

What it should say: before writing an `arxiv` or `doi` field, fetch it and
compare its title with the `bib` text. An identifier that answers 200 proves
only that some paper has it. The arXiv export API gives the title in one
request: `https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=cs/0309020`.

Evidence: `curl -s https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0504070` title line, 2026-09-16;
`https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:"threshold values"
AND ti:SAT` returns `cs/0309020v2`, *Threshold values of Random K-SAT from
the cavity method*.

## Match a published table by its values, not by its indices

What happened: two papers tabulate the thresholds of cuckoo hashing, the
density up to which the random $k$-uniform hypergraph can be oriented with at
most $\ell$ edges per vertex. Fountoulakis–Khosla–Panagiotou (arXiv 1309.6772)
write $c^*_{k,\ell}$ with $\ell$ the bucket capacity. Dietzfelbinger et al.
(arXiv 0912.0287) print a ten-digit table of $c_{k,\ell}$ in which $\ell$ is
the capacity **plus one**, because they index by the core that obstructs the
orientation: their $c_{3,2}=0.9179352767$ is capacity one, their
$c_{2,3}=1.7940237365$ capacity two. Compared by index, every entry of a
correct table disagrees with the paper; compared by value, all 36 agree to
the tenth place. In the same subject a third paper (Dembo–Montanari,
math/0702007) writes the density as vertices per edge, the reciprocal of both.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent",
and "Normalisation, branch and indexing" among the things to write down.

What it should say: when a check is a published table, first locate one entry
by value, to find the paper's indexing and normalisation, and only then
compare the rest by index. A disagreement across a whole row or column by a
constant shift or factor is a convention, not an error, and the Comments
should name the other convention.

Evidence: 2026-09-16, `/tmp/thr/thr.py` and `/tmp/thr/prec.py` under
`agents/sage.sh` against the table of 0912.0287 read from
`https://arxiv.org/html/0912.0287v3`.
