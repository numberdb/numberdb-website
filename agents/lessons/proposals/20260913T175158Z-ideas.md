## Ehrhart polynomials in Sage without LattE or Normaliz: count and interpolate, and know where that stops

What happened: `Polyhedron(vertices=...).ehrhart_polynomial()` raised
`FeatureNotPresentError: count is not available` (LattE is an optional
package). `ehrhart_polynomial(engine='normaliz')` raised `TypeError: The
polyhedron's backend should be 'normaliz'`, and PyNormaliz was not
installed. What does work in a stock Sage is counting
`(k*P).integral_points()` for $k=0..d$ and interpolating
(`R.lagrange_polynomial`). For $h^*$, multiply $\sum_k L(k)z^k$ by
$(1-z)^{d+1}$ and truncate to degree $d$. This reproduced OEIS for the
Birkhoff, hypersimplex, permutohedron and root polytopes, but its cost climbs
steeply with dimension and vertex count. $F_4$ (dimension 4, 48 vertices) took
2.5 s at $k=4$, $E_6$ (dimension 6, 72 vertices) 67 s at $k=3$, $E_7$
(dimension 7, 126 vertices) 146 s at $k=2$, and $E_8$ (dimension 8, 240 vertices,
19,440 facets) did not produce $k=1$ at all: the process was killed after
about 90 s at 1.2 GB of memory.

What the skill says now: nothing about Ehrhart polynomials or lattice-point
counting.

What it should say: `ehrhart_polynomial()` needs LattE (`count`) or a
Normaliz backend (`sage -pip install pynormaliz`, then
`Polyhedron(..., backend='normaliz')`). Without either, count
`integral_points()` of dilates and interpolate. Fine to about dimension 6
with tens of vertices; beyond that install Normaliz rather than wait.

Evidence: `/tmp/feat.py` (the two errors), `/tmp/ehr.py` and `/tmp/exc.py`
(counts and timings), `/tmp/e8.py` (printed `polyhedron built, dim 8 facets
19440 4.3s`, then exit 137), Sage 10.9, 2026-09-13.

## An Ehrhart polynomial depends on the lattice, and Sage's two root-system coordinates are different lattices

What happened: the root polytope $\mathrm{conv}(\Phi)$ was counted in
`RootSystem(ct).ambient_space()` coordinates and in
`RootSystem(ct).root_lattice()` coordinates (simple-root basis). For $A_n$,
$B_n$ and $G_2$ the $h^*$-polynomials agree. For $C_2$ they are $1+6z+z^2$
(root lattice) against $1+10z+5z^2$ ($\mathbb Z^2$). For $D_4$ they are
$1+20z+54z^2+20z^3+z^4$ against $1+28z+102z^2+60z^3+z^4$. For $F_4$ and $E_n$
the ambient roots have half-integer coordinates, so the polytope is not a
lattice polytope in $\mathbb Z^n$ at all. The closed forms in the literature
and the OEIS coordination-sequence triangles (A108558, A109001) are the
root-lattice ones.

What the skill says now: "Decide what the table says before computing
anything ... which quantity, exactly", with the $p$-adic AGM as the example.
Nothing on lattices.

What it should say: a lattice-point count is a property of the pair
(polytope, lattice). State the lattice in the definition. When building a
polytope from Sage's root-system objects, `ambient_space()` gives
$\mathbb Z^n$ and `root_lattice()` gives $\mathbb Z\Phi$; they differ for
$C_n$, $D_n$, $F_4$, $E_n$.

Evidence: `/tmp/ehr.py`, section 4, Sage 10.9, 2026-09-13.

## An OEIS triangle named "coefficients of the Ehrhart polynomial" can hold the $h^*$-vector

What happened: A275514 is named "the coefficient [t^k] of the Ehrhart
polynomial of the 2-hypersimplex in dimension n". Its rows `1,5,5` and
`1,9,15,1` are the $h^*$-vectors of $\Delta(2,5)$ and $\Delta(2,6)$, computed
here by direct count. The Ehrhart polynomials themselves have rational
coefficients, for example $L_{\Delta(2,4)}(t)=\tfrac23t^3+2t^2+\tfrac73t+1$.
A check that compared Ehrhart coefficients with this triangle would report a
disagreement that is the sequence name's fault.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent";
the Hilbert-class-polynomial lesson says to look for polynomial families in
OEIS as triangles.

What it should say: before comparing against an OEIS triangle, compute one
row by brute force and see which encoding the triangle holds. Ehrhart
polynomial and $h^*$-vector, or a polynomial and its reversal, are easily
swapped in a name.

Evidence: OEIS A275514 text (`fmt=text` search), and `/tmp/ehr.py` section 2
giving $h^*_{\Delta(2,5)}=1+5z+5z^2$ and $h^*_{\Delta(2,6)}=1+9z+15z^2+z^3$.

## Search the issues for the misspelling too: #94 is "Erhart"

What happened: GitHub's issue search on "Ehrhart polynomial" in titles
returned nothing, although #94 *Erhart polynomials for interesting integral
polytopes* is open with label `table wanted`. Searching "Erhart" finds it.
GitHub issue search matches words, not near-spellings, so a contributor
checking "has anybody asked for this?" gets a confident no.

What the skill says now: "Answer a 'table wanted' issue in the issue itself",
and nothing on finding the issue.

What it should say: when checking whether a table was asked for, read the
`table wanted` label's list of titles rather than only searching it; names
are misspelt there (#94 "Erhart", #129 "hypergemetric", #28 "Maas").

Evidence: `already_asked('Ehrhart polynomials')` returned `[]` and
`already_asked('Erhart polynomials')` returned
`#94 [open] Erhart polynomials for interesting integral polytopes`,
2026-09-13.
