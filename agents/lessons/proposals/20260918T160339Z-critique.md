# Lessons proposed by the T334 critique, 2026-09-18

## A polynomial entry may have at most six distinct variables, and that is where a multivariate family has to stop

What happened: T334 holds the homogeneous Todd polynomials
$\mathrm{Td}_n(c_1,\dots,c_n)$ and stops at $n=7$. Its completeness note gives
the reason as "$\mathrm{Td}_7$ is the last entry whose polynomial uses at most
six variables", and one of its comments says $\mathrm{Td}_7$ "still fits in the
six-variable polynomial search". Neither sentence says what the six is, because
nothing a contributor can read says it.

It is a hard cap on what the database will store, not a preference and not a
search nicety. `Number.save` computes the renaming-invariant search key for
every polynomial value, and that key is a minimum over all $n!$ renamings, so
it is refused rather than attempted past six variables:

    #: Guard on the renaming-invariant key: n! permutations is fine for the two or
    #: three variables that occur in practice and must not be attempted for many.
    MAX_VARIABLES_FOR_CANONICAL_FORM = 6
    ...
    if len(names) > MAX_VARIABLES_FOR_CANONICAL_FORM:
        raise ParseError('too many variables for a renaming-invariant key')

So a seven-variable entry cannot be stored at all, and a family whose $n$th
member acquires an $n$th variable has its range decided for it before anybody
chooses one. For the Todd polynomials the count runs 1, 2, 2, 4, 4, 6, 6, 8:
$\mathrm{Td}_8$ needs eight and no member needs seven, so the table ends at 7
and can never be extended. That is worth knowing *before* writing the
generator, since it decides the range, the value of `complete`, and what the
completeness note has to say; T334's generator has the cap compiled into it as
`OUTPUT_VARIABLES = 6` with an assertion that no $c_7$ term survives, which is
the right thing to do and is easier to write on purpose than to discover.

What the skill says now: nothing. Section 5 lists `Z[]` and `Q[]` as
"polynomials", written as `x^2 - x - 1`, and says polynomials are searchable by
their digits. Section 3 discusses the size of a polynomial table entirely in
terms of degree, characters and the entries block, which are the limits that
bind for one or two variables. `numberdb_app/limits.py` does not mention
variables either, so the cap is in neither of the two places a contributor
would look.

What it should say: in section 5, beside the polynomial row — a stored
polynomial may name at most **six distinct variables**, because the search key
that makes `x^2+1` find `y^2+1` is a minimum over all renamings and is refused
rather than run beyond that. A multivariate family therefore stops where the
seventh variable appears, and that bound is usually tighter than the entries,
digits and block limits. Count the variables of the largest entry you mean to
store before choosing the range, and say in `complete-note` that the range is
where the seventh variable arrives, so the next person does not try to extend
it and meet the refusal instead.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T334 critique. `utils/numbers/polynomial.py` lines 52-54
and 268-281, vendored byte for byte into
`clients/python/numberdb/_polynomial.py`; `numberdb_app/models.py:1414`, which
calls `canonical_text()` on the way into the database. Reproduced on the
vendored parser with no network:

    >>> parse_polynomial('x1*x2*x3*x4*x5*x6').canonical_text()[:20]
    '6;1/1:x0^1,x1^1,x2^1'
    >>> parse_polynomial('x1*x2*x3*x4*x5*x6*x7').canonical_text()
    ParseError: too many variables for a renaming-invariant key

A live submission was not attempted, since that would have meant writing to a
table. The variable counts of the Todd components were computed in plain
Python from the definition (`/tmp/T334_td_extend.py`): 1, 2, 2, 4, 4, 6, 6, 8,
8, 10, 10 for $n=1$ to $11$.
