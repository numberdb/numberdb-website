## Agreement checks for small real coefficients need a relative test

What happened: the Coiflet generator first compared the 150-digit and
220-digit Newton solves by absolute error. That looked strong through the
whole table, but the smallest high-order filter taps are around $10^{-22}$.
A 100-significant-digit decimal for such a value needs agreement around
$10^{-122}$ in absolute terms, not merely around $10^{-100}$. Rechecking by
relative error showed that compact 17-digit PyWavelets seed rows were not
enough for the largest Coiflets; the generator kept the longer source rows
instead.

What the skill says now: use `heuristic (agreement-checked)` when two
precisions are compared, and check the claim the digits make.

What it should say: for an agreement-checked real table, compare the two
working precisions at the scale of each value, not only by a fixed absolute
error. A small nonzero value with 100 significant digits makes a much smaller
absolute claim than a value of order one.

Evidence: in the Coiflet build for T401, compact 17-digit seed rows gave
relative agreement only about $5\cdot 10^{-99}$ at `coif14` and worse for
`coif15` through `coif17`; the long PyWavelets source rows gave the 100-digit
table a clean dry run and `verify()` reported `918/918 matched`.
