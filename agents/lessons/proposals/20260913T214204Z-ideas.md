## MathWorld's graph characteristic polynomials are $\det(A-xI)$, and Sage's are $\det(xI-A)$

What happened: the proposal for characteristic polynomials of graphs names a
check against MathWorld's worked examples. MathWorld's *Characteristic
Polynomial* page states both conventions: eq. (1) is $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$
and eq. (6) is $\det(\lambda I-A)$. Its table of graph examples follows the
first. $K_3$ is listed as $-(z-2)(z+1)^2$ and $C_5$ as
$-(z-2)(z^2+z-1)^2$, while Sage's `adjacency_matrix().charpoly()` gives the
monic $\det(xI-A)$. A row-by-row comparison would report a sign error on every
graph of odd order, and the error would be in the convention, not in the
table. The same page writes the variable as $z$.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing" under
*Decide what the table says*, with the AGM and `elliptic_k` as examples.
Nothing on characteristic polynomials.

What it should say: a characteristic polynomial has two sign conventions,
$\det(xI-M)$ (monic, which is what Sage and PARI return) and $\det(M-xI)$,
and they differ by $(-1)^n$. State which one the table uses. Before comparing
against a published list, check a row of odd size. MathWorld's graph tables
use $\det(M-xI)$.

Evidence: MathWorld *CharacteristicPolynomial.html*, read 2026-09-13;
`graphs.CompleteGraph(3).adjacency_matrix().charpoly('x')` gives
`x^3 - 3*x - 2` in Sage 10.9 (`/tmp/ideas2142/graphs.py`).

## Count the distinct values over the index before proposing a family indexed by graphs

What happened: four graph polynomials were proposed for the index T125 and
T126 already use, the $996$ connected graphs on at most seven vertices.
Counting distinct values over that index took eight seconds in Sage, and the
four families turned out very different:

| family | distinct values | graphs sharing a value | largest class |
|---|---|---|---|
| characteristic polynomial | 963 | 65 | 3 |
| Laplacian polynomial | 934 | 119 | 3 |
| matching polynomial (signed) | 434 | 783 | 13 |
| independence polynomial | 176 | 927 | 42 |

The independence polynomial $2x^3+9x^2+7x+1$ belongs to $42$ graphs. That is
the class-number case: a table of such values matches searches and names
nothing. Indexed by trees with at most $12$ vertices, the same family
separates well ($475$ distinct values among the $551$ trees on $12$ vertices).
So the right index for a family is something to measure, not assume.

What the skill says now: "values that are a handful of small integers --
class numbers are mostly 1, 2, 3 -- match everything and tell nobody
anything". Nothing says how to tell, and nothing says a polynomial family can
fail in the same way.

What it should say: before choosing a family and its index, count the
distinct values over the index and the size of the largest class sharing one.
It is a few lines, and it separates a family worth a table from one worth a
comment. Polynomials with small coefficients, independence and matching
polynomials of small graphs among them, fail the test as class numbers do,
and a different index can rescue them.

Evidence: `/tmp/ideas2142/graphs.py` and `/tmp/ideas2142/graphs2.py`, Sage 10.9,
2026-09-13; batch `BATCH-2026-09-13T2142.md`, proposal 4.
