## Choose the stored precision from agreement between source columns

What happened: Packomania gives three interdependent columns for equal circles
in a circle: small-circle radius, point separation, and reciprocal radius. The
printed columns look like they support ten or more digits, but across
$2\leq n\leq500$ the derived values first disagree at 10 significant digits
because independently rounded columns can fall on opposite sides of a last
digit. At 9 significant digits, all three columns agree for every row in the
stored range.

What the skill says now: census rows get "the digits the census supports",
declared per entry.

What it should say: when a numerical source gives the same value in several
normalisations, do not choose the number of stored digits from the longest
printed column. Convert the columns into the table's normalisations and store
only the precision at which they all agree over the whole range being used.
If a single source column is more precise than the others, say why that column
is authoritative before keeping more digits.

Evidence: `agents/table-build/dry_run.py` on T394 accepted 998 entries at 9
significant digits. A private sweep of the Packomania radius, distance and
ratio files found 0 disagreements at 9 significant digits, 7 disagreements at
10, 18 at 11, and 148 at 12.

## A source's proof marker can lag the current literature

What happened: Packomania's circle-in-circle page marks proven rows by bold
radius, and the copied 25-Dec-2024 source still marks $n=1,\ldots,13$ and
$n=19$. The current literature proves $n=14$ as well: Dinesh B. Ekanayake
and Douglas J. LaFountain, "Tight partitions for packing circles in a circle",
Italian Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics 51 (2024), 115-136. The
entry comments for T394 therefore cite Packomania for the best-known values
but cite Ekanayake and LaFountain for optimality at $n=14$.

What the skill says now: each entry comment says either "optimality proven by
CITE{...}" or "best packing known; optimality is open"; `rigour details` names
which $n$ are proven optimal and by whom.

What it should say: for tables whose source distinguishes proven optima from
best-known configurations, treat the source's proof marker as a claim from
that source rather than as the current proof frontier. Check a second current
source for the boundary cases just beyond the marked range before writing
"optimality is open".

Evidence: Packomania's 25-Dec-2024 CCI page marks the proven rows by bold
radius. The Ekanayake-LaFountain paper proves the $n=14$ case; T394 records
that proof in the entry comments and rigour details.
