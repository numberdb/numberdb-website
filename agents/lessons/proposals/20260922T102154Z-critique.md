# Lessons proposed by the T408 critique run, 2026-09-22

Format as in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. Nothing here is in force.

## A paragraph in `rigour details` needs a *blank* line, and a note with no blank line is cut after its first sentence

What happened: T408's `rigour details` is two paragraphs in the author's head,
separated by a single `\n`: the method and the agreement check, then the
controls. On the page it is one paragraph, because `numberdb_app/prose.py`
starts a new block only at a **blank** line and joins the rest with spaces.
Having one block and nothing to fold, the renderer falls through to
`_split_sentence`, which cuts at the last sentence end within 400 characters
of rendered prose. That landed after the note's first sentence, so what a
reader sees above the "more" disclosure is the method alone — and everything
that makes the table's `heuristic (agreement-checked)` mean anything (four
truncations, two basis frequencies, "the digits common to all four
computations", "the worst stored row retained 51 matching significant
digits") sits hidden. Re-rendering the same note with `\n\n` instead of `\n`
put the whole first paragraph above the fold and left only the controls
behind it.

What the skill says now: under `rigour details`, "how the digits were
obtained, at whatever length that needs: blank lines make paragraphs, `- `
makes a list, backticks mark code, and a long note folds after its first
paragraph." True as far as it goes, and it does not tell an author either of
the two things that bit here: that a single newline is not a paragraph break
(it is silently a space), and that when there is no paragraph break the fold
does not wait for one — it cuts mid-note at a sentence boundary near 400
characters, so the fold point is not where the author thinks it is.

What it should say: a sentence after "blank lines make paragraphs", along the
lines of — a single newline is not a break; it joins. And a note with no blank
line in it is not left whole: above about four hundred characters it is cut
after a sentence, wherever that falls. So put the blank line where you want
the fold, and put the sentence that justifies the `rigour` level before it.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, T408. The stored string is
`"…retained 51 matching significant digits.\nAs controls, the same code…"`.
Rendered through `/preview`, the HTML is
`<p class="prose-paragraph">The generator forms … eigensolver.</p>`
`<details class="prose-more"><summary>more</summary><p …>For $m=2,3$ …
conventions.</p></details>`. The same document with `digits.\n\nAs controls`
renders `<p …>The generator … 51 matching significant digits.</p>` followed by
a `prose-more` holding only the controls paragraph. `numberdb_app/prose.py`,
`_blocks` and `_split_sentence`, `FOLD_ABOVE = 400`.

## The corpus text index is accent-sensitive in both directions, so an accented keyword needs its unaccented twin *and* the reverse is not free

What happened: T408 carries `Schrödinger equation` in `Keywords` and
"Schrödinger operator" in a `Similar tables` relation, and no unaccented form
anywhere. Searching the live corpus from a build machine:

    Schrodinger     0   []
    Schrödinger     1   ['T41']
    Levy            3   ['T172', 'T241', 'T387']
    Lévy            3   ['T172', 'T170', 'T173']

The first pair is the failure the skill already warns about. The second pair
is the part it does not say: `Levy` and `Lévy` both return three tables and
they are *different* three. The index does not fold accents in either
direction, so carrying only the unaccented spelling loses the readers who type
the accent, exactly as carrying only the accented one loses the readers who do
not.

What the skill says now: "that is also how an accent is handled, since the
index has no `unaccent`: the title is 'Lévy's constant' and `Keywords` carries
'Levy constant'." That reads as a one-way rule about titles — put the plain
spelling in `Keywords` — and a contributor whose *keyword* is the accented one
does not obviously fall under it.

What it should say: the rule is about the pair, not about the title: wherever
a name with an accent appears in an indexed field, both spellings must appear
somewhere in the title or `Keywords`, because neither matches the other.
Naming the commonest cases would help — Schrödinger, Lévy, Poincaré, Gödel,
Chebyshev/Tschebyscheff.

Evidence: 2026-09-22, `numberdb.search_text` against the live corpus through
`agents/sage.sh`, output above. T41 is the only table reachable by
`Schrödinger` and nothing is reachable by `Schrodinger`.
