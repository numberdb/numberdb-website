Format as `PROPOSALS.md`: what happened, what the skill says now, what it
should say, evidence. Nothing here is in force.

## A request can hide behind a typo, so `already_asked` is not the backlog

What happened: the batch was anchored on numberdb-data#129, and the screen was
run on the family's proper name. `already_asked('hypergeometric series')`
returned the empty list. The request is open, it is the oldest one in this
subject, and it is titled *Values of some **hypergemetric** series*. Only
`already_asked('hypergemetric series')` finds it.

That is not a search that stems or fuzzes: `already_asked` builds a GitHub
`in:title` query out of the words longer than four letters, and GitHub matches
title words literally. A misspelling in the title makes the request invisible
to every correctly spelled query, which means the check answers "nobody asked"
for a family somebody asked for five years ago, and answers it in exactly the
tone of a real negative.

What the skill says now: a lesson from the Ehrhart run already records one
instance of this, "Search the issues for the misspelling too: #94 is 'Erhart'".
It reads as a fact about that one issue.

What it should say: the general form. The backlog is read, not queried. Run
`screen.py requests` and read all of the open titles before screening a name,
and treat `already_asked` as a check on the *closed* issues — where a hit means
the table exists — rather than as evidence that nothing was asked for. Two
misspellings in a backlog of this size is a rate, not an accident.

Evidence: 2026-09-19 ideas run.

    already_asked('hypergeometric series')  -> []
    already_asked('hypergemetric series')   -> ['#129 [open] Values of some hypergemetric series']

## `CBF(a).gamma(z)` is the incomplete gamma with `self` as the parameter

What happened: the batch needed $\Gamma(a,x)$, and Sage's `ComplexBall.gamma`
takes an optional argument. The docstring says only "the image of this ball by
the Euler Gamma function (if `z = None`) or the incomplete Gamma function
(otherwise)", and does not say which of the two slots `self` fills.

It is the **parameter**: `CBF(a).gamma(CBF(x))` is $\Gamma(a,x)$, the upper
incomplete gamma. The other reading, `CBF(x).gamma(CBF(a))`, is a perfectly
good number as well, so getting it backwards produces no error and no `nan` —
it produces $\Gamma(x,a)$, a table of the right shape with the arguments
transposed. On a symmetric-looking grid that survives a lot of eyeballing.

What the skill says now: nothing. It warns that `elliptic_k` takes the
parameter $m$ and not the modulus $k$, "and they differ from the second entry
onwards", which is the same kind of trap in a different function.

What it should say: the same warning generalises to every arb entry point
where one of the function's arguments is `self`. Settle it on a closed form
before writing the generator, and choose one where the two readings disagree:
$\Gamma(1,2)=e^{-2}=0.1353\ldots$ against $\Gamma(2,1)=2/e=0.7357\ldots$ says
which slot is which in one line, where $\Gamma(1,1)$ says nothing at all.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, in the deployed image.

    CBF(1).gamma(CBF(1))        = [0.36787944117144232159... +/- 6.00e-61]   = 1/e
    CBF(1/2).gamma(CBF(x))      = sqrt(pi) * erfc(sqrt(x))   at x = 1, 1/4, 4
    CBF(1).gamma(CBF(x))        = exp(-x)                    at x = 1, 3/2, 5

## arb's incomplete elliptic integrals take $m=k^2$, and `elliptic_pi_inc` leads with $n$

What happened: three of the proposed tables are incomplete elliptic integrals,
and the corpus stores the complete ones by the parameter $m=k^2$ (T25, T26,
T59). The DLMF writes $F(\varphi,k)$ with the **modulus**, and Abramowitz and
Stegun write $F(\varphi\backslash\alpha)$ with $\sin\alpha=k$, so a builder
comparing against either published table is comparing against a different
index unless they square first. Sage and arb agree with the corpus and not
with the DLMF.

The argument order is the second half of it. `ComplexBall.elliptic_f` reads
$F(\text{self},m)$, `self` being the angle; `ComplexBall.elliptic_pi_inc`
reads $\Pi(\text{self};\varphi,m)$, `self` being the **characteristic** $n$
and the angle moving into the first argument. Two functions in the same family,
two meanings for `self`.

What the skill says now: it warns about `elliptic_k` taking $m$ rather than
$k$. It says nothing about the incomplete family or about the argument order.

What it should say: state both, and give the one-line settlement, which is
that every incomplete elliptic integral collapses onto a complete one at
$\varphi=\pi/2$ and the corpus holds all three complete ones. That check costs
nothing, catches the modulus-versus-parameter error immediately (the values
differ from the second entry onwards, as with `elliptic_k`), and catches a
transposed argument order as well.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, in the deployed image, at 100 digits with
`numberdb.bits(100, losing=64)`.

    CBF(pi/2).elliptic_f(CBF(m))            == CBF(m).elliptic_k()     m = 1/4, 1/2, 9/10
    CBF(pi/2).elliptic_e_inc(CBF(m))        == CBF(m).elliptic_e()     m = 1/2, 9/10
    CBF(n).elliptic_pi_inc(CBF(pi/2), CBF(m)) == CBF(n).elliptic_pi(CBF(m))   n = 1/3, m = 1/2

all to $10^{-100}$, with the widest ball over the whole proposed grid of radius
$5.2\cdot10^{-117}$ at $m=99/100$, $\varphi=\pi/2$.

## The incomplete beta function is rational on a whole sub-grid, and says nothing about it

What happened: a proposal for $B(x;a,b)$ took a grid with $a,b$ running over
half-integers and small integers, and the rows with $a$ and $b$ both positive
integers came back exact rationals: $B(1/2;2,3)=11/192$. The integrand
$t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}$ is then a polynomial, so the integral is a polynomial in
$x$ with rational coefficients, and every such row is a small rational.

It is the monomial argument of numberdb-data#128 arriving in a table that
looks entirely transcendental from its definition. The same thing happens to
${}_2F_1(a,b;c;z)$ when $a$ or $b$ is a non-positive integer and the series
terminates.

What the skill says now: it says exactness is decided from the definition and
never from the digits, and that a table whose values are a handful of small
numbers makes search worse for everybody. It does not say that one family can
be both — transcendental on most of its grid and rational on a slice of it.

What it should say: before fixing a grid, ask which of its rows the definition
makes rational, because the answer is rarely "none" and is usually a whole
axis. Those rows are the ones to state as a closed form in `Formulas` and
leave out of the table, and the completeness note has to say which they were,
or the next person reads the gap as unfinished work.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, in the deployed image.

    B(1/2; 2, 3) = 0.05729166666666666666666666666...  = 11/192 exactly
    B(1;   a, b) = B(a,b)  at (a,b) = (1/2,1/2), (2,3), (1/3,5/2)   [the T177 check]

## A parity restriction in a table's `Parameters` is usually a theorem, not a range

What happened: numberdb-data#62 asks for "zeros (or local extrema) of
polygamma functions", and T127 holds the zeros of $\psi^{(n)}$ "for even $n$
and $k\geq1$", over $n=0,2,\dots,40$. Read as a range — somebody got round to
the even ones — that leaves the request half open and invites a table of the
odd-$n$ zeros. Read as a theorem it closes the request outright.

It is a theorem. $\psi^{(n)}(x)=(-1)^{n+1}n!\sum_{k\geq0}(x+k)^{-(n+1)}$, so
for odd $n$ the exponent is even, every term is positive, and $\psi^{(n)}$ has
no real zeros anywhere. The odd-$n$ table would have had no entries. And
because the local extrema of $\psi^{(n)}$ are the zeros of $\psi^{(n+1)}$, the
extrema exist only for odd $n$ and are T127's rows at index $n+1$: the second
half of the request is in the corpus too, under the other word.

What the skill says now: it says a parameter states the constraint the entries
actually satisfy rather than this afternoon's run, and it gives the case where
a table claimed a constraint its entries broke. It does not say what to do
when reading somebody else's constraint.

What it should say: the same sentence read backwards. When an existing table's
constraint excludes half an index, find out why before proposing the other
half. It is nearly always the definition speaking, and the cost of assuming
otherwise is a table that cannot have any rows.

Evidence: 2026-09-19, in the deployed image, $\psi^{(n)}$ built from
`CBF(n+1).zeta(CBF(x))` (arb's Hurwitz zeta) over twelve points spanning three
intervals between poles:

    n=1  + + + + + + + + + + + +
    n=2  - - - + - + - - - + - +
    n=3  + + + + + + + + + + + +
    n=4  - - - + - + - - - + - +

`ComplexBall` has no `polygamma`; the Hurwitz zeta identity is the way to it
under named imports, and the $n=0$ case is not available that way at all,
since it needs $\zeta(1,x)$.
