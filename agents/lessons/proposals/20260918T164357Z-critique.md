## A `HREF{slug}` with no caption prints the slug, underscores and all

What happened: T335's Formula (2) was written

    $\mathrm{ch}_n=p_n/n!$, where $p_n=\sum_i x_i^n$ is the $n$-th power sum
    symmetric polynomial HREF{Power_sum_symmetric_polynomials} in the Chern
    roots.

which a reader meets as

> ... is the $n$-th power sum symmetric polynomial
> **Power_sum_symmetric_polynomials** in the Chern roots.

The renderer's fallback for a missing caption is the address itself
(`views.py`, `caption = ref`), not the linked table's title, so the slug is set
down as a word in the middle of the sentence. Nothing in the stored document
looks wrong, and the link resolves; the fault exists only on the page. The same
document links two tables *with* captions in `Similar tables`, which is why the
author had no reason to doubt the third.

The audit does have a rule for this shape -- "writes `X HREF{X}` -- put the
link on the name" -- and it was silent, because it fires only when the words
immediately before the link are the target's bare title. T119 is "Power sum
symmetric polynomials $p_k$" and the sentence ends "polynomial", singular. The
same plural/singular gap silenced the companion rule, "names a family and does
not link it", on a second sentence in the same table: comment (6) says "the
$j$-th elementary symmetric polynomial" where T118 is titled "Elementary
symmetric polynomials $e_k$", and no finding was raised.

What the skill says now: it writes `HREF{slug}[caption]` in its examples and
says "put the link on the name", and it says `manage.py audit_table` confirms
slugs. It never says what a caption-less `HREF{}` renders as, and it presents
the audit as the check that catches this.

What it should say: a `HREF{}` inside a sentence always takes a caption, because
the fallback is the slug and a slug in prose reads as a file name. Write
`HREF{Power_sum_symmetric_polynomials}[power sum symmetric polynomial]`, with
the caption the words the sentence was going to use anyway, singular or plural
as the sentence needs. The bare form `HREF{slug}` is for a place where the
address is the whole of the text, such as `repeats`. And: the audit's link
rules match the target's title exactly, so a family named in the singular, or
by a shortened name, passes them; that is a thing to read for, not to audit
for.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T335 critique. Document from `GET /api/table?id=T335`;
page rebuilt by the sqlite draft-render recipe (6 records, status 200, 28,833
bytes), which contains
`<a class="HREF" href="Power_sum_symmetric_polynomials">Power_sum_symmetric_polynomials</a>`;
`numberdb_app/views.py` lines 744-766; `GET /api/table/T335/audit` returns one
finding, about a tag, and neither link rule
(`numberdb_app/management/commands/audit_table.py` lines 907-947) fires.
