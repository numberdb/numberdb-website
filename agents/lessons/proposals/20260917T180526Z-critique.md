## PARI's `polclass(D, inv)` names a class invariant only up to which cube (or other) root it took

What happened: T310 stores the $\gamma_2$ class polynomials, and both its
`Programs` line and its generator's independent check are
`polclass(Delta, 5)`. PARI's manual, which the table cites, says that the
algorithm defines the invariant only algebraically, by the modular polynomial
relating it to $j$, so "the polynomial returned by `polclass(D, 5)` can be the
minimal polynomial of any of $\gamma_2(\tau)$, $\zeta_3\gamma_2(\tau)$ or
$\zeta_3^2\gamma_2(\tau)$". That is a warning that the routine does not fix a
convention, and a table that defined its rows as "what `polclass` returns"
would inherit the ambiguity.

T310 does not: it defines its rows as the unique monic degree-$h(\Delta)$
factor of $H_\Delta(x^3)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, which is a construction, and
uses `polclass` only to check it. Factoring $H_\Delta(x^3)$ for all 100 stored
discriminants gives exactly two irreducible factors, of degrees $h$ and $2h$,
every time, so the degree-$h$ factor really is unique and PARI's three
candidates collapse to one polynomial. The week before, T308 met the other
outcome with the same routine: `polclass(D, 1)` is the Weber class polynomial
only after $x\mapsto-1/x$ or $x\mapsto 1/x$, depending on $D$.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing", with
`elliptic_k`'s parameter as the example. It says nothing about a library
routine that declines to fix one.

What it should say: a class polynomial routine (PARI's `polclass`, and any
routine that works modulo small primes from a modular polynomial) identifies
the invariant up to the roots of that modular polynomial. Define the table by a
construction that a second person can carry out -- the factor of a known
polynomial, the minimal polynomial of a named value -- and then use the library
as an independent check rather than as the definition. If the construction says
"the unique factor of degree $d$", verify that it is unique on every stored
parameter before writing "unique": that verification is what makes the library's
caveat harmless.

Evidence: PARI/GP manual, `polclass`, invariant list and the paragraph after
it, fetched 2026-09-17; `/tmp/crit310.py` run through `agents/sage.sh` on the
100 discriminants of draft T310 (no non-unique factorisation; `polclass` equal
to the stored row at $\Delta=-4,-23,-32,-71,-299$); `agents/critiques/T308.md`
for the opposite case.

## A comparative in a comment can be measured from the two tables' own entries

What happened: T310's `comment-height` says its polynomials are "smaller class
polynomials than the Hilbert class polynomials for the same discriminants" with
"coefficients of much smaller height". Both tables are in the database, so the
claim is checkable without recomputing anything: fetch both documents from
`/api/table?id=<slug>`, parse the coefficients out of the stored polynomial
strings, and compare. The degrees turn out to be equal in all 100 rows -- both
are $h(\Delta)$ -- so "smaller class polynomials" can only mean the
coefficients, and the measurement is sharper than the comparative:
$\log(\text{height } H_\Delta)/\log(\text{height } P_\Delta^{\gamma_2})$ is
3.00 at the minimum, the median and the maximum, because $\gamma_2$ is a cube
root of $j$. "About a third as many digits" is a fact; "much smaller" is an
impression, and it leaves a reader wondering whether the degree shrank too.

The same check answers a Similar-tables relation: at the ten discriminants with
$h(\Delta)=1$, the stored row is $x-\gamma_2$ and $\gamma_2^3$ is the singular
modulus in the Hilbert table, $(-15)^3=-3375$ at $\Delta=-7$. That is a genuine
cross-table identity found with a dozen lines of Python and no Sage.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent", with
identities between two tables as the example. It is about values, and it does
not say the prose is checkable the same way.

What it should say: a comparative claim about another table in the database
("smaller", "of much smaller height", "the same up to a factor") is a
measurement waiting to be made from the two tables' entries, and making it
usually produces a better sentence than the comparative. It also catches the
reading you did not intend, when the thing that did *not* change is the thing a
reader assumes did.

Evidence: T310 and its linked Hilbert class polynomial table, 2026-09-17;
`agents/critiques/T310.md`, "What reads well" and finding 6.
