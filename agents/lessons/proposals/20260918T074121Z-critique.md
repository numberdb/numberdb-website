## A `comments` key on a parameter is accepted, stored, and never rendered

What happened: T324 put the sentence that defines its index --

    Parameters:
      n:
        type: Z
        title: index of the positive zero
        display: $n$
        constraints: $n\geq1$
        comments: >-
          For fixed $k$ and $i$, the positive ordinates are ordered
          increasingly and counted from $n=1$.

-- under the parameter it belongs to. The API accepted the document,
`audit_table` answered `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`, and the page prints
`$n$ — index of the positive zero ($n\geq1$)` and not a word of the sentence.
The renderer's parameter loop reads `display`, `title`, `type`, `constraints`,
`values` and `show-in-parameter-list`; anything else on a parameter is dropped
in silence. So the table's only statement of where counting starts existed in
the document and nowhere a reader could see it. Two of the 189 `table.yaml`
files in `generators/` use a parameter `comments`, which is about how often
somebody guesses that the key works.

What the skill says now: its table of sections says `Parameters` holds "each
parameter's type and the constraint the entries actually satisfy", and section
2 says `"the nth zero" needs a statement of where counting starts`. It does not
say which keys a parameter may carry, and nothing warns that an unknown one is
kept rather than refused.

What it should say: name the keys a parameter renders -- `display`, `title`,
`constraints`, `values`, `show-in-parameter-list` -- and say that any other key
is stored and never shown. Where counting starts is a fact about the family and
goes in the `Definition` or in `constraints`; there is no per-parameter comment
field.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T324 read as a reader. `Parameters: n: comments:` in
`generators/level-one-cusp-form-l-zeros/table.yaml`; rendered through
`/preview?table=`, the string "ordered increasingly" appears in the echoed
source and nowhere in the page. `numberdb_app/views.py`, the loop over
`data['Parameters']` at about line 844.

## `rigour details` folds after about 400 characters, cutting a single paragraph at its first sentence

What happened: T324's `rigour details` is one paragraph of four sentences,
ending "PARI's zero search is not a certified enclosure method, so the table
does not claim proven zeros" -- the caveat, and on a table of zeros the part a
reader most needs. The rendered page shows only the first sentence and puts the
other three behind a `more` disclosure, so the field reads as an untroubled
account of two-precision agreement until somebody clicks.

The rule is in `numberdb_app/prose.py`: above `FOLD_ABOVE = 400` characters the
note folds, and when the note is a single paragraph -- which is what most of
them are -- the split is made at the first full stop followed by a capital, not
at a paragraph break. The threshold counts rendered HTML, so `backticks` and
`CITE{}` push a note over it sooner than its visible length suggests: T324's
field is about 490 characters of prose and rather more of markup.

What the skill says now: in its table of sections, that `rigour details` holds
"how the digits were obtained, at whatever length that needs: blank lines make
paragraphs, `- ` makes a list, backticks mark code, and a long note folds after
its first paragraph".

What it should say: that "folds after its first paragraph" means *after the
first sentence* when the note has no blank line in it, and that the first
sentence is therefore the whole of what a reader sees with the page closed. Put
the caveat -- what is not proven, what the method cannot guarantee -- in the
first sentence, or keep the note under about 400 characters so nothing folds.

Evidence: 2026-09-18, T324 rendered through `/preview?table=`. The page emits
`<p class="prose-paragraph">The generator uses PARI/GP's lfunmf and
lfunzeros [3] at two bit precisions, ... agree between the two
runs.</p><details class="prose-more"><summary>more</summary>...` with the two
remaining sentences inside the `details`. `numberdb_app/prose.py`,
`FOLD_ABOVE` and `_split_sentence`.
