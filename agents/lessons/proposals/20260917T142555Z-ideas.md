## Sage's `KnotInfo` holds ten knots unless `database_knotinfo` is installed, and says nothing

What happened: to measure the knot-invariant proposals, a script iterated
`for K in KnotInfo if K.is_knot()` and asked for crossing numbers up to 10.
It got **ten** knots, $0_1$ to $7_2$, and no error or warning. Sage ships a
small sample of KnotInfo, and the full database is the optional pip package
`database_knotinfo`. A table built from that loop would have looked complete
and held one knot in twenty-five. The columns differ too: in the sample,
asking a knot for `khovanov_polynomial` or `homflypt_polynomial` raises
`KeyError: 'item not available for knots'`, because those names are the
*link* columns. The knot columns are `homfly_polynomial` and
`khovanov_unreduced_integral_polynomial`.

What the skill says now: nothing about KnotInfo. T139's rigour details
mention `database_knotinfo 2026.9.1` in passing.

What it should say: before trusting any bundled database in Sage, count what
it returned against a number you know independently. For prime knots the
counts are OEIS A002863 (1, 1, 2, 3, 7, 21, 49, 165 for 3 to 10 crossings,
249 in all). For KnotInfo, `sage -pip install database_knotinfo` first.

Evidence: `/tmp/k2.py` printed `knots 10`. The PyPI wheel
`database_knotinfo-2026.9.1` holds 250 rows with crossing number at most 10.

## A knot polynomial "in the usual variables" is three different polynomials

What happened: Sage's `Link.homfly_polynomial()` returned
`L^-2*M^2 - L^-2 - L^-4*M^2 + L^-4 + L^-6` for $5_2$, and KnotInfo's column
holds `-v^6 + v^4*z^2 + v^4 + v^2*z^2 + v^2`. These are the same invariant.
Sage's default is `normalization='lm'` (Lickorish–Millett), and its
`normalization='vz'` output is identical to KnotInfo on $3_1$, $5_2$ and
$6_2$. The `'lm'` form is the `'vz'` one under $v^2=-L^{-2}$ and $z^2=-M^2$,
so coefficients change sign and no renaming of variables matches them. Search
by polynomial here is invariant under renaming and nothing else. The Kauffman
polynomial is the same kind of trap: in KnotInfo's convention
$V(t)=F(-t^{-3/4},t^{1/4}+t^{-1/4})$ holds on four knots, and the other
three sign and exponent choices fail.

What the skill says now: "Which normalisation, branch, and indexing", with
`elliptic_k` as the example.

What it should say: for a two-variable invariant, state the convention by a
specialisation a reader can test, such as "$V(t)=P(t,t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2})$",
not by naming the variables. Check which convention your tool returns by
default: Sage's is not KnotInfo's.

Evidence: `/tmp/k4.py`, `/tmp/k5.py` and `/tmp/k9.py` under Sage, in
`agents/table-ideas/BATCH-2026-09-17T1425.md` proposals 1 and 3.

## A source's description of its convention can contradict its own data

What happened: KnotInfo's page on Chern–Simons invariants says "all
Chern-Simons invariants are given as reals between 0 and 1/2". Its data runs
from $-0.24647666$ to $0.247414919$, and $5_2$ is $-0.153204133$, which is
SnapPy's representative in $(-\frac14,\frac14]$. A table built from the prose
would add $\frac12$ to half the values and disagree with every program a
reader uses.

What the skill says now: "Check new values against something independent".

What it should say: when a source documents a convention, check the
documentation against the source's own numbers before adopting either. Take
the range, the sign and the representative from the data, and note the
disagreement in the table.

Evidence: <https://knotinfo.org/descriptions/chern_simons_invariant.html>,
read 2026-09-17, and the `chern_simons_invariant` column of
`database_knotinfo` 2026.9.1.

## SnapPy gives more than one cusp shape for the same knot

What happened: for $5_2$, built as the closure of KnotInfo's braid word,
SnapPy's `cusp_info('shape')` is $-2.4902\ldots+2.9794\ldots i$. The ratio
longitude/meridian from `cusp_translations(verified=True)` on the same
manifold is $-2.4902\ldots-2.9794\ldots i$, and KnotInfo's published
translations give $2.4902\ldots-2.9794\ldots i$. These are conjugates and
negatives of one another, and that sign is exactly what distinguishes a knot
from its mirror image. For $4_1$ the two SnapPy routes did not even give the
same kind of number.

What the skill says now: nothing about orientation conventions in geometric
invariants.

What it should say: an invariant that changes under mirror image, such as a
cusp shape or a Chern–Simons invariant, has a sign convention. Fix it by one
knot whose chirality is known independently before computing the rest, and
compare two routes through the same program as well as two programs.

Evidence: `/tmp/k6.py` under Sage with SnapPy; KnotInfo's
`meridian_translation` and `longitude_translation` columns for $5_2$.

## Importing `numberdb.sage` first does not make `sage.groups.braid` importable

What happened: a script began `import numberdb.sage` and then
`from sage.groups.braid import BraidGroup`, following the skill's advice to
name imports rather than import `sage.all`. It failed with
`AttributeError: cannot access submodule 'function' of module 'sage.symbolic'
(most likely due to a circular import)`, raised from
`sage/functions/generalized.py`. `from sage.all import *` works. The knot
modules (`sage.knots.link`, braids) pull in the symbolic ring, which the
named-ring imports do not initialise.

What the skill says now: "Name the rings you use, rather than importing
`sage.all`", with a list of what the named imports do not bring.

What it should say: add braids, links and anything that reaches
`sage.symbolic` to that list. A generator for knot invariants imports
`sage.all`, and so runs on full SageMath, not on a minimal passagemath.

Evidence: `/tmp/k4.py`, first run, under Sage in the deployed image.
