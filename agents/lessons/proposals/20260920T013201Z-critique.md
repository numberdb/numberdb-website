## Count distinct values, not rows, when a family has a symmetry

What happened: T293 holds the regulator of each positive-rank elliptic curve
over the six real quadratic fields of smallest discriminant, out to conductor
norm 250. That is 1171 entries, 97.6% of the 1200-entry soft limit, and the
range had to be cut to fit: the same source gives 1213 entries one step
further out, at conductor norm 255.

Those 1171 rows hold **569 distinct numbers**. 1093 of the rows, 93% of them,
share their value with another row. The cause is Galois conjugation: a curve
and its conjugate over a real quadratic field have the same regulator, and the
table is keyed by the curve, so both are stored. Per discriminant the ratio is
the same throughout -- 17 rows and 10 distinct values for $D=5$, 348 and 177
for $D=21$.

Nothing about that is wrong, and the table says so in a comment. But it means
the entry budget bought half as many searchable numbers as it looks like, and
it is *why* the soft limit arrives at conductor norm 250 rather than somewhere
near 500. A contributor choosing the range from the row count alone -- the
obvious thing to do, and what the `complete-note` there records -- never sees
this, and would make the same choice again one field later without knowing
what it costs.

The same doubling is waiting in any family with an involution on the objects
rather than on the values: curves over a number field indexed by curve, ideals
indexed by ideal rather than by ideal class, quadratic forms under equivalence,
anything stored per embedding. It is not the `repeats:` case, which is about
two tables; this is one table repeating itself.

What the skill says now: nothing about duplication inside a table. Section 3
sizes a range by entry count, block bytes and the largest entry, and section 2
warns off the opposite failure -- "values that are a handful of small integers
... match everything and tell nobody anything". Between those two there is no
advice for a family whose values are perfectly distinctive and whose *rows* are
two-to-one onto them.

What it should say: in section 3, beside the entry limits, one line --

> Count the distinct values as well as the rows. A family with a symmetry on
> its objects that fixes the quantity -- Galois conjugation, an involution on
> the index, two embeddings -- stores two rows per number, and the entry limit
> arrives at half the range you expected. Storing both is usually right, since
> a reader holding the number wants to be told about both objects; but measure
> it, say it in a comment, and let it be part of why the range stops where it
> does rather than a surprise at the limit.

Could a contributor with a laptop and Sage hit this? Yes. It is a property of
the family and the source, visible in three lines against the document:
`Counter(row['number'] for row in rows)`.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T293 growth critique. `numberdb.table('T293')` through
`clients/python`: 1171 entries, 278,879 bytes in the entries block, 569
distinct values, 1093 rows sharing a value. Per-discriminant distinct counts
10/33/126/93/130/177 against row counts 17/63/253/195/295/348. Source counts
recomputed from ecnf-data at commit
`10b28418e80392032b106ea00e6c5aa109d28e7b`: 1171 positive-rank rows at
conductor norm $\leq 250$ over $D\in\{5,8,12,13,17,21\}$, 1195 at $\leq 254$,
1213 at $\leq 255$. Written up in `agents/critiques/T293-growth.md`.
