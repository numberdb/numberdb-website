## A definition that allows signs must pick one representative of each identity

What happened: T280 stores primitive solutions of
$a_1^k+\cdots+a_{k-1}^k=b^k$, and allows negative summands so that it can
hold the Scher–Seidl and Braun fifth-power rows. With signs allowed, moving
terms across the equals sign turns one identity into several solutions with
different $b$. The Braun row $(-1340632)^5+719115^5+1331622^5+1956213^5=1956878^5$
is also $(-1331622)^5+(-719115)^5+1340632^5+1956878^5=1956213^5$. Signs also
admit trivial solutions such as $1^5+(-1)^5+0^5+2^5=2^5$. The generator
refused zero summands and cancelling pairs, and it took $b$ to be the largest
term. The definition said none of this, so it described infinitely many rows
that the table did not hold.

What the skill says now: "Which quantity, exactly" and "what constraint they
actually satisfy". Neither says anything about equations whose solutions can
be rearranged.

What it should say: when the definition of a Diophantine family allows signed
variables, list the rearrangements and trivial solutions that the signs
admit, and write the condition that picks one of them (here $b>|a_i|$, no
zero terms, no cancelling pairs) into the definition, not only into the
generator. Test the condition on a rearranged copy of a stored row: it should
be rejected.

Evidence: `agents/critiques/T280.md` finding 1; exact check
`1956878**5+1340632**5-719115**5-1331622**5==1956213**5` is `True`,
2026-09-16.

## Resolve every DOI and read the title that comes back

What happened: T280's two DOIs were wrong in different ways. The one for
Elkies 1988 (`…-0930229-3`) returns 404 from `doi.org`. The one for Frye 1988
(`10.1109/SUPERC.1988.74142`) resolves to a different paper in the same
proceedings, Stampen and Davis on congressional coalitions. The right DOIs
differ in a few digits (`…-0930224-9`, `…74138`). Nothing in the build
checked identifiers, and the page rendered both as working "(doi)" links.

What the skill says now: the References row names `doi` as an identifier
that "renders as a link". It says nothing about checking it.

What it should say: before storing a `doi`, fetch
`https://doi.org/<doi>` with `Accept: application/vnd.citationstyles.csl+json`
and compare the returned title, authors and pages with the `bib`. A DOI one
digit off is usually another paper in the same volume, and it still resolves.

Evidence: `curl -L -H 'Accept: application/vnd.citationstyles.csl+json'
https://doi.org/10.1109/SUPERC.1988.74142` gives the title "Multi-issue
coalitions in the Congress (voting alignments on supercomputer)",
2026-09-16.
