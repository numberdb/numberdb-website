# Lessons from the ideas run of 2026-09-23T04:28Z (random matrix theory)

Two things this run met that the skill does not cover. Both are true for a
contributor with a laptop and `mpmath`; the deployment findings from the same
run are in `docs/agent-environment.md`.

## Get a distribution's moments from its density, not by parts from its CDF

What happened: the batch rests on one check, that an independent integration
of Painlevé II reproduces the published Tracy–Widom constants. The first two
attempts said it did not, and both were the check being computed wrongly
rather than the values being wrong.

The integration gives the CDF $F(s) = e^{-J(s)}$ directly, so the obvious
route to the mean is integration by parts,

    E[X] = int_0^inf (1 - F) ds  -  int_{-inf}^0 F ds

which needs no density. It lost eleven of the twelve digits the ODE had
earned. Composite Simpson on the ODE's own grid gave $-1.768664$ against the
published $-1.771086807411$; an adaptive quadrature over a lookup into the
same grid gave $-1.771049$. Both are three to five digits from a solution
carrying twelve.

The reason is that the by-parts integrands are $1 - F$ on the right and $F$
on the left, and both are **differences of nearly equal numbers** over most of
the range: at $s = 8$, $1 - F_2 \approx 6.5\times10^{-17}$ computed as $1$
minus $0.99999999999999993$. The moment is an integral of catastrophic
cancellation, and no amount of working precision in the ODE reaches it,
because the cancellation happens after.

The density was available the whole time and costs nothing. With
$I' = -q^2$ and $J' = -I$ carried alongside the ODE, $F = e^{-J}$ and

    f = I * exp(-J)

is one multiplication per grid point, positive everywhere, and every moment is
then $\int s^k f\,ds$ with no cancellation at all. Same grid, same step, same
precision: mean $-1.77108680852$ against $-1.771086807411$, variance
$0.81319479479$ against $0.813194792815$, skewness $0.22408419845$ against
$0.224084203610$, excess kurtosis $0.09344809451$ against $0.093448087600$.
Nine, eight, eight and eight digits, up from three.

What the skill says now: nothing about this. Section 7 is about how well the
digits are known and what rigour level to claim, and would have been satisfied
by all three of these runs — the ODE's own error estimate is the same in each.
The skill's nearest sentence is that division in a generator is where
exactness goes; this is the same shape of mistake one step later, in the
*check* rather than in the value, and a check that silently loses eight digits
is worse than a wrong value, because it condemns a correct table.

What it should say: when the quantity to be checked is a moment, a cumulant or
an expectation of a distribution the generator computes, integrate the
**density**. A CDF reaches $0$ and $1$, so $F$ and $1 - F$ each lose every
digit at one end of the range, and integration by parts puts both of those
ends inside the integrand. If only a CDF is available, difference it before
integrating rather than integrating it by parts — and either way, print
$\int f\,ds$ and check it against $1$ before believing any moment. That
diagnostic is one line, it came out $1 - 4.0\times10^{-14}$ on the run that
worked and would have failed loudly on both runs that did not.

Evidence: 2026-09-23 ideas run, `/tmp/tw_check.py`, `/tmp/tw_check2.py`,
`/tmp/tw_check3.py`, each run through `agents/sage.sh`. Same ODE
($q'' = sq + 2q^3$, RK4 in `mpmath` at 40 digits, step $1/400$, started from
$q = \operatorname{Ai}$ at $s = 10$), three ways of taking the mean:
by-parts with adaptive quadrature $-1.771049$, by-parts with Simpson
$-1.768664$, density with Simpson $-1.77108680852426$.

## `source_names_it` matches lowercased substrings, so an accent or a plural fails a real name

What happened: four of this run's `source_names_it` calls failed on names that
are exactly what the family is called, and every one of the four was spelling
rather than mathematics.

    "Hastings-McLeod solution of Painleve II" vs DLMF 32.11
        -> the source does not mention painleve
    "cumulants of the Tracy-Widom distributions" vs Wikipedia
        -> the source does not mention cumulants
    "Level spacing distributions of the Gaussian ensembles" vs Wikipedia
        -> the source does not mention distributions
    "nearest-neighbour spacing distribution" vs Wikipedia
        -> the source does not mention nearest, neighbour

The first is an accent: DLMF writes "Painlevé", and `painleve` is not a
substring of `painlevé`. The second and third are plurals: the check is
`word not in text` against the whole page as one lowercased string, so
`cumulants` fails a page that says "cumulant" and `distributions` fails a page
that says "distribution" — and the singular would have passed both, since
`distribution` *is* a substring of `distributions`. The fourth is a spelling
the page does not use.

The failures read identically to the one the check exists for, a family
nobody has heard of. "The source does not mention painleve" against the DLMF
chapter on Painlevé transcendents is not a signal about the family at all.

What the skill says now: nothing — `source_names_it` is described in the
ideation prompt, not in the skill, but the underlying habit is the skill's:
cite a source and confirm it names the family. The existing environment note
covers the neighbouring case, a real name a page genuinely words differently
("covering density" against pages that say "least dense"). This is narrower
and mechanical: the name and the page agree, and the string comparison does
not.

What it should say: before concluding from a `source_names_it` failure that a
name is wrong, retry the failing word in the singular and with the accents the
source uses. Write the name the way the source writes it — "Painlevé", not
"Painleve" — because that is also how the table's own title should read, and
the site's text index has no `unaccent` either, so the accented title needs
the unaccented spelling in `Keywords` regardless. A failure that survives both
retries is a real one and belongs in the proposal.

Evidence: 2026-09-23 ideas run. `source_names_it("Painlevé transcendents",
"https://dlmf.nist.gov/32.11")` → `None`, where the same call spelled
`Painleve` → `does not mention painleve`.
`source_names_it("level spacing distribution Gaussian ensembles",
"https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_unitary_ensemble")` → `None`, where
`distributions` on the Wigner surmise page → `does not mention distributions`.
