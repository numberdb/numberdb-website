## Look for rows that are secretly rational before publishing a family of reals

What happened: T241 stores 626 differential entropies as 100-digit decimals.
Its generator had already written two rows exactly, Gamma$(1,1)=1$ and
Beta$(1,1)=0$. A comment said the exact-integer rows had been left out.
Eleven more rows are exact all the same:

- every $F(2,m)$ entropy is $1+2/m$ nats
- the unit-variance hyperbolic secant is $\log 4$ nats, which is exactly
  $2$ bits

They are stored as `3.000…`, `1.666…67` and `2.000…`. The closed form in
the generator is a combination of $\log B$ and digamma values, so nothing
about it looked rational. The author followed the skill's rule and still
did not see them, because the rule needs an argument and nobody knew
there was one to look for.

What the skill says now: "Decide exactness from the definition, never from
the digits", and "Surds cancel more often than one expects".

What it should say: the digits cannot *decide* exactness, but they are a
cheap way to find where to look. Before publishing, compare every stored
value with its nearest fraction of small denominator, to most of its digits:

    from fractions import Fraction
    from decimal import Decimal, getcontext
    getcontext().prec = 120
    f = Fraction(Decimal(text)).limit_denominator(1000)
    suspicious = abs(Decimal(f.numerator) / f.denominator - Decimal(text)) < Decimal('1e-90')

Each hit is a claim to prove, or to explain as a coincidence. A proof makes
the row exact and usually yields a formula worth putting on the page:
$Y=1+2X/m$ turns $F(2,m)$ into a Pareto variable. Convert units too before
running the check: a value that is irrational in nats can be an integer in
bits.

Evidence: T241 critique, 2026-09-14. Of 626 values, 11 matched a fraction of
denominator at most 1000 to $10^{-90}$: $F(2,m)$ for $m=1,\dots,10$, and the
unit-variance hyperbolic secant in bits. The $F(2,m)$ identity was proved by
hand, and it agrees with all ten stored rows.
