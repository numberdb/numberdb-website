# Lessons from the T347 critique, 2026-09-20

## An independent check must include a case where the convention you chose is wrong

What happened: T347 stores $W_n(P)=\psi_n(P)$, values of division polynomials
at a point. There are two things called the $n$-th division polynomial: the
full $\psi_n\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]$, and the $x$-only polynomial
$\psi_n/(2y+a_1x+a_3)$, which is what Sage's `division_polynomial` returns by
default and what PARI's `elldivpol` returns. They differ for even $n$ by a
factor of $\psi_2(P)$.

The table's `rigour details` reports an independent check: the first terms for
37a1, 43a1 and 53a1 against OEIS A006769, A050512 and A178627. All three of
those curves have $\psi_2(P)=1$ at the stored point, so the two conventions
produce **identical sequences** on all three. The `Programs` snippet computes
37a1, for the same reason. The table also holds 57a1 and 58a1, where
$\psi_2(P)$ is 3 and 2, and there the two readings diverge from $n=2$:

    57a1, P=(2,1)   stored   1, 3, 9,  81, 1458, 19683
                    default  1, 9, 9, 243, 1458, 59049
    58a1, P=(0,1)   stored   1, 2, -4, -16, -64, 1024
                    default  1, 4, -4, -32, -64, 2048

The stored values are right. But every check the table reports, and the program
it hands a reader, was drawn from the subfamily on which the choice is
invisible. A wrong choice would have passed all of them.

This is a degenerate-control problem, and the corpus has met its cousin twice:
the Chebyshev control that returned zero for everything because of a coercion
error, and the nan ball that overlapped every interval. Those are controls that
confirm nothing because they answer the same way whatever happens. This is a
control that confirms nothing because the *inputs* were chosen from the locus
where the two candidate answers coincide. It is harder to see, because the
check is sound and the numbers really do agree.

What the skill says now: section 10 says "Check new values against something
independent", "A measurement needs a control that returns a known answer", and
"Run the control first and check it gives the answer you already know". All
three are about the check being capable of failing in general. None asks
whether the *particular arguments* discriminate between the conventions the
table had to choose between in section 2.

What it should say: when section 2 made you choose a normalisation, branch or
indexing, the check in section 10 has to include an argument at which the
choice you did not make gives a different answer. Find one before you pick the
sample. If every value you cross-checked would be unchanged by the other
convention, you have verified arithmetic and not the convention — and the
convention is the thing section 2 says goes wrong. A one-line sanity test:
compute the rejected convention on your sample too, and if it agrees
everywhere, extend the sample until it does not.

The same rule pays twice, because the sample that discriminates is also the
example worth putting in `Programs`: a snippet computed on a curve where the
conventions coincide cannot show a reader which one the table means.

Evidence: 2026-09-20, T347 critique. `/tmp/t347_check2.py` under
`agents/sage.sh` gives the table above; `/tmp/t347_check3.py` shows the table's
own Formula (3), $x(nP)=x(P)-W_{n-1}W_{n+1}/W_n^2$, is false under the rejected
convention on both discriminating curves ($x(2P)=17/9$ on 57a1 and $1/4$ on
58a1, against the true 1) and true on the three where they coincide. T341, the
sibling table that stores the polynomials rather than their values, states the
distinction in its Comment (7) and cites Sage's documentation for it; T347
inherited that Link, uncited, without the sentence.
