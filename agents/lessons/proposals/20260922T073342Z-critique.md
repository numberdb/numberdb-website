## A `Programs` snippet written as the whole table has to repeat the generator's exclusions, and silently will not

What happened: T9 carries, as its entire `Programs` section, a comprehension
that looks like the generator:

    numbers = {a/b: gamma(RIF(a/b))
                    for b,a in cartesian_product(([1..30],[-30..30]))
                    if gcd(a,b) == 1}

The attached `generate.sage` has one more line that this does not:

    if b == 1 and a <= 0:
        continue

Those are the poles of $\Gamma$. Without the line the comprehension enumerates
1111 pairs against the table's 1080, and at the 31 extra ones it does not
raise: `RIF(0).gamma()` returns `[-infinity .. +infinity]`. So a reader who
copies the snippet gets a dict with 31 more keys than the page shows, each
holding an interval that contains every real number, and nothing anywhere says
so. The second half of the same problem is precision: `RIF` is the 53-bit
interval field, about 16 decimal digits, and the table holds 100 --
`RIF(1/3).gamma()` prints `2.678938534707748?` against a stored value forty
times longer. A reader who ran this to check a row could not have checked more
than a sixth of it.

Nothing in the table was wrong. The values are right (the reflection formula
holds on 387 stored pairs with 94 digits of room, and a value moved ten units
in its last place is rejected). What was wrong was the one piece of code on
the page a reader is invited to run.

What the skill says now: "**`Programs` and `generate.py` answer different
questions.** `Programs` is the standard incantation in Sage, PARI or mpmath
for a reader who wants one more value. `generate.py` is the program that
reproduces and extends *this* table, attached to it." That states the
distinction, and it is right, but it reads as a matter of taste -- two homes
for two things -- rather than as a thing that goes wrong.

What it should say: add the failure mode to that paragraph. A snippet that
enumerates the family instead of evaluating one argument is a second,
unmaintained copy of the generator, and it is wrong in two ways that nobody
sees:

- **It has to restate every exclusion the generator makes, and a missing one
  does not announce itself.** An interval or ball evaluated at a pole comes
  back as the whole line, not as an error, so the copy produces entries the
  table does not have and calls them computed. (Same shape as the
  infinite-radius ball in `20260922T064051Z-ideas.md`; there it is arb's
  `rgamma` prefactor and here it is the argument grid, and in both cases
  `rigour: proven` has nothing to say about a value whose error is infinite.)
- **It has to restate the working precision, and the default is never the
  table's.** `RIF` and `RealBallField()` with no argument are 53 bits. A table
  holding 100 digits needs its field named in the snippet or the reader
  reproduces sixteen of them.

Write the incantation for one argument instead, at the table's own precision,
and let the attached generator be the thing that reproduces the table:

    sage: R = RealIntervalField(680)      # 100 digits, with room
    sage: (R(7)/R(11)).gamma()

Then there is one range in the document rather than two, and the one that can
drift out of date is the one nobody runs.

Worth saying because no check catches it: `audit_table` looks at a `Programs`
snippet only for a literal `[0..N]` or `range(0, N)` to compare against the
entry count. T9's is written `[1..30]` and `[-30..30]`, so the audit is clean
and the 31-entry discrepancy is unreported.
