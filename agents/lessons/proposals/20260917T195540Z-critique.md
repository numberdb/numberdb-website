# Proposed from the T312 critique, 2026-09-17

## A Laurent polynomial in a `Z[]` table is a shifted representative, and the header has to say by what

What happened: T312 holds the HOMFLY-PT polynomials of 250 knots, in two
normalisations, as 958 `Z[]` entries. A HOMFLY-PT polynomial is a *Laurent*
polynomial -- Sage hands back `v^2*z^2 + 2*v^2 - v^4` for the trefoil and
`v^-2*z^2 + 2*v^-2 - v^-4` for its mirror image -- and a `Z[]` table cannot
hold a negative exponent: the server parses a polynomial with
`SR(s).polynomial(QQ)` (`utils/utils.py`, `parse_polynomial`), which has no
Laurent ring behind it, so `v^-4 + 1` is not a value. Everybody storing such a
family therefore multiplies by a monomial and stores a representative, and
then has to put the removed exponent somewhere. It is not an exotic case: four
tables in this corpus are knot polynomials, and three of them are Laurent
families.

T312 stores the representative whose lowest exponent in each variable is zero,
heads the value column `shifted HOMFLY-PT polynomial`, and writes the removed
exponent as the entry comment `a=2`. Both of those are read before the
Definition, because the numbers block is drawn first, and neither says what
was removed. The three sibling tables built earlier head the same column with
a symbol that carries the shift: `$t^{-m}V_K(t)$`, `$\Delta_{n_k}(t)$`,
`$\nabla_{n_k}(z)$`. A reader arriving with Sage's own output of the invariant
-- which is the case NumberDB exists to answer -- has a polynomial that
matches no row until they know what the shift is and which representative was
chosen.

What the skill says now: nothing. It does not contain the word "Laurent", and
its `Z[]` line shows `x^2 - x - 1`, an ordinary polynomial. It does say that
`number-header` is a claim about every value under it, with T174's
$\lambda_n$-headed-$|\lambda_n|$ as the example, but not that a Laurent family
makes that claim unavoidable, and not that the shift is part of the value.

What it should say: a `Z[]` or `Q[]` table cannot hold a negative exponent, so
a Laurent family is stored as a monomial multiple. Say which multiple in the
definition and name it in `number-header`, as `$t^{-m}V_K(t)$` rather than
"shifted Jones polynomial", so that the column header and the definition agree
on what the table holds; and record the removed exponent in the entry as
mathematics, `$m=1$`, not as `m=1`. Pick the representative by a rule that a
reader can apply to their own polynomial -- lowest exponent zero in each
variable is the obvious one, and it is what T312 does on all 958 entries --
and state the rule rather than the fact that a rule was used.

Evidence: 2026-09-17, T312 critique. `GET /api/table?id=T312` with the owner's
key; `Display properties.number-header` is `"shifted HOMFLY-PT polynomial"`
and 822 of 958 entries carry a comment of the form `a=-8`. Parsing every
stored entry shows the lowest exponent is zero in both variables in all 958,
so the rule is real and unstated. The published
`Jones_polynomials_of_the_prime_knots_with_at_most_ten_crossings`,
`Alexander_...` and `Conway_...` head the same column with a symbol; their
documents are readable anonymously through `/api/table?url=<slug>`.
