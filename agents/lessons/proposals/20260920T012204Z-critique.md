## A range chosen to fit the limit can exclude every instance of the definition's general case

What happened: T293 holds the regulator $R_{E/K}=\det(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle)$
of positive-rank elliptic curves over six real quadratic fields, cut off at
conductor norm $250$ because $N\leq255$ would give 1213 entries and breach the
1200-entry soft limit. Of its 1171 rows, 1163 have rank 1 and 8 have rank 2.
In a rank-1 row the determinant in the definition is a $1\times1$ determinant:
the regulator is a single height, and the pairing, the basis-independence and
the determinant are all unexercised. The whole of what makes the definition a
determinant rests on eight rows, and those eight are there by luck — the
smallest conductor norm carrying a rank-2 curve is $235$ over $D=21$, against
$478$, $637$, $814$, $1031$ and $1831$ over the other five fields. A cutoff of
$230$, or the obvious "equal depth per field" reshape (which needs per-field
cutoffs of 655, 361, 213, 244, 188, 153, every one below its own field's first
rank-2 curve), would have left a table of 1171 rows in which the general case
occurs nowhere.

This is not about elliptic curves. Any definition with a free integer in it —
a rank $r$, a dimension $n$, a degree $d$, a number of variables — has a
degenerate case that is overwhelmingly the commonest one, and a range chosen
by size lands in it. Size and the general case are uncorrelated, so the range
that fits is not the range that instantiates.

What the skill says now: §3 chooses ranges by what fits and by what a reader
is likely to meet ("include what is common, and stop"; "let the arguments
choose it rather than the count"), and §10 checks *values* against something
independent. Neither asks whether the chosen range contains any instance of
the definition's general case. The nearest thing is the warning that 78 of 89
Hecke polynomials were $(x-a)^2$ — which is presented as a reason the values
are uninformative, not as a reason the definition goes unexercised.

What it should say: add to §3, after "let the arguments choose it rather than
the count": *once the range is chosen, check it against the definition rather
than only against the limits. Where the definition has a free integer in it —
a rank, a dimension, a degree — count how many entries fall outside its
smallest case. A table of $r\times r$ determinants in which every row has $r=1$
holds no determinant, and a reader cannot tell the convention was even chosen.
If the count is zero, the range is wrong however well it fits; if it is small,
say in `complete-note` where the general case begins, because that is the
sentence that makes the cutoff an argument about the mathematics rather than
about the entry limit.*
