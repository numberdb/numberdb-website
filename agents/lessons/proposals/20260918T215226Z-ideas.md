# Lessons from the ideas run of 2026-09-18T2152 (information theory batch)

## Search the corpus by value at proposal time, not only by name

What happened: the batch proposed a table of run-length-limited capacities,
whose values are $\log_2\lambda(d,k)$ for the Perron root $\lambda$ of a
constraint graph. Searching the corpus for the *name* found nothing: "capacity"
returns two cuckoo-hashing tables, "run-length" returns sixty tables matched on
the words "run" and "length" separately, "subshift" returns nothing. Searching
for the *numbers* found three real relations in one minute:

    numberdb.search_real_ball('1.3247179572', 1e-9)
      -> Pisot numbers less than the golden ratio        (T286)
      -> Growth rates of hyperbolic Coxeter triangle groups (T222)
    numberdb.search_real_ball('1.4655712318', 1e-9)      same two tables
    numberdb.search_real_ball('0.4812118250', 1e-9)      # log of the same root
      -> Regulators of real quadratic fields             (T131)
      -> Entropy constants of lattice models             (T153)
      -> Periodic windows of the logistic map            (T169)

The smallest RLL Perron roots are Pisot numbers the corpus already holds, and
the golden-mean shift's entropy is stored three times over, as the regulator of
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$, as a lattice entropy constant and as a logistic-map
window. None of those tables shares a word with "run-length limited", so no
name search could have reached them, and the screen's `already_here`, which
searches names, reported nothing.

What the skill says now: "Ask what the family is a specialisation or a multiple
of, and search for that too" -- and every worked example there is a search on a
*name* ("nothing in polygamma points at Hurwitz").

What it should say: compute three or four of the values you intend to store,
before writing the proposal, and search for those with `search_real_ball`,
`search_rational`, `search_integer` or `search_polynomial`. It is the only
check that crosses subjects: a name search can only find a table that shares a
word, and the relations worth knowing about are the ones between families that
do not. It costs one script and about a minute, and what it returns goes
straight into the proposal's `Similar tables` and its `equals` links.

Evidence: 2026-09-18 ideas run, `/tmp/valsearch5.py` and `/tmp/valsearch6.py`;
the capacities themselves from `/tmp/rll.py` under `agents/sage.sh`.

## `search_real_ball` fills `.total` and the list, and leaves `.tables` empty

What happened: the value searches above looked empty at first. The result object
prints nothing useful, `result.tables` is `[]`, and the documented gotcha for
`search_text` -- "the result is an object with a `.tables` list, not a list" --
teaches you to look at exactly that attribute. For a *number* search the hits
are the object itself: `len(result)`, iteration, and `result.total`.

    r = numberdb.search_real_ball('1.3247179572', 1e-9)
    r.tables      -> []            # reads exactly like "nothing in the corpus"
    r.total       -> 2
    list(r)       -> [Result('1.32471795724...', table='Growth rates of ...'),
                      Result('1.32471795724...', table='Pisot numbers less ...')]

So the two searches in the client want opposite habits, and the habit the skill
teaches for one silently reports an empty corpus for the other.

What the skill says now: the `.tables` gotcha, for `search_text` only.

What it should say: the same sentence, plus its mirror. A text search answers
in `.tables`; a value search answers in the object, with `.total` beside it and
each hit's table on `hit.table`. If you want the tables a value is in, read
them off the hits.

Evidence: 2026-09-18 ideas run, `/tmp/valsearch4.py` (`total=2 tables=[]` for
five different values) and `/tmp/valsearch5.py` (the same five, iterated).

## A logarithm is not a multiple, so both units have to be stored

What happened: the batch's four tables all hold expected logarithms, which the
field quotes in two units. Checking whether the bits form was already covered
by the nats form:

    0.4812118250596034   = log(phi)     -> in the corpus three times
    0.6942419136306173   = log2(phi)    -> nowhere

The two differ by the factor $1/\log 2 = 1.4427$. The skill's test for whether
a family is already covered is whether a reader can get from the number in
their hand to the stored one by trying "a half, a two, a pi"; $1/\log2$ is not
on that list, and neither is any other change of base.

What the skill says now: "two conventions of one object -> one table", with
$E_1$ and $\operatorname{Ei}$, and $\beta$ and $e^\beta$, as the examples.

What it should say: name the change of logarithmic base as the third standard
example, and say that for a quantity defined as a logarithm -- an entropy, a
capacity, a growth rate, a regulator -- the units a reader quotes are part of
the convention and both belong in the table, as T241 already does with its
`unit: nats | bits` parameter and T153 does with $\kappa$ and $h=\ln\kappa$.
The related trap is the same fact from the other side: a family of logarithms
of numbers the corpus already holds is *not* covered by those numbers, because
the number search cannot follow a logarithm any more than a reader can.

Evidence: 2026-09-18 ideas run, `/tmp/valsearch4.py` and `/tmp/valsearch6.py`;
T241's `Parameters['unit']`, T153's `expression` parameter, T172's
`normalisation` parameter.
