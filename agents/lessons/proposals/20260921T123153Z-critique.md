# Lessons proposed by the T382 critique, 2026-09-21

## Polynomial search normalises variable names, and caps at six variables

What happened: the T382 critique had to answer whether a reader holding one of
the table's discriminants would actually find it. The table stores its
polynomials in `a0, a1, ...`; a reader holding the depressed cubic
discriminant writes it in $p$ and $q$, or $b_0$ and $b_1$. If search matched
variable names literally, the table's whole purpose would fail for everyone
who names them differently, and the naming convention would be the most
important decision in the table.

It does not. Against the live corpus:

    numberdb.search_polynomial('-c1^3 + 2*c1*c2 - c3')
        -> Result(..., table='Segre classes ... in terms of Chern classes')
    numberdb.search_polynomial('-b1^3 + 2*b1*b2 - b3')
        -> Result('-c1^3 + 2*c1*c2 - c3', table='Segre classes ...')

The second query names no variable the stored entry uses and finds it anyway.
So a multivariate polynomial table may choose whatever variable names read
best; the choice is a matter of the page, not of findability.

There is a limit, and it is on the *number* of variables rather than on the
names. Probing with a monomial of each width:

    5 variables  accepted, 0 results
    6 variables  accepted, 0 results
    7 variables  refused

So a polynomial table indexed by something that grows the variable count --
degree, rank, dimension -- becomes unsearchable past six variables, whatever
its entries weigh. That is a reason to stop a family early that has nothing to
do with the block-size limits the skill already gives, and it is invisible
until somebody tries it.

Two tables in the corpus turn on this and neither could have known it from the
skill: T381 stops at degree 5 because degree 6 would need seven coefficient
variables, and T382 stops at degree 6 for length alone -- its degree-7 entry
would have six variables and would still have been searchable.

What the skill says now: section 3 gives the entry, digit and block limits
from `numberdb_app/limits.py`, and says "the block limit is the one that binds
for polynomials". For a multivariate family that is not true: the variable
count binds first.

What it should say: two sentences beside the limits table. That
`search_polynomial` matches up to relabelling of the variables, so the names
in a polynomial table are a presentation choice and not a findability one. And
that a polynomial in more than six variables cannot be searched, so a family
whose variable count grows with its index should stop at six -- checked with a
query, not assumed, since the limit is not in `limits.py` with the others.

Evidence: 2026-09-21, T382 critique. `/tmp/psearch3.py` and `/tmp/psearch4.py`
run through `agents/sage.sh` against the live corpus; T338 (Segre classes) and
T99 (Chebyshev $U_n$) as the published tables probed. The seven-variable
refusal arrives as an HTTP 500 rather than a message, which is a separate site
bug and is written up in `docs/agent-environment.md`.
