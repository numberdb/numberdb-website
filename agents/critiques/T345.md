### 2. The Definition never says what a kernel polynomial is; the sentence that does renders fifth

The Definition is

> Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be the reduced global minimal model recovered from
> $(c_4,c_6)$ by Formula **(1)**. For a rational isogeny $\varphi:E\to E'$ of
> prime degree $\ell\geq5$, the entry is its monic kernel polynomial
> $K_\varphi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$ **[1]**.

It names the object and sends a reader to an LMFDB knowl for what it is. The
sentence that actually defines it is in `Comments`, and the page draws
`Formulas` before `Comments`, so a reader meets it *fifth*:

    (3)  The source curve is identified by $N$, $c_4$ and $c_6$. …
    (4)  Degree $2$ and degree $3$ rational isogenies are not listed. …
    (5)  The roots of $K_\varphi(x)$ are the $x$-coordinates of the nonzero
         points of $\ker\varphi$.

So the one fact a reader holding `x^2 - 21*x + 80` needs — what the roots of
this polynomial are — arrives after a note about label conventions and a note
about what is absent. (The authored `table.yaml` in this repository has
`comment-roots` first; the served document has it last, so the page's order is
not the one the file suggests.) The cited knowl is also unreadable from this
runner: `https://www.lmfdb.org/knowledge/show/ec.isogeny` answers 200 with a
reCAPTCHA page, the standing note about LMFDB, so I cannot confirm the link
carries the definition a reader is being sent to it for.

This is `Definition` material by the skill's own table — it says what the
object *is*, in one clause — and moving it makes the definition self-contained.

**Smallest fix:** end the Definition "…the entry is its monic kernel
polynomial $K_\varphi(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]$, whose roots are the $x$-coordinates
of the nonzero points of $\ker\varphi$", and delete `comment-roots`.

### 3. Comment (4)'s reason for leaving out degrees 2 and 3 is true of every row that is here

> Degree $2$ and degree $3$ rational isogenies are not listed. Their kernel
> polynomials are linear, so their roots are already $x$-coordinates of torsion
> points rather than genuinely new polynomials.

Comment (5) says the roots of the stored polynomials are the $x$-coordinates of
the nonzero points of $\ker\varphi$, and those points have order $\ell$. So
"their roots are already $x$-coordinates of torsion points" is true of all 118
rows, and a reader who reads (4) and (5) together finds the same property given
as the reason for excluding some rows and, implicitly, for including the rest.
"Genuinely new polynomials" is the other half of the problem: it is a judgement
about what is worth storing on this website rather than a fact about the
mathematics.

The real distinction is available and short: for $\ell=2,3$ the kernel
polynomial is $x-a$ with $a\in\mathbb{Q}$, so the entry would be one rational
number written as a polynomial.

**Smallest fix:** "Degree $2$ and degree $3$ rational isogenies are not listed.
Their kernel polynomials are linear, so each is a single rational
$x$-coordinate written as a polynomial."

### 4. `complete-note` gives no reason for 300, and its database clause reads as a restriction it is not

It renders as

> Table is complete: no (it holds every rational isogeny of prime degree
> $\ell\geq5$ whose source curve lies in Sage's mini Cremona database **[3]**
> with conductor $N\leq300$)

Two separate problems in one sentence.

*The database clause promises less than the table delivers.* Sage's mini
Cremona database has `largest_conductor() = 9999` and 64,687 curves; 1,172 of
them have $N\leq300$, and that is every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ of
conductor at most 300. So "lies in Sage's mini Cremona database … with
conductor $N\leq300$" is exactly "conductor at most 300", and a reader who does
not know the database's range cannot tell whether some curves of small
conductor are missing from it. I checked the coverage claim itself and it holds
exactly: reading every prime degree off the isogeny-class matrix of all 1,172
curves gives $\ell=5$: 82, $\ell=7$: 26, $\ell=11$: 6, $\ell=13$: 4, total
118, which is the entry count.

*300 has no argument behind it.* The skill asks the completeness note to say
which range was chosen and why, because that sentence is what a reader checking
whether their own number belongs here reads. The entries block is 14.8 KB
against the ~160 KB the skill aims at and the longest value is 94 characters,
so nothing about cost or readability explains stopping at 300 — and a reader
holding a kernel polynomial of a conductor-500 curve has no way to tell whether
the table is silent because 500 is out of range or because there is nothing
there.

**Smallest fix:** "it holds every rational isogeny of prime degree $\ell\geq5$
of every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ of conductor $N\leq300$", with a
clause saying why 300 — or raise the bound, which the size figures leave ample
room for.

### 5. Formula (2) names the division polynomial and does not link the table of them

> For prime $\ell\geq5$, $K_\varphi(x)$ has degree $(\ell-1)/2$ and divides the
> division polynomial $\psi_\ell(x)$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$.

The corpus holds them, at
`Division_polynomials_psi_n_of_elliptic_curves_over_mathbb_Q` (200 live), and
this table links it twice elsewhere — in `Similar tables` and inside `rigour
details`. The skill's rule is first mention, once per section, and `Formulas`
is a section where the first mention is bare. The audit misses it for the
reason given above: it matches the whole bared title, and the prose says
"$\psi_\ell(x)$".

**Smallest fix:**
`divides HREF{Division_polynomials_psi_n_of_elliptic_curves_over_mathbb_Q}[the division polynomial] $\psi_\ell(x)$`.

### 6. Nothing says why 34 of the 118 values are not in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$

The first two rows a reader meets are

    11  496, 20008  5  375376, 229985128:  x^2 - 21*x + 80
    11  496, 20008  5  16, -152:           x^2 + x - 29/5

and there is nothing on the page from which to tell whether the second row uses
a different normalisation from the first. It does not. Over all 118 values the
denominator is 1 or exactly $\ell$, never anything else: $\ell=5$ gives 57
integral and 25 with denominator 5, $\ell=7$ gives 17 and 9, $\ell=11$ gives 6
and 0, $\ell=13$ gives 2 and 2.

And the explanation is half-written already, in Formula (2): $\psi_\ell$ has
leading coefficient $\ell$, so a monic factor of it can have denominators
dividing $\ell$. One clause turns a reader's "is this row inconsistent?" into
a fact about the mathematics.

**Smallest fix:** extend (2) — "…divides the division polynomial
$\psi_\ell(x)$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$, which has leading coefficient $\ell$, so a
monic kernel polynomial need not have integer coefficients."

### 7. $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is called a model, and then an isogeny is taken of it

"Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be the reduced global minimal model recovered from
$(c_4,c_6)$" makes $E$ an equation; the next clause writes "a rational isogeny
$\varphi:E\to E'$", which is a map of curves. The sibling has the sentence
right, and the fix is its five words: "Let $E/\mathbb{Q}$ be **the elliptic
curve with** reduced global minimal Weierstrass model recovered from
$(c_4,c_6)$…" (T341, verbatim). It matters slightly more here than it reads,
because the model is exactly what makes $x$ a coordinate and the kernel
polynomial a specific polynomial rather than one up to change of variables.

### 8. `rigour details` hides its own independent check

> …and has a target curve whose $(c_4,c_6)$ invariants are the target
> parameters.

That reads as a self-consistency check on Sage's own output. What
`generate.py` actually does is recompute the codomain with PARI's
`ellisogeny` from the stored kernel polynomial and compare invariants — a
second library, from the stored value, which is the strongest evidence this
table has and the thing a reader judging `exact` wants to see. T341's rigour
details names PARI explicitly for the same reason. (The generator also raises
if two isogenies of one curve share $(c'_4,c'_6)$, which is the claim comment
(3) makes; that check is not mentioned either.)

**Smallest fix:** "…and, from the stored kernel polynomial alone, that PARI/GP's
`ellisogeny` returns a curve whose $(c_4,c_6)$ are the target parameters",
with `CITE{}` on a PARI link.

### 9. The attached generator enumerates $\ell$ from a fixed list that is missing three degrees

`generate.py`, which a reader downloads from `/files/T345/generate.py`, has

    PRIME_DEGREES = tuple(ZZ(p) for p in (5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 37))

Over $\mathbb{Q}$ the prime degrees a rational isogeny can have are
2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 37, **43, 67, 163** (Mazur, Kenku). Nothing is lost
at $N\leq300$ — I read every degree off the isogeny-class matrices instead of a
list and got exactly 5, 7, 11, 13, matching all 118 rows — so this is not a
fault in any value. It is a fault in the file: the next person who raises
`CONDUCTOR_BOUND` inherits a loop that cannot find a 43-, 67- or 163-isogeny,
while the table's own `complete-note` promises "every rational isogeny of prime
degree $\ell\geq5$".

**Smallest fix:** `source.isogenies_prime_degree()` with no argument, and keep
the $\ell\geq5$ filter; or add 43, 67, 163 to the tuple with the theorem that
closes the list named in a comment.

### 10. The keyword "Velu isogeny" promises something the page does not contain

`Keywords` carries `Velu isogeny` (unaccented, which is right — the index has
no `unaccent`). Vélu is named nowhere in the table: not in the definition, not
in a comment, not in a formula, not in `References`, which does not exist here.
Somebody who searches for Vélu is looking for the formulas that turn a kernel
polynomial into the isogeny, arrives on the one table in the corpus that stores
those kernel polynomials, and finds no mention of them.

**Smallest fix:** either drop the keyword, or earn it with a sentence — the
kernel polynomial is the input to Vélu's formulas for $\varphi$ and $E'$ — and
a reference for them.

## What I checked hardest, and what came back clean

*The Definition rebuilds the table.* This is the check the skill's
"precisely enough that two people would build the same table" asks for, and it
is the reason I would not hold this table back over any finding above. I
implemented Formula (1) from the rendered page — not from `generate.py` — and
for each of the 118 rows recovered the model from $(c_4,c_6)$, confirmed its
conductor is the row's $N$, enumerated the $\ell$-isogenies, picked the one
whose minimal codomain has the row's $(c'_4,c'_6)$ and compared kernel
polynomials, and checked the source Cremona label in the entry comment against
`cremona_label()` of the recovered model. **118 rows rebuilt, 0
disagreements.** Formula (1) is also well posed: the six admissible $b_2$ are
distinct mod 12 and $-c_6\equiv b_2^3\equiv b_2$ there, so the recovery is
unique, and $b_4$, $b_6$ came out integral on every row.

*The completeness claim is exactly true.* 118 of 118, by a route that does not
share the generator's list of degrees (see finding 9).

*The page renders whole.* 200, 127,889 bytes, all 118 rows present with 118
unique anchors (`11,496,20008,5,375376,229985128` …). All 41 distinct `$…$`
fragments typeset through `mathjax-full@3` with **no errors**, the deliberately
broken control fragment failing as it should. No `CITE{` or `HREF{` leaking,
no "Math input error", no `argument ()`, no `href="None"`, no `<` or `>` and no
`--` anywhere in the document, no odd `$` count in any field. `Formula
CITE{formula-model-from-invariants}` renders "Formula (1)"; the `rigour
details` fold works; `Programs` keeps its indentation inside `<pre><code>`. All
four `Similar tables` slugs answer 200 live and all eight `Links` URLs answer
200 (two LMFDB ones with a reCAPTCHA body, per finding 2).

*A reader arriving with one of these numbers is served.* `api/lookup` with
`{"kind":"polynomial","value":"x^2-21*x+80"}` answers T345 and nothing else,
and with `x^2+x-1` answers T146, T217 and T345. The title carries the words
somebody holding one of these would type. Entry order is by conductor and then
by Cremona class and curve number — 11a1, 11a2, 11a3 at the top, 294b2 at the
bottom — so the small cases lead, which is what the skill asks and what the
batch's convention 5 specified.

*It is one table.* One quantity, `$K_\varphi(x)$` at the head of its column,
six integer parameters none of which names a choice of quantity. The
$(N,c_4,c_6)$ naming of a curve and the $(c'_4,c'_6)$ naming of the target are
the skill's "parts of one object" case, and the target pair is load-bearing:
it is what distinguishes the two rows a curve with two $\ell$-isogenies has.
