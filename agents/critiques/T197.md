## Findings, ranked

### 1. Six functions wearing one title

The page holds $\operatorname{erf}$, $\operatorname{erfc}$,
$\operatorname{erfi}$, Dawson's integral $F$, and the Fresnel integrals
$S$ and $C$, selected by a parameter called `function`. The skill's
rule is stated on this exact case: a table holding
$\operatorname{Ei}$, $\operatorname{li}$, $\operatorname{Si}$,
$\operatorname{Ci}$, $\operatorname{Shi}$ and $\operatorname{Chi}$
together "is six tables wearing one title", and "seven functions
evaluated at the same rational $x$ are still seven functions". The same
batch proposed those seven as one table (proposal 5 of
`BATCH-2026-09-09T1936`) and they were built as T188, T192, T193, T194
and T195, one function per title. Proposal 4, this table, kept its six
in one.

What a reader sees as a result:

* The definition, and every word saying what $S$ and $C$ are, comes
  after 360 rows. A reader holding $C(1)=0.7798934\ldots$ meets it on
  row 312; the word "Fresnel" is on the first screen only in the title.
* The parameter list reads "function — function": the key, its title
  and its display are the same word, and the line tells a reader
  nothing.
* Three of the five function glosses under Similar tables ("in place of
  the Fresnel integrals' quadratic phase", "the corresponding hyperbolic
  integral") are about a third of the table, and the Gamma link is about
  another third.
* The title names "the error function" for three of the six columns.

What mitigates it: every name is in the title or in `Keywords`, so
search by name reaches this table for all six, and the six are one DLMF
chapter, which is the proposal's argument for keeping them together.

Smallest change if the table stays as it is: none for search, and
findings 2 to 4 below. If it is split, the T188 precedent
($E_1(x)=-\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$ stored beside $\operatorname{Ei}$)
says $\operatorname{erf}$, $\operatorname{erfc}$ and
$\operatorname{erfi}$ are one object in three conventions and stay in
T197; Dawson's integral is its own table; and the Si/Ci precedent says
$S$ and $C$ are two. The decision has to be made before publication,
because the T-number and the parameter keys freeze then, and it is a
person's decision, not mine. Worth deciding; the rest of this report is
small beside it.

### 2. The definition does not define

A reader sees: "This table gives standard real values of
$\operatorname{erf}(x)$, $\operatorname{erfc}(x)$,
$\operatorname{erfi}(x)$ [1], Dawson's integral $F(x)$ [2], and the
Fresnel integrals $S(x)$ and $C(x)$ [3], for rational $x>0$ [4]."

"Standard" is carrying the whole normalisation decision, which is the
decision the proposal says it exists to record, without saying what it
is; a reader guesses. The definitions themselves are formulas (1) to
(3), and a reader who does not know what $S$ is learns it from a
numbered formula after the parameter list. The fourth citation is
attached to "for rational $x>0$", which DLMF does not say.

Smallest fix: put the definitions in the definition and let (1) to (3)
go or stay as they are: "$\operatorname{erf}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^x
e^{-t^2}\,\mathrm{d}t$ [4], $\operatorname{erfc}=1-\operatorname{erf}$,
$\operatorname{erfi}(x)=-i\operatorname{erf}(ix)$, Dawson's integral
$F(x)=e^{-x^2}\int_0^x e^{t^2}\,\mathrm{d}t$, and the Fresnel integrals
$S(x)=\int_0^x\sin(\pi t^2/2)\,\mathrm{d}t$ and
$C(x)=\int_0^x\cos(\pi t^2/2)\,\mathrm{d}t$, at rational $x>0$." Worth
doing if the table stays as one; if it is split, each new definition
is one function and the problem dissolves.

### 3. Dawson's integral has three names on the page

The title says "Dawson's function", the definition and formula (2) say
"Dawson's integral $F(x)$", and comment (8) says "Dawson's integral
here is the standard $D_+(x)$", a symbol that appears nowhere else. A
reader asks whether $D_+$ is $F$, and "standard" again does the work a
sentence should. Smallest fix, in (8): "Dawson's integral $F(x)$ is the
function written $D_+(x)$ in [2]. The variant
$D_-(x)=e^{x^2}\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,\mathrm{d}t$ is not stored; it is
$(\sqrt\pi/2)e^{x^2}\operatorname{erf}(x)$." And one name in the title
and the body. Worth doing, and small.

### 4. Three keywords promise things the page never says

`Keywords` carries "probability integral", "Cornu spiral" and
"clothoid", the first search weight, and none of the three words occurs
in the rendered page. The facts behind them are short and are the ones a
reader from outside analysis needs. Suggested, as one comment: "The
error function is also called the probability integral; the tail of the
standard normal distribution is
$\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(x/\sqrt2)$ [1]. The curve $t\mapsto(C(t),S(t))$
is the Cornu spiral, or clothoid [3]." The tail relation is the
sentence the proposal itself gives for why a reader meets
$\operatorname{erfc}$. Otherwise drop the keywords. Worth doing, and
small.

### 5. The Similar-tables glosses point rather than name

"the neighbouring exponential-integral functions", "the corresponding
hyperbolic integral", "the companion hyperbolic cosine integral":
neighbouring, corresponding and companion to what, a reader asks, and
has to look back at the previous line to guess. Name the integrand:
"stores $\int_0^x\sinh(t)/t\,\mathrm{d}t$", and for T188 "whose
integrand $e^{t}/t$ replaces the Gaussian". Marginal; the links are
right and the relation is guessable.

## Noted only

* **Comment (10)** says $\operatorname{erfc}$ "is stored as its own
  normalisation because it is the small tail value for large positive
  $x$". It is not a normalisation of anything, and the sentence is
  about the table. The fact is stronger: $\operatorname{erfc}(5)=
  1.5\times10^{-12}$, and $1-\operatorname{erf}(5)$ from a hundred
  decimal places of $\operatorname{erf}(5)$ keeps eighty-eight of them.
* **Comments (7) and (9)** end with a reference used as a predicate:
  "their conversion is (5)", "The parity and complementary-error-function
  relation are (6)". "Values at negative arguments follow from (6)"
  reads as a sentence.
* **Formula (4)** opens "With the same normalisation", pointing back at
  (3); "With $S$ and $C$ as in (3)" stays right when the formulas are
  reordered.
* **P1 computes $\operatorname{erfc}$ as `RBF(1) - x.erf()`.** That is
  the right incantation, not a shortcut: the container's Sage real
  balls have no `erfc` (checked), and at $x=5$ the difference still
  carries 107 significant digits with the 64-bit guard, which is why the
  rigour note's radius bound holds for the smallest value in the table.
* **The title carries no symbols**, where its five siblings write
  "$\operatorname{Si}(x)$" and the like after the name. Consistency
  only.
