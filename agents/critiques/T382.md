# T382, Discriminants of the depressed polynomial of degree $n$

A critique. Nothing here was changed; somebody decides.

Five rows: the discriminant of $x^n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}a_ix^i$ as an element of
$\mathbb Z[a_0,\dots,a_{n-2}]$, for $n=2,\dots,6$. It is a good table and it
renders cleanly. The parameter describes the family and not the run, the value
column is headed with a quantity, the completeness note says what is actually
here, and the one thing that could have gone wrong in the rendering -- the
literal `<` inside `formula-roots` -- does not.

The findings are small. Four are worth doing and three of those are the same
root cause: this table describes itself in T381's variables, which are not its
own. The fourth is about when it may be published rather than about its text.

There is also a site bug, not a fault in this table, at the end.

## The audit

Clean, and I agree with it. Every `CITE{}` resolves -- the four to formula
labels render `(1)` to `(4)`, the three to `Links` render `[1]` to `[3]` --
both `HREF{}` targets exist (`/Elementary_symmetric_polynomials` is T118 and
answers 200; `T381` exists as a draft), both tags reach other tables, the
generator is attached, the value column is headed `$\operatorname{disc}(f_n)$`
and not the word `value`, and the one-quantity checks have nothing to say
because this is one quantity of one object under one integer parameter.

The rule that came closest to firing and stayed silent is the one about a
family named but not linked. The `rigour details` field writes "the elementary
symmetric polynomials" and *does* link it, which is why there is nothing to
report; had it written $e_k$ instead, as T381 does, the audit would also have
had nothing to match. That the check passes here is the table's doing, not the
audit's.

Nothing the audit missed is a bug in the audit. Findings 1 to 3 below are all
prose judgements no rule states.

## Worth doing

### 1. The table describes itself in variables it does not have

Three sentences describe this table as a specialisation of T381, and all three
do it with $a_n$ and $a_{n-1}$:

> **(6)** This is the specialization of *the discriminant of the general
> degree-$n$ polynomial* obtained by setting $a_n=1$ and $a_{n-1}=0$.

> *Discriminants of the general polynomial of degree $n$* — gives the same
> discriminant before the specialization $a_n=1$ and $a_{n-1}=0$

> It checks Formula (2) by substituting *the elementary symmetric polynomials*
> of the roots after imposing $a_{n-1}=0$, …

In T381 those are real variables: its polynomial is $\sum_{i=0}^n a_ix^i$ and
its entries live in $\mathbb Z[a_0,\dots,a_n]$. In T382 they are not. The
page's own comment (5), which renders *immediately above* comment (6), says so
in as many words:

> **(5)** The variable $a_i$ multiplies $x^i$, so $a_0$ is the constant term
> and the variables in the degree-$n$ entry are $a_0,\dots,a_{n-2}$.

So a reader is told the variables stop at $a_{n-2}$, and in the next sentence
meets $a_{n-1}$ and $a_n$ with no referent. The mathematics is right -- I
checked degrees 2 and 3 against T381's stored entries, and setting $a_2=1,
a_1=0$ in $a_1^2-4a_0a_2$ does give $-4a_0$, with the surviving variable names
lining up -- but the reader has to reconstruct which table each symbol belongs
to before they can see that.

**Smallest fix:** say whose they are, once, in comment (6), and let the other
two lean on it. Something like "…the specialization of T381's
$f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^n a_ix^i$, obtained by setting *its* $a_n=1$ and
$a_{n-1}=0$". In `rigour details`, "after imposing $a_{n-1}=0$" can simply
become "on roots summing to zero" -- which is what the generator's own
docstring calls it ("the root-product definition on sum-zero roots") and needs
no foreign variable at all.

### 2. The Definition never says the coefficients are indeterminates

> For $n\geq2$, let $f_n(x)=x^n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-2} a_i x^i$ be the monic
> depressed polynomial [1], with the coefficient of $x^{n-1}$ equal to $0$.
> The entry is the discriminant $\operatorname{disc}(f_n)$ [2] with the
> normalization of Formula (1).

Read alone, this defines a discriminant of *a* polynomial, which is a number.
The entries are polynomials. Nothing in the Definition says the $a_i$ are
formal, and the ring $\mathbb Z[a_0,\dots,a_{n-2}]$ is named exactly once on
the whole page -- inside `rigour details`, a provenance field that folds shut
after its first paragraph and that nobody reads to find out what a table
holds.

T381's Definition does not have this gap: it says "the general degree-$n$
polynomial **with formal coefficients** and leading coefficient $a_n$". T382
dropped the phrase, and the definite article in "**the** monic depressed
polynomial" (and in the title) then reads as naming one particular
polynomial.

The page does eventually correct the reader -- the type line says "integral
polynomial" and the entries are visibly polynomials -- but not in the
Definition and not in the first screen.

**Smallest fix:** borrow the sibling's four words: "…be the monic depressed
polynomial with formal coefficients $a_0,\dots,a_{n-2}$".

### 3. The completeness note argues against a premise it never states

> Table is complete: no (it holds every degree $2\leq n\leq6$; the degree $7$
> entry has six coefficient variables but is about 8099 characters, beyond the
> readable range chosen here)

Two problems in one clause.

**"about 8099 characters"** hedges a number that is exact. I computed the
degree-7 discriminant in the throwaway: 8,099 characters, 320 terms. (Degree 6
is 1,615 characters, which matches what is stored.) A four-digit figure behind
"about" makes a reader wonder which digits to trust. Drop the word.

**"has six coefficient variables but"** is a contrast with something the page
never says. The "but" only makes sense if the reader already believes six
variables would be *acceptable* -- a belief available only from T381, whose
own note says seven variables is "more than polynomial search accepts". I
checked: `search_polynomial` accepts a six-variable query and refuses a
seven-variable one, so T381 is right and the implicit premise is true. But a
reader of T382 has not been told it, and cannot tell why the variable count is
mentioned at all.

The honest reading is that this table stops for one reason only: length. It is
nowhere near any server limit -- the whole entries block is about 5 KB against
a 320 KB soft limit -- so the reason is editorial, which is fine, and
"beyond the readable range chosen here" is a vague way to say it.

**Smallest fix:** drop the variable count and the hedge, and name the reason:
"…it holds every degree $2\leq n\leq6$; the degree $7$ entry runs to 8099
characters and 320 terms, too long to read on a page."

### 4. Every link this table makes to T381 is currently a 404

`/T381` answers 404 to an anonymous reader: T381 is still a draft. T382 points
at it three times -- comment (6), the `Similar tables` row, and `rigour
details` -- and the `Similar tables` row is the *only* thing in that section,
so if T382 is published first, its "Similar tables" heading renders with one
dead link under it and nothing else.

This is not a fault in the text and there is nothing to fix in the document.
It is a note for whoever publishes: **publish T381 first, or publish them
together.** Worth saying because the two tables were built one after the other
and the reviewer reading T382 will not necessarily be the one holding T381.

(T381 does not link back. Its `Similar tables` is absent entirely. That is
T381's business, not this critique's, but the pair is asymmetric and somebody
touching one should look at the other.)

## Noted only because I noticed

**The program's first line is unused and load-bearing at the same time.** The
published program opens

    import numberdb.sage as numberdb
    from sage.rings.integer_ring import ZZ
    from sage.rings.polynomial.polynomial_ring_constructor import PolynomialRing

and never mentions `numberdb` again -- the computation is pure Sage. I ran the
program verbatim in the throwaway and it works. I also ran it with the first
line deleted, and it fails:

    AttributeError: partially initialized module 'sage.rings.integer_ring' has
    no attribute 'ZZ' (most likely due to a circular import)

So the apparently decorative import is what initialises Sage and makes the two
deep submodule imports resolve. A reader who tidies it away gets an error that
names none of that. T381's program uses `from sage.all import PolynomialRing,
ZZ` and needs no such prop. Replacing the three lines with that one would be
both shorter and honest, and I would take it if the table is being edited
anyway -- but it is a program that works, so it is not worth an edit of its
own.

**The Definition's last clause restates the formula it follows.**
"$f_n(x)=x^n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-2} a_i x^i$ … with the coefficient of $x^{n-1}$
equal to $0$": the sum already has no $x^{n-1}$ term. A reader meets the
clause as an extra condition and only then works out it is a gloss on the word
"depressed". It earns its place -- it is the definition of the word -- but it
would read better attached to the word than to the formula.

**`rigour details` says "exact" three times in its first sentence**: "computes
exact integer coefficients … and all arithmetic is exact", after a type line
that already says `exact`. Inherited verbatim from T381; not worth fixing in
one table alone.

**The keyword list has cubic through sextic but not "quadratic
discriminant".** I checked whether this is an oversight and I do not think it
is. The depressed cubic's discriminant $-4a_1^3-27a_0^2$ *is* what everyone
calls the cubic discriminant, so that keyword is earned; the depressed
quadratic's $-4a_0$ is not what anyone means by "the quadratic discriminant"
($b^2-4ac$, which is T381's degree-2 row). Omitting it looks deliberate and
correct. Recorded so the next reader does not re-open it.

## What I checked hardest and found nothing

**The rendering.** `formula-roots` contains a literal `<` inside mathematics
($\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}$), which has eaten the rest of a section elsewhere
in this corpus. It is escaped: the page carries `i&lt;j` and formulas (2), (3)
and (4) all render in full after it. No field has an odd number of `$`. No
LaTeX command word (`operatorname`, `geq`, `leq`, `sum_`, `prod_`, `alpha`,
`mathbb`, `dots`) appears anywhere in the document without its backslash, so
there is no JSON escape that ate one. The degree-5 and degree-6 entries -- 700
and 2,500 characters on one line -- wrap and scroll rather than overflowing.

**Findability**, since that is the point of the table. `search_polynomial`
normalises variable names: a query written `-b1^3 + 2*b1*b2 - b3` returns the
entry stored as `-c1^3 + 2*c1*c2 - c3`. So a reader who arrived holding one of
these discriminants in their own notation -- $p$ and $q$, or $b_0,b_1$ -- will
still land here, and the `a0, a1` convention costs them nothing. Five- and
six-variable queries are accepted, so every row this table holds is reachable,
and so would degree 7 have been.

**The first screen.** A reader arriving on `-4*a1^3 - 27*a0^2` sees the title,
the column headed $\operatorname{disc}(f_n)$ with their row in it, and then
the Definition and the sign convention. Subject to finding 2, they are told
what they are holding without scrolling.

## Not a fault in this table: a private draft's files and history are public

Found while fetching the generator, and reported here because a reviewer of a
draft should know. `/T382` correctly answers 404 to an anonymous request, as
do `/bundle/T382`, `/blame/T382`, `/discuss/T382` and `/preview/T382`. These
three do not:

    /files/T382               200
    /files/T382/generate.py   200   (20,517 bytes, the whole source)
    /history/T382             200   (leaks the title in <title>)

`views.table_files` and `views.table_file` never call `_refuse_a_draft`, which
every other route on that list does. So the draft is invisible, answers no
search and 404s on its own page, while its attached generator -- which carries
the table's full mathematics, its T-number and its title in the docstring --
is served to anybody who guesses the number.

This is a site bug and nothing the table can do about it. It is written up in
`docs/agent-environment.md`.
