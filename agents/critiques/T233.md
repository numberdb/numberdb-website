### 1. The page does not render: `Programs` holds a string where the page expects a record (must be done)

**What a reader sees.** An error, not a table. `table_context` in
`numberdb_app/views.py` reads each program as `program['language']` and
`program['code']` (lines 912–924). The stored section is

    Programs:
      sage: "Run `sage -python generate.py` to compare ..."

so `program['language']` indexes a string, and the handler at line 1458
re-raises this as `ValueError('Error while Parse program sage: …')`. The table
page goes through the same `table_context` via `render_table` (line 444), so
`/T233` fails for its owner too, not only in the preview. I could not load the
draft page as its owner to see the status code, but the preview uses the same
function and printed the error.

The content is wrong as well as the shape. The skill says `Programs` is "the
standard incantation … for a reader who wants one more value", and that
`generate.py` is a different thing. This text describes the generator, and it
ends "to fill the draft", which will be false the day the table is published.

**Smallest fix.** Replace the section with a record that holds an incantation:

    Programs:
      program-sage:
        language: Sage
        code: |
          P = Polyhedron(vertices=[r.to_vector() for r in
                                   RootSystem(['E', 6]).root_lattice().roots()])
          [len((t*P).integral_points()) for t in range(4)]   # L(0), ..., L(3)
          P.ehrhart_polynomial()   # needs LattE or PyNormaliz

I ran the first two lines, as named imports in `/tmp/crit233/count.py`, for
$G_2$, $B_3$, $E_6$ and $E_7$, and they give the stored values. **I have not
run `ehrhart_polynomial()`**, because this Sage image has neither engine. If
nobody can run it, drop that line. The generator is attached and needs no
sentence in `Programs`.

### 2. The kissing-number relation is false as worded, and the Hermite one names no lattice (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** The Kissing numbers entry says "$L_\Phi(1)-1$ is the
kissing number for the simply-laced root lattices in dimensions $2$, $3$, $4$
and $8$". Dimension 4 holds $A_4$ and $D_4$, and $L_{A_4}(1)-1=20$ while
$\tau_4=24$. Dimension 8 holds $A_8$, $D_8$ and $E_8$, with 72, 112 and 240,
and only $E_8$ gives $\tau_8$. What is meant is four particular lattices.

The Hermite's constants entry says "the same exceptional lattices attain known
Hermite constants". "Exceptional lattices" is not a term, nothing earlier
names them, and the lattices T149 credits are $A_2$, $A_3$, $D_4$, $D_5$,
$E_6$, $E_7$ and $E_8$, which are mostly classical.

**Smallest fix.**
- Kissing numbers: "for simply-laced $\Phi$ the minimal vectors of
  $\mathbb{Z}\Phi$ are the roots, so $L_\Phi(1)-1=|\Phi|$ is its kissing
  number, and for $A_2$, $A_3$, $D_4$ and $E_8$ it is $\tau_n$". Link the
  four entries if wanted: `HREF{Kissing_numbers#8}[$\tau_8$]`.
- Hermite's constants: "$\gamma_n$ for $2\leq n\leq8$ is attained by the root
  lattices $A_2$, $A_3$, $D_4$, $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$ and $E_8$".

### 3. Formula (3) states for every $\Phi$ a theorem its sources give for $A_n$, $C_n$ and $D_n$ (worth doing; words only)

**What a reader sees.** "If $s_\Phi(m)$ counts root-lattice points of word
length exactly $m$ … then $\sum s_\Phi(m)z^m=h^*_{P_\Phi}(z)/(1-z)^r$
[ABHPS] [ConwaySloane] [BaakeGrimm]." This is written for every root system.

**Why it matters.** ABHPS prove it for three families. Their introduction
says "we examine the growth series for the classical root lattices $A_n$,
$C_n$ and $D_n$", and it adds that their approach "would need to be modified
to prove the formula in the $B_n$ case". Conway–Sloane and Baake–Grimm compute
coordination sequences, the left-hand side, and say nothing about a polytope.
So on this page nothing sources the identity for $B_n$ or the exceptional
types.

It matters beyond the citation. `rigour details` says the $B_n$ rows "use the
coordinator polynomial" and the exceptional rows "are recovered from the
coordination sequences". Those rows are Ehrhart polynomials only because of
this identity. The direct counts, the build's and mine, agree wherever they
were made: $B_2$, $B_3$, $G_2$ and $F_4$ in the build, $E_6$ and $E_7$ at small
dilates here. For $B_n$ the identity also follows in a line: the word length
of $x\in\mathbb{Z}^n$ is $\max(\|x\|_\infty,\lceil\|x\|_1/2\rceil)$, and
$tP_{B_n}=\{\|x\|_1\leq2t,\ \|x\|_\infty\leq t\}$. I found no error, only a
claim wider than its citations.

**Smallest fix.** Either cite a source for the general statement, or say
where it is proved. Bacher, de la Harpe and Venkov, *Séries de croissance et
polynômes d'Ehrhart associés aux réseaux de racines*, Ann. Inst. Fourier 49
(1999), is the paper ABHPS credit with the classical proofs, and its title
suggests it is that source. **I have not read it**, so check what it covers
before citing it. Then add one sentence to `rigour details` naming which rows
rest on the identity and which were counted directly.

### 5. The Comments are vague exactly where a reader needs them, and $A_1$ is dropped without a reason (worth doing; small)

**What a reader sees.**
- Comment (7): "for some types the polynomial obtained in ambient coordinates
  is different". Which types, and which ambient coordinates? The reader cannot
  tell whether their own computation is one of them.
- Comment (8): "The Bourbaki ranges avoid duplicate low-rank names: $D_3$ is
  $A_3$, and $B_2$ and $C_2$ give the same root polytope in these
  coordinates." In the usual coordinates $P_{B_2}$ and $P_{C_2}$ are
  different squares. They are the same *up to a lattice isomorphism*, because
  $B_2\cong C_2$. "Avoid duplicate low-rank names" is a remark about the
  parameter's range.
- "The rank-one segment $A_1$ is not listed" gives no reason and no values.
  The Type constraint, "$A_n$ with $n\geq2$", then describes this table's
  choice rather than the family, which is the skill's parameter rule.

**Smallest fix.**
- (7): "Counting in $\mathbb{Z}\Phi$ matters: for $C_n$ and $D_n$ in the usual
  coordinates, $\mathbb{Z}^n$ also contains $\pm e_i\in P_\Phi$, and counting
  there gives a different polynomial."
- (8): "$n$ runs over Bourbaki's ranges, which list each irreducible root
  system once: $C_2\cong B_2$ and $D_3\cong A_3$. $A_1$ is omitted, since its
  root polytope is a segment with $L_{A_1}(t)=2t+1$ and
  $h^*_{A_1}(z)=1+z$."
- Set the Type constraint to $A_n$ with $n\geq1$, and let `complete-note` say
  $A_1$ is given in a comment.

### 6. The pattern of palindromes among the $h^*$ rows is visible and unexplained (worth considering)

**What a reader sees.** $h^*_{A_n}$, $h^*_{C_n}$, $h^*_{D_n}$, $h^*_{E_6}$ and
$h^*_{B_2}$ are palindromes. $h^*_{B_n}$ for $n\geq3$, $h^*_{E_7}$,
$h^*_{E_8}$, $h^*_{F_4}$ and $h^*_{G_2}$ are not. $E_7$ fails only in its
middle pair, 8211 and 8787, which looks like a typo until one checks. I
checked all 80 $h^*$ rows for this pattern. ABHPS remark on the $B_n$ case
(quoted in finding 3). The page says nothing.

**Smallest fix.** One comment stating the fact: since the origin is interior,
$h^*_{P_\Phi}$ is palindromic exactly when $P_\Phi$ is reflexive with respect
to $\mathbb{Z}\Phi$. That is Hibi's theorem, T. Hibi, *Dual polytopes of
rational convex polytopes*, Combinatorica 12 (1992). Then list which stored
types are palindromic. $G_2$ has a further visible reason: its six short roots
are interior lattice points, so $h^*_{G_2}(z)$ ends in $7z^2$. **I have not
checked the Hibi reference against the paper**; its statement is standard.

  The T232 critique noted the same draft-stage wording.
- **`complete-note`** gives the range, $n\leq20$, but not the reason. The
  skill asks for the reason there. One clause would do, for instance that
  $L_{A_{20}}(t)$, the longest entry, is 569 characters.
- **Formula (6)** carries prose, "since the listed root polytopes have the
  roots and the origin as their lattice points". It is true, and short enough
  to leave. "The listed" can go: the statement holds for $A_1$ too.
- **Similar tables, T147:** "the same root lattices, with packing data rather
  than growth polynomials". T147 holds $A_n$, $D_n$ and $E_n$, not lattices
  named $B$, $C$, $F$ or $G$, and "growth polynomials" is not this page's word.
  "the lattices $A_n$, $D_n$ and $E_n$, with packing data" is exact.
- **Comment (7)** first sentence, "uses all roots of $\Phi$, not only the
  positive roots", repeats the Definition. It earns its place only if it names
  the other convention it guards against: $\operatorname{conv}(\Phi^+\cup\{0\})$,
  which some authors also call a root polytope.
- **`Keywords`** carries both "root polytope" and "root polytopes". The index
  stems, so one is enough. It costs nothing.
- **$h^*$ rows print in descending powers** and Formulas (5) in ascending
  powers, so a reader holding $1,232,7228,\dots$ reads the row backwards. That
  is the client's order, as on T232, and I do not suggest changing it.

## What reads well

- **The Definition defines.** It fixes the root system up to isomorphism, the
  polytope, and, most importantly, the lattice $\mathbb{Z}\Phi$ that both
  polynomials depend on. Two people would build the same table from it. The
  audit's length warning is not a reason to cut it.
- **Every formula is consistent with every row.** The four classical closed
  forms, the binomial transform, $L_\Phi(1)=|\Phi|+1$ and the five exceptional
  polynomials were checked against all 160 entries. The independent
  lattice-point counts agree too. Apart from Programs, the mathematics renders
  cleanly in MathJax.
- **No sentence apologises, and nothing points backwards.** The one link the
  corpus invites, to the lattice tables, is made. Findings 2 and 7 concern how
  those links are glossed, not whether they exist.
