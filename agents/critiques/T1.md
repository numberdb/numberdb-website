# T1 critique: "One"

Read 2026-09-21 as a reader meeting the page, not as its author. T1 holds one
entry, the exact integer `1`, with no parameters; type `Z`, rigour `exact`,
`complete: yes`. The stored document is 716 bytes as `/bundle/T1` serves it.

**The table reads well and answers the question it exists to answer.** A reader
who arrives holding the number 1 gets this table first out of 98, and the first
screen then tells them what they have. Three things are worth an edit, all of
them one field each, and none of them touches a number.

**This table was edited earlier today.** `T1/README.txt` says "as of
2026-09-21 21:25, by zeta3"; `agents/critiques/T1-growth.md` and its
`-repaired` note record what that edit was — `complete: yes` with the T8
clause, the `#1` anchor dropped from both links to the integers table, and a
doubled space removed. I read the repaired table, have not re-raised those, and
say below where I checked one and it held.

**T0 was read under this same brief an hour before me** and its report predicted
one of my three findings by name. I confirmed it live rather than inheriting it,
and I have written T1's own version of the fix; where T0's measurements already
answer a question I do not re-measure it, and I say which number is whose.

## 1. `Similar tables` reads "integers —  contained in", a gloss with no subject

What a reader sees, in full, under `Similar tables`:

    integers —   contained in

The em dash is the site's punctuation between a table and the relation glossing
it; the two words after it are the author's, and they have no subject. As
printed, the nearest noun is *integers*, so the sentence asserts that the
integers are contained in something it does not name. The reader has to supply
"[the number here is] contained in [the]" and reverse the direction while doing
it.

This is not my measurement. The T0 critique walked 195 tables and found exactly
three relations written this way — T0, T1 and T42, the three hand-made
single-integer tables, which share the phrase because they share an author and a
morning — and everywhere else in the corpus the **linked** table is the gloss's
subject: "its derivative", "the same knots", "holds $\kappa_{-4}=\pi/4$ as its
entry $a=1/4$". T0 has since been repaired to

    relation: includes $0$ as its entry $n=0$

and T1 is now one of the two left. I confirmed the phrase is still live on T1
and checked that the parallel sentence is true of T2 as it stands.

Smallest change, one field, no other edit:

    Similar tables:
    - relation: includes $1$ as its entry $n=1$
      table: HREF{Integers}[integers]

T2 has parameter `n` of type `Z`, runs $-10\leq n\leq 10$, and its `Numbers` key
`"1"` is `{"number": "1", "equals": "HREF{One}"}` — so the sentence is true, it
puts the linked table in the subject where the rest of the corpus puts it, and
it tells a reader what they will find when they follow the link. **Worth doing.**
If T1 is done, T42 wants the same sentence with 42 in it; doing one and not the
other leaves the phrase in the corpus anyway.

## 2. Nothing in this corpus answers "multiplicative identity" — worth doing

T1's `Keywords` is one phrase, `neutral element`. The standard English name of
the object this table defines is not in the document anywhere: not in the title
("One"), not in `Keywords`, not in the tag (`integer`), not in the definition
("The neutral element of multiplication in the ring of integers"), not in the
comment. The word *identity* does not occur in T1's indexed text at all.

Measured against the live corpus through the client:

    search_text('multiplicative identity')    0 tables
    search_text('additive identity')          1 table  — Zero, rank 1
    search_text('identity')                  20 tables — Zero 2nd, One absent
                                                         from all 20 returned
    search_text('neutral element')            6 tables — One 1st, Zero 2nd

The second line is the control, and it is a fresh one: `additive identity` was
added to T0 by its growth repair a few hours ago, and it now returns that table
alone, first. The first line is the finding — a reader who knows what 1 *is* and
types its name gets nothing from a corpus that holds it.

Smallest change, one line:

    Keywords:
    - neutral element
    - multiplicative identity

Two checks before anybody does it. This is not the case the T0 lesson proposal
(`agents/lessons/proposals/20260921T223734Z-critique.md`) rules out: that lesson
says `Keywords` cannot raise a table for the word it is *named after*, because
keywords sit at weight A with the title and only add another occurrence of a
word already there. Here the phrase is absent from the index rather than
repeated in it, and the lesson blesses exactly this — "adding a synonym reaches a
query the title misses". And `multiplicative identity` currently returns nothing,
so the phrase is uncontested and T1 becomes its only hit, which is the same
rank-1 position `additive identity` buys T0. **Worth doing.**

I would not add `unity`, `unit` or `identity element` as well. `unity` returns 18
tables led by *Roots of unity*, `unit` returns 20; a keyword dropped into a
crowded field buys a low rank rather than a hit, and `identity element` already
reaches T1 second — through the word *element* in the keyword it has.

## 3. The one comment is not a sentence, and does not say where "successor" lives

The whole of `Comments`, as it renders:

    (1)  Successor of zero.

Four words and a full stop, with no subject: a reader is asked to carry the
title down two sections and supply it. The skill's rule is flat — "Everything a
reader sees is read as English … Telegraphic is not concise; it is unfinished" —
and the neighbour that has a comment writes a sentence: T2's is "The integers
form a ring with their usual addition and multiplication."

T1 is the only one of the three hand-made single-integer tables that has a
comment at all — T0's and T42's `Comments` are both empty — which is why no
previous read of this neighbourhood has reached it.

There is a second thing in the same four words. The definition places 1 in the
ring $\mathbb{Z}$; *successor* is Peano's function on $\mathbb{N}$, and nothing
on the page says so. The skill asks that notation be defined where it is used,
and a reader who does not already know what the sentence is about cannot tell
whether "successor" means the Peano axioms, the order on $\mathbb{Z}$, or the
ordinals.

Smallest change, one field, and it fixes both:

    comment-successor: It is the successor of HREF{Zero}[zero] under the Peano
      axioms for the natural numbers.

**Worth doing**, and it is the cheapest of the three.

## 4. The audit's one finding: right in form, and I would not act on it

    Definition links to another table; a cross-reference belongs in
    Similar tables or a comment

The rule is a flat "no `HREF{` in `Definition`", and the fault it aims at — a
definition that has grown a relation clause — is real and is one of the two the
skill's section 4 opens with. It cannot tell a relation from a naming, and this
is a naming: "the ring of integers" is the first and only mention of an object
this corpus holds, and the skill's instruction cuts the other way, "Link the
first mention of a thing to what explains it. A table if the corpus holds one."
Removing the link would satisfy the audit and disobey the skill.

Two earlier reads reached the same verdict on the same sentence and I agree with
both; what I add is that the part which *was* wrong is now right. The definition
used to read `HREF{Integers#1}[integers]`, so the link under the words "the ring
of integers" landed on the row holding 1 — the row the reader had just come
from. It is `HREF{Integers}` today and points at the ring. The audit does not
look at the target, so it fired before the fix and fires after it, unchanged.

I would leave the link and spend the edit on §1.

## 5. Noted only, and I would edit none of them

- **`rigour details` carries two `--`** where the skill asks for a comma or a
  full stop: "An exact value -- an integer, a rational or a polynomial -- so
  there is no precision to choose and nothing to be wrong about." It reaches the
  page as two hyphens. It is **not this table's text**: it is written by
  `numberdb_app/management/commands/set_rigour.py:32` and applied by the 2026-08
  rigour migration, and the T0 walk found 31 of 70 `exact` tables carrying it
  verbatim. A fix belongs in that command, once.
- **"the ring of integers"** is a phrase that in a corpus holding Dedekind zeta
  functions of cubic and quartic fields can be read as $\mathcal{O}_K$ for an
  unnamed $K$. The link resolves it to T2 and the tag says `integer`, so a reader
  does get there; writing "the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ of HREF{Integers}[integers]"
  would remove the beat of doubt. Cheap, and I would not spend an edit on it
  alone — T0 has the identical sentence and the two want doing together if at
  all.
- **The definition is a noun phrase, not a sentence.** "The neutral element of
  multiplication in the ring of integers." is completed by the title above it.
  The corpus writes both this and the full sentence (T7: "$\pi$ is the area of
  the unit circle."). It reads. T0's report reached the same place.
- **`Programs` is the literal `numbers = [1]`, labelled Sage.** On most tables a
  literal where an incantation belongs would be a finding. Here `complete: yes`
  is true and there is no next value to fetch, so the line is honest about that,
  and the three siblings read the same way.
- **One outside link, Wikipedia's article on 1**, which is largely about the
  numeral and its history rather than about the multiplicative identity — the
  thing the definition actually names. `Identity element` would be the article
  that explains the sentence. One line, low value, and T0 has the same shape.
- **One tag, `integer`**, where two is typical. It reaches T0, T2 and T42, so it
  is a way through the corpus and not a tag of one table, and T2's `ring` and
  `Abelian group` are true of the integers and false of a number.
- **`Parameters` is the empty string** where T2 stores a dict. Invisible to a
  reader; the page correctly renders no `Parameters` block.

## 6. What I checked hardest, and it held

*The page renders, and there is nothing in it that only the HTML shows.* No
`Math input error`, no `<` eating a section, no `argument ()`. There is in fact
no mathematics in any of T1's own fields — the only `$` in the whole rendered
body is the site's own `NumberDB$^\beta$` in the navbar — so nothing to typeset
and nothing to break. The seven section headings are Number, Definition,
Comments, Programs, Links, Similar tables, Data properties: no `Parameters`
block, which is right, and the value column carries the site's default heading
`Number` rather than the fallback word `value`. The relative `href="Integers"`
resolves from both URLs this page is served at, `/T1` and `/One` (both 200);
`/T1/` answers 404 rather than serving a page whose links are broken.

*A reader who arrives holding the number is served, in the first screen, and
this is the test that matters.* `search_integer(1)` returns 98 results across
the corpus and puts **T1 first**, ahead of Integers, Bernoulli numbers and
ninety-five others that hold an exact 1 — the crowdedness of the value is
exactly what would have made this fail, and it does not.
`/api/search?expression=1` agrees, T1 first. The page then gives, above the
fold, the title, the number, and one sentence saying what it is. That is the
question this database exists to answer.

*The pairing with the integers table runs in the right direction and renders.*
T2's row for $n=1$ carries `equals: HREF{One}` and prints on `/Integers` as
"1 equals: One", linking back here. `equals` is the strong claim — the same
number, which search reads — and it is stored on the general table pointing at
the specific one, so search answers with One and names Integers beside it. T1
correctly carries no `equals` of its own; adding one would state the same thing
twice in the weaker direction.

*It is one table.* One number, no parameter, no axis to run along, and a
uniqueness proof rather than a range: if $e$ and $e'$ are both multiplicative
identities then $e=ee'=e'$. The completeness clause finishes the rendered
sentence properly — "Table is complete: yes (it holds the single number named by
the definition)" — and says what the table covers rather than merely that it is
finished.

*Every claim on the page is a fact about the mathematics.* Nothing about how
distinctive the value is, how good a search hit would be, or what a reader ought
to conclude. Nothing is named that should be linked: the two objects the corpus
holds, the integers and zero, are both linked, each at its first and only
mention in its section. Nothing is pointed at rather than named — no "the
former", no "as above" — except the missing subject in §1, which is the finding.

  `.tables`. I read `len(search_integer(1).tables) == 0` as an empty corpus for
  two turns, which is precisely the trap the skill warns about for the sibling
  call and, by warning about it, walks you into. Already proposed twice:
  `agents/lessons/proposals/20260916T015945Z-critique.md:43` and
  `…/20260921T110445Z-critique.md:3`.
