# T324 — Zeros of the $L$-functions of level one cusp forms

Read 2026-09-18 as a reader, against <https://numberdb.org/skill> fetched the
same day. The table is a private draft: 480 entries, the first twenty positive
ordinates for each of 24 eigenforms over fourteen weights $12\leq k\leq40$,
type `R`, 30 significant digits, rigour `heuristic (agreement-checked)`.

`GET /api/table/T324/audit` answers `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`, and on
its own terms it is right: every `HREF` resolves, the value column is headed
`$t_n$` and not the word `value`, the parameters describe the family and not
the run, `complete-note` finishes its own sentence. Nothing in its rule set
reaches any of the findings below, and one of them (finding 1) is invisible in
the stored document altogether — the YAML says the right thing and the page
does not print it.

**The one thing to know before reading the list.** T323 is this table's
sibling: the same $L$-functions, the same eigenform ordering, linked from the
first `Similar tables` row. It was critiqued at 07:10 today and repaired at
07:22; this draft was created at 07:36. Four of the seven repairs were carried
across — the Definition no longer gives a divergent Dirichlet series, $a_2$ is
an exact integer where it is one, the `Programs` block sorts the embeddings and
is tested at weight 24 where the order can be got wrong, and nothing says "this
draft". Three were not, plus two of the noted items, and they are findings 3,
4, 5, 7 and the first "noted only" below. The repairs were applied to the live
document; `generators/level-one-cusp-form-l-values/table.yaml` in this
repository still holds the pre-repair text, and that is evidently where these
sentences were copied from. A reader of the two tables side by side gets two
different accounts of the same normalisation.

## Findings

### 1. The page never says where $n$ starts counting, though the document does

A reader sees, in the `Parameters` section:

> $n$ — index of the positive zero ($n\geq1$)

and that is the whole of it. The document has the sentence a reader needs —

> For fixed $k$ and $i$, the positive ordinates are ordered increasingly and
> counted from $n=1$.

— but it is stored as `Parameters: n: comments:`, and the renderer never looks
at that key. The parameter loop in `numberdb_app/views.py` (around line 844)
reads `display`, `title`, `type`, `constraints`, `values` and
`show-in-parameter-list`; anything else on a parameter is dropped in silence.
Rendered through `/preview`, the string "ordered increasingly" appears in the
echoed source and nowhere in the page. Two of the 189 `table.yaml` files in
`generators/` use a parameter `comments`, and the skill's table of sections
does not list it, so this is a field that looks like it works and does not.

The consequence is exactly the thing the skill asks about in section 2: *"the
nth zero" needs a statement of where counting starts*. The Definition does not
supply it either — it gives "the positive ordinates $t_n$ of zeros
$L(f_{k,i},k/2+\mathrm{i}t_n)=0$" with no word about order — so a reader
holding $13.9075$ cannot tell from the page whether $n=2$ means the second
smallest or the second one PARI happened to print.

Smallest fix: put it in the Definition, where it costs three words and also
buys the ordering claim: "…gives, in increasing order, the positive ordinates
$t_n$ of zeros…". Or fold it into the `n` constraint, which does render.

### 2. `rigour details` names a check the attached generator does not contain, omits the one it does, and hides its caveat behind the fold

The field reads:

> The generator uses PARI/GP's `lfunmf` and `lfunzeros` [3] at two bit
> precisions, sorts the embedded $L$-functions by the real value of $a_2$, and
> stores only digits that agree between the two runs. **A separate check
> evaluates the $L$-function near the stored ordinates and checks the
> functional equation with `lfuncheckfeq`.** PARI's zero search is not a
> certified enclosure method, so the table does not claim proven zeros.

The file the table carries — `/files/T324/generate.py`, byte-identical to
`generators/level-one-cusp-form-l-zeros/generate.py` — contains no
`lfuncheckfeq` and never evaluates $L$ at a stored ordinate. So the sentence a
reader would follow to repeat the check leads to a file where the check is not.

The check that *is* there is the stronger one and goes unmentioned:
`_assert_a2_order` recomputes the characteristic polynomial of $T_2$ in Sage,
takes its roots exactly in `AA`, and refuses the whole run unless PARI's sorted
$a_2$ agree with them to fifty decimals — which is what makes $i$ mean what the
table says it means — and a second loop refuses a weight with repeated $a_2$.

Third, the question a table of *zeros* has to answer is not asked. $n$ is an
index, so a missed zero does not spoil one row, it shifts every later row of
that group. PARI's own documentation of `lfunzeros`, which is link [3] on this
page, says it uses "a naive algorithm which may miss some zeros" and tells the
caller to raise `divz`. The generator answers this — it searches to height 80
with `divz = 16`, twice the default mesh — and none of that reaches the page,
which speaks only about digits.

And a reader with the page closed sees none of the caveat anyway: the field is
over the 400-character fold (`numberdb_app/prose.py`), and because it is one
paragraph the renderer cuts at the first sentence break, so "PARI's zero search
is not a certified enclosure method" sits behind `more` while the untroubled
first sentence is what shows.

Smallest fix: drop the `lfuncheckfeq` sentence; say instead that PARI's
embedding order is checked against the exact $T_2$ characteristic polynomial,
that the zeros were searched to height 80 on a mesh twice PARI's default
because its search may otherwise skip one, and put "not a certified enclosure
method" in the first sentence where it will be read.

### 3. "the critical strip is $0<\operatorname{Re}s<k$" is the one sentence a zeros table must get right

> The table uses the arithmetic normalisation: $a_1=1$ and $L(f,s)$ is the
> analytic continuation of $\sum a_mm^{-s}$ from its half-plane of convergence,
> so the critical strip is $0<\operatorname{Re}s<k$ and the centre is $s=k/2$.

Every number in this table is the ordinate of a zero, so "where are the zeros"
is the reader's question, and this is the only sentence that answers it. In
this normalisation $L(f,s)\neq0$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$, because the
Euler product converges absolutely there by Deligne's bound, and the functional
equation carries that to $\operatorname{Re}s<(k-1)/2$ apart from the trivial
zeros at $s=0,-1,-2,\ldots$. The strip that holds the zeros this table lists is
therefore $(k-1)/2<\operatorname{Re}s<(k+1)/2$ — width one, not width $k$. At
$k=40$ the sentence offers a reader a region forty units wide for zeros that
are confined to one.

T323 carried the identical sentence, was critiqued for it, and now reads "In
this normalisation the functional equation relates $s$ and $k-s$, the centre is
$s=k/2$, and the critical integers are $s=1,\ldots,k-1$." There the term was
loose; here it is load-bearing, and the replacement has to say something
different because this table has no critical integers to name.

Smallest fix: "…so the functional equation relates $s$ and $k-s$, the centre is
$s=k/2$, and every nontrivial zero lies in
$(k-1)/2\leq\operatorname{Re}s\leq(k+1)/2$; the zeros here are the ones on
$\operatorname{Re}s=k/2$."

### 4. `comment-ordering` is the pre-repair T323 paragraph, and $\chi_{k,2}$ is still defined nowhere

> Library orderings are not used as table orderings. In each weight, the
> generator embeds the Hecke field into the real numbers, computes the
> corresponding value of $a_2$, and then sorts the forms by that real value.
> Each entry comment names the same $a_2$ ordering through the row $(k,2)$ of
> the Hecke polynomial table.

Three sentences, and a reader can use none of them: "Library orderings" names
no library, the middle sentence says what the generator did rather than what
$i$ is (and `rigour details` says it again), and "names the same $a_2$ ordering
through the row $(k,2)$" is not a sentence anybody parses once, besides
pointing at a row instead of naming it. $\chi_{k,2}$ appears in all 480 entry
comments and is introduced nowhere on this page. That matters more here than it
did on T323: the site prints the `Numbers` block above the `Definition`, capped
at half the viewport, so on the rendered page the first prose a reader meets is
the entry comment "$a_2=-24$, the root of $\chi_{12,2}$" — a symbol the page
never defines, above the sentence that would have.

The fix is already written, in the live T323: "The index $i$ counts the
eigenforms of weight $k$ in increasing order of the embedded $T_2$-eigenvalue
$a_2$; this is not necessarily the order Sage or PARI lists them in. Each entry
gives its own $a_2$ and links to $\chi_{k,2}$ in [the Hecke polynomial table],
whose roots are the $a_2$ values in that weight." It needs no adaptation.

### 5. "shifting $s$ by $(k-1)/2$" does not say which way — and here the answer a reader wants is that the number does not move

> The analytic normalisation often written with centre $1/2$ is obtained by
> shifting $s$ by $(k-1)/2$.

A reader has to guess the sign. Worse, on this table the shift is not the
interesting part: because it is real, the ordinate is *the same* in both
normalisations, $L(f,k/2+\mathrm{i}t)=0$ exactly when
$L^{\mathrm{an}}(f,1/2+\mathrm{i}t)=0$. Somebody arriving with $9.2223793999$
from a source that uses the analytic normalisation — which is most of them —
is asking precisely whether their $t$ is this $t$, and the page makes them
work it out.

Smallest fix, the repaired T323 sentence plus one clause:
"$L^{\mathrm{an}}(f,s)=L(f,s+(k-1)/2)$, so the ordinates $t_n$ are the same in
both normalisations."

### 6. "increasing real $T_2$-eigenvalue" makes "real" do work the embedding does

The Definition orders the forms "by increasing real $T_2$-eigenvalue $a_2$",
which reads as if some eigenvalues were complex and were being passed over. All
of these are real; what has to be chosen is the *embedding* of the Hecke field,
and that is what `comment-ordering` and the entry comments are about. The
repaired T323 says "ordered by increasing embedded $a_2$", which is one word
different and correct.

### 7. `Keywords` promises Ramanujan and the page never mentions him

`Ramanujan tau L-function zeros` is a keyword; neither "Ramanujan" nor $\tau$
appears anywhere a reader can see. Somebody who searched that phrase and landed
here has the twenty $k=12$ rows in front of them — the zeros of
$\sum\tau(n)n^{-s}$ — and nothing on the page says so. T323 closed this in its
repair with one clause; the linked Hecke-polynomial table says it outright
("For $k=12$, $\chi_{12,p}(x)=x-\tau(p)$"). One clause in
`comment-normalisation` or in the $k=12$ entry comments would do it.

## Noted only

* **The search remark in `comment-central-zero`.** "Those forced zeros are
  omitted from the entries rather than making every search for $0$ match this
  table." The first half is the fact; the second is a remark about this
  website's search, and T323's repair deleted the same clause from the same
  sentence. Everything before it is good and is worth keeping — a reader who
  computed $L(f,k/2)=0$ at $k\equiv2\pmod4$ is told why there is no row.

* **`i` and $\mathrm{i}$.** The Definition writes
  $L(f_{k,i},k/2+\mathrm{i}t_n)=0$, where the italic $i$ is this table's
  eigenform index and the upright $\mathrm{i}$ is the imaginary unit, four
  characters apart. MathJax does distinguish them, and a reader scanning does
  not. Naming the critical line instead —"the positive $t_n$ with
  $L(f_{k,i},s)=0$ on $\operatorname{Re}s=k/2$"— avoids the collision and is
  shorter.

* **The two sibling zero tables index both signs and this one does not.**
  `Zeros of the Riemann zeta function` has $n\neq0$ and
  `Zeros of Dirichlet L-functions` has $k\neq0$, both listing negative
  ordinates as negative indices; T324 has $n\geq1$ and `comment-symmetry`
  explaining that the negatives are the mirror image. Defensible, and I would
  not change it — but the `Similar tables` rows that point at those two tables
  say only "stores zero ordinates", which is where a reader would want to be
  told that the neighbouring convention differs.

* **"the first twenty" is a count, not a reason.** `complete-note` says what is
  here and the skill asks the note to say why this range: twenty is the shape
  of a decision about size, and the sentence a reader checking whether their own
  number belongs would rather have is which heights are covered — every group's
  $t_{20}$ falls between 40.5 and 49.3, so the table is "every zero below about
  40" wearing a count.

* **The `Programs` snippet searches differently from the build.** It asks for
  `lfunzeros(P[i][2], 50)` — PARI's default mesh, height 50 — where the
  generator used height 80 and `divz = 16`. I ran the default-mesh search at
  all 24 groups: the first twenty zeros agree with the stored ones everywhere,
  so the snippet is right today. It is right by the same kind of luck the T323
  critique found in Sage's embedding order, and the one-character fix
  (`lfunzeros(P[i][2], 50, 16)`) would also stop the snippet contradicting a
  `rigour details` that mentioned the mesh.

* **480 entries carry 24 distinct comments.** Each is repeated twenty times,
  once per zero of its group. The same thing was raised on T323 and declined
  for good reasons, which apply here too; noting it only so the next reader
  knows it was seen.

## What I checked hardest and found clean

* **The rendering.** All eleven pieces of the document went through
  `/preview?table=` — Definition, each of the four comments, Formulas,
  Programs, Similar tables, Data properties, Keywords, and a block of entries
  from a one-dimensional and a multi-dimensional weight — and every one
  answered 200 and rendered in full. Both `<` in
  $0<\operatorname{Re}s<k$ arrive as `&lt;` and eat nothing. The parameter list
  has no empty `()`. The entry rows print $k$, $i$, $n$ and the number under a
  `$t_n$` header with no `param-latex` standing in for a value, and the entry
  links render as `Hecke_polynomials_of_level_one_cusp_forms?entry=12,2`, which
  answers 200 and focuses the row. Finding 1 is the only thing the rendering
  loses.

  The whole page was then rebuilt from the API document on a throwaway sqlite
  database by the recipe in `docs/agent-environment.md`: 480 records, status
  200, 415,744 bytes, sections in the site's own order (Numbers, Definition,
  Parameters, Formulas, Comments, Programs, Links, Similar tables, Data
  properties), tag strip `number theory L-function zero`, all 480 rows present
  with their comments, and the `rigour details` fold closed over its last two
  sentences. It agrees with the pieces.

* **The TeX.** All 76 distinct `$…$` fragments in the document, including both
  shapes of entry comment, typeset under MathJax 3 with no error; a deliberately
  broken control fragment (`\frac{1`) did error, so the check was live.

* **The `Programs` block, run verbatim.** Under Sage-hosted GP the six lines
  return

      1.88602626271288641226254344200199050657389571427872430096035595461...
      stored (24,1,1): 1.88602626271288641226254344200

  matching the stored entry to all thirty digits and the value printed in the
  snippet's own comment. Weight 24 has two eigenforms and PARI lists them in
  decreasing $a_2$, so this snippet is tested where the ordering can go wrong —
  which is what T323's finding 2 asked for.

* **That the index $n$ is not off by one anywhere.** The count of zeros of
  $\Lambda(f,s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(f,s)$ up to height $T$ is
  $\theta(T)/\pi+S(T)$ with
  $\theta(T)=\operatorname{Im}\log\Gamma(k/2+\mathrm{i}T)-T\log2\pi$. Calibrated
  on $\zeta$ (it gives 2.39, 9.42 and 29.00 where the true counts below 25, 50
  and 100 are 2, 9 and 29), it puts $\theta(t_{20})/\pi$ between 19.11 and 20.22
  at all 24 groups — consistent with twenty zeros below $t_{20}$ and not with
  twenty-one. No group's stored list has room for a skipped zero.

* **That $i$ labels what it says.** $\chi_{12,2}=x+24$, $\chi_{24,2}=x^2-1080x-20468736$
  and $\chi_{40,2}=x^3-548856x^2-\cdots$ on the linked table have exactly the
  roots the entry comments quote, in the order they quote them; the $a_2$ of
  each weight sum to that polynomial's trace. All fourteen `#k,2` anchors exist
  on that page and all five `Similar tables` slugs answer 200 to an anonymous
  `curl`, T323 among them.

* **The claim that the central zero is simple.** `comment-central-zero` states
  flatly that the forced zero at $s=k/2$ for $k\equiv2\pmod4$ has order 1. The
  functional equation gives only that the order is odd, and the generator does
  not test it; PARI's `lfunorderzero` returns 1 at all nine such groups
  ($k=18,22,26,30,34,38$), so the claim is true as far as computation reaches.
  I am not making a finding of it, but it is the one unhedged statement on a
  page whose rigour is "heuristic" everywhere else.

* **The stored values themselves**, lightly, since the build and `verify` own
  this: all 480 carry exactly 30 significant digits and each group is strictly
  increasing in $n$.

## Not about the table

`/files/T324/generate.py` and `/files/T324` serve this draft's attached source
and file list to an anonymous request — 200 with the full script — while
`/T324` answers 404 to the same caller. `table_file` and `table_files` in
`numberdb_app/views.py` never call `_refuse_a_draft`, which the table page and
`/preview` both do. This is the `/preview/T133` fault in another route and has
nothing to do with the table's content; written up in
`docs/agent-environment.md`.

## Verdict

Two worth doing before this is published: finding 1, because the index is the
table and the page does not define it, and finding 2, because a reader who
follows `rigour details` to the attached file will not find the check it names.
Finding 3 is a third, and cheap. Findings 4 to 7 are a sentence each, and every
one of them has its replacement already written in the repaired T323 — a pass
that copies those four sentences across would close them in one edit. The rest
is noted because I noticed it.
