# T251 critique: Zernike polynomials $Z_n^l$

Read 2026-09-15. T251 is a private draft. It holds 188 exact integer
polynomials: 100 radial polynomials $R_n^{|l|}(t)$ for $n\leq20$ and 88
Cartesian polynomials $Z_n^l(x,y)$ for $n\leq12$, the two halves selected by a
parameter `form`. Every entry carries a comment giving the Noll, OSA/ANSI,
Fringe and Wyant single indices.

**The mathematics is in good order and the page renders.** I checked hardest at
three places and found nothing wrong at any of them: every single index in
every comment that Wikipedia also lists agrees with it exactly, including the
four that are easiest to transpose; all 1365 TeX fragments typeset; and the
`Programs` snippet runs and reproduces the stored $R_{12}^0$ and $R_2^0$.

What is wrong is structural and it is the first finding. The rest are wording
and one lost pair of braces.

- **1 is the decision somebody has to take**, and I think the answer is two
  tables. The `audit_table` finding about it exists and did not fire; I
  disagree with its silence, and say why below.
- **2 to 5 are worth doing** whatever is decided about 1.
- **6 to 8 are small** and worth doing while the file is open.
- **The notes at the end are there only because I noticed them.** One of them
  is a warning not to "fix" something that is right.

## `audit_table`

Django is not installed on this host and the builder image has no app, so
`manage.py audit_table` could not run. `GET /api/table/T251/audit` returned

    {"tid": "T251", "title": "Zernike polynomials $Z_n^l$",
     "findings": [], "clean": true}

**I agree with it on everything it checks, and I disagree with its silence on
the one thing it was built to catch.** The skill says "the audit now reports it
on a draft" about a parameter whose values are names. It did not report it
here, and both of its two rules for this miss for a reason:

- `_one_table_or_several` requires the grid to be orthogonal:
  `len(grid) * len(labels) > len(records) * 1.05` makes it `continue`. Here
  the 188 entries sit on 152 distinct $(n,l)$ pairs, of which only 36 carry
  both forms, so the test is $304 > 197.4$ and the check bails out. The
  docstring says a ragged parameter "does not trip this" because raggedness
  means a genuine family -- a shape only some objects have. In T251 the
  raggedness has the opposite cause: the two halves were each given the range
  that suited them ($n\leq20$ and $n\leq12$), and the radial half indexes by
  $|l|$ while the Cartesian half indexes by signed $l$. Two tables with
  different ranges look exactly like one ragged table to that test.
- `_column_names_its_quantity` fires only when the header is a word in
  `GENERIC_HEADERS`. T251's header is `$R_n^{|l|}(t)$ or $Z_n^l(x,y)$` -- the
  same fallback the rule describes, written in LaTeX with an "or" in it
  instead of the word `value`, and so invisible to a membership test.

Neither of these is T251's fault, and I have written both up in
`docs/agent-environment.md`. Findings 2 to 8 below are all things no rule in
the command looks at.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. `form` is two tables sharing one title and one value column (a decision for a person; I would split)

**What a reader sees.** The value column is headed

> $R_n^{|l|}(t)$ or $Z_n^l(x,y)$

and every row's `form` cell repeats one half of that disjunction. The title is
"Zernike polynomials $Z_n^l$", and 100 of the 188 entries are not $Z_n^l$.

**Why it is wrong.** This is the tell the skill describes, in the most literal
form it takes: "a table of one quantity heads its column with that quantity's
symbol ... and a table of two falls back on the word `value`, because no
symbol is true of every row". T251 does not fall back on the word; it writes
the disjunction out. That is the same admission.

The three good answers do not apply. It is not one number in two conventions:
$R_4^2(t) = 4t^4-3t^2$ and $Z_4^2(x,y) = 4x^4-4y^4-3x^2+3y^2$ are different
polynomials in different numbers of variables, and a reader arriving with one
is not arriving with the other. It is not a parameter that is an argument:
`radial` and `cartesian` are names. The remaining case is "parts of one
object", and the evidence in the table is against it:

- **The parts are not co-present.** Only 36 of the 152 $(n,l)$ pairs carry
  both forms. Rows with $n\geq13$ are radial only; rows with $l<0$ are
  Cartesian only. The $abc$-triple stores $a$, $b$ and $c$ on every row
  because a triple is the thing; here each row has whichever half its own
  range reached.
- **The two halves have different ranges, chosen separately**, $n\leq20$ and
  $n\leq12$, for the size reason in finding 4.
- **The two halves index differently.** $l$ is titled "signed azimuthal
  degree", and it is signed only on the Cartesian half; on the radial half it
  is $|l|$, which is the $m$ that every formula in the table calls $m$. One
  parameter is carrying two meanings, and `comment-forms` -- which is comment
  (10) on the page, below nine formulas -- is where a reader is told.
- **The table already has the second name.** `Keywords` begins "Zernike radial
  polynomials".

The corpus has settled this shape before, in the case the skill names. `form:
ehrhart | h-star` became four tables: T234 "Ehrhart polynomials of the
hypersimplices" with header `$L_{\Delta(k,n)}(t)$`, T244 "Ehrhart
$h^*$-polynomials of the hypersimplices" with header
`$h^*_{\Delta(k,n)}(z)$`, and the same pair for the permutohedra at T235 and
T245. Each ended with one symbol at the top of its column.

**The smallest change that would fix it.** Two tables:

- **"Zernike polynomials $Z_n^l$"**, parameters $n$ and signed $l$, header
  `$Z_n^l(x,y)$`, $n\leq12$;
- **"Zernike radial polynomials $R_n^m$"**, parameters $n$ and $m\geq0$,
  header `$R_n^m(t)$`, $n\leq20$;

related in `Similar tables` by the sentence that says what the relation is --
"$R_n^m$ is the radial factor: $Z_n^l(\rho,\varphi)=R_n^{|l|}(\rho)\cos
(l\varphi)$ for $l\geq0$" -- which is the valuable part and which one table
cannot hold, because inside one table there is nothing to relate. Both keep
the same `Formulas`, the same `Links` and the same tags. Nothing is lost:
"Zernike radial polynomials" stops being a keyword bolted onto somebody else's
title and becomes a title, which is the higher-weighted field.

**The argument for leaving it alone**, which somebody should weigh rather than
take from me: $R$ genuinely is a part of $Z$, and an optics reader who has one
often wants the other beside it. If that argument wins, then the title, the
number-header and the $l$ parameter all have to be made true of both halves --
"Zernike polynomials and their radial parts", a header naming both quantities
honestly, and $l$ retitled to say it is unsigned on the radial rows -- and the
two ranges need the justification of finding 4 even more than they do now.

### 2. $\operatorname{Re}(x+iy)^m$ reads as $(\operatorname{Re}(x+iy))^m$ (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** `formula-cartesian` renders as

> If $R_n^m(\rho)=\sum_s c_s\rho^{m+2s}$, then $Z_n^l(x,y)=\sum_s
> c_s(x^2+y^2)^s\operatorname{Re}(x+iy)^m$ for $l=m>0$ ...

**Why it is wrong.** The natural parse of `Re(x+iy)^m` is
$(\operatorname{Re}(x+iy))^m = x^m$, which is not what the formula means and
gives the wrong polynomial for every $l\neq0$ row. The intended reading is the
real part of the $m$-th power. This matters more than a usual typographic
slip: `formula-cartesian` is the only place a reader can reconstruct the
Cartesian half, `rigour details` names it as how the entries were built, and
the generator's own docstring gets it right (`Re((x+i y)^m)`), so only the
LaTeX lost the brackets.

**The smallest change.** Brackets on both:
`\operatorname{Re}\left[(x+iy)^m\right]` and
`\operatorname{Im}\left[(x+iy)^m\right]`.

### 3. "The entries are unnormalised" does not say which scale (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** `comment-normalisation` opens "The entries are
unnormalised", then gives the factors $\sqrt{n+1}$ and $\sqrt{2(n+1)}$ that
recover the OSA/ANSI and Noll modes.

**Why it is wrong.** "Unnormalised" says which scale the entries are *not* in.
A reader holding a polynomial from a package that scales differently needs the
positive statement, and the only place the table makes it is inside `rigour
details`, as one of the checks the generator runs: "the radial polynomials
must satisfy $R_n^m(1)=1$". That is a fact about the run standing in for a
convention. `Comments` is the section for conventions.

**The smallest change.** One clause: "The entries are unnormalised, scaled so
that $R_n^m(1)=1$."

### 4. The completeness note gives two ranges and no reason for either (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

> complete: no (it holds every non-monomial radial polynomial with $n\leq20$
> and every non-monomial Cartesian polynomial with $n\leq12$)

**Why it is wrong.** The skill asks the completeness note to carry the
argument: "Say which range you chose and why ... That sentence is the argument,
and it is what a reader checking whether their own number belongs here actually
reads." Two different bounds with no reason read as arbitrary, and a reader
with a degree-14 Cartesian mode cannot tell whether the table stops there on
purpose or was not finished. The reason is in the entries and is a good one:
the Cartesian entries grow much faster. Measured on this table, the largest
entry at each $n$:

    n      radial   Cartesian
    8          38         159
    10         51         250
    12         65         365
    20        134           -

**The smallest change.** Finish the sentence: "... and every non-monomial
Cartesian polynomial with $n\leq12$, where they stop sooner because at the same
degree they are about five times as long: $Z_{12}^0$ is 365 characters against
65 for $R_{12}^0$." (If finding 1 is acted on, each table carries its own half of
this.)

### 5. `comment-uses` misdescribes where the aberration names are, and how far they go (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** The last sentence of `comment-uses`: "The classical
aberration names for the first modes are included in the Cartesian entry
comments."

**Why it is wrong.** Two things a reader would have to discover by scrolling.
The names are in the *radial* comments too -- "defocus" on $R_2^0$, "primary
spherical aberration" on $R_4^0$, "vertical secondary astigmatism" on $R_4^2$,
four radial entries in all. And "the first modes" is $n\leq4$: 16 of the 188
entries carry a name and 172 do not, so a reader at $n=5$ cannot tell whether
the name was omitted or does not exist.

**The smallest change.** "Entries with $n\leq4$ carry the classical aberration
name, in both forms; the higher modes have no standard name."

### 6. The `Similar tables` relation for the Bessel zeros describes the formula, not the relation (small)

**What a reader sees.**

> Zeros of Bessel functions of the first kind -- the Hankel transform in (9)
> has $J_{n+1}$ in its numerator

**Why it is wrong.** That sentence is about formula (9). It gives a reader no
reason to follow the link, because having $J_{n+1}$ in a numerator is not a
relation to a table of *zeros*. The relation is that the Hankel transform of
$R_n^m$ vanishes exactly at those zeros, which is a fact about both tables and
is the reason somebody would click. The other two relations in the section are
written the right way round.

**The smallest change.** "the Hankel transform of $R_n^m$ in (9) vanishes
exactly at the zeros of $J_{n+1}$".

### 7. "the checked initial Noll and Fringe lists" -- checked against what? (small)

**What a reader sees.** `rigour details` ends "and the single-index formulas
must reproduce the checked initial Noll and Fringe lists."

**Why it is wrong.** Every other clause in that paragraph names what it
checked against: the Jacobi identity at (4), $R_n^m(1)=1$, the orthogonality
relation at (5). This one names a list by an adjective. A reader cannot repeat
the check, and "checked" inside a description of the checking is circular.
The source exists and the table already declares it: the table in CITE{Wiki}
gives all four single indices up to $n=4$, and I confirmed the stored comments
against it.

**The smallest change.** "must reproduce the four single indices tabulated for
$n\leq4$ in CITE{Wiki}".

### 8. `Programs` answers "one more value" for the radial half only (small)

**What a reader sees.** The snippet defines `radial(n, m)` and ends
`radial(22, 0)  # the next even radial row after this table`.

**Why it is wrong.** `Programs` is "the standard incantation ... for a reader
who wants one more value", and half the table's values are Cartesian. A reader
who wants $Z_{13}^{1}(x,y)$ is sent to `formula-cartesian`, which is the
formula that finding 2 is about. Four more lines over
`PolynomialRing(ZZ, ('x','y'))` would cover it.

**The smallest change.** Add the conversion, or -- if finding 1 is acted on --
give the Cartesian table its own snippet and leave this one as the radial
table's.

## Notes

**A. The trefoil names are right; do not "fix" them.** The table puts
"vertical" on $l>0$ and "oblique" on $l<0$ for astigmatism ($n=2$), secondary
astigmatism ($n=4$) and quadrafoil ($n=4$), and the other way round for
trefoil: $Z_3^{3}=x^3-3xy^2$ is "oblique trefoil" and $Z_3^{-3}=3x^2y-y^3$ is
"vertical trefoil". It looks like a transposition and it is not. CITE{Wiki},
which the table cites, says exactly that, and the four single indices in both
comments match it too. The inconsistency is the source's. A reviewer's eye
will land on this, so half a clause in `comment-uses` -- "the trefoil names
follow CITE{Wiki}, which names them opposite to the astigmatism and quadrafoil
pairs" -- would save the next person from correcting a correct table. I would
not regenerate 188 entries for it.

**B. The radial comment at $l=0$ is not a sentence.** For $l>0$ it reads
"Radial part for $l=2$ (Noll $j=12$, ...) and $l=-2$ (Noll $j=13$, ...)",
which parses. For $l=0$ the same template collapses to "Radial part for Noll
$j=4$, OSA/ANSI $j=4$, Fringe $j=4$, Wyant $j=3$; defocus." -- a radial part
*for* an index. "Radial part of the mode with Noll $j=4$, ..." is the fix. It
is the 10 radial entries with $l=0$ and needs a regeneration, so it is worth
doing only when the generator next runs.

**C. The `form` column and the value column say the same two things.** A row
reads `4 | 2 | $R_n^{|l|}(t)$: | 4*t^4 - 3*t^2`, printing generic $n$ and $l$
beside the columns that give their values, and the header above repeats both
labels joined by "or". Naming the values `$R$` and `$Z$`, or leaving them as
the words `radial` and `cartesian`, would read better. This is the right
mechanism -- the values of `form` are words, which is what per-value LaTeX is
for -- and the noise goes away entirely under finding 1, so it is not worth
touching on its own.

**D. `form`'s `display` is its own key.** The column is headed `form`, which
is the parameter's address rather than a word a reader knows. `representation`
would do. Cosmetic, and also dissolved by finding 1.

**E. Size.** 188 entries, an entries block of 45 KB, largest entry 365
characters. Comfortably inside every limit and inside the readability bound the
skill asks about -- $Z_{12}^0$ is a third the length of the $F_{100}$ the skill
settles on as the house range. Nothing to do.
