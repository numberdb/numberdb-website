## What reads well

The Definition defines the table and then stops. It gives $\varphi$, says
what $\theta_r$ is by rank inside $(1,\varphi)$, and names what is stored: the
monic minimal polynomial. Two people would build the same table from it. The
parameter describes the family ($r\geq1$), and the range is in the
complete-note. The column header $m_r(x)$ matches what is stored.

It is also right that this is a table apart from T286. A number and its
minimal polynomial are not one value in two conventions: they are of
different types, and T286 could hold the polynomials only as text in its row
comments, where no search reaches them.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Every row says "selected from $P_n$", which does not say how the row relates to $P_n$ (worth doing)

A reader sees this on all 50 rows, in the first screen:

> $\theta_{1}$ is the root in $(1,\varphi)$; this polynomial is selected from
> $P_{2}$.

"Selected from" is the generator's verb, not a statement about mathematics.
A reader has to guess whether $m_1$ *is* $P_2$ or a factor of it, and the
answer differs from row to row. Row 2 is $Q_2$ itself. Row 1 is $P_2/(x-1)$,
and row 4 is $P_3/(x^2-1)$. "The root in $(1,\varphi)$" also runs ahead of
its antecedent: the root of what only becomes clear at "this polynomial",
after the semicolon.

**Smallest fix:** one sentence per row that states the relation, using the
quotients checked above:

- $Q_n$ and $E$ rows: `$m_{2}=Q_{2}$. Its root in $(1,\varphi)$ is HREF{...#2}[$\theta_{2}$].`
- $P_n$ rows: `$P_{2}(x)=(x-1)\,m_{1}(x)$. Its root in $(1,\varphi)$ is HREF{...#1}[$\theta_{1}$].`
  For odd $n$ the factor is $(x^2-1)$.

This is a change to the generator's comment template, not to 50 hand edits.

### 2. The keywords promise the plastic ratio, the supergolden ratio and Mahler measure, and the page never mentions them (worth doing)

`Keywords` carries "plastic ratio", "supergolden ratio" and "Mahler measure".
Once the table is published, somebody who types "plastic ratio" lands here,
or somebody who arrives holding $x^3-x-1$, the likeliest polynomial on the
page. Nothing on this page tells them what they have. T286 does: its row 1
says "This is the plastic ratio, the smallest Pisot number", its row 4 says
"This is the supergolden ratio", and its `comment-mahler` explains the
Mahler measure. T300 copied the keywords but not the sentences. (The
lesson that a keyword is a promise the page must keep is already proposed in
`agents/lessons/proposals/20260913T200305Z-critique.md`.)

The Mahler-measure fact is more at home here than in T286, because it is a
fact about these polynomials: every $m_r$ has exactly one root outside the
unit circle, so $M(m_r)=\theta_r$.

**Smallest fix:** add "This is the minimal polynomial of the plastic ratio,
the smallest Pisot number" to row 1 and "…of the supergolden ratio" to row 4.
Add a comment such as "The Mahler measure of $m_r$ is $\theta_r$, since
$m_r$ is monic and its other roots lie inside the unit disc." Or drop the
three keywords.

### 3. The second sentence of `comment-classification` points at polynomials it does not name (worth doing, small)

> The polynomials $m_r(x)$ here are the irreducible factors whose roots in
> $(1,\varphi)$ give that increasing sequence, as recorded in [2] and [3] for
> the classification.

The sentence says "irreducible factors" but never says what they are factors
of: $P_n$, $Q_n$ and $E$ are introduced only later, in Formula (1). "As
recorded … for the classification" trails off, and a reader cannot tell
whether [2] and [3] record the polynomials, the factors or the
classification.

**Smallest fix:** "Each $m_r(x)$ is the irreducible factor with a root in
$(1,\varphi)$ of one of the polynomials $P_n$, $Q_n$ or $E$ of Formula
CITE{formula-families} CITE{Bertin} CITE{McKeeSmyth}."

### 4. `comment-roots` describes the page, not the mathematics (worth doing, trivial)

> Each entry comment links to the matching root $\theta_r$ in the
> Pisot-number table.

This is a remark about how the website is laid out. The reader can already
see the links on every row, and `Similar tables` states the relation to T286
properly. It also points at "the Pisot-number table" instead of naming it.
**Smallest fix:** delete it. If a link to T286 is wanted near the top, put
it on $\theta_r$ in the Definition. That is also the first mention of the
family T286 holds.

## Noted only, not worth a change on their own

- **Formula (1) says the roots "come from" $P_n$, $Q_n$ and $E$.** T286's
  version of the same formula is precise ("The set $\{\theta_r\}$ consists of
  the roots in $(1,\varphi)$ of …"). Copying that sentence would cost
  nothing. If finding 1 is done, the vagueness matters less.
- **The `Programs` snippet assigns `RR = RealIntervalField(400)`.** Pasted
  into a Sage session, this replaces Sage's global `RR`. T286's snippet calls
  it `IR`. The snippet is otherwise correct.
- **The complete-note ends "matching the range in the root table exactly".**
  "Exactly" adds nothing. T286's note gives the actual reason for stopping
  at 50: the two families are within $3\cdot10^{-6}$ of $\varphi$ by then.
  A reader of this table would be better served by that reason than by the
  cross-reference.
- **T286's rows 1, 4, 6, 9, … link to the Coxeter triangle growth rates, and
  T300 has no such link.** That is fine: the growth rate is a fact about the
  number, and T286 is where it belongs.
