## Findings, ranked

### 1. The page says "Table is complete: false", and the generator's own `table.yaml` says `no`

**What a reader sees.** Under *Data properties*:

> Table is complete: **false** (it holds every $n$ with $1\leq n\leq7$)

**Why it is wrong.** Two reasons, and the second is the interesting one.

Read as English, "Table is complete: false" is a programmer's word in a
sentence a person reads. The corpus does not use it: of the 34 tables T310–T343,
27 say `no`, 6 say `yes`, one has no `complete` at all, and **none says
`false`**. The site's own vocabulary for the field is `('yes', 'no',
'unknown')`, and `metadata_form.py` flags anything else as `complete_is_odd`,
so this table will show up in its own edit form as "something else".

And the document is not what the generator says it is.
`generators/division-polynomials-short-weierstrass/table.yaml:41` reads
`complete: no`. The live document stores the **string** `"false"`. That is a
complete YAML-1.1 round trip and it is reproducible:

    yaml.safe_load('complete: no')      -> False        # YAML 1.1 boolean
    json.dumps(...)                     -> false
    yaml.load(json, Loader=BaseLoader)  -> 'false'      # what the API stores

The API reads incoming documents with `BaseLoader` precisely so that
`complete: no` survives (`numberdb_app/api.py:706`), but that only protects a
document posted *as YAML*. This one went through a loader that coerced it
first. Posting the same `table.yaml` bytes unchanged keeps `'no'`; I checked.

**Smallest fix.** Set `Data properties: complete` to `no`, matching the
committed `table.yaml`. Worth doing.

### 2. "specialises division polynomials to reduced global minimal models" — that specialisation does not generally exist

**What a reader sees.** In *Similar tables*, beside *Division polynomials
$\psi_n$ of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$*:

> specialises division polynomials to reduced global minimal models of
> elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$, while this table keeps the universal
> short-model coefficients $A$ and $B$

**Why it is wrong.** A reader takes "specialises" at its word: substitute
numbers for $A$ and $B$ in an entry here and you get an entry there. That is
false for most curves. The sibling's Definition recovers a *reduced global
minimal Weierstrass model* from $(c_4,c_6)$, and such a model generally has
$a_1,a_2,a_3$ nonzero — its own comment (7) writes the two-torsion factor as
$\psi_n/(2y+a_1x+a_3)$, which is the giveaway. This table's $\psi_n$ are for
$y^2=x^3+Ax+B$, i.e. $a_1=a_2=a_3=0$. The two agree only on the curves whose
minimal model happens to be short.

That matters more here than it would elsewhere, because the reader most
likely to follow this link is one holding a $\psi_n$ and trying to decide
which of the two tables answers them.

**Smallest fix.** Name the relation instead of asserting a substitution:
"holds the same polynomials for individual curves over $\mathbb{Q}$, in their
reduced global minimal Weierstrass models, where this table keeps $A$ and $B$
formal in a short model". Worth doing.

### 3. The ranks table is described by what it is indexed by, not by what it records

**What a reader sees.** Beside *Highest known ranks of elliptic curves over
$\mathbb{Q}$ with prescribed torsion subgroup*:

> records rational torsion subgroups, while the roots of division polynomials
> detect torsion points on individual elliptic curves

**Why it is wrong.** It does not record torsion subgroups; it records, for
each possible rational torsion subgroup $T$, the highest known rank. A reader
who follows the description rather than the title expects a list of subgroups.
The sibling table states the same relation correctly — "records the highest
known rank for each rational torsion subgroup, while the roots of $\psi_n$
detect torsion points on individual curves" — so this is a degraded copy of a
sentence that already exists in the corpus.

**Smallest fix.** Use the sibling's sentence. It is also the shorter one, and
it uses this table's own symbol $\psi_n$ where the current text says "division
polynomials" for the third time on the page. Worth doing.

### 4. Comment (2) specialises to the model the reader already has

**What a reader sees.**

> The variables are named $x$, $y$, $A$ and $B$. Under the specialisation to
> a short model $y^2=x^3+Ax+B$, the roots of $\psi_n$ are the nonzero
> $n$-torsion points away from the poles of $x$.

**Why it is wrong.** The Definition has already fixed $E: y^2=x^3+Ax+B$. So
"under the specialisation to a short model $y^2=x^3+Ax+B$" instructs a reader
to specialise to what they are already looking at, and they have to guess what
was meant — which is: specialise the *formal* $A$ and $B$ to elements of a
field. The sentence never says that, and never says $4A^3+27B^2\neq0$, without
which the claim is false. The nonsingularity condition appears nowhere in the
prose; the nearest thing is "five nonsingular integer pairs $(A,B)$" buried in
*How they were obtained*.

The second half is true but reads two ways. Taking $\psi_n$ as a function on
$E$, its zeros are exactly the points $P\neq O$ with $nP=O$ — correct, and
correct for even $n$ too. But the entry is presented as a *polynomial* in
$\mathbb{Z}[x,y,A,B]$, and read that way "the roots" are values of $x$, not
points; PARI's manual states the same fact as "the roots of $f_n$ are the
$X$-coordinates of the affine points in $E[n]$". A reader has to decide which
reading is intended. And "away from the poles of $x$" excludes only $O$, which
"nonzero" has already excluded — so the qualifier looks like it is holding
something back and is not.

**Smallest fix.** Replace the second sentence with something like: "Specialise
$A$ and $B$ to elements of a field $K$ with $4A^3+27B^2\neq0$. As a function
on $E$, $\psi_n$ then vanishes exactly at the points $P\neq O$ of
$E(\overline{K})$ with $nP=O$; for odd $n$, its roots in $x$ are the
$x$-coordinates of those points." Worth doing.

### 5. No `Programs` block, on a table that stops at $n=7$ on purpose

**What a reader sees.** No *Programs* section. The page tells them the table
holds $n\leq7$ and gives them nothing for $\psi_8$.

**Why it is wrong.** The skill lists `Programs` as "the standard incantation
in Sage, PARI or mpmath for a reader who wants one more value", and says a
table wants it *and* a generator "where both apply". Eighteen of the nineteen
tables T325–T343 have one; the sibling division-polynomial table has one; this
one does not. The omission bites harder here than usual, because the range
stops for a readability reason rather than a computability one — the reader
who wants $\psi_8$ is not an edge case, they are the expected visitor.

**Smallest fix.** Three lines, which I ran:

    sage: S.<A,B> = QQ[]; E = EllipticCurve(S.fraction_field(), [A, B])
    sage: E.division_polynomial_0(8)        # psi_8/(2*y); multiply by 2*y
    sage: E.division_polynomial_0(9)        # psi_9, odd n, as stored

Worth doing.

### 6. Formula (3) translates PARI's convention but not Sage's

**What a reader sees.** Formula (3) tells them how `elldivpol` relates to the
stored $\psi_n$. Nothing on the page mentions Sage.

**Why it is worth changing.** The skill's reader has Sage, and Sage offers
three conventions under two names. I checked all three at $A=B=1$:
`E.division_polynomial(4)` is $8x^9+48x^7+\dots$, i.e. it agrees with PARI's
`elldivpol` and equals the stored $\psi_4$ times $2y$;
`E.division_polynomial_0(4)` is $2x^6+10x^4+\dots$, i.e. $\psi_4/(2y)$;
`two_torsion_multiplicity=1` gives $\psi_4$ itself. A reader comparing a Sage
result against a row here gets a factor of $2y$ or $(2y)^2$ wrong and has
nothing on the page to tell them which. The sibling table names both PARI and
Sage in its comment (7).

**Smallest fix.** One clause in Formula (3) or Comment (1): "Sage's
`E.division_polynomial(n)` follows the same convention as `elldivpol`;
`E.division_polynomial_0(n)` gives $\psi_n$ for odd $n$ and $\psi_n/(2y)$ for
even $n$." Worth doing, cheaply.

### 7. "in the coordinate ring of $E$" is stranded at the end of Formula (2)

*Noted because I noticed it.* Formula (2) gives two recurrences and ends "in
the coordinate ring of $E$". It belongs to the second one, because that is
where $\psi_m/\psi_2$ has to be exact; as written it sits after both and a
reader has to work out which it qualifies. Moving it inside — "where
$\psi_m/\psi_2$ is taken in the coordinate ring of $E$" — costs nothing.

### 8. Comment (2) opens by restating the Definition

*Noted because I noticed it, and I would leave it.* "The variables are named
$x$, $y$, $A$ and $B$" follows a Definition that already said
$\mathbb{Z}[x,y,A,B]$. It earns its place anyway: the entries are stored as
literal strings containing `A` and `B`, and a reader matching text against a
row wants the letters named. If finding 4 is acted on, this sentence should
survive the edit.

## What I checked hardest and found nothing

**The PARI sentence, which I expected to be backwards.** Formula (3) says
$f_n(x)=\psi_n(x,y)\cdot2y$ for even $n$ — multiplying, where the obvious
guess is that PARI strips the $2y$. It is right. PARI's manual states, in its
own notation, "$f_n(X)=\psi_n(X)$ for $n$ odd, and
$f_n(X)=\psi_n(X,Y)(2Y+a_1X+a_3)$ for $n$ even", which for a short model is
the table's sentence verbatim. Confirmed numerically: at $A=B=1$,
`elldivpol(E,4)` is the degree-9 $8x^9+48x^7+168x^6+\dots$, and the stored
$\psi_4$ times $2y$, reduced by $y^2=x^3+Ax+B$, is the same polynomial. Same
at $(A,B)=(-2,3)$. Whoever wrote that formula got a genuinely easy thing right.

**The values.** All seven rebuilt independently from Sage's
`E.division_polynomial_0(n)` over $\mathbb{Q}(A,B)$ — a different code path
from the generator's own recurrence and from PARI — and converted to the
table's convention ($\times\,2y$ for even $n$). Every one matched exactly:
`ALL MATCH: True`. Degrees and leading coefficients are the expected
$(n^2-1)/2$ and $n$ for odd $n$, $2y\cdot$(degree $(n^2-4)/2$, leading $n/2$)
for even.

**The range.** $\psi_7$ written out is 1,109 characters and $\psi_8$ is 2,043.
The skill draws its readability line between $F_{100}$ at 1,107 characters
(kept) and $F_{150}$ at 2,248 (dropped), so the stop at $n=7$ lands on it
almost exactly. Size is not what binds and is not the argument: $n\leq12$
would be about 38 KB of entries against a 320 KB soft block limit. I measured
this before reading the generator, which records the same two numbers in its
`UP_TO` comment. The range is right and `complete-note` says what it covers.

**The rendering.** No "Math input error", no `argument ()`, no
"(Unknown key)", no stray `<` or `&` — the document contains none of those
characters at all, so the unescaped-prose bug cannot bite here. All seven
entry rows present with their anchors. All **57** distinct `$...$` fragments
in the document typeset under MathJax 3 with zero errors; a deliberately
broken control fragment did error, so the check was live.

**The links and the tags.** Both `Similar tables` slugs resolve 200, and both
captions are the targets' titles verbatim — no slug invented from a
half-remembered name. Both outward URLs answer 200. All three tags —
`number theory`, `elliptic curves`, `polynomial` — are existing corpus tags on
`/tags`, not singletons invented here. (A previous commit, `09d9803`, had
already dropped a one-table `torsion` tag.)

**Is it one table?** Yes, and not marginally. One integer parameter `n`, no
`param-latex`, and the value column is headed $\psi_n(x,y)$ — one symbol true
of every row — rather than falling back on the word `value`.

**Would a reader arriving with one of these be served?** Yes. The entries
block is the first screen; the value column names what they are; and
Comment (1) answers the one question such a reader actually has, which is
whether the $2y$ is in or out. That comment is the best thing on the page and
should not be touched.
