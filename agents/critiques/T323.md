# T323 — Special values of the $L$-functions of level one cusp forms

Read 2026-09-18 as a reader, against <https://numberdb.org/skill> fetched the
same day. The table is a private draft: 681 entries over fourteen weights
$12\leq k\leq40$, type `R`, 30 significant digits, rigour
`heuristic (agreement-checked)`.

`GET /api/table/T323/audit` answers `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. I agree
that nothing in its rule set applies here — every `HREF` target resolves, the
value column carries a symbol rather than the word `value`, the parameters
describe the family and not the run, and `complete-note` finishes its own

looks at: one is a mathematical claim in the Definition, the other is a
`Programs` block that is right where it was tested and wrong one weight over.

## Findings

### 1. The Definition defines the value by a series that does not converge at about half the entries

A reader sees, in the first screen:

> this table gives the critical value $L(f_{k,i},s)=\sum_{n\geq1}a_n n^{-s}$
> of its $L$-function

and, four sections down, the table's own Formula (2):

> For $\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$, $L(f,s)=\prod_p(\dots)^{-1}$.

The Dirichlet series $\sum a_n n^{-s}$ converges (absolutely, by Deligne)
exactly where that Euler product does, $\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$. The
parameter $s$ runs over $1\leq s\leq k-1$. So for $k=12$ the equation in the
Definition is false at $s=1,\dots,6$ — six of the eleven rows, including
$s=6$, the row the `Programs` block prints — and across the table it is false
at roughly half of the 681 entries. Two people building this table from the
Definition alone could not, because at most rows the right-hand side is not a
number.

What the table means is the analytic continuation, and it should say so. It is
also the sentence that makes the word "critical" mean something: the critical
integers are exactly $1,\dots,k-1$, and the Definition is where a reader looks
for the reason the parameter stops there.

Smallest fix: "…gives the value at $s$ of its $L$-function
$L(f_{k,i},s)$, which for $\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$ is
$\sum_{n\geq1}a_nn^{-s}$ and continues to an entire function of $s$."

### 2. The `Programs` block returns the right value and the wrong form one weight over

Both snippets, run verbatim, return the stored $(12,1,6)$ entry to all thirty
digits:

    0.792122838646030569355944890486735838151910794278905311841540293658…
    stored: 0.792122838646030569355944890487

Sage's `Newforms(1, 12, names='a')[0].lseries(prec=330)(6)` and PARI's
`lfun(lfunmf(mfinit([1,12],0))[1][1], 6)` both give it. That much is honest.

But weight 12 is one of the nine weights here with $\dim S_k=1$, where the
index $i$ cannot be got wrong. A reader who wants one more value — which is
what `Programs` is for — changes the `12` and keeps the `[1][1]`, and at
weight 24 gets the row this table files under $i=2$:

    k=24  PARI lfunmf embedding order of a_2:  5096.351, -4016.351   NOT increasing
    k=34                                      49679.437, -171359.437  NOT increasing
    k=38                                     286011.754, -480411.754  NOT increasing
    k=28, 30, 32, 36, 40                      increasing, by luck

Three of the eight multi-form weights in the table. Sage's embedding order
happened to be increasing at all eight, so the Sage snippet extends safely
today and is not guaranteed to.

The table already knows about this: `comment-ordering` opens "Library
orderings are not used as table orderings." Then `Programs` hands the reader
a library ordering with no word about re-sorting, and the two fields
contradict each other from four inches apart.

Smallest fix: one line in the PARI snippet that picks the embedding by $a_2$
rather than by position — or, if the snippets are to stay as short as they
are, a clause in `comment-ordering` naming `lfunmf` as an ordering that must
be re-sorted before it matches $i$.

### 3. `comment-ordering` is three sentences and a reader can use none of them as written

> Library orderings are not used as table orderings. In each weight, the
> generator embeds the Hecke field into the real numbers, computes the
> corresponding value of $a_2$, and then sorts the forms by that real value.
> Each entry comment names the same $a_2$ ordering through the row $(k,2)$ of
> the Hecke polynomial table.

Three separate things:

* "Library orderings" names no library. A reader cannot tell whether Sage,
  PARI, the LMFDB or all three are meant, and finding 2 is the case where it
  matters.
* The middle sentence is what the generator did, not what $i$ is. `rigour
  details` already says it — "It sorts the embeddings of each Hecke orbit by
  the embedded value of $a_2$" — so it is said twice, and the copy in
  `Comments` is in the wrong home.
* "names the same $a_2$ ordering through the row $(k,2)$" is not a sentence
  anybody parses on first reading, and "the row $(k,2)$" points at a row
  instead of naming it. The 681 entry comments call it $\chi_{k,2}$; so does
  the table they link to.

Smallest fix, roughly: "The index $i$ counts the eigenforms of weight $k$ in
increasing order of $a_2$; this is not the order Sage or PARI lists them in.
Each entry gives its own $a_2$ and links to $\chi_{k,2}$, the characteristic
polynomial of $T_2$, whose roots are the $a_2$ of that weight."

That last clause also fixes a smaller thing: $\chi_{k,2}$ appears in every one
of the 681 entry comments and is defined nowhere in this table.

### 4. $a_2$ is written as a sixteen-digit decimal where it is an integer

Nine weights have $\dim S_k=1$, and every one of their rows reads

> $a_2=-24.00000000000000$, the root of $\chi_{12,2}$.

with an equals sign. $a_2=\tau(2)=-24$, exactly; the fourteen zeros claim a
measurement where there is an integer, which is what "decide exactness from
the definition, never from the digits" is about. The comment on the
multi-dimensional weights gets this right — "$a_2\approx -4016.351171716245$"
— so the two shapes disagree with each other about what is being asserted.

Smallest fix: `$a_2=-24$` on the nine one-dimensional weights (−24, 216, −528,
456, −288, −48 at $k=12,16,18,20,22,26$ and likewise elsewhere).

### 5. "the critical strip is $0<\operatorname{Re}s<k$" sits one line from "critical value" and means something else

The strip where the nontrivial zeros lie, under this normalisation, is
$(k-1)/2<\operatorname{Re}s<(k+1)/2$: the table's own Euler product is
absolutely convergent for $\operatorname{Re}s>(k+1)/2$ and so has no zeros
there, and the functional equation reflects that to
$\operatorname{Re}s<(k-1)/2$. $0<\operatorname{Re}s<k$ is the region the
functional equation reflects, and it is where the critical *integers*
$1,\dots,k-1$ live — which is what the sentence wants to say, and is the only
place on the page that would earn the parameter's range.

I am calling this loose rather than flatly wrong: Wikipedia's Riemann
hypothesis article does write "the critical strip $\Re(s)\in(0,n)$" for the
arithmetic zeta function of a scheme of dimension $n$, so the usage exists.
But a reader meeting "critical strip" a line away from "critical value" will
take the two to be the same idea, and for $k=12$ they are $5.5<\sigma<6.5$ and
$\{1,\dots,11\}$. I could not reach the LMFDB knowl for house usage — it
answers this runner with a Cloudflare interstitial.

Smallest fix: "…so the functional equation relates $s$ and $k-s$, the centre
is $s=k/2$, and the critical integers are $s=1,\dots,k-1$."

### 6. "shifting $s$ by $(k-1)/2$" does not say which way

> The analytic normalisation often written with centre $1/2$ is obtained by
> shifting $s$ by $(k-1)/2$.

A reader has to guess the sign, and guessing wrong moves every value in the
table. The relation is one formula and it is shorter than the sentence
pointing at it: $L^{\mathrm{an}}(f,s)=L(f,s+(k-1)/2)$.

### 7. `rigour details` says "this draft"

> …so this draft stores the precision supported by both implementations.

It stops being true the moment somebody publishes it, and the sentence before
it already gave the number ("stores only $30$ digits"), so the vague phrase
is carrying nothing. "edge critical values", in the same sentence, is not
defined anywhere; a reader guesses it means $s$ near $1$ and $k-1$.

Smallest fix: "PARI and Sage did not stably agree to $100$ digits at the
critical integers nearest $s=1$ and $s=k-1$, so the table stores $30$."

## Noted only

* **The search remark in `comment-central-zero`.** "Those forced zeros are
  omitted from the entries rather than making every search for $0$ match this
  table." The first half is the fact and is enough; the second is a remark
  about this website's search, and `complete-note` already records the
  omission. Everything before it in that comment is good — the sign is
  $(-1)^{k/2}$, the zero is forced, and a reader holding $0$ is told why the
  row is not here.

* **The ordering is stated three times** — in the Definition, in the
  constraint on $i$, and in `comment-ordering`. Also "ordered by increasing
  real $T_2$-eigenvalue $a_2$" in the Definition reads as if there were
  complex eigenvalues being excluded; the word doing the work is the
  embedding, not "real".

* **"Ramanujan tau L-function" is a keyword the page never earns.** Neither
  "Ramanujan" nor $\tau$ appears anywhere a reader can see. Somebody who
  searched that phrase and landed here must follow the `Similar tables` link
  to the $q$-expansion of $\Delta$ to learn that the eleven $k=12$ rows are
  the values of $\sum\tau(n)n^{-s}$ — and the linked Hecke-polynomial table
  says it outright ("For $k=12$, $\chi_{12,p}(x)=x-\tau(p)$") where this one
  does not. One clause would close it.

* **Three `Similar tables` rows carry the identical relation.** Ranks 1, 2 and
  3 of the elliptic-curve tables each read "stores leading Taylor coefficients
  for modular $L$-functions attached to elliptic curves". The relation is the
  valuable part of that field, and here it is said once and then repeated
  twice verbatim. What distinguishes them from this table — the same kind of
  $L$-function at weight 2 and higher level, taken at the centre rather than
  across the critical range — would be worth one sentence in place of three
  copies.

* **Three quarters of the entries block is 24 sentences repeated 681 times.**
  The block is 139 KB; strip the comments and it is 36 KB. Each comment is
  constant across every $s$ in its $(k,i)$ group, so a reader scrolling weight
  40 meets the same sentence 39 times. Nothing is near a limit and the fact
  the comment carries — which eigenform this row belongs to — is worth having;
  it is just worth knowing that it is the whole of the difference between this
  table and a third of its size.
