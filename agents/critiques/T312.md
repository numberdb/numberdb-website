# T312: HOMFLY-PT polynomials of the prime knots with at most ten crossings

Read on 2026-09-17, against the skill fetched that day from
<https://numberdb.org/skill> (47,533 bytes). T312 is a draft holding 958 `Z[]`
entries: the unknot and every prime knot $n_k$ with at most ten crossings, each
chiral one also as its mirror image, each in both of the $(v,z)$ and $(l,m)$
normalisations. `rigour: exact`, `complete: yes`.

The mathematics is in order and the table is one table, not two. Its faults are
all about what a reader is told and where. The important one is that **the two
things the page shows first -- the value column's header and the comment on
each row -- are the two that do not say what they mean**: the header says
`shifted HOMFLY-PT polynomial` without saying by what, and 822 rows carry the
bare text `a=2`, `a=-4`. The explanation is two sections further down. The
second is that the table's own justification for being one table -- that the
two normalisations are an invertible change of variables -- is asserted three
times and never written as a formula; T312 has no `Formulas` section at all,
while its three sibling knot tables do.

## How it was read

  `curl` reaches the site from this box, as `docs/agent-environment.md`
  records. `/T312` answers 404 to a request carrying an API key rather than a
  session, which is what a draft does. I read the document with the key
  (`/api/table?id=T312`) and rendered it through `/preview?table=` in nine
  pieces under the 4094-byte request line. All nine returned 200 and drew
  their sections: Definition, Parameters, Display properties, Comments,
  References, Links, Similar tables, Programs, Data properties, and the value
  table with real rows. Every `CITE` resolved to its numbered link or
  reference, and every `HREF` to an anchor. Nothing rendered broken.
- **TeX.** 59 distinct `$...$` fragments, typeset with `mathjax-full` (base +
  ams) in node: 0 errors. A control of three fragments, one with a missing
  brace and one with an undefined macro, reported 2 errors, so the check was
  doing something.
- **Audit.** `GET /api/table/T312/audit` returns two findings, both the
  structural "N names, each carrying entries of its own" question, for `knot`
  and for `normalisation`. I agree that it is right to ask and that both
  answers here are the good kind; see "What reads well". It does not notice
  that the invertibility it asks to be stated is stated nowhere in a form a
  reader can use.
- **Checked, because claims depended on them.** Not the values: the generator
  checks those against KnotInfo's own `homfly_polynomial` column, and `verify`
  and the audit check them again. What I checked is what the document asserts
  about the *shape* of what is stored, on every row, by parsing the entries:
  - Every one of the 958 stored polynomials has lowest exponent zero in both
    of its variables, so the shift is exactly the one `comment-normalisation`
    describes, and the longest entry is 131 characters, as `rigour details`
    says.
  - On all 479 knot-and-mirror rows, substituting $v=i/l$ and $z=im$ into the
    unshifted $(v,z)$ entry gives the unshifted $(l,m)$ entry exactly. The
    invertibility the definition claims is true, and the entry comments' $a$
    are the right shifts.
  - On all 479, the unshifted $(v,z)$ entry at $v=t$, $z=t^{-1/2}-t^{1/2}$
    equals the corresponding entry of the published
    `Jones_polynomials_of_the_prime_knots_with_at_most_ten_crossings`, put
    back through its own $t^{-m}$ shift. So the specialisation claimed in
    `Similar tables` holds, and the two tables agree about which mirror image
    is which for all 229 chiral knots.
- **Also checked.** All four `Similar tables` slugs and the `equals` target
  `One` answer 200. `/files/T312` carries both `generate.py` and
  `knotinfo_prime_knots.py`, so the table has `Programs` and a generator, as
  the skill asks. The counts are consistent with each other and with the
  entries: $229$ chiral $+\,20$ amphichiral $+\,1$ unknot $=250$ knots, $(250
  + 229)\times 2 = 958$ entries, and the 20 knots named in
  `comment-chirality` are $1,1,5,13$ for $n=4,6,8,10$ as it says.

## What reads well

- **It is one table, and for both of the reasons the skill allows.** `knot`
  names *what the number is of* -- $K$ and $\bar K$ are two knots, not two
  quantities -- and `normalisation` is one number in two conventions, which I
  confirmed is genuinely invertible on every row. The value column is not
  headed the word `value`, which is the tell the audit's rule is built on, and
  there is no `param-latex` restating a column header one row at a time.
- **The parameters describe the family.** $n$ and $k$ are bounded by the
  title's own range, not by what somebody ran, and `complete: yes` carries the
  rest.
- **The order is the one a reader wants.** The unknot first, then $3_1$
  upwards, $K$ before $\bar K$, $(v,z)$ before $(l,m)$.
- **`comment-numbering` and `comment-range` are the two paragraphs a reader
  holding a ten-crossing polynomial most needs**, and both are genuine
  sentences that say where the naming comes from and where it stops.

## Findings

### Worth doing

**1. The value column's header does not say what the shift is, and it is the
first thing on the page.** The numbers block is drawn above the Definition
(confirmed on the published Jones page, whose section order is Polynomials,
Definition, Parameters, ...), so the first screen a reader gets is the column
header `shifted HOMFLY-PT polynomial` over rows like

    10  165  $K$  $(v,z)$:  2*v^6*z^2 - v^4*z^4 + ... + z^2    comment: a=-8

A reader who arrived holding `v^2*z^2 + 2*v^2 - v^4` out of Sage is told the
column is "shifted" but not by what, and `a=-8` is a letter they have not met.
Every sibling heads its column with a symbol that says exactly what is stored:
Jones `$t^{-m}V_K(t)$`, Alexander `$\Delta_{n_k}(t)$`, Conway
`$\nabla_{n_k}(z)$`, hyperbolic volumes `$\mathrm{Vol}(S^3\setminus n_k)$`.
*Smallest fix:* `number-header: $v^{-a}P_{n_k}(v,z)$ or $l^{-a}P_{n_k}(l,m)$`.

**2. The 822 entry comments are not mathematics and not sentences.** They read
`a=2`, and they render as `comment: a=2` -- plain text, in the one place the
skill says everything is read as English. The Jones table writes the same fact
as `$m=1$` inside a comment that also names the knot. *Smallest fix:* store
`$a=2$`, so it typesets as the $a$ of `comment-normalisation`. Worth doing at
the same time as finding 1, since the two are read together and only the pair
of them tells a reader what the row holds.

**3. The change of variables between the two normalisations is asserted and
never written.** The Definition says "two invertible coordinate systems",
`rigour details` says "one invertible change of variables", and the audit asks
for exactly this to be stated -- but no line of the table says what the
substitution *is*, so a reader holding an $(l,m)$ polynomial cannot get to the
$(v,z)$ rows, and a reviewer cannot check the claim that makes this one table
rather than two. *Smallest fix:* a `Formulas` entry: "$P_K(l,m)=P_K(v,z)$
under $v=i\,l^{-1}$ and $z=i\,m$; for a knot only even powers of $z$ occur, so
the sign of $m$ does not matter." I verified that substitution on all 479
pairs.

**4. There is no `Formulas` section, and the two skein relations are in
`Comments`.** The skill puts identities in `Formulas` and conventions in
`Comments`, and all three sibling polynomial tables have a `Formulas` section
(Conway's opens with the skein relation, Alexander's with the Seifert matrix).
`comment-normalisation` currently carries the definition of the invariant, two
skein relations, and the shift rule $x^{-a}y^{-b}$ in one paragraph. *Smallest
fix:* move the two skein relations, the shift rule and the substitution of
finding 3 into `Formulas`, and leave `comment-normalisation` saying which
convention this table follows and where the shift is recorded. The three
specialisations that `Similar tables` gestures at -- and that `rigour details`
says were checked -- are formulas too, and naming them ($v=1$, $z=t^{1/2}-
t^{-1/2}$ for Alexander; $v=t$, $z=t^{-1/2}-t^{1/2}$ for Jones; $v=1$ for
Conway) costs three lines and turns "specialises to the Jones polynomial" into
something a reader can use.

**5. The Definition explains the parameters by their keys and does not say
what is stored.** It renders as

> The HOMFLY-PT polynomial $P_K$ [2] of the unknot and of every prime knot
> $n_k$ with at most ten crossings, named after Perko's correction [1]. The
> parameter `knot` chooses KnotInfo's diagram or its mirror image;
> `normalisation` chooses one of two invertible coordinate systems.

Three things. A knot is not "named after Perko's correction"; the naming is
Rolfsen's, *corrected* by Perko, which is what all three siblings say ("named
as in the Rolfsen table CITE{Rolfsen} with Perko's correction CITE{Perko}").
The backticked keys reach the page as monospaced `knot` and `normalisation`,
and the skill is explicit that a parameter's key is an address and not a word
for prose; both sentences also repeat what the `Parameters` block says two
sections below. And the one fact the definition is missing is the one a reader
needs first: that what is listed is the Laurent polynomial shifted to have a
nonzero constant term. Jones's definition ends "listed as $t^{-m}V_K(t)$ with
nonzero constant term", and that is the model. *Smallest fix:* rewrite as one
sentence in the siblings' shape, naming Rolfsen, Perko, KnotInfo's diagram and
the shift, and drop the second sentence.

**6. `Rolfsen` is declared in `References` and cited nowhere.** Two places name
the Rolfsen table in prose -- the title of parameter $k$, and
`comment-numbering`'s "The numbering is Rolfsen's after Perko's correction" --
and neither cites it, so the entry is furniture. Fixing finding 5 fixes this:
`CITE{Rolfsen}` at the first mention.

**7. `rigour details` ends with a size measurement and a design decision.**

> The complete entry block was measured at 958 entries, with longest entry 131
> characters and 165.6 KB of stored entries; the table keeps both common
> normalisations because the family convention treats them as one invertible
> change of variables.

Neither clause is how the numbers were obtained. The first is a note from the
build about fitting under the block limit; the second is the argument for the
table's shape, which is what the Definition and finding 3's formula should
carry. *Smallest fix:* delete the sentence. Everything before it is exactly
what the field is for and reads well.

### Smaller, and cheap

**8. "the reciprocal substitution in the first variable" points instead of
naming** (`rigour details`). Which variable is first depends on which
normalisation the reader has in mind, and the symbol is shorter than the
phrase: "checked against $P_{\bar K}(v,z)=P_K(v^{-1},z)$".

**9. The parameter line renders `normalisation — normalisation`.** The title
repeats the key and adds nothing; the block heading already says the word.
*Smallest fix:* a title that says what the values are, for instance
"coordinates for the HOMFLY-PT polynomial". While there: the constraint calls
$(v,z)$ and $(l,m)$ "Sage's HOMFLY-PT normalisations", which makes two
conventions from the literature sound like a feature of one program -- the
same comment already credits the invariant to two 1985-88 papers. "the two
standard normalisations, computed here with Sage" says it without the
attribution.

**10. `comment-range` points at the parameters rather than naming them:**
"which the two integer parameters here do not express". Write "$n$ and $k$".

### Noted only

**11.** The `Programs` snippet's closing comment says "The entry is `z^2 - v^2
+ 2`", while the table stores `-v^2 + z^2 + 2`. The same polynomial, written in
a different term order from the one the reader sees one section above. One
character's worth of consistency.

**12.** The stored block is 165.6 KB against the skill's "aim at about
160 KB". It is within a rounding of the aim and well under the 320 KB soft
limit, and the range is fixed by the Rolfsen table rather than chosen, so
there is nothing to do; I note it only because the next person extending this
family to eleven crossings cannot, which `comment-range` already explains for
a better reason.

## Left for a person

Nothing is blocked. Findings 1 to 7 are prose and metadata edits to a draft
that nobody has reviewed yet; none of them touches a value. Whether the
$(v,z)$ and $(l,m)$ rows should stay in one table is the audit's question and
mine is that they should: the substitution in finding 3 is invertible and I
checked it on every row, which is the test the skill sets.
