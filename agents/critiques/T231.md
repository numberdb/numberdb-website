## Findings, ranked

### 1. Every row with $a\geq1$ has an elementary closed form, and the page gives one only at $(2,1)$ and $(4,1)$ (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Two rows carry an expression,
$(\gamma+\log2)/2$ and $(\gamma+\pi/2+\log2)/4$. The only general formula is
`formula-digamma`, which trades $\gamma(a,q)$ for $\psi(a/q)$. Somebody who
arrives with $0.1903893\ldots$ from a sum over $n\equiv2\pmod5$ learns that
it is $-(\psi(2/5)+\log5)/5$. That is another special value, not an answer.

**Why it matters.** The reader this table serves summed $1/n$ over a
progression and wants to know what they have. Gauss's digamma theorem makes
each row a finite expression in $\gamma$, $\log2$, $\pi\cot$ and
$\log\sin$:

$$\gamma(a,q)=\frac1q\Bigl(\gamma+\log2+\frac\pi2\cot\frac{\pi a}q-2\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor(q-1)/2\rfloor}\cos\frac{2\pi an}q\,\log\sin\frac{\pi n}q\Bigr)\qquad(1\leq a<q).$$

This holds on all 435 rows to within $5.0\cdot10^{-101}$, and at $(2,1)$
and $(4,1)$ it gives the two comments. The build already checked an
equivalent complex form: `rigour details` mentions "an independent
root-of-unity logarithm formula from [1]" but does not write it, so the page
alludes to a closed form and leaves it out. T226 carries the same theorem as
its `formula-gauss`.

**Smallest fix.**
- Add the identity to `Formulas` as `formula-gauss`: "For $1\leq a<q$,
  $\gamma(a,q)=\frac1q\bigl(\gamma+\log2+\frac\pi2\cot(\pi a/q)-2\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor(q-1)/2\rfloor}\cos(2\pi an/q)\log\sin(\pi n/q)\bigr)$,
  by CITE{formula-digamma} and Gauss's digamma theorem." A `Links` entry for
  Wikipedia's *Digamma function* would give the theorem a citation.
- In `rigour details`, "an independent root-of-unity logarithm formula from
  [1]" can then name what it checked. Either "the equivalent root-of-unity
  form of CITE{formula-gauss} given in CITE{WikiEuler}", or write
  Wikipedia's form out.
- Optional: $\gamma(2,4)=\gamma/4$ is the row the cited Wikipedia section
  singles out. A comment on it costs one line in `_comment`.

### 2. The keyword "Lehmer constant" names a different constant (worth doing; one deletion)

**What a reader sees.** Nothing on the page, but a search does. `Keywords`
has the same weight as the title, and "Lehmer's constant" is already the
name of another number. Finch, *Mathematical Constants* §6.6, and MathWorld's
"Lehmer's Constant" use it for the slowest Lehmer cotangent expansion,
$0.59263\ldots$ (OEIS A030125). "Lehmer's number" $1.17628\ldots$ is a
third, and T222 holds it. A reader who types "Lehmer constant" holding
either of those would reach this table first.

**Smallest fix.** Delete `Lehmer constant` from `Keywords`. "Euler-Lehmer
constant" already matches the stem.

### 3. $\gamma$ is used from the first formula on and never defined (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** The page draws `Formulas` before `Comments`. Formula
(1) is $\gamma(0,q)=(\gamma-\log q)/q$, so the reader meets a bare $\gamma$
beside the table's own $\gamma(a,q)$ with nothing to say what it is. The
nearest statement comes later, in comment (5): "$\gamma(0,1)$ is Euler's
constant". That defines $\gamma(0,1)$, not $\gamma$. A reader has to put it
together with formula (2) at $q=1$ to conclude that $\gamma=\gamma(0,1)$.

**Why it is wrong.** The skill says to define notation where it is used. The
title's $\gamma(a,q)$ and a free $\gamma$ on the same page are exactly the
pair a reader wonders about.

**Smallest fix.** Rewrite `comment-euler-constant` as
"$\gamma=\gamma(0,1)$ is Euler's constant, the Stieltjes constant
HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}[$\gamma_0$]." Also add "where $\gamma$ is
Euler's constant" to `formula-digamma`, since that is where the reader first
meets it.

### 4. Two citations do not carry what they are attached to, and Lehmer's paper is not referenced (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**
- In `formula-digamma`, `[1]` sits after
  $\gamma(a,q)=-(\psi(a/q)+\log q)/q$. The Euler–Lehmer section of
  Wikipedia's *Euler's constant* article gives the definition,
  $\gamma(0,q)$, the sum, the root-of-unity form and the gcd relation. It
  has no $\psi$. (I read the article's wikitext, lines 689–706.)
- `comment-representative` reads "The representative is Lehmer's
  $0\leq a<q$ convention [1]". The cited article does not attribute a
  convention to Lehmer. The sentence also does not parse as meant: a
  representative is not a convention. The part a reader needs is the second
  clause, that the multiples of $q$ are the row $a=0$.
- The table is named after D. H. Lehmer, "Euler constants for arithmetic
  progressions", *Acta Arith.* 27 (1975) 125–142,
  doi 10.4064/aa-27-1-125-142. The doi resolves, and Crossref gives the
  title. The page has no `References`.

**Smallest fix.**
- Add `References: Lehmer1975` with `bib` and `doi`, and cite it from the
  Definition beside `CITE{WikiEuler}`.
- In `formula-digamma`, move `CITE{WikiEuler}` to follow the $\gamma(0,q)$
  clause. Cite `Lehmer1975` on the $\psi$ clause only once somebody has seen
  it there. I could not read the paper.
- Rewrite the comment as "Residue classes are represented by $0\leq a<q$,
  so the multiples of $q$ are the row $a=0$."

### 5. The T226 relation says "the formula" and implies coverage T226 does not have (worth doing; small)

**What a reader sees.** "Values of the digamma function at rational numbers
— the formula for $\gamma(a,q)$ with $1\leq a<q$ uses $\psi(a/q)$". "The
formula" is pointed at, not named. A reader who follows the link expecting
$\psi(a/q)$ for their row will find it only when $a/q$ in lowest terms has
denominator at most 12, which T226's complete-note sets as its range. That
is 177 of the 435 rows. $\psi(2/13)$ through $\psi(29/30)$ are not there.

**Smallest fix.** "holds $\psi(a/q)$ for reduced denominators up to 12, and
CITE{formula-digamma} gives $\gamma(a,q)=-(\psi(a/q)+\log q)/q$ from it".

### 6. Noted only

- **Row comments** $(2,1)$ and $(4,1)$ are bare expressions ending in a full
  stop, "$(\gamma+\log2)/2$.". Under the value this reads well enough. If
  finding 1 is taken, "$\gamma(1,2)=(\gamma+\log2)/2$." is the clearer shape.
  The $(1,0)$ comment, comment (5) and the Stieltjes relation all say that
  $\gamma(0,1)=\gamma_0$. The row comment is the one a reader holding
  $0.5772\ldots$ meets, so keep that and do not add a fourth.
- **`rigour details`**: "using exact integer parameters" trails off the
  first sentence without saying what it modifies. The parameters are integers
  by definition, so the phrase can go.
- **`complete-note`** says what is held, but not why the range stops at
  $q=30$. The skill asks for that sentence. It is minor, since moduli up to 30
  are plainly the ones people use.
- **`HREF{T226}`** is written by T-number rather than slug. It resolves, and
  `audit_table` accepts both forms. T226 is itself an unpublished draft, so
  T231 must not be published before it. The audit would say so once T231 is
  published.
- **The Mertens relation**, "store constants left by harmonic sums over
  arithmetic progressions", is loose ("left by"). It is understandable, and I
  do not press it.

## What reads well

- **The Definition defines.** It is one sentence with the limit written out,
  and the two conventions that could differ, the range of $a$ and the
  $\log x/q$ normalisation, are both fixed in it. Two people would build the
  same table from it. It stops there.
- **The parameters describe the family.** The range $q\leq30$ is in
  `complete-note`, where it belongs.
- **No sentence talks about the website** or narrates the build outside
  `rigour details`, and nothing is pointed at by position except the relation
  in finding 5.
- **The page renders cleanly.** Every `<` is escaped, all TeX typesets, and
  every link resolves. The Sage program runs as printed.
