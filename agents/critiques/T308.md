## Findings, ranked

### 1. Neither program returns $W_D$. Worth doing.

**What a reader sees.** The reader who wants one more value copies P1:

    P = polclass(-23, 1)      \\ PARI's Weber class-invariant polynomial
    Pol(Vec(P))               \\ x^3 - x^2 + 1; W_{-23}(x)=x^3-x-1 after the reciprocal/sign transform

It prints $x^3-x^2+1$, which is not a row of this table. `Pol(Vec(P))` gives
back `P` unchanged, so the second line does nothing. Its comment is a note to
self that names a transform without stating it. It also implies that one
transform works for every $D$, and none does. Over the 100 entries, PARI's
polynomial is $\pm x^hW_D(-1/x)$ for 76 values of $D$ and $\pm x^hW_D(1/x)$
for 24. Examples of the second kind are $-55$, $-95$, $-119$ and $-143$, all
of even degree, and no congruence on $D$ separates the two kinds.

P2, the Sage program, prints a factorisation into **ten** polynomials for
$D=-23$. Two of them have degree 3, $x^3-x-1$ and $x^3-x+1$, and nothing on
the page says which one is $W_{-23}$.

**Why it is wrong.** `Programs` is "the standard incantation for a reader who
wants one more value". These two programs give that reader a different
polynomial and a list of candidates.

**Smallest fix.** Pick the factor that vanishes at $\omega_D$, using PARI's
`weber`. I tested both programs below: the PARI one reproduces all 100
entries, and the Sage one reproduces $D=-23,-55,-175$.

    \p 100
    D = -55;
    P = polclass(D, 1);                 \\ minimal polynomial of -1/omega_D or 1/omega_D
    w = weber(I*sqrt(-D))/sqrt(2);      \\ omega_D
    Q = polrecip(P); S = polrecip(subst(P, x, -x));
    if (abs(subst(S, x, w)) < abs(subst(Q, x, w)), Q = S);
    Q / pollead(Q)

Compare the two residuals rather than testing one of them against a
tolerance. At the default 38 digits, a test `abs(...) > 1e-10` chose wrongly
for $D=-671,-767,-935,-1007,-1079,-1111,-1159$, because $|\omega_D|^h$ there
is past the working precision. `\p 100` gives a margin for the whole range.
For Sage, keep the resultant and end with:

    w = ComplexField(200)(pari('weber(I*sqrt(%d))' % -D)) / sqrt(RealField(200)(2))
    min((p.univariate_polynomial() for p, e in factor(H(j).resultant(relation, j))),
        key=lambda p: abs(p(w)))

### 2. A reader holding PARI's polynomial is not told how it relates to $W_D$. Worth doing.

**What a reader sees.** PARI's `polclass` is the most common way to meet a
Weber class polynomial. Somebody who ran `polclass(-23,1)` holds $x^3-x^2+1$.
The page mentions the relation only in the folded rigour note, and only as
"after the reciprocal transform and the sign change in $x$ when PARI's chosen
Weber class invariant has the opposite sign". That is a fact about the
generator's check, in the place a reader looks last.

**Why it matters.** A reader who comes from a calculation needs to know what
they are holding.

**Smallest fix.** Add one comment, for example: "PARI's `polclass(D,1)`
CITE{PARIpolclass} returns the minimal polynomial of $-1/\omega_D$ for some
$D$ and of $1/\omega_D$ for others, so $x^3-x^2+1$ there is $W_{-23}$ here."
Storing PARI's convention as a second normalisation would make it searchable.
That is a design decision for a person, and I do not recommend it on this
evidence, because the sign varies with $D$.

### 3. Two of the three references are never cited. Worth doing; it is small.

Cox and Yui–Zagier appear under References, but nothing on the page points
to them. Yui and Zagier's paper is *the* source for
$\mathfrak f(\sqrt{-m})/\sqrt2$ with $m\equiv7\pmod8$ as a class invariant,
so it belongs beside `CITE{Schertz2002}` in the Definition. Cox would support
"ring class field" in the discriminant comment. If nothing cites Cox, drop it.

### 4. The $G_n$ relation does not say how the two tables' numbers are related. Worth doing if the prose is touched anyway.

The relation in `Similar tables` reads "stores individual Weber values at
$i\sqrt n$; this table stores the minimal polynomial when $n=-D$ and the
Weber value is a class invariant". The minimal polynomial here is not the
Weber value's; it belongs to $\mathfrak f/\sqrt2$. The linked table stores
both $G_n$ and $\mathfrak f(i\sqrt n)=2^{1/4}G_n$ for $n\leq100$. The one
fact a reader needs is $\omega_D=2^{-1/4}G_{-D}$. For example, $W_{-23}$
vanishes at $G_{23}/2^{1/4}$, and eight rows of that table are roots of rows
here. Suggested relation: "stores $G_n$ and $\mathfrak f(i\sqrt n)$; for
$n=-D$ the roots here include $\omega_D=2^{-1/4}G_n$". Use
`HREF{...#23}` if a single row is meant.

## Noting only, because I noticed

- **Definition, last sentence.** "Listed is the monic minimal polynomial
  $W_D(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]$ of $\omega_D$" describes the page rather than
  defining $W_D$, and "monic" repeats "minimal". "$W_D(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]$ is
  the minimal polynomial of $\omega_D$" says the same thing.
- **Comment (1).** "The point $i\sqrt m$ has discriminant $4D$": a point
  does not have a discriminant; the order $\mathbb Z[i\sqrt m]$ does. The
  comment also leaves out the fact that makes the indexing harmless: for
  $D\equiv1\pmod8$, $h(4D)=h(D)$, so the two ring class fields coincide.
- **Comment (2)** describes the entry comments. Its one mathematical claim,
  that $h(D)=\deg W_D$, would sit better in comment (1). Also, $h(D)$ in each
  entry comment repeats the degree, which the row already shows.
- **Comment (3).** "These are smaller class polynomials than the Hilbert
  class polynomials for the same discriminants" does not say what "these"
  refers to or in what sense they are smaller. "The coefficients of $W_D$
  are much smaller than those of $H_{4D}$" names both. The linked Hilbert
  table stops at $|\Delta|=300$, so it holds $H_D$ only for the first quarter
  of this range, and $H_{4D}$ only for $|D|\leq75$.
- **`Similar tables`.** The first relation says "store" and the second
  "stores". The last is a sentence with its own subject, "Formula (2)
  relates…", while the other three continue the table's name.
- **`complete-note`** says what is covered but not why the range stops at
  $|D|<1200$. The skill asks for that sentence. The largest entry, $D=-1151$,
  is 429 characters, which is still readable, so size is not the reason.
- **Formula (2)** begins "For $m=-D$", which the Definition has already set.
