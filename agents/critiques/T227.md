## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Sage program on the page raises an error. Worth doing.

A reader who copies `Programs` into Sage gets

    AttributeError: 'ComplexBallField_with_category' object has no attribute 'bernoulli'

from the line `log_A = CBF.bernoulli(k + 1) * CBF(H) / CBF(k + 1) - ...`.
`RealBallField` has a `bernoulli` method and `ComplexBallField` does not. The
build found this and fixed it in commit 77d86cd, in `table.yaml`, but never
sent the change to the site. Commit 1d1623c sent a later comment edit. The
document on the site still has the old snippet, and the repository has
the working one:

    from sage.arith.misc import bernoulli
    ...
    log_A = CBF(QQ(bernoulli(k + 1)) * H / QQ(k + 1)) - CBF(-k).zetaderiv(1)

**Fix:** send the repository's `Programs` block to T227 unchanged. I ran it
at $k=0,1,19$ and it reproduces the stored values.

### 2. `comment-product` points at a polynomial, a limit and a definition it never states. Worth doing.

> The same constants occur as the normalising constants in the asymptotic
> expansion of $\prod_{m=1}^n m^{m^k}$. With the Bernoulli convention
> $B_1=0$ in that normalising polynomial, the limit definition gives the same
> values as the zeta-derivative formula in the definition.

A reader has to guess four things. "The same constants" means the same as
what? "That normalising polynomial" is never written down anywhere on the
page. "The limit definition" is never given, and "in the definition" makes
the reader go back and look. The sentence copies Wikipedia's remark about its
$P_k(n)$, which has the term $\frac{n^k}{2}\log n$ written out separately.
Without $P_k$ in front of them, a reader who writes the obvious
Euler-Maclaurin normalisation with $B_1=0$ loses that term. The limit then
diverges: at $k=1$, $n=2000$ it gives 7601 rather than
$\log A_1+\tfrac1{12}$. So the sentence is worse than unclear, because it
leads a careful reader to a wrong computation. `comment-classical` beside
it is fine.

**Fix:** drop the $B_1$ sentence and name the things. For example:

> $A_k$ is the constant factor in the asymptotic formula for
> $\prod_{m=1}^n m^{m^k}$ CITE{WikiGlaisher}: $A_0$ is the constant in
> Stirling's formula for $n!$, and $A_1$ the one in the corresponding formula
> for the hyperfactorial $\prod_{m=1}^n m^m$. The constants are also called
> Glaisher-Kinkelin-Bendersky constants CITE{Dowker}.

A reader who wants the product limit written out needs $P_k(n)$ in full.
That belongs in `Formulas`, not in this comment. The sentence above is
enough without it.

### 3. The Definition names the Bernoulli numbers without linking T13, and it has no room left for the link. Worth doing.

> Listed are the generalized Glaisher constants, also called Bendersky
> constants, $A_k$ CITE{WikiGlaisher} for integers $k\geq0$, together with
> their logarithms. They are defined here by $\log A_k=\ldots$, where
> $H_0=0$, $H_k=\sum_{j=1}^k1/j$ for $k\geq1$, and $B_n$ are the Bernoulli
> numbers.

The corpus holds the Bernoulli numbers as T13, and this is where a reader
meets $B_n$. "Listed are ... together with their logarithms" describes the
table rather than the object, and `quantity` already says it. $H_0=0$ is
simply the empty sum. With both cut, the link fits:

> The generalized Glaisher constants, or Bendersky constants, are the numbers
> $A_k$ CITE{WikiGlaisher}, $k\geq0$, with
> $\log A_k=\frac{B_{k+1}}{k+1}H_k-\zeta'(-k)$, where $H_k=\sum_{j=1}^k1/j$
> and $B_n$ are the HREF{Bernoulli_numbers}[Bernoulli numbers].

The audit will still complain about an `HREF` inside a Definition. The skill
says to link the first mention in each section and says nothing against
doing so in a Definition, so this is the audit rule to overrule. If a
reviewer disagrees, add T13 to `Similar tables` instead. The unlinked-family
finding will then remain.

### 4. The `Similar tables` row for factorials uses a name it never explains, and half of it is not about factorials. Worth doing; small.

> $A_0$ is the Stirling constant, while higher $A_k$ normalise products
> $\prod_{m=1}^n m^{m^k}$

"The Stirling constant" is not defined anywhere on the page. The clause
after "while" says nothing about the table it glosses, which holds $n!$.
**Fix:** "$A_0=\sqrt{2\pi}$ is the constant in Stirling's formula
$n!\sim\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n$."

### 5. `formula-barnes-g` links the Barnes table where the sentence means one value. Cheap; worth doing if the prose is being edited anyway.

`HREF{Values_of_the_Barnes_G-function_at_rational_numbers}[The Barnes
$G$-function] satisfies $G(1/2)=\ldots$` sends the reader to the top of a
table of 1080 values. T93 has an entry with key `1/2`, so the
formula can link that row directly:
`HREF{Values_of_the_Barnes_G-function_at_rational_numbers#1/2}[$G(1/2)$]`.
The whole-table link in `Similar tables` stays as it is.

### 6. `complete-note` says what range is here but not why. Noting only.

"it holds $A_k$ and $\log A_k$ for every integer $0\leq k\leq20$" finishes
its sentence. The skill asks that note to give the reason for the range as
well. Beyond $k\approx15$ the values jump from $929840$ at $k=18$ to
$10^{-28}$ at $k=19$. Wikipedia tabulates $0\leq k\leq10$. If there is a
reason for 20, one clause would give it. I cannot supply one.

### 7. Noted only because I noticed.

- The parameter `quantity` is titled "$A_k$ or $\log A_k$", which lists its
  values rather than naming it. Its `display` is the key word `quantity`, so
  the column header reads "quantity". "the constant or its logarithm" would
  be a title. This costs a reader nothing.
- `comment-classical` says "The row $A_0$ is $\sqrt{2\pi}$". A row is not a
  number, and the $k=0$ entry comment and `formula-first` already say the
  same thing. "$A_0=\sqrt{2\pi}$" would be enough.
- `Links` has no OEIS entry. Wikipedia gives A074962 for $A$ and an A-number
  for each $A_k$ up to $k=10$.
