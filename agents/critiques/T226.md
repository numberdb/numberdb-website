# T226, Values of the digamma function $\psi(x)$ at rational numbers

Read 2026-09-13. Private draft. It has 729 entries of type `R`, rigour
`proven`, 100 digits, with two parameters: `x` and `quantity`. `quantity` is
`psi` or `H`. There are 365 arguments $x=a/b$ with $b\leq 12$ and
$-4<x\leq 4$: 184 positive, 180 negative, and zero. Six entries have a
comment. The prose matches
`generators/digamma-rational-values/table.yaml` in every field but one,
`complete` (finding 4).

None of them reported anything. The Definition is 248 characters, below

The table is sound and well aimed. A reader holding $-1.9635\ldots$ or
$0.6137\ldots$ lands on a row that names $\psi(1/2)$ or $H_{1/2}$ and gives
its closed form. The rows run by denominator with $x$ and $-x$ adjacent, so
the arguments anybody meets come first. The parameter `x` describes the
family rather than the run, and the range is in `complete-note` as a clause
that finishes its sentence. The faults are all in the prose around the
numbers: a constant that is never defined, a parameter that introduces
itself by its own name, and a range comment that says less than the note it
repeats.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. $\gamma$ is never defined, and the Definition's one sentence has two readings.

What a reader sees: "This table gives the digamma function
$\psi(x)=\Gamma'(x)/\Gamma(x)$ at rational arguments, using the DLMF
convention [1], and the harmonic-number continuation
$H_x=\psi(x+1)+\gamma$ [2]. Only finite real values are listed."

Why it is wrong. There are three separate faults.

- $\gamma$ appears in the Definition, in two comments, in two formulas and
  in `rigour details`, and nothing says what it is. The only gloss on the
  page is at the foot, in `Similar tables`, where it is called $\gamma_0$.
- "using the DLMF convention [1], and the harmonic-number continuation" can
  be read with "using" governing both halves. The table would then give
  $\psi$ by means of the continuation, rather than giving the continuation
  as a second quantity.
- "Only finite real values are listed" is a remark about the range. The
  parameter constraints and `complete-note` already say it.

Fix: "Listed are the digamma function $\psi(x)=\Gamma'(x)/\Gamma(x)$
CITE{DLMFPsi} and the harmonic number $H_x=\psi(x+1)+\gamma$
CITE{WikiHarmonic} at rational $x$, where $\gamma$ is the Euler–Mascheroni
constant." The link to the constant's row belongs in a comment rather than
the Definition, where `audit_table` refuses an `HREF`. `comment-convention`
already uses $\gamma$ and could carry
`HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}[$\gamma$]`. T16 has the row `0`. Worth doing.

### 2. The parameter list says "quantity — quantity".

What a reader sees under Parameters: "$x$ — argument (...)" and then
"quantity — quantity". The column header above the numbers also reads
"quantity".

Why it is wrong. The site prints a parameter's display, a dash, then its
title. Both are the word "quantity", so the line says nothing, and it is the
only place in Parameters that could tell a reader which two quantities the
table holds. It is the same class of fault as an earlier table's parameter
line `argument ()`: correct in the document and empty on the page.

Fix: set the title to "$\psi(x)$ or $H_x$". The line then reads
"quantity — $\psi(x)$ or $H_x$". Keep the display "quantity" for the
column header. Worth doing; one field.

### 3. `comment-range` repeats `complete-note`, and its second sentence points at a formula.

What a reader sees, as Comment (3): "The listed rational arguments have
denominators at most $12$ and lie in $-4<x\leq 4$. Integer shifts are
listed because (2) changes the value by rational summands."

Why it is wrong. The first sentence is `complete-note` again, a few lines
lower on the page. The second sentence says "Integer shifts" without saying
shifts of what. "(2)" is a formula number the reader has to look up, and
"changes the value by rational summands" is the recurrence restated. What
it means is a reason for the range: $\psi(x+k)$ differs from $\psi(x)$ by
$\sum_{j<k}1/(x+j)$, a rational number that a reader holding one value
would not guess, so the shifted values need rows of their own. That reason
belongs in the completeness note, which is the only place the page says why
the range is what it is. The note gives no reason now, either for $b\leq12$
or for the 180 negative arguments, half the table.

Fix: delete `comment-range`. End `complete-note` with the reason: "...at
which the quantity is finite; the arguments run past $0<x\leq1$ because
$\psi(x+k)-\psi(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}1/(x+j)$ is a rational number that a
reader holding one of the two values would not guess". If there is a reason
for 12, add it; if there is none, that is worth knowing too. Worth doing.

### 4. The page says "Table is complete: false (it holds ...)".

What a reader sees at the foot of the page: "Table is complete: false (it
holds both quantities for every rational argument ...)".

Why it is wrong. Every other table says "no" here. `table.yaml` has
`complete: false`, an unquoted YAML boolean, and the stored document holds
the string `"false"`. The help page explains why the corpus writes the
word `no`. The metadata form only knows the answers `yes`, `no` and
`unknown`, so it will mark this value as odd. The rendered clause also reads
like a flag rather than an answer.

Fix: `complete: 'no'` in `table.yaml` and in the stored document. Worth
doing; one word. It is also proposed as a lesson in
`agents/lessons/proposals/20260913T140605Z-critique.md`.

### 5. The keyword "generalized harmonic number" names a different object.

What a reader sees: nothing on the page. The effect is on search.
`Keywords` carries the top weight in the text index, and "generalized
harmonic number" is the standard name for $H_{n,m}=\sum_{k\leq n}k^{-m}$.
That is the section of that name in the Wikipedia article the Definition
cites. Somebody searching for $H_{n,2}$ would be sent here, and the table
holds nothing of the kind.

Fix: replace it with "harmonic number", which is what a reader holding
$H_{1/2}$ types. Worth doing.

### 6. The `Similar tables` line for T16 links the whole table where it means one row.

What a reader sees: "Stieltjes constants — contains $\gamma_0$, the
Euler-Mascheroni constant appearing in $H_x$". The link goes to the top of
T16.

Why it matters. The sentence means one number. The skill's rule is to link
the entry: T16's row key for $\gamma_0$ is `0`. The `table` field has to
name the table, so put the row link in the relation.

Fix: "its row HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}[$\gamma_0$] is the
Euler–Mascheroni constant $\gamma$ in $H_x=\psi(x+1)+\gamma$". Worth doing.
It becomes more useful once finding 1 names $\gamma$.

### 7. Gauss's formula does not say that $p$ and $q$ are integers.

What a reader sees, as Formula (4): "For $1\leq p<q$,
$\psi(p/q)=-\gamma-\log(2q)-\ldots$".

Why it matters. Read literally, $p$ and $q$ could be any reals, and then the
floor in the upper limit and the finite sum make no sense. Fix: "For
integers $1\leq p<q$". Worth doing; one word.

## Noted only

- **`comment-exact-harmonic`** says "The rows $\psi(n)$ still involve
  $\gamma$ and are stored as real values." "Still involve" is loose. The
  fact a reader holding $0.42278\ldots$ can use is $\psi(n)=H_{n-1}-\gamma$,
  so write that: "The rows $\psi(n)=H_{n-1}-\gamma$ are stored as real
  values." Do not replace it with a claim of irrationality; that is open.
- **$H_0=0$ and $H_1=1$** are the kind of value the skill says matches every
  search and tells nobody anything. They are two rows, and they complete
  the family visibly at the top. I would drop them, but it is a judgement.
  $3/2$, $11/6$ and $25/12$ are recognisable and can stay.
- **Formula (5)** ends "as in [the table of Dirichlet $L$-values]". That is
  prose in `Formulas`, and the `Similar tables` line says the same thing.
  It is true (T145's own formula is the same identity), so it can stay.
- **The Hurwitz relation** gives $\psi^{(m)}$ for $m\geq1$, which this table
  does not hold. $\zeta(s,x)=1/(s-1)-\psi(x)+O(s-1)$ ties T94 to $\psi$
  itself, and is the relation a reader of this table would want.
- **Programs** imports `numberdb` only to compute a precision, and binds
  the name `RBF`, which Sage already defines. For somebody who wants one
  more value, `RealBallField(400)(1/3).psi()` is the usual incantation.
  The snippet as written runs.
- **Closed forms stop at denominator 3.** $\psi(1/4)$, $\psi(3/4)$,
  $\psi(1/6)$ and $\psi(5/6)$ have short classical forms, and $\psi(3/2)$
  has none on its row although $H_{1/2}$ does. Formula (4) gives all of
  them, so this is a convenience, not a gap.
