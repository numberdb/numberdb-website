# T320: Complementary Shapiro polynomials $Q_n$

Read on 2026-09-18, against the skill fetched the same day from
<https://numberdb.org/skill> (47,534 bytes, fetched directly: the SOCKS proxy at
`127.0.0.1:1080` refuses every connection on this machine, which
`docs/agent-environment.md` has recorded several times already).

T320 is a draft of eight exact integer polynomials, $Q_n$ for $0\leq n\leq7$,
indexed by the construction parameter $n$, so the largest is $Q_7$ of degree
127. `type: Z[]`, `rigour: exact`, `complete: no`. It is the third table of the
Fekete/Shapiro batch and the second half of a pair: T319 holds $P_n$ and is
already public. T320 was built after T319 was critiqued and repaired, and it
carries the repairs: the Definition names the pair rather than claiming the
name for one sequence, the reciprocal relation $Q_n(x)=(-1)^nx^{2^n-1}P_n(-1/x)$
is present, the `Programs` block writes `**` rather than `^`, and
`complete-note` says why the range stops where it does. Five of T319's six
findings do not recur here; the sixth, its coefficient rule, reappears in a
different form as finding 1 below.

answers 404 to the zeta3 key. I rebuilt the page by the recipe in

## What I checked hardest, and found nothing wrong with

* **The page renders.** 30,777 bytes, status 200. Zero `CITE-broken` spans,
  zero "(Unknown key)", zero "Math input error", no unbalanced `$`. Eleven
  labelled blocks, (1) to (9), (P1) and [1], and seven resolved `CITE` anchors
  between the Definition and the rigour note. The one
  hazard in the document is the bare `<` in formula (3), `$1\leq k<2^n$`, and
  the renderer escapes it: the HTML holds `k&lt;2^n`, which is what MathJax
  needs, so the fault that ate a section elsewhere this year does not arise
  here. There is no `&`, no `--` and no non-ASCII anywhere in the prose. The
  tags render as `polynomial` and `combinatorics`.
* **Every stored value comes back out of the Definition.** Building the pair
  from $P_0=Q_0=1$, $P_{n+1}=P_n+x^{2^n}Q_n$, $Q_{n+1}=P_n-x^{2^n}Q_n$ and
  printing $Q_n$ the way the site prints an entry: eight of eight identical to
  the stored strings, from `1` to the 908 characters of $Q_7$.
* **All five formulas hold on every entry.** (1) the reciprocal relation, for
  $n=0\dots7$; (2) the complementary identity, as a polynomial identity, for
  $n=0\dots7$; (3) the autocorrelations, all $2^n-1$ shifts at each $n$, 247
  shifts in all; (4) the one-polynomial recurrence for $n=1\dots6$, and its
  "$n\geq1$" really is needed, since at $n=0$ the right-hand side is $1+x$ and
  $Q_1=1-x$; (5) $Q_n(1)$ and $Q_n(-1)$ at every $n\leq7$, including the
  $Q_0(-1)$ exception, which unlike T319's is a real one. Comment (6)'s degree
  claim and comment (8)'s two claims about the coefficients hold at every $n$
  too.
* **It is one table, and the split from T319 is the good kind.** One parameter,
  an integer index, no parameter whose values are names, and a value column
  headed with a quantity. The larger question the batch raises is whether
  $P_n$ and $Q_n$ should have been one table, since they are defined together
  and the point of them is the identity relating them. Two tables is right:
  they are two named objects sharing a subject, "Shapiro polynomials" and
  "complementary Shapiro polynomials", each findable by its own name, which is
  the corpus's own pattern for Bessel first and second kind and for the three
  elliptic integrals. They are not one number in two conventions, so neither is
  derivable from the other by a factor a reader would guess, and `repeats`
  would be false. The connection is carried where the skill puts it, in
  `Similar tables` with the relation stated, and that relation is a good one:
  it names the identity rather than saying "related".
* **`complete-note` answers the range question with a measured number.** "it
  holds every construction index $n$ with $0\leq n\leq7$, stopping before the
  printed polynomials reach about two thousand characters; $Q_8$ has 1,931
  characters". I computed $Q_8$ two ways and it is 1,931 characters exactly.
  Whether the stop belongs at 7 or 8 was argued in the T319 critique and
  answered by its repair; I am not reopening it. The sentence tells a reader
  holding a $Q_8$ why it is not here, which is what it is for.
* **The `Programs` block runs as stored and returns what it claims.** Preparsed
  and executed in the Sage image, `shapiro_pair(8)[1]` gives a polynomial of
  degree 255 equal to an independent run of the recurrence, 1,931 characters, so
  "the next polynomial after this table" is accurate, and `[1]` is the right
  index of the pair. The three imports look like a script's rather than a
  session's, but they are load-bearing: in this image
  `from sage.rings.integer_ring import ZZ` on its own fails with "partially
  initialized module ... has no attribute 'ZZ'", and `import numberdb.sage`
  first is what rescues it. Nothing to change.
* **The attached generator is the repository's.** `/files/T320/generate.py`
  answers 200 to the key, 6,494 bytes, and the source in it is byte-identical to
  `generators/complementary-shapiro-polynomials/generate.py`.
* **A reader arriving with one of these numbers is served.** The rows are the
  first thing under the title, the index column is $n$ and the value column
  $Q_n(x)$, and the Definition is the first prose below them. There is no
  `param-latex`, no label repeating the block heading, and the index runs
  $0,1,\dots,7$ with the small cases first.
* **Nothing is named that the corpus holds and does not link.** Searching the
  corpus for Littlewood, Golay, Shapiro, Fekete and "binary sequence" turns up
  no table this one should point at beyond T318 and T319, both of which it
  links. The keyword "Littlewood polynomials" is a true claim: every
  coefficient of every stored $Q_n$ is $\pm1$.

## Findings

### 1. The table refers twice to a coefficient sequence it never writes down

Comment (8) opens:

> Coefficients are read low degree first. Each coefficient of $Q_n$ is $1$ or
> $-1$, and the constant coefficient is $1$.

and the folded half of `rigour details` says the entries were checked

> against the direct Golay-Rudin-Shapiro coefficient construction and the
> reciprocal relation in (1)

A reader meets both and can act on neither. The page lists no coefficients: it
prints polynomials, and it prints them high degree first, so row 3 begins
`-x^7 + x^6 - x^5` and the constant coefficient that sentence is about is the
last thing on the line. Taken literally against what is on the screen the first
sentence is false; taken as a convention it is a convention for something the
page does not do. It came from the generator, whose docstring records the
family convention asked for in the "table wanted" issue, "the variable is x,
coefficients run low degree first" — true of the request and of the sign
sequence behind it, and not of this table's rows. And "the direct
Golay-Rudin-Shapiro coefficient construction" names a rule the reader has no way
to reach: it is stated on T319, in that table's formula (1), and T320 neither
states it nor sends anybody there.

The second and third clauses of (8) are fine and are worth keeping; I checked
both at every $n$.

**Smallest fix**, one of two. Either delete the first sentence of (8) and let
the rigour note cite where the construction lives, or — better, because it
makes both references land and gives the reader the reconstruction T319's
readers have — add a sixth formula stating the rule:

> Equivalently, for $n\geq1$, $Q_n(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}b_jx^j$, where $b_j=1$
> if the binary expansion of $j$ has an even number of pairs of consecutive
> ones, counting overlapping pairs separately, and $b_j=-1$ otherwise, with the
> sign reversed for $j\geq2^{n-1}$.

That is T319's repaired sentence plus one clause. The clause follows from the
recursion — $Q_{n+1}$ agrees with $P_n$ below $2^n$ and is its negative above,
and $P_{n+1}$ agrees with $P_n$ below $2^n$ — and I checked it coefficient by
coefficient for $n=1\dots8$, all 510 of them. With it on the page, "read low
degree first" has something to be true of.

Worth doing.

### 2. Comment (9) compresses a name into something that is not one

> The corresponding first Shapiro polynomials are held in
> [Shapiro polynomials $P_n$](/T319).

"the corresponding first Shapiro polynomials" reads, on first pass, as "the
first few of the corresponding Shapiro polynomials" — that is, as a claim about
how much of T319 is there — and a reader has to reach the end of the sentence
and back out of it. What it means is $P_n$, the first sequence of the pair, and
the table's own `Similar tables` says that clearly two sections later: "are the
first polynomial sequence in the same recursion". The skill's rule is to name
the thing rather than point at it, and the symbol is shorter than the phrase
here.

**Smallest fix**: "The other sequence of the pair, $P_n$, is held in
HREF{T319}[Shapiro polynomials $P_n$]."

Worth doing.

### 3. Formula (3)'s parenthetical does not follow from what $f$ is said to be

> If $f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}b_jx^j$ has aperiodic autocorrelations
> $c_k(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1-k}b_jb_{j+k}$, so $c_0(f)=2^n$, then
> $c_k(P_n)+c_k(Q_n)=0$ for $1\leq k<2^n$.

$f$ is introduced as any polynomial of degree less than $2^n$, and for such an
$f$, $c_0(f)=\sum_j b_j^2$ is not $2^n$; it is $2^n$ exactly when every
$b_j=\pm1$, which the sentence never says. So the "so" is an inference a reader
can check and find wrong, in the one formula that defines its own notation. The
conclusion is unaffected — I verified $c_k(P_n)+c_k(Q_n)=0$ at all 247 shifts
with $n\leq7$, and $c_0(Q_n)=2^n$ at each $n$ — but the reader who stops to
check the parenthetical is the reader the formula was written for.

**Smallest fix**: say what $f$ is. "If $f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}b_jx^j$ has
every $b_j=\pm1$, its aperiodic autocorrelations are
$c_k(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1-k}b_jb_{j+k}$, so $c_0(f)=2^n$, and then ..."

Worth doing; it is one clause.

### 4. The attached generator tells a reader how to publish on a machine they do not have

`/files/T320/generate.py` is what a contributor downloads from the table page.
Its header gives the three `sage -python` lines the skill asks for, and then
eight more:

>     On the NumberDB build machine, where arguments are not passed through
>     agents/sage.sh, publish with:
>
>         $ cat "$NUMBERDB_KEY_FILE" | NUMBERDB_KEY_FROM_STDIN=1 NUMBERDB_PUBLISH=1 \
>               agents/sage.sh generate.py

`agents/sage.sh` is in a repository the reader does not have, `$NUMBERDB_KEY_FILE`
is set on one build box, and the whole paragraph is about this deployment. The
file is otherwise a good one: it opens with the recurrence, says what was
decided and why, and its `check_identities` is a real test suite.

This is inherited rather than decided here — T319's attached generator carries
the same paragraph and is already public — so it is one edit in two files, and
it belongs to whoever owns the batch rather than to this table alone.

**Smallest fix**: delete that paragraph. The build box's invocation belongs in
`docs/` or in `agents/`, not in a file served to the public.

Worth doing, in the batch.

## Noted because I noticed, not because they are worth an afternoon

* **The Definition names the Shapiro polynomials and does not link them, and
  that is correct.** This is the one thing on the page that looks like a
  finding and is not, so it is written down to stop the next reader "fixing"
  it. The audit refuses an `HREF` in a Definition outright — "Definition links
  to another table; a cross-reference belongs in Similar tables or a comment" —
  and T320 does the right thing by linking in Comments (9) and in `Similar
  tables`. Worth knowing that the audit's unlinked-family check is also silent
  here for a second and unrelated reason: it skips any table whose name
  contains, or is contained in, this table's own, and "Shapiro polynomials" is
  inside "Complementary Shapiro polynomials". So on this pair the check is off
  in both directions, and a genuine missing link would have to be found by
  reading. I have proposed that as a lesson.
* **T319 has no way back here.** T319's Definition introduces $Q_n$ and its
  `Similar tables` names only T318, so a reader of the public table cannot
  reach this one. That cannot be fixed today: a published table may not link to
  a draft, and the audit says so. It is a note for whoever publishes T320 —
  the same act should add `HREF{T320}` to T319, and the relation is already
  written, in T320's own `Similar tables`, ready to be said the other way
  round.
* **"Before the draft was filled" in `rigour details`.** The field is the right
  home for what was checked, and the list that follows is exactly what belongs
  there. But the opening clause dates the work to the draft's lifetime, and it
  will still be there when the table is published and nobody remembers there
  was a draft. Deleting three words leaves "Every entry was checked against
  ...", which is the same sentence about the mathematics.
* **`HREF{T319}` rather than `HREF{Shapiro_polynomials}`.** Both resolve, the
  audit blesses both by name ("a cross-reference may name a table by its
  address or by its number"), and the caption is what a reader sees either way.
  Not a fault. Noting it only because the skill talks about slugs and a reader
  of the document might think the number is a mistake.
