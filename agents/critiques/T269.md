# T269 critique: Meixner polynomials $M_n(x;\beta,c)$

Read 2026-09-16. T269 is a private draft. The SOCKS proxy on 127.0.0.1:1080
refused the connection (`curl` returned 000), but numberdb.org answered
`curl` directly. `/T269` answers 404, as a draft should. I read the document
through `GET /api/table?id=T269` with the key, and rendered it through
`/preview?table=` in six pieces. Each piece carried `Parameters`, one or two
entries and the links its `CITE`s need, and all six answered 200 with no
"Error while parsing". The table holds 425 entries of type `Q[]` with rigour
`exact`: five values of $\beta$ times five of $c$, each with every degree
$0\leq n\leq16$. There are no entry comments. The largest entry,
$M_{16}(x;\tfrac32,\tfrac34)$, is 780 characters of large rationals. That is
long, but it is still one polynomial a reader can scan.

`GET /api/table/T269/audit` reports `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. I agree
that the form is sound: the four `HREF` slugs (`Hahn_polynomials`,
`Charlier_polynomials`, `Krawtchouk_polynomials_of_the_Hamming_scheme`,
`Laguerre_polynomials`) all answer 200, every `CITE` resolves, and the
sections are in their places. The audit cannot see finding 1, which is a
fault in the rendering, or findings 2 and 3, which are about what a sentence

## 1. `\047` is printed on the page in place of an apostrophe (must fix)

**What a reader sees.** In `Similar tables`:

> Krawtchouk polynomials of the Hamming scheme — are rescaled specialisations
> of the same Hahn-class family outside this table\047s orthogonality range

**Why it is wrong.** The stored string contains a literal backslash, `0`,
`4`, `7`. That is an octal escape for `'` that some shell or `printf` step
never decoded. The preview prints it verbatim. It is in the stored JSON
(`table\\047s`), so it is not a rendering bug. It is the only occurrence in
the document.

**Fix.** Replace `\047` with `'`. Finding 2 rewrites the whole sentence
anyway, and that removes it too.

## 2. The Krawtchouk relation points at its relation instead of stating it (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** The same sentence as above: "are rescaled
specialisations of the same Hahn-class family outside this table's
orthogonality range".

**Why it is wrong.** "The same Hahn-class family" points back at something
without naming it. The reader has to guess that it means the Meixner family
itself. "Rescaled" and "specialisations" do not say by what factor or at
which parameters. "Outside this table's orthogonality range" describes what
the table does not hold rather than the relation. The skill asks that
`Similar tables` name the relation, and here the relation is short and exact.
I checked it for $q=2,3,5$, $N=3,6$, every $k$ and every $x\in\{0,\dots,N\}$.
Without the factor, 140 of those cases fail, which shows the factor is
needed:

$$\mathcal{K}_k(x;N,q)=\binom{N}{k}(q-1)^k\,M_k(x;-N,1-q).$$

**Fix.** Set the relation to something like "are
$\mathcal{K}_k(x;N,q)=\binom Nk(q-1)^kM_k(x;-N,1-q)$, the Meixner polynomial
at the negative integer $\beta=-N$ and $c=1-q$, outside the range this table
holds". Use the Krawtchouk table's own symbol $\mathcal{K}_k(x;N,q)$, which
its definition uses.

## 3. The Laguerre polynomials are named in a comment, but their relation is not in `Similar tables` (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** `comment_1`: "These are also called the discrete
Laguerre polynomials." It links to the Laguerre table. `Similar tables` lists
Hahn, Charlier and Krawtchouk, and not Laguerre.

**Why it is wrong.** The name "discrete Laguerre" comes from a limit, and
that limit is the most useful relation this table has to a table the corpus
holds. The Laguerre table (`Laguerre_polynomials`) holds only
$L_n=L_n^{(0)}$, so the matching case is $\beta=1$:
$M_n\bigl(x/(1-c);1,c\bigr)\to L_n(x)$ as $c\to1$. More generally,
$M_n\bigl(x/(1-c);\alpha+1,c\bigr)\to L_n^{(\alpha)}(x)/L_n^{(\alpha)}(0)$.
This is in DLMF §18.21(ii); I did not confirm the equation number, and only
checked the limit numerically. A reader who follows the link from the comment finds
the Laguerre table with nothing on either page saying how the two are
connected.

**Fix.** Add a `Similar tables` entry: `HREF{Laguerre_polynomials}[Laguerre
polynomials]` with the relation "are the limit of $M_n(x/(1-c);1,c)$ as
$c\to1$", citing the DLMF limit equation through a new `Links` key. The comment can then
keep only the alternative name.

## 4. The keyword `gamma` catches searches for something else (worth doing, cheap)

**What a reader sees.** Nothing on the page. The effect shows in search:
`Keywords` carries `gamma`, and keywords are indexed at the title's weight.

**Why it is wrong.** Wikipedia writes this table's $c$ as $\gamma$, and that
is presumably where the keyword came from. Nobody looking for Meixner
polynomials types "gamma" alone, but people looking for the gamma function or
Euler's $\gamma$ do, and they would get this table as a title-weight hit. The
other two keywords, "discrete Laguerre polynomials" and "Meixner polynomial",
are the right kind.

**Fix.** Delete `gamma` from `Keywords`.

## Noticed only

* **`comment_1`, second sentence.** "The parameter $x$ is the non-negative
  integer support variable of the negative binomial weight, extended to a
  polynomial variable." $x$ is not a parameter of this table; the parameters
  are $\beta$, $c$ and $n$. "Support variable ... extended to a polynomial
  variable" is also dense. A plainer version: "The variable $x$ runs over the
  non-negative integers, where the weight $(\beta)_xc^x/x!$ is a negative
  binomial distribution, and the entries are polynomials in it."
* **`complete-note` ends in a noun phrase.** "... and every degree
  $0\leq n\leq16$, a grid of small rational negative binomial shape and weight
  parameters." The appended phrase restates the list rather than saying why
  these values were chosen. If there is a reason, such as $\beta=1$ being the
  geometric distribution and $c=\tfrac12$ the fair case, say it. Otherwise
  stop after "$n\leq16$".
* **`formula-wikipedia-normalisation` is a convention, not a formula.**
  Under the skill's section table it belongs in `Comments`. It is one short
  sentence and reads fine where it is.
* **The Hahn relation reuses $\beta$ in a different slot.** "tend to these as
  $N\to\infty$ with parameters $(\beta-1,N(c^{-1}-1),N)$" is correct (DLMF
  18.21.4). But the Hahn table's own second parameter is also called
  $\beta$, and here it receives $N(c^{-1}-1)$. Writing
  "$Q_n(x;\beta-1,N(c^{-1}-1),N)\to M_n(x;\beta,c)$ as $N\to\infty$" would
  remove the need to recall the Hahn argument order.
