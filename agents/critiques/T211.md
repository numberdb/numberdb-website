## What was checked hardest

* **The reader holding a number.** The numbers are the first thing on
  the page, headed "height", with $N$, $c_4,c_6$ and the quantity
  beside them, and every row's comment names the curve twice, by LMFDB
  and by Cremona label, with a link. That reader is served, except that
  they will often land on two or four rows with the same digits and be
  told nothing about why (finding 1).
* **Parameters versus run.** The parameter lines say $N\geq1$ and give
  $c_4$, $c_6$ and the quantity without a range, which is the family;
  the range actually computed is in the complete-note, written as a
  clause that finishes "complete: no (…)". This is the split the skill
  asks for. The clause itself names Sage's database where it should
  name the curves (finding 5).
* **The rigour note against the generator.** The note's 192 guard bits
  are the generator's `WORKING_GUARD_BITS`; the area is computed in
  `RealBallField` from the AGM of the same three numbers Sage's period
  lattice uses, with Sage's own `faltings_height` as an outside check
  and not as the source, which is what the generator's docstring says
  and what its code does.
* **Formula (2) against the sources.** The LMFDB knowl and the Sage
  docstring both give $h_{\mathrm{st}}=h+\frac1{12}(\log\operatorname{denom}(j)-\log|\Delta|)$,
  and both say the two heights agree for semistable curves because
  $\operatorname{denom}(j)=|\Delta|$, which is comment (6).

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The first screen shows pairs of equal values and nothing says why

Under $N=15$ the rows for LMFDB $15.a5$ and $15.a4$ hold the same 100
digits, as do $15.a2$ and $15.a8$, $15.a1$ and $15.a3$; under $N=17$,
$17.a3$ and $17.a1$; under $N=21$ two pairs; under $N=24$ two pairs of
$h$ rows and two pairs of $h_{\mathrm{st}}$ rows. Of the 466 stored
values only 281 are distinct. The one comment on the subject, (8),
says the height "is not constant on an isogeny class", which is true
and is the opposite of what the reader is looking at. A reader who
arrives with $-0.40227757\ldots$ is answered with two curves and has to
work out for themselves that this is not a mistake.

The fact is the isogeny formula. If $\varphi\colon E\to E'$ is an
isogeny over $\mathbb{Q}$ of degree $d$ with $\varphi^*\omega_{E'}=c\,\omega_E$
for the Néron differentials, then $h(E')=h(E)+\frac12\log d-\log|c|$;
comment (8)'s ratios $5$ and $25$ are this formula at $d=5$ with $c=1$
and $c=5$. A cyclic $4$-isogeny with $c=2$, or a $9$-isogeny with
$c=3$, leaves the height unchanged, and that is every equal pair inside
a class in this table: Sage finds 50 such pairs among the 306 curves,
48 of degree $4$ and 2 of degree $9$, and no other. Across classes the
same digits recur where a curve is the quadratic twist by $-1$ of
another, which changes neither the lattice area nor the minimal model
($20.a$ and $80.b$, $24.a$ and $48.a$, $40.a$ and $80.a$, $96.a$ and
$96.b$), or a twist by $d$ composed with a $|d|$-isogeny ($50.a$ and
$50.b$ by $5$, $30.a$ and $90.c$ by $-3$, $32.a$ and $64.a$ by $2$),
where $\frac12\log|d|$ from the twist cancels against the isogeny.

Fix: one formula and one clause. Add a formula "If $\varphi\colon E\to E'$
is an isogeny of degree $d$ with $\varphi^*\omega_{E'}=c\,\omega_E$,
then $h(E')=h(E)+\frac12\log d-\log|c|$", and end comment (8) with
"…are $25$, $5$ and $1$. A $4$-isogeny with $c=2$ leaves the height
unchanged, which is why LMFDB $15.a4$ and $15.a5$ hold the same value,
and a quadratic twist by $-1$ does the same across classes, as for
$20.a$ and $80.b$." Worth doing; it is the one thing on the first
screen a reader is left to guess.

### 2. A reader with $c_4$ and $c_6$ cannot write down the curve

The parameters are titled "fourth c-invariant" and "sixth c-invariant"
and comment (7) says "The curve is identified by $N$, $c_4$ and $c_6$,
as in the neighbouring elliptic-curve tables". Nothing on the page says
what a c-invariant is or how to get a model from two of them. The
tables this one calls neighbouring do say it: T72 titles the parameters
"invariant $c_4$ of a minimal model of $E$" and has a comment "A model
of $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is given by $E\colon y^2=x^3-27c_4x-54c_6$". This
page should use the same words, since a reader moving between the
tables should meet one name for one thing, and "fourth c-invariant"
reads as the fourth item in a list.

Fix: retitle the two parameters "invariant $c_4$ of a global minimal
model of $E$" and likewise for $c_6$, and add the model sentence to
comment (7) in place of "as in the neighbouring elliptic-curve tables",
naming the table it is shared with: "the same identification as the
tables of real periods, HREF{Real_periods_of_elliptic_curves_over_Q_of_rank_1}[…]".
Worth doing.

### 6. The definition names a hypothesis every curve satisfies

"For an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ with global minimal model, this
table gives…" Every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ has a global
minimal model, so the phrase reads as a restriction on the family
where it means that $h(E)$ is computed on that model. The definition
then defers the whole content to "(1) and (2)", so a reader has to
scroll to find out what $h(E)$ is, and comment (5) then repeats
formula (1)'s $-\frac12\log A$ in words.

Fix: "The Faltings height of an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ is
$h(E)=-\frac12\log A$, where $A$ is the area of the period lattice of
the Néron differential on a global minimal model of $E$, and the stable
height $h_{\mathrm{st}}(E)$ is given by (2); this table gives both in
the LMFDB and Sage normalisation." Then the first sentence of comment
(5) can go. Worth doing if the page is being edited anyway; not on its
own.

### 7. "The unstable height" and "the Neron lattice" are names the page has not introduced

Comment (8) opens "The unstable height is not constant…", the first
and only time $h(E)$ is called that; the title, definition and column
call it the Faltings height. LMFDB and the Sage docstring do say
"unstable", so the word is right, but it should be introduced once, in
the definition or comment (5), before it is used alone. Formula (4)
says "the Neron lattice of $E$", where formula (1) called the same
object "the period lattice $\Lambda_E$ of the Neron differential"; one
name, and $\Lambda_E$ is already there to use. Néron carries an accent
in the prose; the unaccented form is right in Keywords, where the index
needs it, and nowhere else. Noted only, each a word.

### 8. "Those" and "therefore" in comment (6)

"If $E$ is semistable, then … $h_{\mathrm{st}}(E)=h(E)$. Those
stable-height rows are omitted rather than stored twice. A row with
quantity `stable` therefore records a non-semistable curve." The
reader has to resolve "those" to "the $h_{\mathrm{st}}$ rows of
semistable curves", and formula (3) says the first sentence already.
"For a semistable curve $h_{\mathrm{st}}(E)=h(E)$ by (3), and its
$h_{\mathrm{st}}$ row is omitted; a row with quantity `stable` is
therefore a curve that is not semistable" says the same in one
sentence with nothing to resolve. Noted only.

## What is right and should stay

The parameter lines and the complete-note divide family from run
exactly as the skill asks. Every row names its curve two ways and
links the LMFDB page, which is what a reader who arrived with a number
needs first. The rigour note's method, ball arithmetic from the AGM
with Sage's function as an outside check and four LMFDB pages as an
independent one, is the right shape, and the generator's docstring
opens with the three commands. Formula (4) with the Stevens citation
is the one place the corpus states what a Faltings ratio is. None of
that should be touched while fixing 1 to 5.
