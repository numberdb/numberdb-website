Read 2026-09-13. A private draft owned by zeta3, with 996 entries (every
connected graph on at most 7 vertices, in the counts 1, 1, 2, 6, 21, 112,
853), type `Z[]`, rigour `exact`.

The mathematics on the page holds, and so do the comments on the entries.
What a reader will notice is mostly in the entry comments and in three
faults copied from the two published siblings, T125 (chromatic) and T126
(Tutte). Findings 1 to 4 are each a reader being handed something wrong or
unusable. They are worth doing, and 2 to 4 should be done in T125 (and 2,
4 and 5 in T126) at the same time.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The cospectral notes print `#E@]o` as link text, and 8 of the 65 are written differently (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Under 57 entries: "It is cospectral with #E@]o ."
The visible text is the raw anchor, hash sign and all. Under the other 8:
"It is cospectral with the graph with graph6 string `F@?}W`", in code and
not linked. The 8 are exactly the graphs whose mate's key contains `}`:
`F@?}W`, `F?]}w`, `F@h}o`, `FBY}o`, `FY_}w`, `FG]}w`, `FJY}o`, `FBy}w`.
`HREF{#F@?}W}` would end at the first `}`, so the generator fell back to
prose. The fallback was right, but a reader sees two wordings for one fact
and cannot tell why some mates are links and others are not.

The word "cospectral" is also never defined on the page. It is in
`Keywords` and in 65 entry comments. A reader who came holding a
polynomial learns from it that their value does not determine the graph,
but only if they know the word.

**Why it is wrong.** `HREF{#key}` with no caption uses the target as its
text (`views.py`, `caption = ref`), so the `#` is shown. And the skill asks
for one wording per fact across the table.

**Smallest fix.** Use one wording for all 65, naming the mate in code
without a link: "It has the same characteristic polynomial as `E@]o`."
Every key can be written that way, and the reader can find the row with
their browser's find-in-page. A caption cannot carry the key: five of the
65 keys contain `]`, which ends a caption. Then add one comment defining
the term, for example: "Two graphs are *cospectral* when they have the same
characteristic polynomial. The 65 graphs here that share theirs with
another connected graph on the same number of vertices say so in their
entry comment." If links to the mate matter more than a single wording,
the fix belongs in the renderer: default a bare `HREF{#key}` caption to the
key without its `#`. That is a site change, not a table change.

### 2. The wheels are indexed by spokes, against the table's own $K_n$, $P_n$, $C_n$ (worth doing, and in T125 and T126)

**What a reader sees.** `D]{`, on 5 vertices, is "the wheel $W_4$".
`ELrw`, on 6, is $W_5$, and `FIefw`, on 7, is $W_6$.

**Why it is wrong.** Every other named family here counts vertices: $K_5$,
$P_5$ and $C_5$ all have 5. So does Sage: `graphs.WheelGraph(4).order()`
is 4. So do MathWorld and Wikipedia, which both give the $n$-vertex
convention first and name the $(n{+}1)$-vertex one as the alternative
(Gallian). A reader with Sage's `WheelGraph(5)` in hand looks for $W_5$,
lands on a graph with 6 vertices and a different polynomial, and has no
reason to doubt it.

**Smallest fix.** Rename them $W_5$, $W_6$, $W_7$. If spoke-indexing is
kept on purpose, write "the wheel $W_4$ with $4$ spokes and $5$ vertices"
instead. Make the same change in T125 and T126, which carry the same three
comments.

### 3. The Sage command in the graph6 comment renders with literal backslashes (worth doing, and in T125)

**What a reader sees.** "produce the name with
`g.canonical\_label().graph6\_string()`". MathJax sets `\texttt{}` as text,
and without the `textmacros` package, which the site does not load, `\_`
stays as the two characters `\_`. MathML check:
`<mtext mathvariant="monospace">g.canonical\_label().graph6\_string()</mtext>`.
Pasted into Sage, it is a `SyntaxError`.

**Why it is wrong.** This is the one line that tells a reader how to turn
the graph they hold into the key they need to look up, and as printed it
does not run.

**Smallest fix.** Use backticks, which now render as code in every prose
field: "Recover the graph in Sage with `Graph(name)`, and produce the name
with `g.canonical_label().graph6_string()`." T125 carries the same sentence,
live now. T126 has only `$\texttt{Graph(name)}$`, which renders correctly
but would read the same in backticks.

### 4. The Programs example is a graph the table does not hold (worth doing, and in T125 and T126)

**What a reader sees.** `G = Graph('I@OZCMgs?')  # the Petersen graph, by
its name here`.

**Why it is wrong.** `I` encodes 10 vertices, and the table stops at 7. The
string is the Petersen graph, and it is canonical, but it is not a key in
`Numbers`. "By its name here" invites the reader to find it on the page,
and it is not there.

**Smallest fix.** Use a graph the table holds and say so, for example
`Graph('FjaHw')  # the Moser spindle, by its name here`. Or keep the
Petersen graph and drop "by its name here".

### 5. The parameter reads "Symbolic (Unknown type)", and the range lives in the parameter (worth doing, with the siblings)

**What a reader sees.** "$G$ — Symbolic (Unknown type) (a connected graph
on at most $7$ vertices, named by its canonical graph6 string)". Under
Data properties it reads "Table is complete: true" with no note.

**Why it is wrong.** "Unknown type" reads as an error on the page. The
renderer prints it for any `type` it does not know unless the parameter has
a `title`. "At most $7$ vertices" is how far this run went, not the family:
the next person to add 8-vertex graphs has to edit the parameter. And
"complete: true" is true only of that range. T125 at least has a comment
saying what "complete" covers. T237 says it only inside the parameter's
parentheses.

**Smallest fix.** Add `title: graph` to the parameter, which replaces the
type text. Set `constraints` to "a simple connected graph, named by its
canonical graph6 string". Set `complete-note` to "it holds every connected
graph on at most $7$ vertices, all $996$ of them". Whether `complete` stays
`true` or becomes `no` should be decided once for all three graph tables.

### 6. Two comments do not read as sentences (worth doing, small)

- **The comment on connectedness** (`comment-connected`): "Connected graphs only. The characteristic polynomial is
  multiplicative on disjoint unions, $\phi(G\sqcup H,x)=\ldots$." The first
  sentence is a fragment. The second repeats Formula (1) word for word and
  never says why it matters. Better: "The table holds connected graphs
  only: the characteristic polynomial of a disconnected graph is the
  product of those of its components, CITE{formula-disjoint-union}, so it
  factors into entries here."
- **The comment on names** (`comment-named`): "The rest have no common name, so the graph6 string is
  the parameter rather than a convenience." A reader has to guess what
  "rather than a convenience" is set against. "No common name" is also
  too strong: `CN` is the paw, and the gem (on 5 vertices) is here
  unnamed too, and both names are standard. Better: "Thirty-five of the $996$ graphs here have a name in common
  use, and their entries give it. The rest are named only by their graph6
  string."

### 7. "Hückel" is findable only without its umlaut, and the chemistry tag is never explained (worth doing, small)

`Keywords` has "Huckel" and the tag is `chemistry`, but nothing on the page
says what the chemistry is. The search index has no `unaccent`, so a
reader typing "Hückel" gets nothing. **Fix:** add "Hückel" beside "Huckel"
in `Keywords`. Add one comment stating the fact, for example: "In Hückel
molecular orbital theory the $\pi$-electron energy levels of a conjugated
hydrocarbon are $\alpha+\lambda\beta$, where $\lambda$ runs over the roots
of the characteristic polynomial of its carbon skeleton."

## Noted only

- The Definition's second sentence ("This table uses simple connected graphs
  named by their canonical graph6 strings ..., and $\phi(G,x)$ is a monic
  polynomial ...") is about the table and a consequence, not the
  definition. The graph6 naming is said three times on the page and
  "monic" twice. Cutting that sentence back to "for a simple graph $G$"
  loses nothing.
- `Links[WikiSpectral]` is never cited.
- The smallest cospectral pair, $K_{1,4}$ and $C_4\sqcup K_1$, falls outside
  the table because one of the two is disconnected. A reader of the
  $K_{1,4}$ row might like to be told, but nothing is wrong without it.
- On the page, Numbers come before the Definition, as they do for every
  table. A reader who arrives at a row meets an opaque key like `E@Rw`, and
  the way to decode it is in the graph6 comment, several hundred rows down. That is
  the site's layout, not this table's fault.
