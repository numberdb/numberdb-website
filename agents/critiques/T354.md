# T354 critique: Sharp constants in the fractional Sobolev inequality

Read 2026-09-20. T354 is a private draft: `/T354` answers 404, as a draft
should. The SOCKS proxy on 127.0.0.1:1080 refused every connection again
(already documented in `docs/agent-environment.md`), but numberdb.org answers
`curl` directly, so the fetches below went direct.

I read the document through `GET /api/table?id=T354` with the key. I could not
fetch a rendered `/T354`, so I rebuilt the YAML from the document and rendered
it through `/preview?table=` in six pieces — the whole prose block is 3992
bytes and gunicorn refuses any request line past 4094 characters, so it has to
go in pieces. All six answered 200 with no parse error and no alert. I then
typeset every one of the 33 `$...$` fragments outside the entries under
MathJax 3 with all packages: none failed, and a deliberately broken control
did.

The table holds 610 entries of type `R`, rigour `proven`, 100 digits each, in
a 67 KB entries block — comfortably under every limit. There are no entry
comments, and none is wanted: `formula-sharp` says for every row at once what
a comment would have to repeat 610 times.

`GET /api/table/T354/audit` reports two findings, both about tags. I part
company with the audit on them; see finding 7.

## What I checked hardest, and found right

* **The page renders, including the `<`.** The definition and the `s`
  parameter both carry `$0<s<n/2$`, and `formula-classical` carries `$n>2$`.
  All three reach the HTML as `&lt;`/`&gt;` and the rest of each section
  renders after them; nothing is eaten. The parameter list renders as
  `$n$ —   dimension ($n\geq 1$)` and `$s$ —   fractional order ($0<s<n/2$)`,
  with no `argument ()`. The value column heads `$S_{n,s}$`, which is true of
  every row, and the rows print as `1 / 1/4: / 0.9204…` under `$n$`, `$s$`,
  `$S_{n,s}$`.

* **Every cross-reference resolves, in the section a reader meets it in.**
  The page order is Numbers, Definition, Parameters, **Formulas, Comments**,
  Programs, References, Links, Similar tables, Data properties — Formulas
  before Comments. So `CITE{formula-sharp}`, used in `comment-direction` and
  twice in `rigour details`, renders as a live `(1)` pointing back at the
  formula, including inside `rigour details`, which I checked separately
  because that field renders through its own prose path. The two `CITE`s in
  `formula-sharp` render as `[1]` and `[2]` with working arXiv, doi, zbMATH
  and MR links. `HREF{Best_Sobolev_constant_in_W1pRn}` resolves: the slug
  answers 200.

* **It is one table.** `n` and `s` are both arguments of one function, not
  names of several quantities; neither is a parameter whose values are words;
  the value column falls back on no generic label. `Similar tables` names its
  one relation in a sentence rather than pointing. There is no `param-latex`,
  so no column restates its own heading.

* **The parameters describe the family, not the run.** `$n\geq 1$` and
  `$0<s<n/2$` are the constraint the family satisfies; the grid that was
  actually computed is in `complete-note`, which finishes the rendered
  sentence "Table is complete: no (…)" as prose. This is the thing that goes
  wrong most often in this corpus and it is right here.

* **The stored values agree with the stated formula.** All 610 recomputed from
  `formula-sharp` in double precision agree to 11 significant figures, and
  $S_{2,1/2}$ is $\pi^{1/4}$ to every digit double precision has. I also
  checked that the corpus holds nothing else this table should point at:
  `Sobolev`, `Hardy`, `isoperimetric`, `Laplacian`, `Gagliardo-Nirenberg` and
  `sharp` return, between them, only T92. `Similar tables` is complete.

* **No claim on the page is about the website.** No "distinctive", no "a
  search for this would", no `--` used as a parenthesis, no "the former" or
  "as above" outside the one case in finding 8.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The only thing in `Links` is the issue that asked for the table — and it asked for a different table

`Request57` renders in `Links` as a single numbered entry, "Requested in
numberdb-data#57", cited from nowhere in the prose. The skill is explicit:

> A "table wanted" issue is answered **in the issue**, not in the table. …
> The table carries no trace of the request: it is encyclopedic, and who asked
> for it is not a fact about the mathematics. Cite the issue only in the rare
> case that it contains something a reader of the mathematics needs — a
> derivation, a convention, a source — and then cite that content, not the
> request.

This exact finding was made on T339 (`agents/critiques/T339.md`, finding 3),
and one table in 254 generators carries such a link, so it is not house style
either.

Here it is worse than furniture. Issue 57 is titled "Best constant for Sobolev
inequality for Laplace operator", and its body reads: "See Wang's 1993 paper
(mr=1202203, doi=10.1016/0362-546X(93)90162-L). The given formula (unless p=2)
requires numerical solutions to a ODE and integrals". That is a request about
the $p$-family of the ordinary Laplacian — the direction T92 already runs in —
not about fractional order $s$. T354 meets it only at $s=1$, which is T92's
$p=2$ column and was already there. A reader who clicks the one link on the
page lands on a request this table does not answer.

And because it is the only link, deleting it leaves the table with no outward
source at all, which the skill also refuses: every table has Links.

**Smallest fix:** delete `Request57`; add the Wikipedia *Sobolev inequality*
article, which is what T92 links and which covers the fractional case, or a
fractional-specific article. Say in issue 57 what now exists and what does not.
Wang 1993 is a source for the $p$-Laplacian constant, not for these values, so
it does not belong here either.

### 2. "the $L^2$-unitary Fourier transform" names two transforms, and only one of them makes the sentence true

`comment-fourier` is the table's one statement of convention, and the whole of
what pins down what $(-\Delta)^{s/2}$ means:

> Here $(-\Delta)^{s/2}$ is the Fourier multiplier $|\xi|^s$ for the
> $L^2$-unitary Fourier transform.

Two conventions in common use are $L^2$-unitary, and they disagree here:

* $\hat u(\xi)=\int u(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,dx$ sends $-\Delta$ to
  $4\pi^2|\xi|^2$, so the operator with symbol $|\xi|^s$ is
  $(-\Delta/4\pi^2)^{s/2}$, not $(-\Delta)^{s/2}$;
* $\hat u(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int u(x)e^{-i x\cdot\xi}\,dx$ sends $-\Delta$ to
  $|\xi|^2$, and there the sentence is true.

So the comment is self-consistent under exactly one reading, and a careful
reader can back it out — but a reader who takes the ordinary-frequency unitary
transform, which is the one most often called *the* unitary Fourier transform,
and applies the stated multiplier, recomputes every value a factor $(2\pi)^s$
off. Nothing on the page catches them: the $s=1$ agreement with T92 that would
settle it is stated in `Similar tables`, four sections later, in words.

The values themselves are unambiguous — I confirmed the stored $s=1$ rows are
$\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}$-normalised by comparing them with T92 — so this is a
defect in the sentence, not in the numbers.

**Smallest fix:** write the transform, or anchor the normalisation to
something convention-free: "normalised so that
$\|(-\Delta)^{1/2}u\|_{L^2}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}$". The same sentence is in the
generator's docstring (`generators/sharp-constants-fractional-sobolev-inequality/generate.py`)
and wants the same clause.

### 3. `formula-classical` is not a formula, and it is the third statement of one fact

```
For $n>2$, $S_{n,1}$ is the $p=2$, $q=2n/(n-2)$ entry of
HREF{Best_Sobolev_constant_in_W1pRn}[the classical Sobolev table].
```

It states no identity, closed form, generating function or recurrence. It is a
cross-reference, and the corpus has two homes for one — both of which already
hold it. `comment-classical`, one section below, says "When $s=1$, these are
the $p=2$ cases of [the best Sobolev constants for $W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^n)$]",
and the `Similar tables` relation says it a third time and best: "the $s=1$
rows are the same constants as its $p=2$, $q=2n/(n-2)$ rows, in the same
lower-bound direction". A reader meets the same sentence three times, and the
first time it is labelled as a formula.

The link caption is wrong twice over as well: "the classical Sobolev table"
names a page on this website rather than the mathematics, where
`comment-classical`'s caption names the constants.

**Smallest fix:** delete `formula-classical`. The comment and the relation are
both better written and both already there.

### 4. The word "Laplacian" appears nowhere the index can see

The table is about $(-\Delta)^{s/2}$, and $\Delta$ is the only place that
operator is named — inside `$…$`, as a symbol. The index reads the title and
`Keywords` first, then `Tags`, then the `Definition`, then the `Comments`, and
"Laplacian" is in none of them. A reader holding one of these numbers and
typing "fractional Laplacian" matches on "fractional" alone; "Laplacian" by
itself currently returns T238, T84 and T218 and will keep doing so.

Meanwhile the third keyword, "Lieb sharp constant", is not a phrase anyone
types. It earns its place only through the word "Lieb", which the bib already
carries but which `References` is not indexed for — so it is doing one word's
work in three.

**Smallest fix:** in `Keywords`, replace "Lieb sharp constant" with "fractional
Laplacian". If a second name is wanted, "Cotsiolis-Tavoularis" is the
attribution a reader looking for this constant is most likely to have met.

### 5. `complete-note` says which range, not why

> it holds every dimension $n\leq 20$ and every rational fractional order $s$
> in the admissible range whose reduced denominator is at most $4$

That is a clean sentence and it finishes the rendered "complete: no (…)"
correctly. What it is missing is the argument, which the skill asks for in
exactly this field: "Say which range you chose and why in the completeness
note. That sentence is the argument, and it is what a reader checking whether
their own number belongs here actually reads."

A denominator bound is the shape the skill is most sceptical of — "a bound on
the denominator that admits it is counting rather than choosing" — and here it
happens to be defensible, because denominator at most 4 is precisely
$s=\tfrac14,\tfrac13,\tfrac12,\tfrac23,\tfrac34,1,\tfrac54,\ldots$, and
$s=\tfrac12$, $1$, $\tfrac32$ and $2$ are the orders that actually occur. The
note does not say that, so the reader cannot tell the choosing from the
counting.

**Smallest fix:** one clause — "…at most $4$, which is every order in the
range for which the literature computes anything, $s=\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2$
included."

### 6. Fourteen rows are the same constants as T92's, six of them print a different last digit, and nothing on either row says so

The `Similar tables` relation is right and I verified it: for the 14
dimensions where both tables have the row, T354's $s=1$ value and T92's
$p=2$ value are the same constant. Eight agree to all 100 digits. Six —
$n=5,8,12,14,16,18$ — differ by one unit in the 100th, T354 one above T92.

That is inside the convention: a stored real is an interval whose last digit
may be off by one, both intervals contain the constant, and the build recorded
the cause (`agents/lessons/proposals/20260920T034510Z-build.md`: T92 was
serialised under older rounding). Nothing is wrong with the numbers.

What is missing is the statement at the row. A reader who arrives holding
$S_{5,1}$ lands on one of the two pages, and the relation that would tell them
the other page holds the same constant is a sentence in `Similar tables` on
this one and absent on T92. The corpus has the precise instrument for this and
the skill names it: `equals` "says the two entries *are the same number*,
which is stronger than related and which search reads: it answers with the
original and names the other beside it." It sits beside the stored number, not
instead of it (T29 holds a hundred places of $\pi$ and a pointer), and T92's
anchors are already the right shape — I checked that `3,2,6`, `5,2,10/3` and
`20,2,20/9` all exist.

**Smallest fix:** on each of the 14 $s=1$ rows, add
`equals: HREF{Best_Sobolev_constant_in_W1pRn#<n>,2,<q>}` beside the number. If
that is more editing than the draft wants before review, the one-line version
is a clause on the `Similar tables` relation saying the two tables agree to
the precision each of them states — which is what the six last digits mean and
what a reader comparing them will otherwise have to work out alone.

## Noted only because I noticed

### 7. I disagree with the audit's two findings as worded

The audit says:

```
Tags: "functional analysis" reaches only this table; a tag that leads nowhere else leads nowhere
Tags: "inequality" reaches only this table; a tag that leads nowhere else leads nowhere
```

"only this table" is not what is true. Both tags exist already and both reach
**T92**, which is precisely the table T354 should sit beside; `/tags/inequality`
lists T92 and nothing else. The audit reads `Tag.table_count`, which counts
published tables, and T354 is a draft and not in it — so the count of 1 it
found is T92, and the message misattributes it to the table being audited. On
publication each tag reaches two.

The direction is right even so: two is still under the skill's bar of three,
and T354 is not the table that can fix it, because it is doing the thing the
skill asks — "Prefer an existing tag". Carrying them doubles their reach. I
would leave the tags alone and leave the audit findings unactioned, with a
note on the draft saying why. (The misleading wording is a site defect and I
have written it up in `docs/agent-environment.md`.)

### 8. Two places point rather than name

`comment-direction` opens "The table stores the lower-bound direction in the
displayed inequality". There is no displayed inequality: the definition's
inequality is set inline, so a reader looking for a display finds none, and
"the displayed inequality" is a pointer where "the inequality in the
definition" is a name. The same sentence then says "this constant" where
`$S_{n,s}$` is shorter and stays right if the sentence moves.

`comment-classical` opens "When $s=1$, these are the $p=2$ cases", where
"$S_{n,1}$" is both shorter and unambiguous.

Neither would mislead anybody. They are worth a pass if the comments are being
edited for finding 2 anyway.

### 9. $\dot H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is used and never defined

The definition ends "for $u\in \dot H^s(\mathbb{R}^n)$", and nothing says what
that space is. `comment-fourier` gets close — once $(-\Delta)^{s/2}$ is the
multiplier $|\xi|^s$, the natural reading is
$\{u:|\xi|^s\hat u\in L^2\}$ — so a reader who reads both fields recovers it.
The skill's rule is stricter than that ("If a symbol appears in a formula or a
comment, its definition belongs in the same table"), and a clause in
`comment-fourier` would close it.

### 10. The first "check" in `rigour details` is the computation restated

> The reciprocal-square relation in (1) was checked on every entry

The values *are* computed from (1). Recomputing (1) and comparing is arithmetic
about the serialiser, not evidence about the mathematics, and a reader reads
"checked on every entry" as the strongest of the three claims when it is the
weakest. The two that follow are the real ones, and they are smaller than they
sound: one entry ($S_{2,1/2}=\pi^{1/4}$) and 14 of 610 (the $s=1$ agreement
with T92's formula, which I reproduced). Saying that plainly would serve a
reader better than the present ordering does, and it is still a good record —
`proven` does not rest on the cross-checks.

### 11. The `Programs` snippet prints nothing

The last line is `upper.rsqrt()  # S_{3,1/2}`, a bare expression. In a Sage
session it echoes; under `sage -python generate.py`, which is the incantation
the corpus tells readers to use, it computes the answer and discards it. The
variable is also named `upper` for the quantity the table's own
`comment-direction` calls the reciprocal square.

**Smallest fix:** `print(upper.rsqrt())`, and `reciprocal_square` for the name.

---

## Verdict

This is a good table, and most of what is above is polish. The things that
would actually cost a reader something are findings 1 and 2: a page whose only
outward link is a request for a different table, and a convention sentence that
does not pin the convention down. Both are one-line edits. Findings 3 to 6 are
worth doing in the same pass; 7 to 11 are notes, and 7 is a note about the
audit rather than about T354.

The definition defines, the parameters describe the family, the value column
says what it holds, the entries are ordered small-first in both indices, and
there is not one sentence on the page that is about this website. That is more
than most drafts arrive with.
