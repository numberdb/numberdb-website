# T271 critique: Wilson polynomials $W_n(x^2;a,b,c,d)$

Read 2026-09-16. T271 is a private draft. The SOCKS proxy on 127.0.0.1:1080
refused the connection, but numberdb.org answered `curl` directly. `/T271`
answers 404, as a draft should. I read the document through
`GET /api/table?id=T271` with the key. I rendered it through
`/preview?table=` in six pieces: definition, parameters and three entries;
comments; formulas; similar tables, tags and completeness; data properties
with every link; programs. All six answered 200 with no "Error while
parsing". The table holds 770 entries of type `Q[]` with rigour `exact`: every
nondecreasing quadruple from $\{\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2,3\}$ (70 of them), each
at $0\leq n\leq10$. There are no entry comments, and the longest entry is 450
characters.

`GET /api/table/T271/audit` reports `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. I agree
that the form is sound: the one `HREF` (`Jacobi_polynomials`, T106) resolves,
every `CITE` resolves, and each section holds what it should. The audit cannot
see findings 1 to 3, which are about what the prose says. It could have seen
finding 4 but does not look for it.

**What I checked hardest, and found right:**

* *The page renders.* All 39 `$...$` fragments outside the entries typeset in
  MathJax 3 (node, all packages) with no error. A control with an unclosed
  brace did fail. The one `<` in the mathematics, $0<a$ in the definition,
  reaches the HTML as `&lt;`, and the rest of the definition renders after it.
  Rows print as `1/2 1/2 1/2 1/2 2: 12*y^2 - 18*y + 11/4` under the headers
  $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $n$ and $W_n(y;a,b,c,d)$, and each row's anchor is
  `1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2,2`.
* *It is one table, and the columns say so.* $a,b,c,d,n$ are arguments of one
  polynomial, and the header $W_n(y;a,b,c,d)$ is true of every row. The row
  labels are the values themselves. The parameters describe the family
  ($a>0$, $n\geq0$, with the nondecreasing order explained in a comment), and
  the grid that was computed is in `complete-note`.
* *The stated mathematics holds.* The checks ran in Python `Fraction`s,
  independently of the generator:
  * every row agrees with the ${}_4F_3$ sum at $y=0,1,-7/3,5/2,-9$ (3850
    evaluations);
  * the degree and leading coefficient are right on every row;
  * $W_n(-r^2)=\prod_{s\neq r}(r+s)_n$ holds for all four shape parameters on
    every row (3080 checks);
  * the sum, recomputed at all 24 orderings of the parameters, gives the stored
    row (110 rows sampled);
  * the three-term recurrence of Koekoek, Lesky and Swarttouw (9.1.3) holds for
    $1\leq n\leq9$ (3150 checks, 0 failures). The recurrence does not use the
    sum, so this is the independent check. As a control, replacing $y$ by $-y$
    fails 1400 times.
  * The Wilson-Jacobi limit in `Similar tables` is right as written, with
    $t^{2n}$. At $\alpha=\tfrac12$, $\beta=1$, $x=\tfrac13$ and $n=2,3$, in
    exact Gaussian rationals, the error is $1.9\cdot10^{-7}$ at $t=10^3$ and
    $1.9\cdot10^{-13}$ at $t=10^6$, so it falls like $t^{-2}$.

I looked for tables that `Similar tables` is missing and found none it should
hold. The corpus has Hahn (T268), Meixner (T269), Krawtchouk (T266, T270) and
Bateman (T183) polynomials. None of them is a limit or specialisation of
$W_n$ in one step: Hahn is reached through Racah, and Bateman's $F_n$ is a
continuous Hahn case. Jacobi is the one direct relation, and it is there.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Wikipedia comment points at a convention instead of stating it (worth doing)

A reader sees:

> This table uses the DLMF normalisation [1]. The Wikipedia article [2] writes
> a different sign convention for the two upper parameters that contain the
> variable.

"The two upper parameters that contain the variable" points at something
instead of naming it. It also stops before the one fact a reader needs, which
is how to get from Wikipedia's polynomial to this table's. Wikipedia writes
$p_n(t^2)$ with upper parameters $a-t$ and $a+t$. Then
$(a-t)_k(a+t)_k=\prod_j((a+j)^2-t^2)$, so **Wikipedia's $p_n(z)$ is
$W_n(-z;a,b,c,d)$ here**. I checked this on $W_3(y;\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2)$:
the two agree with $z\mapsto-z$ and disagree without it. This matters for the
reader the database is for. Somebody who built the polynomial from
Wikipedia's formula holds $W_n(-y)$, whose odd coefficients have the other
sign, and a search for it will not find this table. Calling this a
"normalisation" also misdescribes it, because nothing is rescaled.

Smallest fix: replace the second sentence with "The Wikipedia article CITE{Wiki}
writes the upper parameters as $a\pm t$ rather than $a\pm ix$, so its
$p_n(z)$ is $W_n(-z;a,b,c,d)$ here." Renaming the key `comment-normalisation`
is optional.

### 2. `rigour details` describes the run and names a file in this repository (worth doing)

A reader sees, after "more":

> Before the draft was filled, all entries were checked for exactness and
> measured by `agents/table-build/dry_run.py`: it found 770 entries, longest
> 450 characters, and a 141.3 KB entries block.

A reader of the site cannot reach `agents/table-build/dry_run.py`. "Before
the draft was filled", the entry count and the block size are facts about
this website's workflow, not about how the values were obtained. The next
sentence is the one that belongs here: the generator checked symmetry, the
special values, the leading coefficient and the Jacobi limit.

Smallest fix: delete the "Before the draft was filled ..." sentence. Keep the
first paragraph and the sentence listing the generator's checks.

### 3. `complete-note` says what is here but not why, and repeats a comment (worth doing, small)

> Table is complete: no (it holds every nondecreasing quadruple with
> $a,b,c,d\in\{\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,2,3\}$ and every degree $0\leq n\leq10$;
> the nondecreasing order uses the shape-parameter symmetry to avoid repeated
> entries)

The skill asks the completeness note to say which range was chosen *and why*.
A reader holding $W_n$ at $a=\tfrac52$ cannot tell whether $\tfrac52$ was
left out on purpose or by accident, since the set jumps from 2 to 3. The
clause after the semicolon repeats `comment-symmetry` almost word for word.

Smallest fix: replace the clause after the semicolon with the reason for this
grid. If the reason is the entries-block budget and nothing about which
parameters turn up, say that plainly. Leave the symmetry to the comment, which
already explains it.

### 4. A link nobody cites (small)

`DLMFConstraints` ("DLMF Table 18.25.1: Wilson-class variables and parameter
constraints") is listed under Links, but no `CITE` in the document points at
it. It renders as reference [2] with nothing in the text referring to it.
Either cite it where a constraint is stated, e.g. after $a>0$ in the
definition or in `comment-symmetry`, or drop it.

### Noted only, not worth a change on their own

* The definition begins "For rational $0<a\leq b\leq c\leq d$". "Rational"
  and the ordering describe this table's grid rather than the Wilson family,
  and "defined by DLMF's terminating ${}_4F_3$ representation ..., with
  $(a+ix)_k(a-ix)_k$ read as $\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}((a+j)^2+y)$" sends the reader
  to DLMF for a formula this page already states as Formulas (1). T268 and
  T269 open in exactly this way, so changing only T271 would make it
  inconsistent with its neighbours. `CITE{formula-hypergeometric}` in place of
  the "read as" clause would be shorter.
* The title writes $W_n(x^2;\dots)$ and the entries are polynomials in $y$.
  The definition and `comment-variable` both say $y=x^2$, which is enough.
  Search ignores the name of the variable (I checked `16*t^5 - 20*t^3 + 5*t`
  and `16*y^5 - 20*y^3 + 5*y`, and both found the stored
  `16*x^5 - 20*x^3 + 5*x`), but a reader who expanded $W_n$
  as a polynomial in $x$ holds a polynomial of degree $2n$ that will not
  match. Storing in $y$ is the standard choice and I would not change it.
* `comment-variable` repeats the definition's "$y=x^2$", and it is harmless.
