## Findings, ranked

### 1. The definition names the functions and cites them, and does not say what they are

A reader sees: "This table gives the real principal values of the
Bessel functions $J_\nu(x)$ and $Y_\nu(x)$ [1] [2], where
$\nu\in\mathbb{Q}$ and $x\in\mathbb{Q}$ with $x>0$."

That is a sentence about the table, and the object is defined by two
footnotes. Compare the corpus's own table of Bessel polynomials, whose
definition gives the recurrence and the differential equation and
stops. Somebody who arrives holding $0.7651976\ldots$ and does not
already know what a Bessel function is learns nothing on this page.
"Real principal values" also carries a caveat the definition does not
need: for $x>0$ there is no branch to choose, and comment (4) already
says so.

Smallest fix, one definition: "$J_\nu$ and $Y_\nu$ are the Bessel
functions of the first and second kind [2]: the solutions of
$x^2y''+xy'+(x^2-\nu^2)y=0$ with
$J_\nu(x)=\sum_{k\geq0}(-1)^k(x/2)^{2k+\nu}/(k!\,\Gamma(\nu+k+1))$ and
$Y_\nu(x)=(J_\nu(x)\cos\nu\pi-J_{-\nu}(x))/\sin\nu\pi$, taken as a
limit at integer $\nu$. The table holds their values at rational
$\nu\geq0$ and rational $x>0$." Worth doing; it is the first thing a
reader reads after the numbers.

### 2. The parameter says $\nu\in\mathbb{Q}$, comment (5) says negative orders are never listed, and the formula it points at is not on the page

The parameter list says "order ($\nu\in\mathbb{Q}$)". Comment (5)
says "Negative integer orders are not listed separately" and that
nonintegral negative orders are expressed "through $J_\nu(x)$ and
$Y_\nu(x)$" by the connection formulas in [3]. So the family the table
holds by design is $\nu\geq0$, and the constraint should say so: the
skill asks for the constraint the entries meet, and every entry here
meets $\nu\geq0$. The batch proposal made this decision explicitly
("Negative orders are excluded").

The second sentence of (5) is also circular as written: a reader is
told that $J_{-\nu}$ is expressed through $J_\nu$ without being told
that $\nu$ has changed sign in between, and the one formula a reader
holding $J_{-1/3}(1)=0.60688\ldots$ needs is left at DLMF. It is a
formula and belongs under Formulas, beside (2), which is its integer
case:

$J_{-\nu}(x)=\cos(\nu\pi)\,J_\nu(x)-\sin(\nu\pi)\,Y_\nu(x)$ and
$Y_{-\nu}(x)=\sin(\nu\pi)\,J_\nu(x)+\cos(\nu\pi)\,Y_\nu(x)$ [3].

Checked in balls at $\nu=\tfrac13,\tfrac23,\tfrac52$ and $x=1,\tfrac74$
against arb's own $J_{-\nu}$ and $Y_{-\nu}$: all overlap with
differences below $10^{-116}$. With that formula on the page, comment
(5) shrinks to "Negative orders are not listed; they are given by (2)
and (4)." Worth doing.

### 3. Comment (4) says why $x=0$ is excluded and not why $x<0$ is, and the parity formula is missing

A reader sees "The point $x=0$ is excluded because $Y_\nu$ is singular
there" and nothing about negative arguments, which the parameter
excludes too. The reason is a fact about the mathematics and is short:
for $x<0$, $Y_\nu(x)$ is not real for any $\nu$ and $J_\nu(x)$ is real
only at integer $\nu$ (checked: $Y_0(-1)=0.0883+1.5304i$,
$J_{1/3}(-1)=0.3654+0.6330i$). The integer case is a parity, and the
batch proposal said it would go under Formulas: $J_n(-x)=(-1)^nJ_n(x)$
for integer $n$ (checked at $n=1,2$, $x=2$). A reader holding
$J_1(-2)=-0.5767\ldots$ is one sign from a row here and the page does
not tell them. Worth doing; one sentence in (4) and one formula.

### 4. Comment (7) and the Bessel-polynomials gloss do not say what they mean

A reader sees "Half-integer orders are elementary combinations of
$\sin x$ and $\cos x$." An order is not a combination, and the values
are not combinations of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ either: $J_{3/2}(x)$ is
$\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\,(\sin x/x-\cos x)$, with the square root in front
and a coefficient in $1/x$. Then "The Bessel polynomials occur in
explicit formulas for the associated spherical and modified spherical
functions [6]": associated with what, and [6] is DLMF 10.49, which
does not name the Bessel polynomials. The Similar-tables gloss says the
same thing as "half-integer spherical and modified spherical Bessel
functions", and the spherical functions are indexed by integers; it is
the cylindrical ones that have half-integer order.

The relation that is actually true, and that the corpus's Bessel
polynomials table alludes to from its side, is one line: for integer
$n\geq0$,
$J_{n+1/2}(x)+iY_{n+1/2}(x)=\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\,(-i)^{n+1}e^{ix}\,y_n(i/x)$,
with $y_n$ the Bessel polynomial. Checked in balls for $n=0$ to $4$ at
$x=\tfrac14,1,\tfrac74$: all overlap, differences below $10^{-113}$.
Suggested (7): "At half-integer order, $J_\nu$ and $Y_\nu$ are
$\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}$ times a combination of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ whose
coefficients are polynomials in $1/x$ [6]; in terms of
HREF{Bessel_polynomials}[the Bessel polynomials] $y_n$, [the identity
above]." And the gloss: "give the closed form of $J_\nu$ and $Y_\nu$ at
half-integer $\nu$". Worth doing with 1 to 3; the present sentences are
not wrong so much as not saying anything a reader can use.

### 5. Sixty rows carry the same comment, and it is formula (3)

Every $\nu=\tfrac12$ row says "$J_{1/2}(x)=\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\sin x$." or
its $Y$ twin, thirty times each, and formula (3) says the same thing
once for all of them. The skill leaves out a comment that "repeats
what a formula in the document already says for every row at once".

The argument for keeping something there is real: a search by number
lands on the row, not on the formula, and the comment is what tells
that reader their value is elementary. If a comment stays, make it the
row's own fact, "$=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sin 1$" on the row for $x=1$, which
is a closed form the formula does not print and which a reader can
type into something else. Marginal; noted so whoever repairs decides
rather than inherits.

## Noted only

* **"[1] [2]" back to back** in the definition. If finding 1 is taken,
  one citation is enough there and Wikipedia stays in Links.
* **The four glosses say "for real order $\alpha$"** while T20's own
  parameter is typed a complex number and its comment restricts to
  half-integers. "for order $\alpha$" would be safe against both.
* **OEIS A334380's own name** is "Decimal expansion of
  Sum_{k>=0} (-1)^k/((2*k)!!)^2", and the caption here says
  "decimal expansion of $J_0(1)$". The digits are the same and the
  caption is true; a reader who follows the link meets a series and
  has to recognise it. "$J_0(1)$, catalogued as
  $\sum_k(-1)^k/((2k)!!)^2$" would spare them that. A344074 is named
  "Bessel Y_0(1)" and its caption matches.
* **All 270 $J$ rows come before all 270 $Y$ rows**, so $J_0(1)$ and
  $Y_0(1)$ are a long scroll apart. That is the parameter order, which
  is frozen, and the reader who wants both has the anchors; nothing to
  do.
* **"These are the ordinary cylindrical Bessel functions"** in (6)
  opens with a pointer, but the referent is the whole table and a
  reader will not miss it.
