## Worth doing

### 1. The definition leaves the object to be assembled from three places, and a citation breaks its noun phrase

Rendered:

> For $k\geq3$ and $r\geq2$, $\lambda_{k,r}$ is the positive minimiser in (3),
> the formula for the random $k$-uniform hypergraph [3] $r$-core threshold.

To find out what is being minimised, a reader jumps to (3). There they meet
$Q$ and jump again to (1). So the definition only defines once two formulas
are read. The last clause stacks five nouns ("random $k$-uniform hypergraph
$r$-core threshold"), and the Wikipedia marker `[3]` sits in the middle of the
stack. "Core threshold" is the first mention of a family the corpus holds, and
it is not linked in this section. The audit missed that because the phrase is
not the companion table's title.

Smallest fix: say what is minimised, in the sentence itself.

> For $k\geq3$ and $r\geq2$, $\lambda_{k,r}$ is the positive $\lambda$ at which
> $\lambda/(kQ(\lambda,r-1)^{k-1})$ attains its minimum, where
> $Q(\lambda,s)$ is the probability that a Poisson variable of mean $\lambda$
> is at least $s$. The minimum is the
> HREF{Core_thresholds_of_random_k-uniform_hypergraphs}[$r$-core threshold]
> $c_{k,r}$ of the random $k$-uniform hypergraph CITE{WikiHypergraph}.

### 2. `Keywords` is the companion table's, word for word, and names nothing on this page

`peeling threshold, pure literal rule, invertible Bloom lookup table, 2-core`
is, word for word, the `Keywords` of "Core thresholds of random $k$-uniform
hypergraphs".
There they are explained: its `comment-peeling` ties $c_{3,2}$ to peeling,
the pure literal rule and IBLTs. Here, nothing on the page says any of those
words. Keywords carry the same weight as the title, so somebody typing
"invertible Bloom lookup table" is offered this table level with the one that
answers them. They then land on a page that never mentions IBLTs.

Smallest fix: replace the list with words for what this table holds, for
example `hypergraph core, Poisson parameter, core threshold minimiser`. The
other option is to leave the field empty and let the title (finding 3) carry
the search.

### 3. The title does not say "hypergraph", and a threshold has no minimiser

"Minimisers of the core threshold" names neither the random hypergraph nor
the thing minimised. $c_{k,r}$ is a number; $\lambda_{k,r}$ minimises the
function whose minimum it is. The only occurrence of "hypergraph" is in the
definition, which has a lower search weight. The corpus also holds graph core
thresholds, where the same minimisation appears.

Smallest fix: "Minimisers in the core threshold formula of random $k$-uniform
hypergraphs". A shorter option is "Minimisers for core thresholds of random
$k$-uniform hypergraphs", which is close to the companion's title so the two
sit together in a listing. The title is not frozen the way parameter keys are,
so this is cheap before publication.

### 4. The graph table's name is misspelt in `Similar tables`

The caption reads "$k$-core thresholds of the Erdos-Renyi random graph". That
table's title, and its slug in this same link, spell it "Erdős–Rényi". A
reader sees two people's names misspelt next to a link that spells them
correctly. Fix: copy the title,
`[$k$-core thresholds of the Erdős–Rényi random graph]`.

## Worth doing if the table is being edited anyway

### 5. `comment-convention` carries formula (3) a second time

> The notation and indexing match the core threshold table: $\lambda_{k,r}$
> gives $c_{k,r}=\lambda_{k,r}/(kQ(\lambda_{k,r},r-1)^{k-1})$.

The formula after the colon is the right-hand side of (3), repeated. Comments
are not the place for formulas. Fix: "The notation and indexing match
HREF{...}[the core threshold table], whose $c_{k,r}$ is obtained from
$\lambda_{k,r}$ by CITE{formula-threshold}."

### 6. Formula (2) is a proof, and `rigour details` gives it again

After the first sentence, (2) goes on: "The sign of $g$ is the sign of the
derivative of …, and $g'(\lambda)=\dots$, so $g$ decreases and then
increases, with one positive zero." That is an argument, and the first
paragraph of `rigour details` makes the same argument. Keep the first sentence
of (2), which is the relation, and perhaps $g'$ as a second formula. Move the
"decreases and then increases" reasoning to `rigour details`, which already
has it.

(I checked the mathematics while reading. $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\,\lambda/Q^{k-1}$
has numerator $g$. $g'$ is as stated. $g(0)=0$ because $Q(0,r-1)=0$ for
$r\geq2$. At $k=3$, $r=2$ the entry gives $c_{3,2}=0.81847$, matching the
companion table.)

### 7. `rigour details` mentions "the $k=2$ extension" that this table does not have

> … and checks the $k=2$ extension against the graph-core threshold table.

This table has $k\geq3$, so a reader does not know what is being extended.
The graph table's own parameter is also called $k$ and means the *core
degree*, which is this table's $r$. So "$k=2$" invites exactly the wrong
reading. Fix: "and checks the same minimisation at edge size $2$, which this
table does not hold, against …". The paragraph's first sentence, "The
minimiser $\lambda_{k,r}$ is the unique minimiser because …", also reads as
circular. "$\lambda_{k,r}$ is the only minimiser because …" says the same
thing.

## Noting only

- The same entry comment appears on all 42 rows: "The associated core
  threshold is $c_{k,r}$." It is worth keeping, because it links the one
  number a reader holding $\lambda_{k,r}$ most likely wants next, and the skill
  asks for exactly that link.
- A reader holding, say, 1.25643 from a peeling analysis learns from the page
  that it is the minimiser. They do not learn what $\lambda_{k,r}$ *is* in the
  random hypergraph at the threshold (a Poisson mean of a degree). The graph
  table spends a sentence on that for its $\lambda_k$. A sourced sentence here
  would serve that reader, but I have not checked the literature's statement
  of it, so I am not proposing wording.
- The graph table stores its own minimisers $\lambda_k$ only in entry comments.
  That is a finding about that table, not this one. It would be the natural
  place for rows if this family ever takes in edge size $2$.
