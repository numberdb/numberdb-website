# Critique of T299, Values of the elliptic alpha function $\alpha(r)$

Read on 2026-09-17, as a draft.

## How it was read

* **Rendering.** T299 is a private draft, so `/T299` answers 404. The SOCKS
  proxy on 127.0.0.1:1080 refused the connection, and numberdb.org answered
  curl directly. Both of these are already recorded in
  `docs/agent-environment.md`. I rendered the document through
  `/preview?table=...` in seven pieces, each under 3 KB. Each piece carried
  one entry, because a preview with no entries renders nothing. All seven
  answered 200. MathJax 3 (`base`, `ams`) typeset all 59 `$...$` fragments in
  the prose and the row comments without error. As a control, two fragments
  with unclosed braces did raise errors. Every `CITE{}` resolves.
  `HREF{T295}` and `HREF{T296}` render as links to `/T295` and `/T296`, but
  both of those are drafts and answer 404 to a reader until they are
  published.
* **Document.** `GET /api/table?id=T299` with the key. It holds 100 entries,
  $r=1,\dots,100$. Rows 1 to 10 have comments.
* **Audit.** `GET /api/table/T299/audit` answered `"findings": [], "clean":
  true`. I agree that nothing it looks for is present. Findings 1 to 3 below
  are not the kind of thing it checks. The notation check its docstring
  promises is still not implemented, and that check would have caught
  finding 2.
* **Two facts I checked before stating them** (plain Python, from the stored
  values):
  * $\alpha(r)-1/\pi$ is $2.63\cdot10^{-5}$, $1.13\cdot10^{-7}$ and
    $1.76\cdot10^{-12}$ at $r=20,40,100$. Those match
    $8(\sqrt r-1/\pi)e^{-\pi\sqrt r}$ to the three figures shown. That
    expression is the first-order term of Formula (2), since
    $\theta_3^4=1+8q+\dots$ and $q\,\theta_4'/\theta_4=-2q+\dots$.
  * The class number of discriminant $-4r$ is at most 12 for $r\leq100$. The
    maximum occurs at $r=89$, so the complete-note is true.

It is one table, with one parameter, holding one object, and the column
labels `$r$` and `$\alpha(r)$` are right. The page renders cleanly. The
problems are in the prose, and most of them trace back to the commit
"shorten elliptic alpha definition" (ecfb279). That commit moved the defining
formula out of the Definition and left the rest of the page still pointing at
it.

## Worth doing

### 1. The Definition names $\alpha(r)$ but does not define it. High.

The page reads:

> For a positive integer $r$, set $\tau=i\sqrt r$ and $q=e^{-\pi\sqrt r}$.
> The entry is Ramanujan's elliptic alpha function $\alpha(r)$, in the
> singular-modulus convention of [1].

A reader who has only this sentence cannot compute a single row. "The
singular-modulus convention" is not explained anywhere on the page, so it
reads as a pointer to MathWorld rather than a statement. The sentence also
sets $\tau$ and $q$ and then never uses them. The formula that actually
defines the entry is Formula (1), further down.

The shortening also made two other sentences false. Both `Similar tables`
relations say that T295 and T296 hold quantities "used in the definition of
$\alpha(r)$", but the definition no longer uses $k_r$, $m_r$ or $K$.

**Fix:** put the formula back and drop the unused symbols:

> For a positive integer $r$, let $m_r=\lambda(i\sqrt r)$, where $\lambda$ is
> the elliptic modular function. The entry is the elliptic alpha function
> $\alpha(r)=\pi/(4K(m_r)^2)+\sqrt r-\sqrt r\,E(m_r)/K(m_r)$, where $K(m)$ and
> $E(m)$ are the complete elliptic integrals in the parameter $m=k^2$
> CITE{MathWorldAlpha}.

Then delete `formula-elliptic-integrals`, which would repeat the Definition
word for word. `rigour details` cites the other two formulas by label, so
nothing breaks. With this change, the two `Similar tables` relations become
true again without being edited.

### 2. Comment (1) uses $k_r$, $m_r$, $K$ and $E$ before anything defines them, and half of it is about arb. Medium.

> (1) MathWorld writes the same value as
> $\pi/(4K(k_r)^2)+\sqrt r-\sqrt r\,E(k_r)/K(k_r)$, with $k_r^2=m_r$. The
> table uses $K(m)$ and $E(m)$ because arb's `elliptic_k` and `elliptic_e`
> take the parameter $m$.

Comments render above Formulas. At this point on the page, $m_r$ is still
undefined: Formula (1) defines it further down, and $k_r$ is defined only in
Formula (3), as $k_s$. So a reader meets both symbols before learning what
they are. "The same value" also points backwards at a formula the reader has
not seen yet.

The second sentence gives a fact about the program as the reason for a
notational convention. The convention is about the mathematics; which
arguments arb takes belongs in `rigour details`, which already says so.

**Fix**, once finding 1 is done:

> $K$ and $E$ here take the parameter $m=k^2$. MathWorld writes them in the
> modulus, as $K(k_r)$ and $E(k_r)$ with $k_r=\sqrt{m_r}$.

### 3. Nothing says that $\alpha(r)\to1/\pi$, and forty rows begin $0.3183098\ldots$. Medium.

Rows 60 to 100 agree with $1/\pi=0.31830988618\ldots$ to between 9 and 12
digits. Someone who arrives holding one of them, or holding $1/\pi$ to eight
places, finds a column of near-identical numbers and no sentence explaining
why. That explanation is a fact about the function, and it belongs on the
page.

**Fix:** add a comment:

> $\alpha(r)$ decreases to $1/\pi$, and
> $\alpha(r)=1/\pi+8(\sqrt r-1/\pi)\,e^{-\pi\sqrt r}+O(\sqrt r\,e^{-2\pi\sqrt r})$,
> which follows from Formula (2).

I got the error term by hand from the next order of the $q$-expansion, where
the $q^2$ coefficient is $40/\pi-48\sqrt r$. Only the leading term was
checked against the stored values. If nobody verifies the error term, state
only the limit and the leading term.

### 4. The keyword "Ramanujan modular equation alpha" sends readers to the wrong table. Medium-low.

In Ramanujan's modular equations, as edited by Berndt, $\alpha$ is the
*squared modulus* $k^2$: "$\beta$ has degree $n$ over $\alpha$". Someone who
types that phrase wants $m_r=k_r^2$, which is in T295, not the Borweins'
$\alpha(r)$. `Keywords` is weighted like the title, so this keyword actively
misdirects that search. The keyword `singular modulus` does the same thing
more mildly, since T295 is the table of singular moduli.

**Fix:** remove both keywords. If the clash is worth stating, it is a
one-sentence comment: "This $\alpha(r)$ is not the $\alpha=k^2$ of
Ramanujan's modular equations, which is $m_r$ in HREF{T295}."

## Noted only

* **Parameter title** "index, with $\tau=i\sqrt r$". "Index" does not say what
  $r$ indexes, and the $\tau$ belongs in the Definition. "argument of
  $\alpha$", or simply "$r$", would do.
* **Comment (2)** about the lattice $\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\,i\sqrt r$ is true,
  but the page never says why it matters. The complete-note's clause about
  class number at most 12 is a property of the range, not a statement of what
  the range covers. Moving that clause into comment (2), together with the
  consequence it supports (that $\alpha(r)$ is algebraic), would give both
  sentences a reason to be there.
* **Row comments** read "MathWorld gives $\alpha(2)=\sqrt2-1$ [1]". The fact
  is the identity, and the citation already says where it comes from:
  "$\alpha(2)=\sqrt2-1$ [1]". The closed forms themselves are exactly what a
  reader holding 0.41421356 needs.
* **Similar tables** lists E(m) and K(m) as holding "the same complete
  elliptic integral at rational parameters". "The same" points at nothing;
  "holds $E(m)$ at rational $m$" says it. The caption for T296 also drops "of
  the first kind" from that table's title.
* **T295 and T296 are drafts.** The links from this page are dead until they
  are published. That is a question of publication order, not of prose.

## What I checked hardest

Whether the page renders: it does, with no TeX errors and every citation
resolving. Whether the Definition alone lets two people build the same table:
it does not (finding 1). And whether a reader holding a value near $1/\pi$ is
served: they are not (finding 3).
