## 1. The Gram polynomials are the discrete Legendre polynomials, not the discrete Chebyshev ones (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

> The rows with $\alpha=\beta=0$ are the Gram or discrete Chebyshev
> polynomials. They are finite-grid analogues of Chebyshev polynomials of the
> first kind and Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, not entries equal
> to either table.

**Why it is wrong.** The name "discrete Chebyshev" is Chebyshev's, not a
statement about the limit. The table's own Similar-tables relation sends
$Q_n(Nx;\alpha,\beta,N)$ to $P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1-2x)/P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1)$.
At $\alpha=\beta=0$ that is the Legendre polynomial. The Chebyshev
polynomials are the Jacobi cases $\alpha=\beta=\mp\tfrac12$, as T106's own
`comment-Chebyshev` says. At $N=20000$, $n=3$ and $x=0.2$, the three
diagonal grids give:

| rows | $Q_3(Nx)$ | $P_3(1-2x)$ | $T_3(1-2x)$ | $U_3(1-2x)/4$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha=\beta=0$ | −0.36014 | **−0.36** | −0.936 | −0.168 |
| $\alpha=\beta=-\tfrac12$ | −0.93623 | −0.36 | **−0.936** | −0.168 |
| $\alpha=\beta=\tfrac12$ | −0.16812 | −0.36 | −0.936 | **−0.168** |

So the comment links two tables that the $\alpha=\beta=0$ rows are not
analogues of. It also leaves unlinked HREF{Legendre_polynomials} (T101), the
table they *are* analogues of. It never says why the grid contains
$\pm\tfrac12$, which is the real Chebyshev connection. Finally, "not entries
equal to either table" is a remark about this website rather than about the
polynomials.

**Smallest fix.** Replace the comment with:

> The rows with $\alpha=\beta=0$ are the Gram, or discrete Chebyshev,
> polynomials. By the limit under Similar tables they tend to
> HREF{Legendre_polynomials}[the Legendre polynomials] as $N\to\infty$. The
> rows with $\alpha=\beta=-\tfrac12$ and $\alpha=\beta=\tfrac12$ tend in the
> same way to HREF{Chebyshev_polynomials_of_the_first_kind}[the Chebyshev
> polynomials of the first kind] and, divided by $n+1$,
> HREF{Chebyshev_polynomials_of_the_second_kind}[of the second kind].

## 2. The reflection comment states a false identity (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

> Both orders of a pair of shape parameters are kept when they occur in the
> grid. The reflection from $(\alpha,\beta)$ to $(\beta,\alpha)$ changes the
> stored polynomial by replacing $x$ with $N-x$, so it is not the same
> convention choice as the $\alpha<\beta$ restriction in the Jacobi polynomial
> table.

**Why it is wrong.** $Q_n(N-x;\alpha,\beta,N)$ is not $Q_n(x;\beta,\alpha,N)$.
Every stored polynomial satisfies $Q_n(0)=1$, and substituting $N-x$ does not
keep that normalisation. On the stored rows with $(\alpha,\beta)=(1,2)$ and
$(2,1)$, $N=9$, the ratio $Q_n(N-x;1,2,N)/Q_n(x;2,1,N)$ is constant in $x$ and
equals $-3/2,\ 2,\ -5/2,\ 3$ for $n=1,2,3,4$. That is
$(-1)^n(\beta+1)_n/(\alpha+1)_n$. The plain substitution fails for every
$n\geq1$. A reader who tries to get the $(2,1)$ row from the $(1,2)$ row as the
comment says gets a different polynomial, and a search for it finds nothing.

The rest of the comment is also a problem. The first sentence describes the
run, and it belongs in `complete-note`. The last clause explains the draft's
choice against a decision made in another table, which reads as a defence
rather than as a fact.

**Smallest fix.** Delete the comment and add a formula:

    formula-reflection: "$Q_n(N-x;\alpha,\beta,N)=(-1)^n\frac{(\beta+1)_n}{(\alpha+1)_n}Q_n(x;\beta,\alpha,N)$."

If the reason for keeping both orders is still wanted, it fits in
`complete-note` (see 4): both orders are held because neither row is a
relabelling of the other.

## 3. The Wikipedia convention is an index shift, not a singular formula (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

> The lower hypergeometric parameter is $-N$, following DLMF. A formula with
> $-N+1$ in that position, as in the Wikipedia article [5], is a different
> convention and is singular for the $k=N$ term.

**Why it is misleading.** Wikipedia writes
${}_3F_2(-n,-x,n+\alpha+\beta+1;\alpha+1,-N+1;1)$ and orthogonality over
$x=0,\ldots,N-1$, so its degrees run to $N-1$. In its own range the sum never
reaches a $k=N$ term, so nothing is singular. Wikipedia's
$Q_n(x;\alpha,\beta,N)$ is simply this table's $Q_n(x;\alpha,\beta,N-1)$. That
is the sentence a reader holding Wikipedia's polynomial needs, because it tells
them which $N$ to look under. "Singular" instead tells them their source is
broken. Also, "the lower hypergeometric parameter" is ambiguous, because there
are two lower parameters, $\alpha+1$ and $-N$.

**Smallest fix.**

> Wikipedia [5] writes $-N+1$ in place of $-N$ and sums the orthogonality over
> $x=0,\ldots,N-1$, so its $Q_n(x;\alpha,\beta,N)$ is
> $Q_n(x;\alpha,\beta,N-1)$ here.

## 4. `complete-note` ends in fragments, and one of its reasons is inaccurate (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

> Table is complete: no (it holds $(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0),\ldots$, every support
> endpoint $2\leq N\leq14$ and every degree $0\leq n\leq N$, a grid including
> the uniform case, both orders of the asymmetric small integer pair, and
> small hypergeometric-distribution shape parameters)

The part after the last comma is an appended list of labels. A reader has to
guess that "the uniform case" means $(0,0)$ (uniform weight), that "the
asymmetric small integer pair" means $(1,2)$, and that "small
hypergeometric-distribution shape parameters" means $\pm\tfrac12$. The last
label is also inaccurate. For $\alpha,\beta>-1$ the weight is a beta-binomial
distribution. The hypergeometric distribution comes from
$\alpha,\beta<-N$, which the table excludes. The real reason for
$\pm\tfrac12$ is the Chebyshev limit in finding 1. The note also does not say
why $N=1$ is absent, although the parameter allows it.

**Smallest fix.**

> it holds every support endpoint $2\leq N\leq14$ and every degree
> $0\leq n\leq N$ for $(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)$, the Gram polynomials;
> $(\pm\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12)$, whose limits are the Chebyshev polynomials;
> and $(1,1),(2,2),(1,2),(2,1)$, with both orders of the asymmetric pair,
> since reflection does not map one onto the other

(Add a clause about $N=1$ if it was left out on purpose.)

## 5. The definition ends in a caveat phrased as jargon (worth doing, small)

> ... is the degree-$n$ polynomial ${}_3F_2(\ldots;1)$, with the terminating sum
> read through $k=n$ as in DLMF [2].

"Read through $k=n$" is shorthand for a real point. Because $(-N)_k$ vanishes
for $k>N$, the ${}_3F_2$ is to be taken as the sum stopped at $k=n$. A reader
has to reconstruct that. It is simpler to define the object by the finite sum
itself, which is already written out as formula (1):

> For rational $\alpha,\beta>-1$, integer $N\geq1$ and $0\leq n\leq N$, the
> Hahn polynomial is
> $Q_n(x;\alpha,\beta,N)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_k(n+\alpha+\beta+1)_k(-x)_k}{(\alpha+1)_k(-N)_k\,k!}$,
> the terminating ${}_3F_2\left(\ldots;1\right)$ of DLMF [2].

## Noticed only

* **Other normalisations of the Gram polynomials.** The keyword "Gram
  polynomials" brings a reader here, but the Gram and discrete Chebyshev
  polynomials are usually normalised differently, for example $t_n(x)$ in
  Abramowitz–Stegun, or orthonormally. A reader holding one of those has a
  constant multiple of a row here. One clause giving the scale would serve that
  reader. I did not compute the constant, and I did not test whether
  polynomial search is scale-invariant.
* **`Keywords: Hahn class`.** The Hahn class also contains Krawtchouk, Meixner
  and Charlier (T267 is a draft of the last one). At title weight, this
  keyword makes T268 the answer for the whole class. It is harmless while
  T268 is the only member, and worth revisiting when T267 is published.
* **`comment-variable`.** The second half, "this table stores the polynomial
  in the indeterminate $x$", is already clear from the type and the header.
  The first half could stand alone.
* **The row order is $\alpha=0,1,2,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12$.** The Chebyshev-limit
  rows come last. That is the document's order, and I would not reorder it.
* **"left/right shape parameter".** "Left" and "right" refer to the ends $x=0$
  and $x=N$ of the support, which a reader can work out from the weight
  $(\alpha+1)_x(\beta+1)_{N-x}$.
* **`rigour details`.** It does not yet mention the reflection check. If the
  formula in 2 is added, the generator should check it too. A check of the
  identity as it is now written would have failed.
