# T267 critique: Charlier polynomials $C_n(x;a)$

Read 2026-09-16. T267 is a private draft. The SOCKS proxy on 127.0.0.1:1080
refused connections again, but numberdb.org answered `curl` directly. `/T267`
answers 404, as a draft should, even with the key. I read the document through
`GET /api/table?id=T267` with the key. I rendered it through `/preview?table=`
in four pieces (the whole document is over the 4094-byte request-line limit),
and all four answered 200. Between them the pieces carry every section, a few
small rows and the largest entry. The table holds 210 entries of type `Q[]`
with rigour `exact`: ten values of $a$, each with every $0\leq n\leq20$. The
entries block is 48 KB. The largest entry, $C_{20}(x;5/2)$, is 813 characters
with 14-digit denominators, and it still reads as one polynomial.

`GET /api/table/T267/audit` reports `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. I agree
that nothing is broken. The audit does not look at what is missing (there is
no `Similar tables` section), and it does not judge whether a keyword helps
search.

**The table reads well.** Here is what I checked hardest:

* *The mathematics typesets.* I took all 48 `$...$` fragments from the rendered
  preview and ran them through MathJax 3 in node, with both the full package
  set and the site's `tex-svg` defaults. None failed. A control with an unclosed
  brace did fail. That includes the `\begin{matrix}-n,-x\\-\end{matrix}` inside
  ${}_2F_0$, where a lost backslash would have shown. Every `CITE` resolves.
* *The formulas are right and the program works.* I checked the hypergeometric
  sum, the generating function, the recurrence, the orthogonality constant
  $a^{-n}e^an!$ and the monic leading coefficient $(-a)^{-n}$ against DLMF
  §18.19–18.23. I ran the Sage program exactly as stored, with
  `agents/sage.sh`, against all 210 stored rows, and all 210 match. A
  deliberately wrong row fails the comparison.
* *It is one table, and the columns say so.* $a$ and $n$ are arguments of one
  polynomial, not names. The value column is headed $C_n(x;a)$, which is true
  of every row, and the parameter columns print `5/2` and `20`. The parameters
  describe the family ($a>0$, $n\geq0$). The range that was computed is in
  `complete-note`, which finishes its "no (...)" sentence properly.

The findings are ranked. 1 and 2 are worth doing and cost a sentence each. 3
and 4 are worth doing and cheap. 5 and 6 are small. Everything under "Noticed
only" is there because I noticed it, not because I would hold the table for it.

## 1. The definition points at a formula, and uses a word nobody has defined (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

> For rational $a>0$ and integer $n\geq0$, the Charlier polynomial $C_n(x;a)$
> is the KLS/DLMF polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ given by (1). The variable $x$
> is the count in the Poisson support.

Three problems:

* "KLS/DLMF" is an abbreviation followed by a slash. KLS (Koekoek–Lesky–
  Swarttouw) is not named or cited anywhere on the page. A reader cannot tell
  what it means, and it does not say what the object is.
* "given by (1)" sends the reader down to Formulas to learn what the table
  holds. The definition is the one place where the object itself should be
  written out.
* "The variable $x$ is the count in the Poisson support" is hard to parse. A
  count is not "in" a support, and the sentence does not say how
  $\binom{x}{k}$ is read when $x$ is an indeterminate, which is what makes the
  entries polynomials. `comment-variable` tries to repair this ("The support
  variable $x$ is extended from nonnegative integers to a polynomial
  variable") without saying how.

**Smallest fix.** Write the sum into the definition with a falling factorial.
That settles the "extension" by construction:

```yaml
Definition: For $a>0$ and integer $n\geq0$, the Charlier polynomial $C_n(x;a)$ is the polynomial $\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1/a)^k\,x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)$ of degree $n$ in $x$, in the normalisation of DLMF CITE{DLMFExplicit}.
```

Then delete `comment-variable`. In `formula-hypergeometric`, the
$\binom{x}{k}k!$ could become $x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)$ to match, but that is
optional because the two are equal. "Rational" can go from the definition,
since the family is every $a>0$ and the parameter's type `Q` already says
what is stored.

## 2. There is no `Similar tables` section, and the corpus holds a limit of this family (worth doing)

Charlier polynomials tend to Hermite polynomials as $a\to\infty$ (DLMF
§18.21(ii)):
$$\lim_{a\to\infty}(2a)^{n/2}C_n\bigl((2a)^{1/2}y+a;a\bigr)=(-1)^nH_n(y).$$
I checked this in Sage at $a=10^{12}$, $y=0.3$, for $n=2,3,5$. It agrees to
about $10^{-6}$, which is the expected $a^{-1/2}$ rate. The physicists' $H_n$
are T103, at slug `Hermite_polynomials_H`. A reader of this page has no route
there.

**Smallest fix.**

```yaml
Similar tables:
- table: HREF{Hermite_polynomials_H}[Hermite polynomials in physicist's convention]
  relation: are the limit $(2a)^{n/2}C_n((2a)^{1/2}y+a;a)\to(-1)^nH_n(y)$ as $a\to\infty$
```

If the formula is wanted on the page as well, it could also go in Formulas
with `CITE{}` to DLMF 18.21.

Two more relations are worth adding once they can be linked. The Krawtchouk
draft T266 is the finite analogue: in DLMF's notation,
$K_n(x;a/N,N)\to C_n(x;a)$ as $N\to\infty$. T266 uses a different
normalisation, $\mathcal{K}_k(x;N,q)$, so the relation should be written in
whichever form that table settles on, and only after it is public. T102
(Laguerre) holds only $\alpha=0$, so the identity
$C_n(x;a)=(-1/a)^n\,n!\,L_n^{(x-n)}(a)$ does not land on its rows. I would not
link it.

## 3. `mu` is a keyword (worth doing)

`Keywords` carries `mu`, and keywords are weighted the same as the title.
Anybody searching for "mu" (the Möbius function, a mean, a measure) gets this
table near the top. Nobody looking for Charlier polynomials types "mu".
`comment-parameter-name` already says that some sources write $\mu$, and that
is enough.

**Smallest fix.** Delete `- mu` from `Keywords`.

## 4. `rigour details` talks about the draft (worth doing)

> Before the draft was offered for review, the entries were checked against …

That describes the site's workflow. Once the table is public there is no
draft, and a reader is left wondering what it refers to. The checks are the
fact worth keeping.

**Smallest fix.** "The entries were checked against the three-term
recurrence, …".

## 5. `complete-note` says which $a$ but not why (small)

> it holds $a=\tfrac14,\tfrac13,\tfrac12,\tfrac23,1,\tfrac32,2,\tfrac52,3,4$
> and every degree $n$ with $0\leq n\leq20$

The skill asks the note to argue the range, because a reader checking whether
their own $a$ belongs here reads that sentence. The one value somebody
plausibly arrives with is $a=1$: it is the OEIS A046716 triangle, and a
Poisson mean of 1 is the standard example. The note gives no hint that the
others are a small grid around it. One clause fixes this, for example "…, a
grid of small means around $a=1$, the case tabulated in OEIS A046716 …", or
whatever the actual reason was.

## 6. The orthogonality relation is in Comments (small)

`comment-orthogonality` is an identity with a one-clause preamble. Per
the skill's section table, identities go in Formulas. T266 puts its
orthogonality relation there. Moving it is one key rename,
`formula-orthogonality: $\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}\ldots$`, keeping the words
"with Poisson weight $a^x/x!$ on the nonnegative integers" if wanted. Nothing
`CITE`s the comment.

## Noticed only

* `comment-monic` says "since this normalisation has leading coefficient
  $(-a)^{-n}$". "This normalisation" points rather than names. Reversed, it
  reads more directly: "$C_n(x;a)$ has leading coefficient $(-a)^{-n}$, so the
  monic Charlier polynomial is $(-a)^nC_n(x;a)$."
* `formula-special-value`, $C_n(0;a)=1$, is immediate from the sum. The
  self-duality $C_n(m;a)=C_m(n;a)$ for nonnegative integers $n,m$ says more.
  I checked it for $a=3/2$ and $n,m<8$. It could replace or join the special
  value.
* The ten $n=0$ rows are all the polynomial `1`, and the $n=1$ rows are
  $1-x/a$. They match many searches and tell the searcher little. The other
  orthogonal-polynomial tables (T102, T103) also start at $n=0$, so I would
  keep them for consistency.
* Rows are ordered by $a$, so the first screen shows $a=1/4$
  ($1-4x$, $16x^2-24x+1$, …) and the most common case, $a=1$, is fifth. That
  is the natural order, and I note it only.
* The program's first line, `import numberdb.sage as numberdb`, is never used
  by the code a reader sees. It exists so that the ring imports initialise
  under passagemath, and a short comment would say so.
* `Keywords` carries both "Poisson-Charlier polynomials" and "Poisson Charlier
  polynomials". The second is probably redundant if the index splits on
  hyphens. I did not test that.
