100 digits, `rigour: proven`, indexed by rank $r=1,\ldots,50$ and headed

## The audit

The audit is clean, and it is right about what it checks. The header
$\theta_r$ is true of every row, the links resolve, and the parameter is a
rank, which is an argument and not a name. None of the findings below is
something a rule could see. Finding 1 needs somebody to run the program. The
others are about what the prose says and what the corpus holds under another
name.

What reads well: the title is the phrase a reader would type. The definition
says exactly what $\theta_r$ is and stops. The parameter describes the family
($r\geq1$) and not the run. The comment on each row gives the minimal
polynomial, which is the one thing a reader holding the number needs and the
number cannot tell them.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The program prints the wrong list. Worth doing.

The program ends with

    sorted(pisot_root(f) for f in [...])[:10]

and `pisot_root` returns a pair `(g, root)`. So `sorted` orders by the
*polynomial* $g$, using Sage's order on polynomials, and not by the root. This
is its output:

    x^3 - x^2 - 1                   | 1.46557123188
    x^3 - x - 1                     | 1.32471795724
    x^4 - x^3 - 1                   | 1.38027756910
    x^5 - x^4 - x^3 + x^2 - 1       | 1.44326879127
    x^5 - x^4 - x^2 - 1             | 1.57014731220
    x^5 - x^3 - x^2 - x - 1         | 1.53415774491
    x^6 - 2*x^5 + x^4 - x^2 + x - 1 | 1.56175206772
    x^6 - x^5 - x^4 + x^2 - 1       | 1.50159480354
    x^7 - x^6 - x^5 + x^2 - 1       | 1.54521564973
    x^7 - x^6 - x^4 - x^2 - 1       | 1.60134733379

The list starts with $\theta_4$, not $\theta_1$. It is out of order. It
leaves out $\theta_{10}$ ($x^8-x^7-x^6+x^2-1$) and puts $\theta_{13}$ in its
place. A reader who runs it to get "one more value" gets a list that
contradicts the table above it. Sorting the same pairs by root gives exactly
rows 1 to 10.

Fix: `sorted(..., key=lambda t: t[1].lower())[:10]`. Renaming `RR` would also
help, because the program reassigns Sage's own name for the real field to an
interval field.

### 2. `Formulas` holds a description of the generator, and the polynomials are written out twice. Worth doing.

The only formula reads: "The generator forms $P_n(x)=\ldots$ for $n\geq2$,
$Q_n(x)=\ldots$ for $n\geq2$, and $E(x)=\ldots$, removes repeated minimal
polynomials, and orders their Pisot roots increasingly." That is prose about a
program, and it is in the section reserved for relations. The rigour details
say the same thing again ("forms the exact integer polynomials in (1),
factors them over $\mathbb Q$, selects ..."). `comment-classification` writes
out $P_n$, $Q_n$ and the exceptional polynomial a second time. It leaves out
$n\geq2$ and does not call the exceptional polynomial $E$, so a reader who
meets $E$ in the formula cannot tell it is the same polynomial.

Fix: make the formula state the classification, for example
"$\{\theta_r: r\geq1\}$ is the set of roots in $(1,\varphi)$ of
$P_n(x)=x^n(x^2-x-1)+1$ and $Q_n(x)=x^n(x^2-x-1)+x^2-1$ for $n\geq2$, and of
$E(x)=x^6-2x^5+x^4-x^2+x-1$." Then let `comment-classification` credit
Dufresnoy and Pisot and cite the formula instead of repeating the
polynomials. The procedure is already in the rigour details, where it
belongs. "In the form used here" can then go too. It is a remark about this
table rather than about the mathematics, and it does not say which other form
it means.

### 3. The relation to T222 is exact, and the page calls it "several of the same". Worth doing.

Every root of $P_n$ that T222 holds is the growth rate of the Coxeter triangle
group $\Delta(2,n+1,\infty)$. Search by number finds rows 1, 4, 6, 9, 11, 13,
15, 17, 19 and 21, which are $P_2$ to $P_{11}$, in T222 under the diagrams
`[inf,3]` to `[inf,12]`, with the same minimal polynomials. No root of $Q_n$
and not $E$ appears there. The Similar-tables sentence says only that T222
"holds several of the same Pisot numbers". A reader who arrives holding
1.3247... from a growth-series calculation is not told that the row they found
is a Coxeter group, and a reader of T286 cannot tell which rows are meant.

Fix: write the relation as "the root of $P_n$ is the growth rate of
$\Delta(2,n+1,\infty)$". State it for the range checked ($2\leq n\leq11$) or
give a source for all $n$. Link the ten row comments to their T222 entries,
e.g. `HREF{Growth_rates_of_hyperbolic_Coxeter_triangle_groups#[inf,3]}`. Both
tables should answer a search, so this is not a case for `repeats`.

### 4. `comment-mahler` gives the wrong reason. Worth doing.

"Each number is an algebraic unit, so it is also the Mahler measure of its
minimal polynomial." Both halves are true, but the "so" is not. The Mahler
measure of a monic polynomial is the product of its roots outside the unit
disc. A Pisot number has no conjugate outside the disc, so every Pisot number
is the Mahler measure of its minimal polynomial, whether or not it is a unit.
A reader who follows the sentence learns a false implication.

Fix: "Each number is an algebraic unit. Its conjugates lie inside the unit
disc, so it is the Mahler measure of its minimal polynomial."

### 5. The row comments are fragments, and $P_2$ is not a family. Worth doing; the change is in one string in the generator.

Row 1 reads "$P_{2}$ family; minimal polynomial $x^{3} - x - 1$; this is the
plastic ratio." It is three clauses separated by semicolons. $P_2$ is one
polynomial, and the family is $P_n$. Row 8 begins in lower case and names
"exceptional Dufresnoy-Pisot polynomial" rather than the $E$ that the formula
defines. The comment also does not say that $P_n$ is never irreducible:
$P_n(1)=0$, and the minimal polynomial of the row always has lower degree. A
reader who compares $P_4$ with the degree-5 polynomial beside it will pause
over that.

Fix: "A root of $P_2$, with minimal polynomial $x^3-x-1$. This is the plastic
ratio, the smallest Pisot number." Use the same pattern with "$Q_n$" and "$E$".
The fact that $\theta_1$ is the smallest Pisot number of all (Siegel 1944) is
what a reader holding 1.3247... most wants to be told, and the page never says
it.

### 6. Nothing on the page says why "the $r$-th smallest" makes sense. Worth doing, one clause.

The definition counts Pisot numbers in $(1,\varphi)$ upwards. That works
because $\varphi$ is the smallest accumulation point of the Pisot numbers.
The page says this only in the Similar-tables gloss on T32, and a reader of
the definition does not look there. Add it to `comment-classification`:
Dufresnoy and Pisot showed that $\varphi$ is the smallest limit point, so the
numbers below it form an increasing sequence converging to $\varphi$. In that
gloss, the sentence means one number, so link it as
`HREF{Golden_ratio#phi}[$\varphi$]` rather than to T32 as a whole.

### 7. The rigour details end with a note about the build, and they leave out the argument for the ranks. Worth doing.

"Before the draft was filled, the range measured $50$ entries, a longest
written value of $258$ characters at $r=1$, and a $19.9$ KB entries block."
This is about the run and the site, not about how the digits were obtained.
"258 characters at $r=1$" means nothing to a reader, since the value in that
row is 102 characters. Delete the sentence.

What the details do not say is the thing `proven` rests on for the *index*.
They do not say how far $n$ was taken (the rows reach $P_{25}$ and $Q_{26}$),
or why no larger $n$ gives a root below $\theta_{50}$. The obvious argument
is that the roots of $P_n$ and $Q_n$ increase with $n$. One sentence stating
that argument, or where it is proved, would make the rank as checkable as the
digits.

### 8. The completeness note does not say what the range reaches, or why it stops. Worth doing if the table is touched anyway.

It renders as "complete: no (it holds the first $50$ ranks in increasing
order)". A table holds values, not ranks, and "in increasing order" is
already in the definition. A clearer version: "it holds $\theta_1$ to
$\theta_{50}$, the roots of $E$, of $P_n$ for $n\leq25$ and of $Q_n$ for
$n\leq26$. $\theta_{50}$ is within $3\cdot10^{-6}$ of $\varphi$." The skill
also asks for one sentence on why the table stops at 50.
