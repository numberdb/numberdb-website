## The audit

**I agree with it, on its own rules**, and the two it would most plausibly have
fired are genuinely satisfied. No parameter takes a handful of names — all six
are integers, so the "two tables sharing a title" question does not arise — and
the value column is headed `$W_n(P)$` rather than the word `value`, because
that symbol is true of all 250 rows. No `param-latex` anywhere.

Two of the findings below are outside its rules rather than missed by them, and
both gaps are general rather than about this table:

* It checks that every `CITE{}` resolves to a Link or a Reference
  (`unresolved_citations`) and never checks the other direction, so a Link that
  is declared and never cited is invisible to it. The T341 critique found the
  same blind spot in September and it is still there.
* Its unlinked-family check matches another table's *full* bared title against
  the prose. T341's bares to `Division polynomials of elliptic curves over`,
  which is not a phrase anybody writes in a sentence, so writing "the $n$-th
  division polynomial" instead passes (finding 2).

The rest — findings 3, 4 and 5 — are about what sentences say, which no rule
reaches.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Definition does not say which division polynomial, and on two of the five curves it matters

The Definition ends:

> the entry is $W_n(P)=\psi_n(P)$, where $\psi_n$ is the $n$-th division
> polynomial.

There are two things called the $n$-th division polynomial, and they differ for
even $n$ by a factor of $\psi_2 = 2y+a_1x+a_3$. T347's own sibling T341 spells
this out in its Comment (7) — "This table stores $\psi_n$ itself. For even $n$,
the $x$-only polynomial without the two-torsion factor is
$\psi_n/(2y+a_1x+a_3)$. PARI/GP's `elldivpol` and Sage's default
`division_polynomial` use the $x$-only convention" — and T347, which stores the
values of exactly those polynomials, does not.

It is not a distinction without a difference here. Recomputed in Sage
(`/tmp/t347_check2.py`), $n=1\ldots6$:

| curve | $P$ | stored (full $\psi_n$) | Sage's default |
|---|---|---|---|
| 37a1 | $(0,0)$ | 1, 1, −1, 1, 2, −1 | 1, 1, −1, 1, 2, −1 |
| 43a1 | $(0,0)$ | 1, 1, 1, −1, −2, −3 | 1, 1, 1, −1, −2, −3 |
| 53a1 | $(0,0)$ | 1, 1, −1, −2, −1, 5 | 1, 1, −1, −2, −1, 5 |
| 57a1 | $(2,1)$ | 1, **3**, 9, **81**, 1458, **19683** | 1, **9**, 9, **243**, 1458, **59049** |
| 58a1 | $(0,1)$ | 1, **2**, −4, **−16**, −64, **1024** | 1, **4**, −4, **−32**, −64, **2048** |

The two readings agree exactly where $\psi_2(P)=\pm1$, which is the first three
curves — and those are the three the `rigour details` names for its independent
check ("the first terms for 37a1, 43a1 and 53a1 against OEIS A006769, A050512
and A178627"), and 37a1 is the curve the `Programs` snippet computes. So
neither the verification the table reports nor running its own program
distinguishes the convention it chose. That is the part worth fixing even
though the stored values are right.

In fairness, the page does pin it down twice, for a reader willing to work:
`Programs` passes `two_torsion_multiplicity=1`, and Formula (3) is false under
the other reading — on 57a1 it gives $x(2P)=17/9$ against the true 1, and on
58a1 $x(2P)=1/4$ against 1 (`/tmp/t347_check3.py`). A reader should not have to
reverse-engineer a convention from an identity.

**Smallest fix**, and it repairs finding 1a below at the same time: one clause
in `comment-convention`, which is already the comment about conventions —

> Here $\psi_n\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]$ is the full division polynomial, so
> $\psi_2=2y+a_1x+a_3$. Sage's default `division_polynomial`
> CITE{SageDivision} returns the $x$-only polynomial
> $\psi_n/(2y+a_1x+a_3)$ for even $n$.

### 1a. `Links` declares `SageDivision` and nothing cites it

It renders as `[3] SageMath reference: division_polynomial` under Links, and no
sentence on the page points at it. The skill: "A reference is for citing, not
for listing; a bibliography nobody points at is furniture."

What makes it worth more than a tidy-up is *why* it is there. In T341 that link
is `[1]`, and it is cited by the sentence that states the convention. T347
inherited the Links block — `comment-curve-labels` is word-for-word T341's
Comment (6) — and lost the sentence. The uncited link is the shape of the
missing sentence in finding 1, so the one clause above fixes both.

### 2. "division polynomial" is named in the Definition and not linked, though the corpus holds that table

The Definition's first mention of the object this table is built out of is
plain text. T341 is linked twice — from `Similar tables` and from
`rigour details` — and not from the sentence where a reader first meets the
phrase. The skill's rule is first mention, once per section.

**Fix:**
`HREF{Division_polynomials_psi_n_of_elliptic_curves_over_mathbb_Q}[the $n$-th division polynomial]`
in the Definition, and the same for "the division-polynomial convention" in
`comment-convention`, which is the first mention in the Comments section.

### 3. The completeness note says the table holds a point

It renders under Data properties as:

> Table is complete: no (it holds the first Mordell-Weil generator, with sign
> chosen so that $W_2(P)>0$, for every rank-one curve in Sage's mini Cremona
> database [4] with conductor $N\leq60$, and every $n$ with $1\leq n\leq50$)

The head noun is wrong: what the table holds is $W_n(P)$, not a generator. And
the last clause, "and every $n$ with $1\leq n\leq50$", has nothing to attach
to — a generator does not come in fifty values of $n$ — so the reader has to
reconstruct for themselves that there is one entry per curve per $n$. This is
the sentence a reader checking whether their own number belongs here actually
reads, and it is the one that should be easiest.

**The claim itself is true**, which is worth saying because it is the kind of
"every" that usually is not. I checked in Sage (`/tmp/t347_check2.py`): the
mini Cremona database holds exactly **five** rank-one curves with $N\leq60$ —
37a1, 43a1, 53a1, 57a1, 58a1 — every one of them has an integral first
generator, and the five stored $(N,c_4,c_6,x_P,y_P)$ and the $W_2>0$ sign
normalisation all match.

**Fix**, keeping every fact and moving the head noun:

> it holds $W_n(P)$ for $1\leq n\leq50$, for every rank-one curve in Sage's
> mini Cremona database CITE{SageCremona} with conductor $N\leq60$, taking $P$
> to be the first Mordell-Weil generator Sage returns, negated where necessary
> so that $W_2(P)>0$

### 4. `rigour details` points at a file the reader does not have

> uses the integral generator points listed in the source file

A reader meets this under "How they were obtained". There is no source file in
front of them, and the phrase does not say what is true of the points, which is
the thing that would let them check. Comment (6) already says it properly, in
the reader's terms ("the first Mordell-Weil generator returned by Sage for a
rank-one curve, replaced by $-P$ when necessary so that $W_2(P)>0$").

**Fix:** delete the clause. The sentence reads "reads exact Weierstrass
coefficients from Sage's mini Cremona database CITE{SageCremona} and evaluates
the exact division polynomials by …", and Comment (6) carries the point choice.

### 5. The five curves are named 250 times on the page and are findable by nobody

Comment (5) says:

> Each entry comment gives the curve's Cremona label.

Two things. It tells the reader where to look instead of telling them, which is
a remark about the table's layout rather than a fact about the mathematics. And
the search index is built from Title + Keywords, Tags, Definition and Comments
only (`numberdb_app/editing.py:664`) — entry comments are in none of the four,
and neither are `Links` titles or `rigour details`. So "37a1" appears on this
page fifty times, is the name by which that curve is universally known, titles
three of the OEIS sequences the table cross-checks against, and answers no
search at all. Nor is any label in `Keywords`.

**Fix**, which does both jobs in one sentence, replacing the first two
sentences of Comment (5):

> The curve is identified by $N$, $c_4$ and $c_6$; in Cremona's labelling
> CITE{SageCremona} the five curves here are 37a1, 43a1, 53a1, 57a1 and 58a1.

That makes the labels searchable (Comments are weight D), and it tells a reader
which curves the table covers without scrolling 250 rows to find out.

### 6. Nothing on the page says why they are called divisibility sequences

Noting this one because I noticed it, not because it is urgent. The title
promises divisibility; Formula (2) gives the recurrence, which is a different
claim; and $W_m \mid W_n$ whenever $m \mid n$ — the property the family is named
for, and the reason anybody computes one — is stated nowhere. A clause in
`Formulas` would carry it.

### 7. Two small ones, noted only

* `Programs` opens `E = EllipticCurve([0, 0, 1, -1, 0])`. That is 37a1, but a
  reader has to recognise it from five integers, when the table indexes curves
  by $(N,c_4,c_6)$ and labels every row by Cremona label. `EllipticCurve('37a1')`
  is accepted by Sage and connects the snippet to the table's first fifty rows.
* `comment-convention` writes `CITE{WikiEDS} CITE{Stange}`, which renders
  "[2] [1]". Swapping them costs nothing.

## What I checked hardest and found nothing wrong with

* **The rendering.** 250 rows, 250 anchors, all unique, `37,48,-216,0,0,1`
  through `58,57,-621,0,1,50`. No "Math input error", no `argument ()`, no
  section eaten by a `<` in mathematics. All ten sections draw. The one `&nbsp`
  without its semicolon, in the `$n$` column header, is the site's own template
  and appears identically on the published T341 page; `nbsp` is in HTML5's
  legacy list, so it renders as a space. Not this table's.
* **Whether it is one table.** It is. `number-header` is `$W_n(P)$` — a symbol
  true of every row, not the word `value`. $N$, $c_4$, $c_6$ are parts of one
  object, the curve, which is the skill's own good case; $x_P, y_P$ are the
  point; $n$ is an index, not a name. Nothing repeats under a parameter whose
  values are words.
* **Whether the parameters describe the family or the run.** The family:
  $N\geq1$, $n\geq1$, and no `$N\in\{37,43,53,57,58\}$` anywhere. What this
  afternoon computed is in `complete-note`, where it belongs. ($N\geq1$ is
  vacuous — every conductor is — but it is noise, not a fault.)
* **The completeness claim**, recomputed rather than read. See finding 3: true.
* **The links out.** All three `Similar tables` slugs answer 200 live, and each
  carries a named relation rather than a bare pointer.
* **The size.** The longest entry is 320 characters ($W_{50}$ on 58a1); the
  entries block is about 25 KB. Inside every limit with room, and still
  something a person can look at.
* **The repeated entry comments.** "Cremona label 37a1." sits on all fifty rows
  of each curve — 4,750 characters against 11,226 of values, which looks like
  the kind of repetition the skill warns about. I would keep them. A reader
  arriving from a search lands on one row, at `#57,112,-1304,2,1,27`, and what
  that row needs to tell them is which curve this is; the Cremona label is not
  recoverable from $(N,c_4,c_6)$ by inspection, so the comment says something
  the row cannot. Finding 5 is about the labels being absent from the *index*,
  not about their being present on the rows.

## Worth doing, and what is not

Findings 1, 1a and 2 are one edit: a clause in `comment-convention` naming the
convention and citing `SageDivision`, and two `HREF{}` at first mention. That
is the one I would not publish without.

Findings 3, 4 and 5 are three sentences rewritten, each cheap and each
improving what a reader reads first. 5 is the one with reach beyond this page,
because it changes what search can answer.

Findings 6 and 7 I am noting because I noticed them. The table reads well
otherwise: the definition defines, the parameters are the family's and not the
run's, the value column is headed by a real symbol, and the completeness claim
survives being checked.
