# T241 critique: Differential entropies of continuous probability distributions

Read 2026-09-14. T241 is a private draft owned by zeta3. It has 22
distributions, 313 of them with a shape value, and each is stored in nats
and in bits, so 626 values. The type is `R`, the rigour is `proven`, and the
entries block is 75 KB.

Everything renders. The definition defines, and the parameter labels say
which density is meant. The main things a reader will notice are what the
page leaves out and one thing it gets wrong:

- Ten rows are exact rationals, stored as 100-digit decimals. Every
  $F(2,m)$ entropy is $1+2/m$ nats. The page never says so, and its own
  comment says the exact-integer rows were left out.
- No closed form for any distribution appears on the page. Three of the six
  Similar tables relations point at "entropy formulas" that a reader cannot
  see.
- The range is in Comments, `complete-note` points at it, and nothing says
  why the range was chosen. The comment on the omitted rows reads as a
  remark about the website, and the rule it states is applied unevenly.

Findings 1 to 3 are worth doing. Findings 4 and 5 are small and worth doing
while the file is open. 6 and 7 are wording. The rest are notes.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Ten $F(2,m)$ rows are exact rationals, stored as decimals (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** The row for $F(n_1,n_2)$ with shape `2, 1` reads
`3.000000…000` in nats, with 99 zeros. `2, 2` is `2.000…`, `2, 4` is `1.500…`
and `2, 3` is `1.666…67`. The unit-variance hyperbolic secant reads
`2.000…` in bits. Nothing on the page explains the zeros. Meanwhile comment
(3) says the rows whose "standard entropies are exactly $1$, $2$ and $0$
nats" were left out.

**Why it is wrong.** $h(F(2,m))=1+2/m$ exactly. For $X\sim F(2,m)$, the
variable $Y=1+2X/m$ is Pareto with shape $m/2$ and minimum 1. The Pareto
entropy is $1+2/m-\log(m/2)$, and scaling back adds $\log(m/2)$. The ten
stored values are $3, 2, 5/3, 3/2, 7/5, 4/3, 9/7, 5/4, 11/9, 6/5$ to 90
places. The unit-variance hyperbolic secant has $\log 4$ nats, which is
exactly $2$ bits. The skill's rule is to decide exactness from the
definition: a decimal claims a number known to 100 places, and understates
exactly the rows where more is known. The generator already writes
Gamma$(1,1)$ as `1` and Beta$(1,1)$ as `0`, so its author meant to follow
the rule and did not see these cases.

**Smallest change.**

- Add a formula: `formula-f-2: $h(F(2,m))=1+2/m$ nats.`
- In `_entropy_nats`, return `ZZ(3)` and `ZZ(2)` for `f` with shape `2,1`
  and `2,2`.
- In `_to_unit`, return `ZZ(2)` for `hypsecant-unit-variance` in bits.

I did not establish whether an `R` table accepts `5/3`; the skill lists no
rational form for `R`. With the formula on the page, the other eight
decimals are at least explained. Then reword comment (3) (finding 3),
because it cannot stand beside a row that reads `3` or `2`.

### 2. No closed form appears on the page, and relations rely on ones that do not (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Take a reader who arrives holding 1.9602792291. The
row tells them this is Student's $t_2$ in nats, and nothing more: no
expression to check it against, and no way to get $t_{40}$ or
Beta$(6,7)$. The Formulas section has scaling, the change of units and the
normal bound. Similar tables then says:

> Values of the Gamma function at rational numbers — Gamma and Beta distribution entropy formulas contain $\log\Gamma$ and digamma values at rational parameters
>
> Values of the Beta function at pairs of rational numbers — Beta and Student entropy formulas contain the Beta function

Neither of those formulas is anywhere on the page. The Programs snippet
gives the Student formula as code, and only that one.

**Why it is wrong.** The skill gives Formulas the job of holding closed
forms. Here they matter more than usual. The build found that the summary
table on Wikipedia has the wrong sign for chi-squared, and that "hyperbolic
secant" names two densities. That is the whole reason for this table: a set
of checked closed forms is the reference a reader cannot easily get
elsewhere. The closed forms are already written, checked against SciPy and
quadrature, in `generate.py` lines 129 to 218. Two facts a reader needs are
also missing:

- Beta$(a,b)$ and Beta$(b,a)$ have the same entropy, since $X\mapsto 1-X$.
  The table stores only $a\leq b$ and never says why, so a reader with
  Beta$(3,1)$ finds their number labelled Beta$(1,3)$.
- The relation to T9 names digamma values, which T9 does not hold.

**Smallest change.**

- Add one formula for each of the nine families with a shape parameter,
  transcribed from `_entropy_nats`. Two I checked against the stored rows:
  - Gamma$(a,1)$: $h=a+\log\Gamma(a)+(1-a)\psi(a)$. At $a=2$ this gives
    $1+\gamma$, and at $a=1/2$ it gives 0.0906099.
  - Pareto$(\alpha,1)$: $h=1+1/\alpha-\log\alpha$. At $\alpha=2$ this gives
    0.8068528.
- Add `$h(\mathrm{Beta}(a,b))=h(\mathrm{Beta}(b,a))$`, and say in the range
  sentence that this is why $a\leq b$.
- Give the 13 distributions without a shape their closed form as an entry
  comment, for example "$h=1+\gamma$" on Gumbel and "$h=\frac12\log(2\pi e)$"
  on the normal. These are one line each, and they are what makes
  1.5772156649 recognisable.
- Change the T9 relation to "the Gamma distribution's entropy contains
  $\log\Gamma(a)$".

### 3. The range is in the wrong place, gives no reason, and the note on omitted rows reads as editorial (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Beside "Table is complete: no" the page says "(it
holds the standard-form named distributions and shape ranges stated in the
comments, in both nats and bits)". The reader has to go to Comments, where
(2) lists eight ranges and (3) says:

> The named exponential, logistic and uniform rows are not listed separately: their standard entropies are exactly $1$, $2$ and $0$ nats. Pareto$(1,1)$ is likewise omitted as a named Pareto row because its entropy is exactly $2$ nats.

**Why it is wrong.**

- The skill puts the range in `complete-note`, as the clause that finishes
  "complete: no (…)", together with the reason for it. Here the note points
  at another section, and no reason is given anywhere: why Gamma runs to 15,
  why $F$ runs to 10, why Weibull skips $k=1$.
- "Named … rows", "listed separately" and "omitted as a named Pareto row"
  describe the website, not the mathematics.
- The rule is applied unevenly:
  - The exponential and uniform distributions *are* listed, as Gamma$(1,1)$
    and Beta$(1,1)$, and those rows' comments say so.
  - Weibull $k=1$, also the exponential, is left out without a word.
  - The table keeps $F(2,1)=3$ and $F(2,2)=2$ (finding 1).
  - The argument that 0, 1 and 2 match everything holds only in nats. In
    bits the logistic and Pareto$(1,1)$ entropies are $2/\log 2 =
    2.8853900817\ldots$, which is not a small integer, and a reader holding
    it finds nothing.

**Smallest change.** Delete comment (2). Set `complete-note` to:

> it holds Student's $t_\nu$, $\chi_k$ and $\chi^2_k$ for $1\leq\nu,k\leq 30$, Gamma$(a,1)$ for $a=1/2,1,\ldots,15$, Weibull$(k,1)$ for $k\in\{1/2,3/2,2,3,4,5\}$, Pareto$(\alpha,1)$ for $2\leq\alpha\leq 10$, von Mises for $1\leq\kappa\leq 10$, Beta$(a,b)$ for $a\leq b$ in $\{1/2,1,\ldots,5\}$ and $F(n_1,n_2)$ for $1\leq n_1,n_2\leq 10$, each in nats and bits

Add the reason for the bounds, which only the author knows. Replace comment
(3) with a statement of fact:

> The standard logistic distribution and Pareto$(1,1)$ both have entropy $2$ nats; the standard exponential and uniform distributions are the rows Gamma$(1,1)$ and Beta$(1,1)$, and Weibull$(1,1)$ is the exponential again.

Whether to hold the logistic distribution as a row, given that its bits
value is distinctive, is a separate decision for the author.

### 4. Three Similar tables relations are not relations (worth doing, small)

**What a reader sees.**

> Entropy constants of lattice models — also stores entropy values, for lattice models rather than probability distributions
>
> Periodic windows of the logistic map by kneading word — has topological entropy rows for one dynamical system
>
> Values of the error function — the normal distribution is another standard source of Gaussian special values

**Why it is wrong.** The skill says this field states the *relation*.

- The first two share only the word "entropy". The entropy per site of a
  lattice model and the topological entropy of a map are not differential
  entropies, and nothing in T241 can be computed from T153 or T169, or the
  other way round. "Rows" is website vocabulary.
- The third names no relation at all: no entry of this table involves erf.

**Smallest change.** Delete all three. If T153 is kept, it needs a relation
a reader can use, and I could not find an honest one.

### 5. Rows marked `equals` print a bare address and do not name their distribution (worth doing, small)

**What a reader sees.** Under $\chi_k$, shape `1`, a reader sees
"equals: #half-normal,-,nats". Under Student's $t_\nu$, shape `1`, they see
"equals: #cauchy,-,nats". Gamma$(1,1)$ and Beta$(1,1)$ carry a sentence
instead: "This is the standard exponential distribution."

**Why it is wrong.** A reader has to decode `#half-normal,-,nats` to learn
that $\chi_1$ is the half-normal distribution. Two neighbouring rows say the
same kind of thing in English.

**Smallest change.** In `_comment`, add "This is the Cauchy distribution." to
$t_1$, and the same for $\chi_1$ (half-normal), $\chi_2$ (Rayleigh), $\chi_3$
(Maxwell-Boltzmann) and Beta$(1/2,1/2)$ (arcsine). Keep `equals` as it is.
The page renders `equals` through the same `render_text` as a comment, so
`HREF{#cauchy,-,nats}[Cauchy]` would probably print a caption. I did not
check that search still folds a captioned `equals`, so the comment is the
safe change.

### 6. The standard-forms comment claims more than is true, and the hyperbolic secant key names a library (small)

**What a reader sees.**

> The standard forms are location $0$ and scale $1$; SciPy continuous distributions [2] use the same convention. The hyperbolic secant distribution has two common standardizations, so the distribution parameter distinguishes the SciPy density …

**Why it is wrong.**

- As mathematics, "location 0 and scale 1" is false for the arcsine and
  triangular distributions (on $[0,1]$), Pareto (minimum 1) and the
  unit-variance hyperbolic secant (scale $2/\pi$). It is true only as
  SciPy's `loc=0, scale=1`, and the labels already state each form
  correctly.
- "The distribution parameter distinguishes" names a parameter by its key,
  which the skill asks authors not to do.
- The key `hypsecant-scipy` is frozen at publication and names a software
  package, not the density.
- The label "unit-variance hyperbolic secant" is the one label that does not
  give its density.
- "Levy" should be "Lévy", with "Levy" in Keywords, the way T172 does it.

**Smallest change.**

- Rewrite the comment as: "Each distribution is in the form its label
  states, which is SciPy's default CITE{SciPyStats} except for the
  unit-variance hyperbolic secant."
- Relabel that distribution as "hyperbolic secant with density
  $\frac12\operatorname{sech}(\pi x/2)$".
- While the table is a draft, rename the key `hypsecant-scipy` to
  `hypsecant`.

### 7. Two formulas and one comment need rewording (small)

- `formula-scale` renders "$h(\mu+\sigma X)=h(X)+\log\sigma$ [1] where $\mu$
  is real": the citation interrupts the clause. Write "For real $\mu$ and
  $\sigma>0$, $h(\mu+\sigma X)=h(X)+\log\sigma$ CITE{WikiDifferentialEntropy}."
- `formula-normal-bound` says the normal distribution "has maximal entropy
  … with equality only for the normal law". That is circular, and "equality"
  has no inequality to refer to. Write "If $X$ has variance $v$, then
  $h(X)\leq\frac12\log(2\pi e v)$, with equality only when $X$ is normal
  CITE{WikiDifferentialEntropy}."
- The lognormal comment ends "since the change of variables adds
  $E[Z]=0$". A reader has to reconstruct the identity, and is not told which
  row to read. Write "$h(e^Z)=h(Z)+E[Z]$, so the standard lognormal
  distribution has the entropy of the normal row, $\frac12\log(2\pi e)$."
  Add "lognormal entropy" to Keywords, since that name appears in no row.

## Notes, not worth a change on their own

- **A.** Four more pairs of rows are the same number and are not marked:
  - $\chi^2_2$ and Laplace, both $1+\log 2$
  - Gamma$(2,1)$ and Gumbel, both $1+\gamma$
  - Beta$(1,2)$ and the symmetric triangular distribution, both
    $\frac12-\log 2$
  - $F(1,1)$ and Cauchy, both $\log 4\pi$, because $T^2\sim F(1,1)$ for a
    Cauchy $T$ and $E\log|T|=0$

  The table uses `equals` only for the same distribution, which is a
  reasonable reading. The closed forms of finding 2 would show these
  coincidences without any marking.
- **B.** The `shape` constraint, "otherwise it is the displayed integer,
  half-integer or comma-separated tuple named by the distribution", points
  rather than names. A clearer version: "$\nu$, $k$, $a$, $\alpha$ or
  $\kappa$, or the pair $(a,b)$ or $(n_1,n_2)$, as the distribution's label
  names them; `-` for a distribution without one."
- **C.** 128 of the 626 values are negative. A one-line comment would spare
  a reader who does not expect that: "Differential entropy can be negative;
  the uniform distribution on $[0,c]$ has $h=\log c$."
- **D.** The labels $t_\nu$, $\chi_k$ and $\chi^2_k$ do not say that the
  index is the degrees of freedom. A statistician will assume it.
