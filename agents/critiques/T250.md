# T250 critique: Values of the polygamma functions $\psi^{(n)}(x)$ at rational numbers

Read 2026-09-15. T250 is a private draft. It holds $\psi^{(1)}$ and
$\psi^{(2)}$ at the same 364 rational arguments as the digamma table T226 and
the harmonic-number table T247, which makes 728 real values to 100
significant digits, with rigour `proven`. No entry has a comment.

**The table reads well.** It renders cleanly and the definition defines. The
parameters describe the family, and the range is in `complete-note`, where it
belongs. It is one table: $n$ is an order, not a name, and the header
$\psi^{(n)}(x)$ is true of every row. The findings below are small. Two of
them add something a reader would use. The rest are wording.

- 1 and 2 are worth doing: the range is given without its reason, and the
  reflection formula is missing.
- 3 and 4 are wording, and worth doing while the file is open.
- 5 and the notes at the end are there only because I noticed them.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The completeness note gives the range but not why (worth doing)

The page shows:

> Table is complete: no (it holds $\psi^{(n)}(x)$ for $n=1,2$ and every
> rational argument $x=a/b$ in lowest terms with $b\leq12$ and $-4<x\leq4$ at
> which the function is finite)

A reader checking whether their number belongs here sees a denominator bound
and a window, with no reason for either. The skill asks for the reason in this
exact sentence ("That sentence is the argument"). The two sibling tables built
on the same grid both give one. T226 ends its note with "arguments outside
$0<x\leq1$ are included because ... the differences $\psi(x+k)-\psi(x)$ are
rational". T247 says the same for $H_x$. T250 uses the same grid and only
drops the reason. The reason still holds: by formula (2), the differences are
rational multiples of $n!$. Nothing says why the table stops at $n=2$ either.

Smallest fix: extend `complete-note` with a clause that finishes the
sentence, for example:

> ... at which the function is finite. These are the arguments of
> HREF{Values_of_the_digamma_function_at_rational_numbers}[the digamma table],
> and those outside $0<x\leq1$ are included because
> $\psi^{(n)}(x+k)-\psi^{(n)}(x)$ is rational for positive integers $k$.

Then add half a sentence on $n\leq2$, if there is a reason beyond "trigamma
and tetragamma are the ones with names".

### 2. The reflection formula is missing (worth doing)

`Formulas` has the Hurwitz zeta expression and the recurrence. It does not
have the reflection formula, DLMF 5.15.6, which is already in the linked
source:

$$\psi^{(n)}(1-x)+(-1)^{n+1}\psi^{(n)}(x)=(-1)^n\pi\,\frac{d^n}{dx^n}\cot(\pi x).$$

It is the identity that links the rows $x$ and $1-x$ of this table. For
$n=1$ it says $\psi'(x)+\psi'(1-x)=\pi^2/\sin^2(\pi x)$. It is also where the
closed forms a reader might be holding come from: $\psi'(1/4)=\pi^2+8G$ and
$\psi'(3/4)=\pi^2-8G$, with $G$ Catalan's constant. T226 states its own
reflection formula. Without it, T250 leaves the reader with only the
recurrence, which links $x$ to $x+1$ but not $x$ to $1-x$.

I checked the formula before proposing it. Computed in arb at 400 bits, the
$n=1$ case at $x=1/4$ and the $n=2$ case at $x=1/3$ both give a residual of
$[\pm10^{-117}]$. The same $n=1$ sum with the wrong sign gives $-5.08$, and
$(\psi'(1/4)-\pi^2)/8$ gives $0.9159655941772190150546\ldots=G$.

Smallest fix: add a `formula-reflection` with the display above, for
nonintegral $x$, `CITE{DLMFPolygamma}`.

### 3. Three `Similar tables` relations say slightly the wrong thing (wording)

- **Digamma, T226:** "stores the order $0$ case of the same notation". A
  notation has no cases. What is meant is the function
  $\psi^{(0)}=\psi$. Fix: "holds $\psi=\psi^{(0)}$, the case $n=0$, at the
  same arguments".
- **Hurwitz zeta, T94:** "gives the Hurwitz zeta values that express these
  derivatives". "These derivatives" points back at something instead of
  naming it, and "gives the values" suggests T94 covers this table. It does
  not. T94 holds $x=1/2,1/3,2/3,1/4,3/4,1/5,2/5,3/5,4/5$ (and $x=1$ through
  `equals`), which is 9 of this table's 364 arguments. Fix: "holds
  $\zeta(2,x)=\psi'(x)$ and $\zeta(3,x)=-\psi''(x)/2$ at the rationals in
  $(0,1)$ with denominator at most 5".
- **Polygamma zeros, T127:** "stores the real zeros of the same functions".
  T127 holds even $n$ only. Its own comment explains that $\psi^{(n)}$ has no
  real zeros for odd $n$, so it has nothing for $\psi'$, which is half of this
  table. Fix: "stores the real zeros of $\psi^{(n)}$ for even $n$, among them
  $\psi''$; $\psi'$ has none".

### 4. In the definition, "at rational arguments $x$" comes after the where-clause (wording)

> Listed are values of the polygamma functions
> $\psi^{(n)}(x)=d^n\psi(x)/dx^n$ for positive integers $n$, where $\psi(x)$
> is the digamma function $\Gamma'(x)/\Gamma(x)$ [1], at rational arguments
> $x$.

The phrase that says where the values are taken is cut off from "values" by
a relative clause about $\psi$. A reader may attach it to the digamma
function. Fix: move it forward, as T247 does:

> Listed are values of the polygamma functions
> $\psi^{(n)}(x)=d^n\psi(x)/dx^n$ of positive integer order $n$ at rational
> arguments $x$, where $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ is the digamma function [1].

### 5. The one comment repeats the first similar table (noted only)

> The case $n=0$ is the digamma function and is stored in the digamma table.

This says again what the first `Similar tables` row says. "Is stored in the
digamma table" is about the website, and "the digamma table" is a nickname
rather than the table's name. If the comment stays, the fact it can state is
$\psi^{(0)}=\psi$. Otherwise it can go, once finding 3 fixes the relation.

## Notes, no action proposed

- `rigour details` writes $\psi'(1)$, $\psi'(1/2)$ and $\psi''(1)$, but the
  table only defines $\psi^{(n)}$. The primes are standard, and the field is
  one a reader opens on purpose.
- Nine $n=1$ rows are, by formula (1), the same numbers as T94's $s=2$ rows.
  I do not suggest `equals`: T94's rows are sparse and older, and the Hurwitz
  formula already says so. A reader comparing the two will see the last digit
  differ at $x=1/2$ (T94 `...9276115`, T250 `...9276116`). The true value
  continues `...92761159`, so T250's digit is the correctly rounded one, and
  both intervals contain the value.
- The entries follow T226's order: integers first, then by denominator, then
  by $|a|$ with the positive first. Each $x$ is next to $-x$, as the skill
  asks.
