## The audit

It is clean, and in its own terms I agree with it: every `CITE{}` resolves,
`HREF{T377}` and `HREF{T379}` both name tables that exist, the tags each reach
more than this table, the generator is attached and carries its run commands,
the digits are indexed as written, and the one-quantity checks have nothing to
object to, because this really is one table of one quantity under one
parameter.

Two of its rules came close to findings 1 and 6 below and stayed silent for
reasons worth knowing. The `Links` rule fires only when a link's *title*
contains another table's title, never when nothing cites a link; the
"family named and not linked" rule matches a table's full title in prose, so
"the zeta table" is invisible to it. Neither is a bug. They are rules, and the
faults below are not mechanical.

## Worth doing

### 1. Nineteen links say this table was requested by people who asked for something else

A reader reaching the foot of the page finds twenty-two numbered references.
`[1]` is CFKRS, `[2]` is Wikipedia, `[3]` is the PARI manual, and `[4]`
through `[22]` are:

>  [4] Requested in numberdb-data#20 … [22] Requested in numberdb-data#136

No sentence on the page cites any of them, which is already the skill's "a
bibliography nobody points at is furniture". What makes them worse than
furniture is that the claim they make is false. Their titles, read from the
GitHub API:

Not one asks for the moments of quadratic Dirichlet $L$-functions. Waring's
problem, the Cantor polynomial and Erdős-Rényi thresholds are not near
neighbours of this subject; they are unrelated requests, and the page tells a
reader that this table answers all nineteen.

The issue that *did* ask for it is `numberdb-data#175`, "Family: the leading
constants of the moment conjectures", which names this table by its title and
its T-number, and it is not linked at all. The nineteen come from that issue's
own body, which writes "(answers #20, #23, …, #136)" against this table's
line; the list was wrong there and was copied here faithfully.

Two more things a reader can see. The published sibling T377, built in the
same batch from the same issue, carries no request links at all, so inside one
family of four tables one page carries nineteen and its twin carries none. And
the skill says a request leaves no trace in the table in any case: "the table
carries no trace of the request: it is encyclopedic, and who asked for it is
not a fact about the mathematics."

**Smallest fix:** delete `Request20` through `Request136`. If the campaign's
convention of recording provenance is kept, one entry, `Requested in
numberdb-data#175`, is the true one; the run prompt's rule is one request per
table, and nineteen is the failure mode of that rule, not its use.

Eight other critiques in this repository report the same fault (T339, T341,
T348, T360, T365, T366, T368, T372), the T359 run wrote it up as a lesson
proposal on 2026-09-20, and eight documents under `generators/` carry request
links today. It is a pipeline fault rather than this author's, and the place
to fix it is the line in `agents/table-build/PROMPT.md` that says to record
the request, together with the "(answers #…)" list the ideation stage writes
into the issue.

### 2. "Integer normalisation" is not true of the thing it names

Comment (6) reads:

> This table stores the arithmetic factor $A_k$ alone. The leading constant
> pairs it with the symplectic random-matrix factor $f_{USp}(k)$, whose
> integer normalisation $g_{USp}(k)$ is stored in [the random-matrix factor
> table].

$g_{USp}(k)$ is not an integer for any $k$ the linked table holds. T379's
symplectic rows are $1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 3/4, 143/16, 4199/8, 1448655/8,
1703061105/4, \ldots$, and T379 says so itself: "it makes the unitary and
orthogonal rows integral, while the symplectic rows remain rational". A reader
sent to look up "the integer normalisation" lands on $143/16$.

**Smallest fix:** "whose factorial normalisation $g_{USp}(k)=\left(k(k+1)/2\right)!\,f_{USp}(k)$
is stored in …". That is T379's `formula-factor` with $e_{USp}(k)=k(k+1)/2$
substituted, so it is both true and the conversion finding 3 asks for.

### 3. $f_{USp}(k)$ is named twice and defined nowhere

$f_{USp}(k)$ carries the whole meaning of this table: formula (1) says the
leading coefficient is $A_k f_{USp}(k)$, and comment (6) says the table stores
$A_k$ alone. Neither says what $f_{USp}$ is, and no formula on the page gives
it. The skill's rule is flat: "Define notation where you use it. If a symbol
appears in a formula or a comment, its definition belongs in the same table."

Both siblings obey it. T377's comment (6) writes "$f_U(k)=G(1+k)^2/G(1+2k)$ is
the random-matrix factor and $G$ is the Barnes $G$-function"; T378 puts the
same identity in a formula. T380 is the only table in the family that names
its $f$ without saying what it is.

The consequence is concrete, not stylistic. A reader who wants the constant
the table's own formula (1) is about must leave the page, and where they are
sent does not close the gap: T379 stores $g_G(k)$, holds only integer $k$ from
1 to 12, and states the conversion in its own formulas. So for the six
half-integer rows here, $k=1/2, 3/2, 5/2, 7/2, 9/2, 11/2$, the partner factor
is nowhere in the corpus at all, and nothing on this page says so.

**Smallest fix:** one formula, `formula-symplectic-factor`, giving
$f_{USp}(k)$ as the leading factor of $\langle\Lambda_A(1)^k\rangle_{USp}$
with the conversion $f_{USp}(k)=g_{USp}(k)/\left(k(k+1)/2\right)!$ beside it,
cited from comment (6). A clause in `complete-note` or in comment (6) saying
that the stored $g_{USp}$ covers integer $k$ only would tell the half-integer
reader where they stand.

### 4. The link to T379 is captioned with a title T379 no longer has, and describes something it does not hold

`Similar tables` renders:

> Moments of the characteristic polynomials of unitary, orthogonal and
> symplectic matrices — stores the random-matrix factors paired with
> arithmetic factors in this moment-constant family

T379's title today is "Random-matrix factors $g_G(k)$ in the moments of
characteristic polynomials of unitary, orthogonal and symplectic matrices".
The caption is the working name from the batch, dropped of exactly the phrase
that says what is stored, so it promises moments of characteristic
polynomials and delivers $e_G(k)!f_G(k)$. The relation clause beside it is
right, which makes the caption the only wrong part.

**Smallest fix:** copy T379's current title into the caption. The companion
entry for T377 already does this and is correct word for word.

### 5. Formula (1) is the sentence that gives the table its meaning, and it is written as notes

> Conjecturally, the leading coefficient in the CFKRS polynomial for
> $\sum_d^* L(1/2,\chi_d)^k$ is $A_k f_{USp}(k)$, with exponent $k(k+1)/2$ in
> the logarithm of the conductor.

A reader has to guess four things: what $d$ runs over, what the star on the
sum restricts, what "the CFKRS polynomial" is a polynomial in, and what "with
exponent $k(k+1)/2$ in the logarithm of the conductor" modifies — the
polynomial's degree, the leading term, or the coefficient just named. Nor is
there any averaging in the displayed sum, so as written the left side diverges
and the right side is a constant.

The sibling shows what this section can look like. T377's formula (1) is
"$\frac1T\int_0^T |\zeta(1/2+it)|^{2k}\,dt\sim c_k(\log T)^{k^2}$, with
$c_k=a_kf_U(k)$": one displayed asymptotic, every symbol in it defined. The
quadratic family has the same shape, over fundamental discriminants
$|d|\leq X$ normalised by their count, with $(\log X)^{k(k+1)/2}$ in place of
$(\log T)^{k^2}$.

$\chi_d$ is undefined too, in both the definition and formula (1); T378 by
contrast opens its comment with "Here $\zeta$ is the Riemann zeta function".
One clause, "where $\chi_d$ is the Kronecker symbol of the fundamental
discriminant $d$", finishes it.

None of this touches the values: $A_k$ is defined by the Euler product in
formula (2), which is complete and reproducible on its own, and comment (7)
settles the branch for rational $k$. This is about the sentence that tells a
reader what the number is for.

**Smallest fix:** write the asymptotic out as a displayed relation, the way
T377 does, and define $\chi_d$ where it first appears.

### 6. Comment (5) is about this corpus's habits, and points at a table instead of naming it

> The same key $k$ is used throughout the moment-constant family. In this
> symplectic family it is the power of $L(1/2,\chi_d)$, while in the zeta
> table the same $k$ labels the $2k$-th absolute moment.

Three things. "The same key $k$" names a parameter by its key, which the skill
calls an address rather than a word; the parameter's display symbol is $k$ and
that is what a reader knows it by. "The moment-constant family" is a family of
*tables here*, not of mathematics, and a reader outside this site cannot tell
what it refers to. And "the zeta table" points at T377 without naming it or
linking it, on its first mention in this section; a reader has to scroll to
`Similar tables` and guess that the table meant is the one there.

The second half of the sentence is the useful fact, and T377 says the same
thing about itself in one line: "The parameter is $k$, not the full exponent
$2k$: $k=1$ is the second moment and $k=2$ is the fourth moment."

**Smallest fix:** "Here $k$ is the power of $L(1/2,\chi_d)$ itself, so $k=1$
is the first moment. In HREF{Arithmetic_factors_in_the_moments_of_the_Riemann_zeta_function}[the
table of arithmetic factors $a_k$] the same letter is half the exponent, and
$k=1$ is the second moment."

### 7. The $k=1$ row is a constant that has been tabulated under another name

$A_1 = 0.70444220099916559273660335032663721018858643141709\ldots$ agrees, in
all 98 digits OEIS publishes, with the decimal expansion A065463 (checked
against `https://oeis.org/A065463/b065463.txt`; the sequence's own page
answers 403 from this box, so its name there could not be read from here).
It is the constant $\prod_p\left(1-\frac{1}{p(p+1)}\right)$, which formula (4)
already gives, and it is met far outside $L$-function moments.

That is the reader the whole database is for: somebody holding 0.7044422 who
does not know it is a moment constant. The page will tell them, once
published, only that it is $A_1$.

**Smallest fix:** an OEIS link in `Links`, cited from the $k=1$ entry comment.
The comment there currently says "The first moment has local factor
$1-1/(p(p+1))$ at each prime", which is formula (4) said again; replacing that
repetition with the identification costs no space.

## Noted only because they were noticed

8. **The title carries no symbol.** The other three tables of this family are
   "Arithmetic factors $a_k$ in …", "Leading constants $c_k$ of …" and
   "Random-matrix factors $g_G(k)$ in …"; this one is "Arithmetic factors in
   the moments of quadratic Dirichlet $L$-functions". The column header is
   $A_k$ and the definition introduces $A_k$, so nothing is lost on the page,
   and a title is what a search reaches: adding "$A_k$" after "factors" would
   make the family consistent. Small, and easiest to do while the table is
   still a draft.

9. **"Keating-Snaith" is a keyword and a name the page never says.** The
   conjecture is attributed to CFKRS alone, and only CFKRS is in `References`;
   T378 cites `KeatingSnaith` beside it. A reader who searches the name lands
   on a page that does not contain it.

10. **The definition's citations render as "[2] [1]".** References are
    numbered before links, so `CITE{WikiDirichlet} CITE{CFKRS}` comes out
    backwards. Cosmetic, and fixed by swapping the two.

11. **For the reviewer, not the author:** T380 links T379, which is also a
    draft. The audit refuses a *published* table that links a draft, so
    publishing T380 before T379 turns comment (6) and one `Similar tables` row
    into 404s and makes the audit dirty. They should go in the other order, or
    together.

## What was checked hardest and is sound

* **The `Programs` block runs as printed and gives the stored value.** Run
  verbatim under Sage's PARI in the throwaway container, the five lines
  produce
  `0.044017231659322219441959566337537204104653010497549614380226785282629440335710600216207611078190944364…`,
  which is the stored $A_3$ to all 100 digits written. The unbound `p` in
  `s = sum(m = 0, floor(k/2), binomial(k, 2*m)/p^m)` becomes the polynomial
  variable `(p + 3)/p`, which is what `prodeulerrat` wants, so the block does
  not depend on the reader's session being clean.
* **Formula (3) is the identity it claims.** $\frac12\left((1-x)^{-k}+(1+x)^{-k}\right)
  = (1-x^2)^{-k}\sum_{m}\binom{k}{2m}x^{2m}$ with $x=p^{-1/2}$, which is
  exactly the printed local factor, and the finite sum is why the note
  "positive integer $k$ only" is on both the formula and the program.
* **Formula (4) follows from formula (2).** At $k=1$ the averaged bracket is
  $1/(1-1/p)$, and the local factor collapses to $1-1/(p(p+1))$, which is what
  the $k=1$ entry comment says and what makes it a usable control.
* **The rendering has none of the faults that live only in the rendering.** No
  bare `<` anywhere in the document, `$` balanced and braces balanced in every
  field, no `--`, the parameter line reads "$k$ — moment parameter ($k>0$)"
  rather than an empty `argument ()`, the entry labels are the numbers
  `1/2 … 12` and not a restated column header, the value column is headed
  $A_k$ and that is what every row holds, and `rigour details` folds after its
  first paragraph as intended.
* **It is one table.** One parameter, numeric values, one quantity, a column
  that names its own symbol. The question the audit asks on a draft does not
  arise here.
* **Range and order.** The entries run $k=1/2$ upward, so the small cases a
  reader arrives with are at the top, and `complete-note` finishes the
  rendered sentence: "Table is complete: no (it holds $k=1/2,1,3/2,\ldots,6$
  and every integer $7\leq k\leq12$)". Eighteen entries at 100 digits is far
  inside every limit.
* The values themselves were not rechecked beyond the two controls above; the
  build checked them against PARI, and `verify` and `sweep_arb` check them
  again.
