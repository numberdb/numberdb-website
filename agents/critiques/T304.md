## What reads well

- **One table, of the good kind.** `method` names what the numbers are *of*:
  one tableau for each of eighteen named methods, like one Hausdorff
  dimension for each named set. `coefficient` enumerates *parts of one
  object*, the tableau, which a reader holding $a_{21}$ wants to see beside
  $b_1$. Neither is "which quantity is taken of it". The column header
  "tableau coefficient" is true of every row.
- **`param-latex` is used where it belongs.** The values of `coefficient` are
  keys, not numbers, so replacing `a2_1` with $a_{21}$ is exactly what the
  field is for.
- **A reader holding a number is served.** Someone holding $35/384$ lands
  on "Dormand-Prince embedded pair", $b^{(5)}_1$, and on $a_{71}$. The comment
  on that method's first row says the seventh stage is the FSAL row and that
  this is SciPy's RK45 tableau. Someone holding $16/135$ lands on RKF45's
  fifth-order weights. That is the right first screen.
- **Parameters describe the family.** The range of this run (the Wikipedia
  revision, what is excluded and why) is in `complete-note` and
  `comment-range`, not in the parameters.
- The small values ($1$, $1/2$, $1/6$) are noise for search by number. They
  are parts of tableaux that cannot be left out, so I do not count them as a
  finding.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The `coefficient` constraint describes keys that do not exist (worth doing)

> tableau coefficient (a key c_i , a_i_j with $j<i$, or bq_i for the
> order-$q$ weight of stage $i$)

The keys in the table are `c2`, `a2_1` and `b2_1`. The constraint's
patterns `c_i` and `a_i_j` have underscores the keys lack, and `bq_i` has a
literal letter q where the keys have the order. A reader who takes the constraint at its word and
cites `T304#rk4,c_2` gets nothing. The sentence also names the document's
addressing scheme in prose, which the skill advises against. The page never
shows the keys, so the pattern means nothing to someone reading it. It
renders as "a key c_i ," with a space before the comma.

Smallest fix: describe the family and leave the keys out. For example: "a
node $c_i$ with $i\geq 2$, a matrix entry $a_{ij}$ with $j<i$, or the weight
$b^{(q)}_i$ of stage $i$ in the order-$q$ formula".

### 2. The Definition does not say what a Butcher tableau is (worth doing)

> An explicit Runge-Kutta method with rational coefficients is determined by
> its Butcher tableau [1]. The table gives the nonzero nodes $c_i$, matrix
> entries $a_{ij}$ with $j<i$, and weights $b^{(q)}_i$ of each listed method
> or embedded pair.

The first sentence states a property: the tableau determines the method.
The second lists $c_i$, $a_{ij}$ and $b^{(q)}_i$ without saying what they
are. The relation that defines them is `formula-step`, three sections
further down. That formula writes $b_i$ with no order, so for an embedded
pair a reader still has to go to `comment-embedded` to learn what $q$ is.
Without `formula-step`, two people could not be sure they would build the
same table. For example, one could use the convention $c_i=\sum_j a_{ij}$
and another the $k_i=hf(\ldots)$ form.

Smallest fix: move `formula-step` into the Definition and delete it from
Formulas. For example: "An $s$-stage explicit Runge–Kutta method advances
$y'=f(t,y)$ by $y_{n+1}=y_n+h\sum_i b_ik_i$, where
$k_i=f(t_n+c_ih,\,y_n+h\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}k_j)$. Its Butcher tableau CITE{WikiList}
is the nodes $c_i$, the matrix $a_{ij}$ and the weights $b_i$. An embedded
pair has one weight row $b^{(q)}$ for each order $q$." "Nonzero" and "each
listed method" are about coverage, and `comment-indexing` and
`complete-note` already say both.

### 3. The Newton–Cotes relation is vague where a precise one exists (worth doing)

> Newton-Cotes weights — another family of rational coefficients for
> classical numerical integration methods

This sentence would fit any two tables of rationals. The real relation is
the one a reader would want. When $f$ does not depend on $y$, the step is
the quadrature rule with nodes $c_i$ and weights $b_i$. Checked against T302:

- `three-eighths` has $c=(0,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,1)$ and
  $b=(\tfrac18,\tfrac38,\tfrac38,\tfrac18)$. These are $C_{3,j}$ in T302's
  unit-interval normalisation, Simpson's 3/8 rule.
- `kutta-3` has $c=(0,\tfrac12,1)$ and $b=(\tfrac16,\tfrac23,\tfrac16)$, which
  are $C_{2,j}$, Simpson's rule.
- `rk4` gives the same rule once its two stages at $c=\tfrac12$ are merged.

`formula-moments` is exactly the statement that this quadrature is exact to
degree $q-1$.

Smallest fix: "for $f$ independent of $y$ the method is the quadrature rule
with nodes $c_i$ and weights $b_i$; the classic fourth-order method and
Kutta's third-order method reduce to Simpson's rule $C_{2,j}$ and the 3/8-rule
method to $C_{3,j}$". The Gauss–Lobatto line has the same weakness in a
smaller way. "Implicit relatives" could be "the nodes are the $c_i$ of the
Lobatto IIIA–IIIC methods, which are implicit and not included here". I have
not checked that sentence against a source. The Gauss–Legendre line is fine
as it is.

### 4. `comment-embedded` ends on a remark about the table's structure (worth doing, one sentence)

> Which order is used to advance the step is a property of the method, not a
> separate normalisation.

This answers the audit's "is it two tables" question. It says nothing about
the mathematics, and a reader who never asked the question cannot tell what
it rules out. The fact a reader needs is *which* order advances each pair.
That fact is on the page, but only in each pair's first entry comment, and
nothing points there.

Smallest fix: "The first entry comment of each embedded pair says which of
its weight rows advances the solution; the other gives the error estimate."

### 5. Programs holds a checker, not an incantation (worth doing if cheap)

The Python block defines `row_sums` and `moment_conditions` and never calls
them. It takes plain lists that the reader has to type in from the page. It
answers "does this tableau satisfy two conditions", which `rigour details`
already says was checked. It does not answer "how do I get these numbers in
code". Two tables here are in SciPy, as the entry comments say. The
incantation `from scipy.integrate import RK23, RK45; RK45.A, RK45.B, RK45.C,
RK45.E` is what a reader with SciPy would type. It returns floats, and the
FSAL stage is not in `A`, so say so in a comment. If the checker is kept,
end it with a call on `rk4` and the output it prints.

### 6. Noting only

- **Hyphens where the title has en dashes.** The title says "Runge–Kutta".
  The Definition, `comment-range`, the method names ("Bogacki-Shampine",
  "Cash-Karp", "Dormand-Prince", "Heun-Euler", "Van der Houwen-Wray"), the
  Links titles and the entry comments say "Runge-Kutta". The Similar-tables
  captions "Gauss-Legendre" and "Newton-Cotes" misspell the titles of the
  tables they link to, which use en dashes. Search treats the two the same
  (checked: `search_text('Gauss-Legendre')` and `'Gauss–Legendre'` return the
  same list), so this is only about how the page reads.
- **"Nystrom" is shown without its umlaut** in the method name and the entry
  comment. `Keywords` already carries both spellings, so the page can say
  "Nyström's fifth-order method".
- **Two adjacent headers say "coefficient".** The Numbers header reads
  "method | coefficient | tableau coefficient". The middle column holds the
  symbol $c_2$ and the right column the value $1/2$. Renaming the
  parameter's `display` to "entry" or "symbol" would separate them.
- **`complete-note` points at "the source revision"** rather than naming it.
  It also repeats `comment-range` almost word for word. "…listed in Wikipedia's
  list of Runge–Kutta methods (revision 1346207143)…" names it. Either the
  note or the comment could then be shorter.
- **The `method` constraint, "one of the listed rational explicit methods"**,
  has the same "listed" with nothing listed nearby. "A named explicit
  Runge–Kutta method or embedded pair with rational coefficients" says the
  family.
- **`formula-error` says a difference of two formulas is their difference.**
  What a reader needs from it is that this quantity is the local error
  estimate used for step-size control. One clause would add that.
- **The forward Euler entry comment, $b^{(1)}=(1)$, restates the only row**
  beside it.
- **"Butcher tableau" is not in `Keywords`.** The index's stemmer does not
  join irregular plurals: `search_text('matrix')` and `'matrices'` return
  different lists, 19 and 9 tables. So a reader typing "Butcher tableau"
  probably matches only on "Butcher". I could not test this, because a
  draft answers no search. Adding "Butcher tableau" costs nothing.
- **One tag, `analysis`.** This is the same as T302 and T303. The critique
  of T303 already raised a "numerical analysis" tag as a question for a
  person, and T304 makes a fourth table for it.
