## What reads well

This is a good table, and the list below is short on purpose.

- **One table.** The three parameters are arguments, not names, and the
  column carries one symbol, $a_{m,r,j}$, that is true of every row.
- **The Definition defines and stops.** It names the formula, the offsets and
  the normalisation ($h^m$ on the derivative side), which is enough for two
  people to build the same table. Nothing else is in it.
- **The parameters describe the family.** The range of this run is in
  `complete-note`, and that clause finishes the "complete: no (...)"
  sentence.
- **Order.** Within a stencil the rows run $j=-r,\ldots,r$. That is the order
  of the vector in the entry comment and of the sum in the Definition, so a
  reader can read a stencil down the page. The skill's
  rule about putting small $|a|$ first is about an index a reader arrives
  wanting. Here a reader wants the stencil in order, and $\pm j$ are one
  symmetry apart anyway.
- **A reader holding a number.** Someone holding $-49/18$ lands on
  $m=2,r=3,j=0$ under the title "Central finite difference coefficients". The
  row $j=-3$ just above it writes the stencil out. That is the right first
  screen.
- The small integers ($1$, $-2$, $6$, $-20$, …) are noise for search by
  number. They are parts of stencils that cannot be left out, so I do not
  count them as a finding.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. `formula-lagrange` uses $\ell$ in a different sense from the table it links to (worth doing)

> $a_{m,r,j}=\ell_{r,j}^{(m)}(0)$, where
> $\ell_{r,j}(x)=\prod_{-r\leq k\leq r,\,k\neq j}\frac{x-k}{j-k}$ is the
> Lagrange basis polynomial for the offset $j$.

Similar tables and `comment-neighbours` send the reader to
HREF{Lagrange_basis_polynomials_for_equally_spaced_nodes} (T112). That table
defines $\ell_{d,i}$ on the nodes $0,1,\ldots,d$: the first subscript is the
degree, and the second counts from the left. Here $\ell_{r,j}$ is a
polynomial of degree $2r$ on the nodes $-r,\ldots,r$, and $j$ counts from the
centre. So $\ell_{2,1}$ has degree 2 in T112 and degree 4 on this page. A
reader who follows the link and differentiates T112's $\ell_{2,1}$ at $0$
gets a wrong number.

The relation in Similar tables, "their derivatives at the centre give the
finite difference coefficients", has the same gap. T112's polynomials have
no centre at $0$. The relation holds only after a shift that nobody states:
$a_{m,r,j}=\ell_{2r,\,r+j}^{(m)}(r)$ in T112's notation. I checked this on
$a_{1,1,1}$: $\ell_{2,2}(x)=x(x-1)/2$ has derivative $1/2$ at $x=1$. I also
checked $a_{2,1,0}$: $\ell_{2,1}(x)=2x-x^2$ has second derivative $-2$.

Smallest fix, in T112's notation, so the link lands on the same symbol:

- `formula-lagrange`: "$a_{m,r,j}=\ell_{2r,\,r+j}^{(m)}(r)$, where
  $\ell_{d,i}(x)=\prod_{0\leq k\leq d,\,k\neq i}\frac{x-k}{i-k}$ is the
  Lagrange basis polynomial for the nodes $0,1,\ldots,d$."
- Similar tables relation: "$a_{m,r,j}$ is the $m$-th derivative of
  $\ell_{2r,\,r+j}$ at the middle node $x=r$".

### 2. The entry comments say "2-order", which is not English and names an order the page never defines (worth doing)

> Derivative order $m=1$, 2-order central formula: $\tfrac{1}{2}(-1,0,1)$.
>
> Derivative order $m=2$, 2-order central formula: $1(1,-2,1)$.

A reader sees this on the first row of every stencil. Four problems:

- "2-order" is a fragment. Read aloud, it is "two order".
- Which order is meant? The page never says. The number is the order of
  accuracy, $2r+1-m$ for odd $m$ and $2r+2-m$ for even $m$. The page gives
  that only as the exponent in `formula-accuracy`, which never uses the word
  "order". A reader coming from Wikipedia's table, which is indexed by
  derivative and accuracy, needs this link most, and it is where the
  comment is least clear.
- "Derivative order $m=1$" repeats the $m$ printed on the same row.
- "$1(1,-2,1)$" reads as the number 1 applied to a vector. The comments for
  $m=4$ and $m=6$ with $r=m/2$ do the same.

Smallest fix: "Accuracy order 2: $\tfrac{1}{2}(-1,0,1)$." Write
"Accuracy order 2: $(1,-2,1)$." where the factor is 1. Then use the word
once in `formula-accuracy` (finding 3), so a reader can find out what it
means. This is 24 generated strings, so the change goes in the generator.

### 3. `formula-accuracy` gives the error of a formula that is not the one on the page (worth doing, one clause)

> The truncation error is $O(h^{2r+1-m})$ for odd $m$ and $O(h^{2r+2-m})$ for
> even $m$.

The Definition and `comment-step` both write the formula as
$h^m f^{(m)}(x)\approx\sum_j a_{m,r,j}f(x+jh)$. The error of *that*
approximation is $O(h^{2r+1})$ or $O(h^{2r+2})$. The exponents given are for
$f^{(m)}(x)\approx h^{-m}\sum_j\ldots$, after dividing by $h^m$. A reader has
to guess which approximation is meant, and the page's own formula points to
the wrong one.

Smallest fix: "The approximation
$f^{(m)}(x)\approx h^{-m}\sum_{j=-r}^{r}a_{m,r,j}f(x+jh)$ has accuracy order
$2r+1-m$ for odd $m$ and $2r+2-m$ for even $m$: its error is
$O(h^{2r+1-m})$ or $O(h^{2r+2-m})$."

### 4. Noting only

- **`comment-step` repeats the Definition.** It writes out the same formula
  again. "The rows use spacing $h=1$" suggests the coefficients depend on
  $h$, when the point of putting $h^m$ on the left is that they do not. The
  comment could go. If kept, one sentence is enough: "The coefficients do not
  depend on $h$, because $h^m$ is on the derivative side."
- **`comment-ordering` is mostly about the site.** "Symmetric duplicates are
  stored" describes storage, "a reader may hold either sign" is a remark
  about search, and the symmetry itself is `formula-symmetry`. The rest,
  offsets from $-r$ to $r$, is what the page already shows. It could go.
- **`comment-zeroes` says more than the Definition defines.** "Any coefficient
  outside the listed stencil is also $0$" gives a value to $a_{m,r,j}$ with
  $|j|>r$, which the Definition and the constraint $-r\leq j\leq r$ do not
  define. The first two sentences are right and worth keeping. The third
  clause could be dropped.
- **"Newton-Cotes" has a hyphen** in `comment-neighbours` and in the Similar
  tables caption. T302's title uses an en dash, "Newton–Cotes weights".
- **`comment-neighbours` restates Similar tables.** It links the same two
  tables with nearly the same relations. That is allowed, but it adds nothing.
- **`complete-note` does not say why the range stops at $m=6$, $r=5$.** Only
  the builder knows.
- **One tag, `analysis`.** T302 has the same single tag. T137, T302, T303 and
  the Gauss–Legendre and Gauss–Kronrod tables would all carry a tag such as
  "numerical analysis". That is more than the three tables the skill asks for
  before proposing a new tag, and it is a question for a person.
- **The Sage snippet ends with a bare call** and does not say what it
  returns. Run with `sage script.sage` it prints nothing. `print(...)` and a
  comment giving the $m=2$, $r=2$ stencil would help.
