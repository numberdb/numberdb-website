## Findings, ranked

### 1. The definition cites instead of defining, and two formulas rest on a value the page never defines

A reader sees: "This table gives the real principal values, as defined
in [4], of $\operatorname{Ei}(x)$, $E_1(x)$, $\operatorname{li}(x)$,
$\operatorname{Si}(x)$, $\operatorname{Ci}(x)$, $\operatorname{Shi}(x)$
and $\operatorname{Chi}(x)$, where $x$ is a positive rational number."

That is a sentence about the table, and the seven objects are defined
by a footnote. The Formulas section then defines four of them
($\operatorname{Si}$, $\operatorname{Ci}$, $\operatorname{Shi}$,
$\operatorname{Chi}$, in (4) and (5)) and gives $\operatorname{Ei}$ as
a series for $x>0$ in (1). The other two are given by (2),
$E_1(x)=-\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$, and (3),
$\operatorname{li}(x)=\operatorname{Ei}(\log x)$, and both evaluate
$\operatorname{Ei}$ at a negative argument, which nothing on the page
defines: (1) is stated for $x>0$ only, and with $\log x$ in it does
not extend. So a reader who arrives holding $0.21938\ldots$ or
$-0.37867\ldots$, the two families most often met, has no way from
this page to the number, and comment (8), which exists to attach the
Wikipedia links to the names, does not close the gap.

Smallest fix, one definition that stands on its own and lets (8) go:
"$\operatorname{Ei}(x)=-\mathrm{PV}\!\int_{-x}^{\infty}e^{-t}\,dt/t$,
$E_1(x)=\int_x^{\infty}e^{-t}\,dt/t$,
$\operatorname{li}(x)=\mathrm{PV}\!\int_0^x dt/\log t$,
$\operatorname{Si}(x)=\int_0^x\sin t\,dt/t$,
$\operatorname{Ci}(x)=-\int_x^{\infty}\cos t\,dt/t$,
$\operatorname{Shi}(x)=\int_0^x\sinh t\,dt/t$ and
$\operatorname{Chi}(x)=\gamma+\log x+\int_0^x(\cosh t-1)\,dt/t$ [4],
with $\gamma$ the Euler-Mascheroni constant. The table holds their
values at positive rational $x$." With that, (2) and (3) become
identities between defined things, which is what a formula is, and
"real principal values" can go: for $x>0$ the only choices are the two
principal-value integrals, which the definition now shows. Worth
doing; it is the first thing a reader reads after the numbers.

### 2. Comment (10) names $E_n$ without defining it, and "the real value of $-\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$" implies a choice that is not there

A reader sees: "The function $E_1$ is the first member of the
generalized exponential integrals $E_n$. For $x>0$, the relation in
(2) makes it the real value of $-\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$."

$E_n$ appears nowhere else on the page, so the first sentence tells a
reader only that something bigger exists. The second repeats (2), and
"the real value of" suggests $\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$ has other values;
for $x>0$ it is a real number (checked: $\operatorname{Ei}(-1)=
-0.21938\ldots$ in arb and in mpmath), so the phrase is a caveat about
nothing. It is $E_1$ that has the branch, on the negative axis, and
the table never goes there.

Smallest fix: "$E_1$ is the case $n=1$ of the generalized exponential
integral $E_n(x)=\int_1^{\infty}e^{-xt}\,t^{-n}\,dt$ [4]." and stop.
Worth doing; one line.

### 3. Formula (7) calls a continuation "real" whose values are not

A reader sees: "The real continuation satisfies
$\operatorname{Ci}(-x)=\operatorname{Ci}(x)+i\pi$ and
$\operatorname{Chi}(-x)=\operatorname{Chi}(x)+i\pi$ across the
principal branch cut."

The right-hand sides are not real, so it is not a real continuation;
it is the analytic continuation with the principal logarithm, and the
negative axis is the cut itself rather than something one crosses.
The relation as stated is the one mpmath returns
($\operatorname{ci}(-1)=0.33740\ldots+3.14159\ldots i$), so the
mathematics is right and the words around it are wrong.

Smallest fix: "$\operatorname{Si}$ and $\operatorname{Shi}$ are odd.
With the principal branch of the logarithm, for $x>0$,
$\operatorname{Ci}(-x)=\operatorname{Ci}(x)+i\pi$ and
$\operatorname{Chi}(-x)=\operatorname{Chi}(x)+i\pi$ [4]." Worth doing
with 1 and 2.

### 4. The prime zeta gloss names no relation

A reader sees under Similar tables: "Values of the prime zeta function
at rational numbers — is another table of real values connected to
the prime number theorem." The relation a gloss is for is missing:
"another table of real values" is true of sixty-odd tables, and neither
page mentions the prime number theorem, $\operatorname{li}$ or
$\operatorname{Ei}$ (T24's definition is $P(s)=\sum_p p^{-s}$ and its
one comment is about the poles). A reader who follows it lands on
nothing they were told about.

Smallest fix: remove the entry. If a connection to prime counting is
wanted on the page, it is a sentence about $\operatorname{li}(x)$ and
$\pi(x)$ in a comment, cited to [2], and it points at no table here.
Worth doing; a deletion.

### 5. Comment (12) makes a constant do the storing, and links a table where it means one number

A reader sees: "The Wilbraham-Gibbs constant stores
$\operatorname{Si}(\pi)$ and its normalisations. The argument $\pi$ is
not rational, so that value is not a row of this table."

The link caption is "The Wilbraham-Gibbs constant" and the verb
belongs to a table, so the sentence reads as if a number stored
things. The sentence means one number, $\operatorname{Si}(\pi)$, and
T40 has a row for it (anchor `WG`, its $G'=\operatorname{Si}(\pi)$),
so the skill's rule is to link the row. The second sentence is a
remark about this table's coverage rather than about the mathematics,
and Similar tables already says the same thing as the first.

Smallest fix: "$\operatorname{Si}(\pi)=1.8519\ldots$ is
HREF{Gibbs_constant#WG}[the Wilbraham-Gibbs constant]." and let the
Similar-tables entry carry the rest. Marginal; noted so whoever
repairs decides rather than inherits.
