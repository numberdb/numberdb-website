## Findings, ranked

### 1. `formula-gregory` calls $\gamma^*_m$ "the Gregory coefficient", which has the opposite sign to A002206's Gregory coefficients for odd $m$ (worth doing)

A reader sees:

> If $\gamma^*_m$ denotes the Gregory coefficient, then
> $\gamma^*_m=(-1)^m\psi_m(0)/m!$, where $\psi_m$ is the Bernoulli
> polynomial of the second kind, and $\beta^*_{s,0}=(-1)^s\gamma^*_s$.

Links offers "OEIS A002206: Gregory coefficients", and `Keywords` includes
"Gregory coefficients". A002206 lists $G_m=1,\tfrac12,-\tfrac1{12},
\tfrac1{24},-\tfrac{19}{720},\tfrac{3}{160},\ldots$. The linked Bernoulli
table's $\psi_m(0)$ is $m!\,G_m$, so the $\gamma^*_m$ defined here is
$(-1)^mG_m$, and $\beta^*_{s,0}=(-1)^s\gamma^*_s=G_s$. The two signs cancel.

Suppose a reader holds $3/160$, perhaps from Gregory's quadrature, which is
where the name comes from. They land on $\beta^*_{5,0}$. The formula tells
them this is $-\gamma^*_5$, so "the Gregory coefficient" is $-3/160$. They
open A002206 and find $+3/160$. Everything stated is consistent on its own
terms. The name is the problem, together with a detour through two sign flips
that hides the plain fact: the oldest Adams–Moulton coefficient *is* the
Gregory coefficient. ($\gamma^*_j$ with these signs is the convention in
Hairer–Nørsett–Wanner. It is legitimate, but it is not what the linked source
calls by that name.)

Smallest fix: state the fact in the linked source's convention and drop
$\gamma^*$:

> $\beta^*_{s,0}=G_s=\psi_s(0)/s!$, where $G_s$ is the Gregory coefficient
> CITE{OEISGregory} and $\psi_s$ is
> HREF{Bernoulli_polynomials_of_the_second_kind}[the Bernoulli polynomial of the second kind].

The Similar-tables relation "their value at $0$ gives the oldest Adams–Moulton
coefficient through the Gregory coefficients" can then say what it means:
"$\beta^*_{s,0}=\psi_s(0)/s!$". Of all the things in this table, this is the
most likely to bring in a reader who did not come from ODEs. It is worth
having it right.

### 2. The Definition names the formula but not what determines it (worth doing)

The Definition says the coefficients are "the rational coefficients in the
implicit $s$-step formula $y_{n+s}=y_{n+s-1}+h\sum\beta^*_{s,j}f_{n+j}$".
Every choice of $\beta^*$ fits that form. What makes them the Adams–Moulton
coefficients is the exactness condition, and that is in `comment-notation`
("chosen so the formula is exact when $f(t,y(t))$ is a polynomial in $t$ of
degree at most $s$; this is the moment condition in the Formulas section").
The second half of that sentence also points instead of naming.

Smallest fix, as in T305: move the clause into the Definition and delete it
from the comment.

> For $s\geq0$, the Adams–Moulton coefficients $\beta^*_{s,j}$ are the
> rational numbers for which the implicit $s$-step formula
> $y_{n+s}=y_{n+s-1}+h\sum_{j=0}^{s}\beta^*_{s,j}f_{n+j}$ is exact whenever
> $f(t,y(t))$ is a polynomial in $t$ of degree at most $s$ CITE{Wiki}.

The Definition's second sentence ("The method has order $s+1$, and an absent
row has coefficient $0$") then goes. The order is a property of the method
and belongs in `comment-notation`, next to backward Euler and the trapezoidal
rule. For the absent row, see finding 3.

### 3. "Nonzero" and "an absent row" describe rows that do not exist (worth doing, small)

`complete-note` renders as "complete: no (it holds every nonzero coefficient
with $0\leq s\leq12$)", and the Definition ends "an absent row has
coefficient $0$". A reader then looks for gaps. There are none. All
$\sum_{s\leq12}(s+1)=91$ coefficients are stored, and none is zero.

Smallest fix: write "it holds every coefficient with $0\leq s\leq 12$" and
delete the clause about absent rows.

### 4. The completeness note does not say why the table stops at 12, and T305's reason does not carry over (worth doing, small)

The skill asks for the reason for the range in `complete-note`. T305's repair
gave "matching VODE's default maximum Adams order". Copied here, that would
be wrong by one. The $s$-step Adams–Moulton method has order $s+1$, so
VODE's maximum order 12 is $s=11$, and this table's $s=12$ row is order 13.
There are two honest options. One is a reason that is true of $s\leq12$, for
example that the range is the same $s$ as the Adams–Bashforth table, so
every predictor there has its corrector here. The other is $s\leq 11$ with
the VODE reason. Whoever chose the range should say which. I would not
remove the $s=12$ row just to fit a citation.

### 5. `comment-step`'s "when $h=1$" suggests $\beta^*$ depends on $h$ (worth doing, small)

"Each stored number is the coefficient multiplying one $f_{n+j}$ when
$h=1$." $\beta^*_{s,j}$ does not depend on $h$. T305 now reads "the
coefficient of $f_{n+j}$ in the formula is $h\beta_{s,j}$". The same
sentence with $\beta^*$ fixes this and keeps the two tables parallel.

### 6. The $j$ parameter is titled "history index", but $j=s$ is the new value (worth doing, small)

In T305, $0\leq j<s$ and every $f_{n+j}$ is history. Here, $j=s$ is
$f_{n+s}$, the unknown value that makes the method implicit, so the title
is wrong for one row in every block. Only the title needs to change, not the
key: "index of the node, counted from the oldest value" is enough.

### 7. `formula-integral` does not link the Lagrange table at its first mention in Formulas (small)

"the Lagrange basis polynomial for the nodes $0,1,\ldots,s$" is plain text,
and the table is linked only in Similar tables. Following the skill's rule
(first mention, once per section), write
`HREF{Lagrange_basis_polynomials_for_equally_spaced_nodes}[Lagrange basis polynomial]`.
Here the notation already agrees with that table, so the link is safe.
