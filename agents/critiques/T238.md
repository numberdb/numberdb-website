# T238 critique: Laplacian polynomials of connected graphs

Read 2026-09-13. A private draft owned by zeta3, offered for review. It has 996
entries (every connected graph on at most 7 vertices), type `Z[]`, rigour
`exact`.

The mathematics holds, the page renders, and the cospectral notes are
complete. What a reader will notice is one gap and three faults. The gap is
that nothing on the page says what a key like `F?Dcw` is. Two of the faults
were fixed in T237 fourteen minutes before this table's generator was
committed from the same template, but the fixes did not reach T238: the parameter prints
"Symbolic (Unknown type)" and states the range computed, and the wheels
count spokes where Sage counts vertices. Findings 1 to 4 are worth doing.
5 and 6 are small and worth doing while the file is open. The rest are notes.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Nothing on the page says what a graph6 key is or how to turn it into a graph (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** A reader arrives holding
`x^7 - 14*x^6 + 73*x^5 - 178*x^4 + 209*x^3 - 112*x^2 + 21*x`. They land on a
row keyed `F?Dcw`, with the comment "It is Laplacian-cospectral with the
graph6 string F?C^G." 961 of the 996 rows carry no name. The only sentence
about the index is `comment-index`: "The index is the canonical graph6 string
used by the chromatic polynomial table and the Tutte polynomial table." That
sends the reader to another page to learn what the key in front of them
means. Nothing here says graph6 is McKay's encoding, that the labelling is
canonical, or how to decode a key. The Programs example builds
`graphs.CompleteGraph(5)`, so it does not show how to use a key either.

**Why it is wrong.** The reader who came with a polynomial needs to know
which graph they have, and that answer should be on the first screen of this
page. T125, T126 and T237 each carry a `comment-graph6` that answers it.

**Smallest change.**

- Replace `comment-index` with T237's `comment-graph6`, keeping its last
  clause about the sibling tables: "Graphs are named by their graph6 string
  CITE{Graph6}, McKay's compact encoding, taken after a canonical relabelling
  so that the name depends on the graph and not on how its vertices happen to
  be numbered. Recover the graph in Sage with `Graph(name)`, and produce the
  name with `g.canonical_label().graph6_string()`. The same names index
  HREF{...}[the chromatic polynomials] and HREF{...}[the Tutte polynomials]."
- Add the `Graph6` link.
- Make the program `G = Graph('D~{')  # K_5, by its name here`, then
  `G.laplacian_matrix().charpoly()`, then the `canonical_label` line, as T237
  does.

### 2. The parameter prints "Symbolic (Unknown type)" and its constraint states the range computed (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Under Parameters: "$G$ — Symbolic (Unknown type) (a
connected graph on at most $7$ vertices, named by its canonical graph6
string)".

**Why it is wrong.** The first half is the site apologising for a missing
`title`. The second half describes this run, not the family. "At most 7"
already appears in `complete-note`, where it belongs, and it would have to be
edited out of the definition of the parameter when the table grows to 8
vertices.

**Smallest change.** Do what T237's repair did. Add `title: graph`, which
renders "$G$ — graph (...)"; I checked that in `/preview`. Then make
`constraints` "a connected simple graph, named by its canonical graph6
string". T125 and T126 have the same fault.

### 3. The wheels are $W_4$, $W_5$, $W_6$ on 5, 6 and 7 vertices, beside $K_n$, $P_n$, $C_n$ counting vertices (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** `D]{` is "the wheel $W_4$", and it has 5 vertices.
Four rows up, `DLo` is "the cycle $C_5$", also with 5 vertices. `FIefw`, a
7-vertex graph, is "$W_6$".

**Why it is wrong.** The table uses two conventions for the subscript and
defines neither. Sage's `graphs.WheelGraph(5)` is `D]{`, and MathWorld's and
Wikipedia's $W_n$ has $n$ vertices. Since this afternoon, T237 names the same
three keys $W_5$, $W_6$ and $W_7$. A reader moving between the adjacency and
Laplacian tables of one graph will see its name change. (T125 and T126 still
say $W_4$ to $W_6$.)

**Smallest change.** Rename the three comments to $W_5$, $W_6$ and $W_7$ in
the generator's name map (lines 56, 63 and 70 of `generate.py`), and
republish the comments. Do the same in T125 and T126 so the four tables
agree.

### 4. Similar tables leaves out T237, the adjacency polynomials of the same graphs (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Three related tables: the chromatic and Tutte
polynomials on the same index, and the lattice entropy constants. There is no
link to the characteristic polynomials of the adjacency matrix, the closest
relative: the same 996 keys, and the same determinant with $A$ in place of
$L$.

**Why it is wrong.** The relation between the two is a fact a reader can use,
and Similar tables is the place for it. For a $k$-regular graph on $n$
vertices, $L=kI-A$, so $\lambda(G,x)=(-1)^n\chi_A(G,k-x)$. I checked this on
all 16 regular graphs in both tables. T237 does not link back to T238
either.

**Smallest change.** In T238, add an entry for T237 with the relation "the
characteristic polynomials $\det(xI-A(G))$ of the same graphs, under the same
names; for a $k$-regular graph on $n$ vertices the two are related by
$\lambda(G,x)=(-1)^n\det((k-x)I-A(G))$". Add the reverse entry in T237. Both
are drafts, and the audit allows two drafts to link to each other. Read
T237's slug from the database rather than writing it from the title.

### 5. The cospectral notes use a term the page never defines, and call a string cospectral (worth doing, small)

**What a reader sees.** 119 comments of the form "It is Laplacian-cospectral
with the graph6 string EBYW." The table comment says "Each row in a shared
class has an entry comment naming the other graph6 strings in that class."

**Why it is wrong.** "Laplacian-cospectral" appears nowhere else on the page.
A graph is cospectral with a graph, not with a string. "A shared class" leaves
the reader to guess that a class is a set of graphs with one polynomial.

**Smallest change.**

- In the entries: "It has the same Laplacian polynomial as F?C^G." For a
  triple: "... as FI_xw and F_oxw."
- In `comment-cospectral`: "The 996 graphs here have 934 distinct Laplacian
  polynomials. 119 of them share their polynomial with one or two other
  graphs, and the entry for each of those names the others."

**Do not put the keys in backticks** the way T237's repair did. 24 of these
comments name a key that contains a backtick (`F?`zo`, `EK`w`, ...), and
code spans end at the first backtick. In `/preview`,
"`` `F`Gyw` ``" renders as <code>F</code>Gyw\`. Six of T237's cospectral
comments render that way today (`F_?~w`, `F?L^?`, `FGC^w`, `FA_~o`, `F@Tl_`,
`FA]rw`), and whoever owns T237 should know. Plain text, as T238 has now, is
the form that renders every key.

### 6. "Algebraic connectivity" is a keyword the page never mentions, and the zero-root comment gives the reason for the wrong fact (worth doing, small)

**What a reader sees.** A search for "algebraic connectivity" reaches this
table through `Keywords`. Nothing on the page says what algebraic
connectivity is, or how it relates to these polynomials. The comment
nearest to it says "Every row has a factor $x$, because the Laplacian of a
connected graph has kernel spanned by the constant vector."

**Why it is wrong.** The factor $x$ is there for every graph, connected or
not, because $L\mathbf 1=0$. Connectedness is why that factor is simple, and
the simple factor is the useful fact: it is how the polynomial shows the
graph is connected. The keyword shows what the comment could go on to say.

**Smallest change.** "The constant vector lies in the kernel of every
Laplacian, so $x$ divides $\lambda(G,x)$. It divides it exactly once because
$G$ is connected. The next smallest root is the algebraic connectivity of $G$
CITE{MathWorldLaplacianPolynomial}."

## Noted only

- **The Tutte relation reads wrongly.** "contains $T_G(x,y)$, whose value
  $T_G(1,1)$ is the number of spanning trees in formula (1)" says the
  spanning trees are in the formula. "... the number of spanning trees, which
  formula (1) reads off $\lambda(G,x)$" says what was meant.
- **The canonical form depends on the algorithm.** Sage's `canonical_label()`
  uses bliss when bliss is installed, and Sage's documentation warns that
  canonical forms from different algorithms should not be compared. All 996
  keys are Sage's own algorithm's output. This runner has no bliss
  (`No module named 'sage.graphs.bliss'`), so I could not show that a
  reader with bliss gets different keys. If they do, the instruction in
  finding 1 should say `canonical_label(algorithm='sage')`. The same applies
  to T125, T126 and T237.
- **The definition carries the range.** It defines the Laplacian polynomial
  "of a connected simple graph". The polynomial is defined for every graph,
  and connectedness restricts what the table holds. That is harmless, since
  the table holds connected graphs only, but "of a simple graph" would define
  the object and leave the range to the parameter.
- **The spanning-tree formula degenerates at $n=1$.** For $K_1$,
  "$x^n+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_1x$" names the leading term twice. The
  formula still gives $\tau(K_1)=1$ if $c_1$ is read as $1$, and nobody will
  trip on it.
- **Tag.** T237 carries `eigenvalue`. The roots here are the Laplacian
  eigenvalues, so the tag would fit equally well. This is a matter of taste.
