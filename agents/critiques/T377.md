## What I checked hardest, and found right

* **The four formulas are consistent with each other and with the two entry
  comments, which is the whole of what the page claims about itself.** Formula
  (2) at $k=1$ has $\Gamma(m+1)/(m!\Gamma(1))=1$, so the local factor is
  $(1-1/p)\sum_m p^{-m}=1$ — which is exactly what the $k=1$ entry comment
  says. At $k=2$ it is $(1-1/p)^4\sum_m(m+1)^2p^{-m}$, and
  $\sum_m(m+1)^2x^m=(1+x)/(1-x)^3$, so the factor collapses to $1-p^{-2}$ —
  exactly what the $k=2$ entry comment says, and exactly formula (4). And
  formula (3) really is formula (2) restricted to integers: at $k=3$ the claim
  is $\sum_m\binom{m+2}{2}^2x^m=(1+4x+x^2)/(1-x)^5$, which I checked through
  $x^2$ (1, 9, 36 on both sides). None of this is a check on the stored digits,
  which the build and `verify` do; it is a check that the page's own
  mathematics hangs together, and it does.

* **The program on the page reproduces the rows it is for.** Run under PARI at
  140 digits exactly as printed, it returns
  `0.0493216735794000917619759100869799891531929217006036853364…` at $k=3$ and
  `0.0002146814097756221813396428504209017832659459921380567761…` at $k=4$,
  both agreeing with the stored entries to every digit I compared. That is the
  one thing `Programs` exists for and it works. It is also finding 3, because
  of what it does at the other twelve rows.

* **It is one table.** One parameter, and it is an argument and not a name:
  $k=5/2$ is one function at one point, not a second quantity wearing the same
  title. The value column heads `$a_k$`, a symbol true of every row, not the
  fallback `value`. No `param-latex`. The parameter states the family
  ($k>0$, type `Q`) and what was actually computed is in `complete-note`,
  which is the fault that recurs most in this corpus and is not on this page.

* **`equals` is used for equality and its anchor resolves.** The $k=2$ entry
  carries `equals:
  HREF{Named_rational_Euler_products_over_primes#squarefree-density}[$6/\pi^2$]`.
  The density of the squarefree integers is $1/\zeta(2)$ and $a_2=1/\zeta(2)$,
  so these are the same number and not merely related, which is the stronger
  claim `equals` makes. `curl https://numberdb.org/Named_rational_Euler_products_over_primes`
  answers 200 and contains `id="squarefree-density"`. The other
  `Similar tables` slug answers 200 too.

* **$a_1$ is stored as the exact integer `1`, not as a hundred zeros.** The
  skill asks that exactness be decided from the definition and not from the
  digits, and here there is an argument — every local factor is 1 — and the
  entry comment gives it. This is the right call in a table whose `rigour` is
  `heuristic (agreement-checked)`, and it is easy to get wrong in the other
  direction.

* **Nothing is eaten and no TeX is mangled.** There is no bare `<` inside any
  `$…$` in the document, no `--`, and every section appears in the rebuilt
  page in the site's own order (`Formulas` before `Comments`). The parameter
  line reads `$k$ — half moment exponent ($k>0$)`, not `argument ()`.

## Findings

### 1. The moment asymptotic is written as a fact, and it is a conjecture for sixteen of the eighteen rows

What a reader sees, in `Definition` and in formula (1):

> For $k>0$ rational, $a_k$ is the Euler product in (2). It is the arithmetic
> factor in (1) for the averaged $2k$-th moment of $\zeta(s)$ [2].

> (1) $\frac1T\int_0^T |\zeta(1/2+it)|^{2k}\,dt\sim c_k(\log T)^{k^2}$, with
> $c_k=a_k f_U(k)$.

The `$\sim$` is stated flat, with no hedge anywhere on the page. It is a
theorem at $k=1$ (Hardy–Littlewood) and at $k=2$ (Ingham), and for every other
$k$ it is the Keating–Snaith / CFKRS **conjecture** — which is what the paper
the page cites for formula (3) is: `[1]` is *Integral moments of
$L$-functions*, whose moment formula is presented there as a conjecture. Two
of the eighteen stored rows sit under a theorem and sixteen under a
conjecture, and the page does not distinguish them.

This is not a doubt about the values. $a_k$ is the Euler product in (2), it
converges for every $k>0$, and the digits are what they are whatever happens
to the asymptotic. What is wrong is the one sentence that tells a reader *why
these numbers exist* — and a reader who arrives holding $7.0017\ldots\times
10^{-84}$ and is told it is the arithmetic factor in an established asymptotic
has been told something the subject does not know.

Smallest fix: one word in formula (1), `$\ldots\sim c_k(\log T)^{k^2}$` becomes
`conjecturally $\sim\ldots$`, plus a sentence in `Comments` saying where the
conjecture is a theorem:

    comment-conjecture: The asymptotic in CITE{formula-moment} is a theorem for
      $k=1$ and $k=2$ and is conjectural for every other $k$ CITE{CFKRS}. The
      arithmetic factor $a_k$ itself is defined by CITE{formula-ak} for every
      $k>0$ regardless.

**Worth doing**, and it is the finding I would fix first. Everything else here
is a reader stumbling; this one is a reader being misinformed.

### 2. `$\log$ CITE{formula-ak}` reaches the page as "log (2)"

In `rigour details`, in the paragraph *above* the fold, so it is the second
thing a reader of that section sees. The stored text is

> computes the middle prime range from the exact power-series coefficients of
> $\log$ CITE{formula-ak}

and the HTML is

    coefficients of $\log$ <a class="CITE" href="#formula-ak">(2)</a>, and

MathJax sets `$\log$` as the word "log", the citation sets as "(2)", and what
a reader gets is **log (2)** — an expression, with an argument, in a sentence
that is otherwise about precision. The first reading is $\log 2$, and $\log 2$
is a plausible enough thing to find in a note about Euler products that a
reader may not notice they have misread it. This is invisible in the stored
YAML: the document reads perfectly well, and only the rendering makes the two
things adjacent.

Smallest fix: put a noun between them.

    the exact power-series coefficients of the logarithm of the local factor in
    CITE{formula-ak}

**Worth doing.** The same shape — a `CITE` immediately after a bare operator
name in maths — will produce this on any table, and it is cheap to avoid.

### 3. The only program on the page refuses twelve of the eighteen rows, and nothing says so

`Programs` holds one entry, and the page prints it with no title and no
commentary:

    default(realprecision, 140)
    k = 3
    prodeulerrat((1 - 1/p)^((k - 1)^2) *
      sum(j = 0, k - 1, binomial(k - 1, j)^2/p^j), 1, 2)

That is formula (3), whose stated hypothesis is "if $k$ is a positive
integer". Twelve of the eighteen stored rows have $k$ a half-integer. A reader
who arrived holding $a_{5/2}=0.23949\ldots$ — a row this table holds — and who
does what the section invites, changing `k = 3` to `k = 5/2`, gets:

    k = 3    -> 0.0493216735794000917619759100869799891531929217006036853364
    k = 4    -> 0.0002146814097756221813396428504209017832659459921380567761
    k = 5/2  -> PariError: _^_: domain error in sqrtn: valuation != Mod(0, 4)
    k = 7/2  -> PariError: _^_: domain error in sqrtn: valuation != Mod(0, 4)

(run under `agents/sage.sh` against `sage.libs.pari`, at the page's own
`realprecision, 140`). The failure is at least loud rather than silently
wrong, which is the better of the two ways this could have gone. But the error
names `sqrtn` and a valuation and says nothing about $k$, and a reader has no
way to learn from the page that the snippet was never meant for their row.

Smallest fix: one comment line at the top of the `code` string, in PARI's own
comment syntax, which `Programs` renders verbatim:

    \\ integer k only: this is the finite product in formula (3)

**Worth doing**, and it costs one line. The stronger version — a second
program covering half-integers via formula (2) — is a nice-to-have and a
judgement call about how much `Programs` should carry; the one-line warning is
not.

### 4. $f_U(k)$ is named twice, defined nowhere, and the corpus holds what it is made of

$f_U$ appears in formula (1) and again in comment (6):

> The leading constant is normalized as $c_k=a_k f_U(k)$. This table stores
> the arithmetic factor $a_k$ alone, not the random-matrix factor $f_U(k)$.

"The random-matrix factor" is a name, not a definition. The skill's rule is
direct: *define notation where you use it — a comment saying the values are
`U_n(x,-1)` is useless until something says what `U` is.* The consequence for
a reader is concrete: the page tells them their number is one factor of $c_k$,
tells them the other factor is called $f_U(k)$, and leaves them unable to
compute $c_k$ — which is the number that actually appears in the asymptotic
and therefore the number they are most likely to want next. The normalisation
of $f_U$ is also exactly where conventions in this literature differ, so
naming it is not decoration.

The standard normalisation is $f_U(k)=G(1+k)^2/G(1+2k)$ with $G$ the Barnes
$G$-function. I checked it against the two moments that are theorems:
$f_U(1)=G(2)^2/G(3)=1$, giving $c_1=a_1f_U(1)=1$ and Hardy–Littlewood's
$\sim\log T$; and $f_U(2)=G(3)^2/G(5)=1/12$, giving
$c_2=(6/\pi^2)/12=1/(2\pi^2)$, which is Ingham's constant. So the formula is
right and the check is one a reader could repeat.

And the corpus already holds the pieces. **T93, *Values of the Barnes
$G$-function at rational numbers*, holds $G(x)$ at every rational up to
$x=30$** — I checked $2, 5/2, 3, 7/2, 4, 9/2, 7, 8, 12, 13, 15, 25$, all
present. Every $G(1+k)$ and $G(1+2k)$ this table's eighteen rows need is in
there; the largest is $G(25)$ for $k=12$. So a reader can get from $a_k$ to
$c_k$ entirely inside NumberDB, and nothing on the page tells them.

Smallest fix: finish comment (6), and add the `Similar tables` row.

    comment-factor: The leading constant in CITE{formula-moment} factors as
      $c_k=a_kf_U(k)$, where $f_U(k)=G(1+k)^2/G(1+2k)$ is the random-matrix
      factor and $G$ is the HREF{Barnes_G-function...}[Barnes $G$-function].
      This table stores $a_k$ alone.

(read the slug off a search result rather than from this file; the title is
"Values of the Barnes $G$-function at rational numbers" and the skill's
warning about guessed slugs applies.)

**Worth doing.** It is the largest thing the page is missing, and the corpus
makes it cheap.

### 5. `Links` carries an entry nothing cites, and the place it belongs is already written

`Links` declares two entries. `WikiZeta` is cited from the definition and
renders `[2]`. The second, `PARIProdEulerRat` — "PARI/GP reference:
prodeulerrat" — is cited by nothing, so a reader meets it as a bare `[3]` at
the end of a numbered list of sources: the one form that reads as "this was
used for something", with nothing on the page saying for what.

The skill: *a reference is for citing, not for listing.* And the citation
point is already there, unlinked, in the folded half of `rigour details`:

> the integer rows against PARI's `prodeulerrat` applied to (3)

Smallest fix: `PARI's CITE{PARIProdEulerRat}` — or, since `prodeulerrat` is
set as code there and a citation reads oddly against `<code>`, append
`CITE{PARIProdEulerRat}` to that clause. Either way `[3]` stops being
furniture.

**Worth doing**, and cheap. The audit does not check this; it is the same
fault T372 had with an uncited request issue, and it is worth knowing it
recurs.

### 6. The corpus has a `moments` tag and this table does not carry it

`Tags` are `number theory` and `L-function`, both right and both broad. But
`https://numberdb.org/tags/moments` answers 200 and exists, carrying one
table: T36, *Laurent series coefficients of the logarithmic derivative of the
completed Riemann zeta function $\xi(s)$ at $s=1$*. A table whose title
contains the word "moments", and whose entire subject is the moments of
$\zeta$, is not on it.

This is the browse-sideways path the skill describes: tags are how a reader
gets from one table to a neighbouring one without knowing its name. As it
stands, a reader on T36 who clicks `moments` sees only T36.

Smallest fix: add `moments` to `Tags`. It is an existing tag, so no
three-table argument is needed, and adding this one takes it from one member
to two — which is the direction a one-member tag needs to go.

**Worth doing**, and cheap. Whether to drop `number theory` to keep the list
at two or three is the author's call; `L-function` is what T36, T228 and T246
all carry and is the one to keep.

### 7. "the finite local factor" reads as "the local factor at the finite places"

The $k=2$ entry comment:

> Here the finite local factor is $1-p^{-2}$, so $a_2=1/\zeta(2)=6/\pi^2$.

"Finite" is doing a job — the infinite sum in formula (2) collapses to the
finite sum in formula (3) — but in a table about $L$-functions, "finite" next
to "local factor" is standing vocabulary for *the non-archimedean places*, as
opposed to the factor at infinity. A reader who knows the subject is the
reader most likely to misread it, and there is no factor at infinity anywhere
on this page for them to be distinguishing it from. The $k=1$ comment says
"Every local factor is $1$" without the word, so the two comments are also
inconsistent with each other.

Smallest fix: drop it, or say what collapsed.

    At $k=2$ the local factor collapses to $1-p^{-2}$, so
    $a_2=1/\zeta(2)=6/\pi^2$.

That also removes the "Here", which points rather than names.

**Worth doing**, and it is one word.

## Noted only, because I noticed them

* **Comment (6) repeats formula (1) verbatim.** "$c_k=a_kf_U(k)$" is stated in
  both, two sections apart. `Formulas` holds the identity and `Comments` holds
  the convention; the comment only needs to say which factor this table
  stores. Finding 4's rewrite happens to fix this by citing the formula rather
  than restating it.

* **"the control value $a_2=1/\zeta(2)$"**, in the `Similar tables` relation
  for the zeta-values table. "Control" is the build's word for a check it ran,
  not a fact about the mathematics — the skill puts what you verified in
  `rigour details`, once, where this table already has it. The relation would
  say the same thing as "contains $\zeta(2)$, and $a_2=1/\zeta(2)$".

* **`$k$ — half moment exponent`** is three nouns in a row, and a reader has
  to guess whether it parses as "half-moment exponent" or "half the moment
  exponent". Comment (5) settles it, one section down. "half the exponent
  $2k$" as the parameter title would settle it where it is read.

* **`complete-note` says what is held and not why.** "it holds
  $k=1/2,1,3/2,\ldots,6$ and every integer $7\leq k\leq12$" is an accurate,
  well-formed clause that finishes the rendered sentence, which is what the
  skill asks of the form. What it does not say is why the half-steps stop at 6
  while the integers run to 12 — a reader wondering whether $k=13/2$ is coming
  or is deliberately absent cannot tell. One clause ("the half-integers are
  where the moment conjecture is usually quoted; beyond $k=6$ only integer $k$
  appears in the literature", if that is the reason) would answer it.

* **"where the product runs over rational primes"** in formula (2). There are
  no other primes anywhere on this page — no number field is in play — so
  "rational" invites a reader to wonder what it is excluding. Harmless, and
  "over the primes" says the same thing.

* **The Definition's first sentence carries no mathematics at all.** "For
  $k>0$ rational, $a_k$ is the Euler product in (2)" is a pointer, and (2) is
  two sections further down the page. I checked whether this is house style
  before calling it a fault: of 44 tables sampled in T150–T199, five cite a
  formula from their definition, and T174 does exactly this. So it is within
  the corpus's practice and I am not reporting it as a finding. The second
  sentence does name what the object is, which is what saves it.

## On the audit

`GET /api/table/T377/audit` returns `{"findings": [], "clean": true}` and I
agree with all of it: the slugs resolve, the definition is inside its length
bound, the `HREF`s are well-formed, the parameter describes the family rather
than the run, there is no `param-latex` misuse and no `value` fallback in the
number header. Every check it makes is one this table passes.

Five of the seven findings above are outside what it looks at, and the two
patterns are worth naming for whoever maintains it:

* **An uncited `Links` entry** (finding 5) is mechanically checkable — every
  key in `Links` and `References` should appear in some `CITE{}` in the prose
  — and this is at least the second table in recent batches with the same
  fault. That one is a rule, and a rule would catch it.
* **A `CITE` immediately following a bare operator name inside maths**
  (finding 2) is also mechanical: `$\log$`, `$\exp$`, `$\sin$` followed by a
  citation produces a rendered expression that is not the one intended. It
  lives only in the rendering, which is where the audit does not look, but it
  is visible in the stored string as the regex `\\(log|exp|ln|sin|cos)\$\s*CITE\{`.

Findings 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 are judgements about the mathematics and the prose
and I would not expect a rule to make them.

## What I did not do

I did not re-verify the stored digits against an independent computation of
the Euler product; the build did that and `verify` does it again, and the
prompt is right that repeating it is the least useful thing available. The two
numerical runs above were both about the page rather than the values: whether
the printed program works, and at which rows it stops working.

I changed nothing in the table.
