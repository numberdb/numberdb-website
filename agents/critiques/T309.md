## What reads well

- **It is one table.** It has a single argument $\ell$. The value column is
  headed $\Phi^c_\ell(x,y)$, which is true of every row, and there is no
  `param-latex`.
- **The parameters describe the family and not the run.** They read "prime
  level ($\ell$ is prime)". The range is in `complete-note`, and "it holds
  every prime $\ell\leq43$" finishes its sentence.
- **Formula (3) is right.** I checked it by hand:
  $\eta(-1/\tau)=\sqrt{\tau/i}\,\eta(\tau)$ gives
  $\mathfrak m_\ell(-1/(\ell\tau))=\ell^s/\mathfrak m_\ell(\tau)$. The
  $\ell^s$ in the definition is exactly what makes the involution integral.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The relation stated for T96 is false for nine of the fourteen rows (worth doing)

A reader sees this under Similar tables:

> Modular polynomials for the $j$-invariant — eliminating the eta quotient
> from $\Phi^c_\ell(\mathfrak m_\ell,j(\tau))$ and its Fricke transform gives
> the classical modular polynomial

This holds only when $v=1$, which is the case for $\ell=2,3,5,7,13$. For
$v>1$, the resultant in $x$ of $\Phi^c_\ell(x,Y)$ and
$x^{\ell+1}\Phi^c_\ell(\ell^s/x,Z)$ has degree $v(\ell+1)$ in $Y$. The
classical $\Phi_\ell$ has degree $\ell+1$. The reason is that
$\mathfrak m_\ell$ has degree $v$ on $X_0(\ell)$, so each value of the eta
quotient has $v$ values of $j$ above it, and only one of them is isogenous.

At $\ell=11$ the resultant has degree 60, and $\Phi_{11}$ is a factor of it
with a degree-48 cofactor. The generator already knows this: its check is
`resultant != stored and resultant % stored != 0`, which is a divisibility
test. The rigour note says the same thing more quietly: "For
$\ell=2,3,5,7,11$, eliminating the eta quotient was checked against the stored
classical modular polynomial". At $\ell=11$ that check found a factor, not an
equality.

The sentence is also hard to parse. $\Phi^c_\ell(\mathfrak m_\ell,j(\tau))$ is
identically zero, so "eliminating the eta quotient from" it does not say which
two polynomials are meant.

**Fix:** "the resultant in $x$ of $\Phi^c_\ell(x,X)$ and
$x^{\ell+1}\Phi^c_\ell(\ell^s/x,Y)$ is divisible by the classical modular
polynomial $\Phi_\ell(X,Y)$, and equals it up to a constant when
$v=1$". In the rigour note, write "was found to divide the resultant" in
place of "was checked against".

### 2. The definition does not determine the polynomial (worth doing)

> Listed is the primitive polynomial $\Phi^c_\ell(x,y)\in\mathbb Z[x,y]$
> satisfying $\Phi^c_\ell(\mathfrak m_\ell(\tau),j(\tau))=0$, normalised so
> that the coefficient of $x^{\ell+1}$ is $1$.

Other polynomials also meet every condition here. At $\ell=2$, the
coefficient of $x^2$ in $\Phi^c_2$ is the constant 48. So
$(x-47)\,\Phi^c_2(x,y)$ still vanishes at $(\mathfrak m_2,j)$, is primitive
by Gauss's lemma, and has $x^3$-coefficient $48-47=1$. What picks out the
table's polynomial is irreducibility, or equivalently $\deg_x=\ell+1$. That
fact appears only later, as Formula (2), where it reads as a consequence.
"Primitive" adds nothing once a coefficient is 1.

**Fix:** "Listed is the irreducible polynomial
$\Phi^c_\ell(x,y)\in\mathbb Z[x,y]$ satisfying …, normalised so that the
coefficient of $x^{\ell+1}$ is $1$". Alternatively, call it the minimal
polynomial of $\mathfrak m_\ell$ over $\mathbb Q(j)$, made monic.

### 3. The definition sends the reader to a formula for the object itself (worth doing)

> Let $\mathfrak m_\ell$ be the eta quotient in Formula (1).

Commit `efcd35f` ("shorten canonical modular definition") moved the
definition of $s$ and $\mathfrak m_\ell$ out of the Definition and into
Formulas. On the page, Formulas sits below Parameters. A reader of the
Definition therefore has to jump down the page before they know what $x$
stands for. Formula (1) is not an identity or a closed form, which is what the
skill says Formulas holds. It is the definition. The earlier wording was two
sentences and did not run long.

**Fix:** restore the earlier Definition, which began "For a prime $\ell$, put
$s=12/\gcd(12,\ell-1)$ and $\mathfrak m_\ell(\tau)=\ell^s(\eta(\ell\tau)/\eta(\tau))^{2s}$,
where $\eta$ is Dedekind's eta function CITE{DLMFEta}". Remove
`formula-eta-quotient`.

### 4. The program returns this table's polynomial at six of the fourteen levels, and nothing says which (worth doing)

```
E = ellmodulareqn(13);
E[2] == 0        \\ true for a canonical equation
E[1]
```

At $\ell=13$ this prints the stored row. At $\ell=11,17,19,23,29,31,41,43$,
PARI returns type 1: an Atkin modular polynomial, a different polynomial. A
reader who changes 13 to 11 gets `0` and a polynomial that is not in this
table, and no other way to get the row. The Comments sentence has the same
gap: "the polynomial is PARI/GP's canonical modular equation when
`ellmodulareqn` returns type $0$". It is true, but a reader still does not
know that this means only $\ell=2,3,5,7,13,37$ in the stored range.

**Fix:** name the levels in `comment-convention`, for example "…returns type
$0$, which for $\ell\leq43$ happens at $\ell=2,3,5,7,13,37$". For the other
levels, a Sage program would serve the reader. It would solve for the kernel
from the $q$-expansions, as `generate.py` does. If that is too long for
Programs, one sentence pointing to the attached generator would do.

### 5. The degree is stated three times, and the Fricke relation twice (worth doing, cheap)

- `comment-degree` ("For $v=s(\ell-1)/12$, the degree in $x$ is $\ell+1$ and
  the degree in $y$ is $v$") repeats Formula (2) almost word for word.
- Every entry comment, for example "$\deg_y\Phi^c_{11}=5$.", repeats
  Formula (2) once per row. The skill names this case: a per-row comment that
  "repeats what a formula in the document already says for every row at once"
  is worth leaving out. The degree is also visible in the polynomial.
- The second sentence of `comment-convention` ("With this convention the
  Fricke involution sends $\mathfrak m_\ell$ to $\ell^s/\mathfrak m_\ell$")
  repeats Formula (3).

**Fix:** delete `comment-degree` and the entry comments. Shorten
`comment-convention` to "The factor $\ell^s$ in $\mathfrak m_\ell$ makes the
Fricke involution $x\mapsto\ell^s/x$ (Formula (3)), and with it the polynomial
is PARI/GP's canonical modular equation …".

### 6. "the reciprocal convention in $x$" is not what the other quotient gives (worth doing, one line)

> The quotient $(\eta(\tau)/\eta(\ell\tau))^{2s}$ gives the reciprocal
> convention in $x$, not another entry of this table.

That quotient is $\ell^s/\mathfrak m_\ell$, not $1/\mathfrak m_\ell$. Its
polynomial is $x^{\ell+1}\Phi^c_\ell(\ell^s/x,y)$ made monic, which differs
from the reciprocal polynomial by rescaling $x$ by $\ell^s$. The clause "not
another entry of this table" is also about this website rather than about the
mathematics.

**Fix:** "The quotient $(\eta(\tau)/\eta(\ell\tau))^{2s}=\ell^s/\mathfrak m_\ell$
satisfies $x^{\ell+1}\Phi^c_\ell(\ell^s/x,j)=0$."

## Noted only

- **Two Similar-tables relations describe use, not mathematics.** The $j$
  table's relation is "the generator uses this modular function in the
  defining relation". That is about the run; "$\Phi^c_\ell$ relates
  $\mathfrak m_\ell$ to $j$" would say the same thing as mathematics. The
  Weber table's relation is "another family of smaller modular equations used
  in place of the classical $j$-invariant equation". The comparison "smaller"
  and the phrase "used in place of" are also about practice, but they are
  harmless.
- **The range is not argued.** The complete-note does not say why 43 is the
  limit. The rows at $\ell=23,41,43$ are 6.6 to 11.7 thousand characters,
  which is well past the length the skill says a reader reads. For this
  family that is probably right, because SEA users want exactly these
  polynomials whole. A clause saying so would answer the question before a
  reviewer asks it.
- **Rigour-note wording.** "The rows in the stored range for
  $\ell=2,3,5,7,13,37$ were compared with PARI/GP's canonical `ellmodulareqn`
  data where that data has type $0$" hedges twice. "At
  $\ell=2,3,5,7,13,37$, where `ellmodulareqn` returns type 0, the rows equal
  PARI's" says the same thing.
- **One possible link.** The first $j(\tau)$ in the Definition could link to
  the $q$-expansion of the $j$-invariant (T79, `Q-expansion_of_the_j-invariant`).
  The audit does not ask for it, and the link is a matter of taste.
