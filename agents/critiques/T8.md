# T8, "Euler's constant e": read as a reader

Read on 2026-09-22 against <https://numberdb.org/skill> as fetched that day.
One entry, 301 significant digits, no parameters, `complete: yes`. The table
came in from the data repository in 2021 and was edited substantively once
since, by zeta3 on 2026-09-21, which fixed the Wikipedia link, added OEIS,
`complete`, `Keywords` and a `Programs` line that produces the stored
precision. That repaired state is what I read; the findings it settled are not
re-raised here, and where I checked one I say so.

**The short version: the page reads well and the number is right. One thing on
it is a claim about mathematics that most of the literature, this corpus's own
prose, and the table's own first link all contradict — the title.** A second
thing, the sentence under "How they were obtained", is a note left by a
migration rather than a sentence written to a reader. Everything else I found
is noted because I noticed it.

I checked three things hardest: what the title says to somebody who knows the
name from elsewhere, whether the 301 digits mean what the page claims they
mean, and whether a reader who arrives holding 2.71828 is served in the first
screen. The third is the one that came out clean, and it is the one that
matters most: they are.

---

## 1. The title is the usual name of a different number, and that number is in this database

**Worth doing. It is a rename of a public table, so it is a person's call, not
a critique's — but the evidence is not a matter of taste.**

The page is headed

> **Euler's constant e**

In the standard modern usage, *Euler's constant* is
$\gamma = 0.5772156649\ldots$, not $e$. Three independent witnesses, none of
them my memory:

- **The table's own `Links[1]`.** It points at
  `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_(mathematical_constant)`, whose first sentence is
  "The number e is a mathematical constant that is the base of the natural
  logarithm and exponential function" — it does not use the phrase. The
  article actually titled *Euler's constant* is a different one, and it opens
  "Euler's constant is a mathematical constant, usually denoted by the
  lowercase Greek letter gamma".
- **This corpus's own prose, in two tables.** T231 (Euler-Lehmer constants)
  carries the comment
  `$\gamma=\gamma(0,1)$ is Euler's constant, the Stieltjes constant HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}[$\gamma_0$]`,
  and its `Formulas` say "where $\gamma$ is Euler's constant". T230
  (Euler-Kronecker constants) carries the same sentence. So the phrase is
  already in service here for $\gamma$, in tables written after this one.
- **$\gamma$ is a number this database holds and answers with.** Searching it
  by value returns two entries:

      search_real_ball(0.5772156649015329, 1e-15)
        -> T16   Stieltjes constants          0.57721566490153286060...
        -> T231  Euler-Lehmer constants       0.57721566490153286060...

The growth review of 2026-09-21 raised this and set it aside on the ground
that "$\gamma$ has no table of its own, so nothing collides today". Read as a
reader, the collision does not need a table of its own to exist — it is
already here, in the search box:

    search_text("Euler's constant")  -> 20 results
      1 T175 Values of the Clausen functions ...
      2 T174 Eigenvalues of the Gauss-Kuzmin-Wirsing operator
      3 T172 Lévy's constant
      4 T8   Euler's constant e
      ...
      7 T231 Euler-Lehmer constants $\gamma(a,q)$

Exactly one of those twenty is *titled* with the phrase, and it is the table
of $e$. T16, which actually holds $\gamma$, does not appear at all. A reader
who types the standard name of $\gamma$ is shown one table that seems to be
about it by name, and it is the wrong number; and a reader who arrives at this
page by any route is told, in the largest text on it, something they will have
to unlearn.

**Smallest change** — three small edits, which want doing together:

1. **Title: `Euler's number $e$`.** That is the name the linked article uses,
   it is already in `Keywords`, and the address `/E` and the T-number do not
   move, so no citation and no `HREF{E}` breaks. Setting the letter as `$e$`
   while the field is open is house style — T37 and T200 put mathematics in
   their titles — and the bare roman `e` in a sentence is the one thing on
   this page a reader cannot tell is a symbol.
2. **Do not simply move "Euler's constant" into `Keywords`.** `Keywords`
   carries the same weight as the title, so that keeps the mis-hit and only
   hides where it comes from.
3. **Say it once, in a `Comments` line**, which is indexed at the lowest
   weight and can tell the truth when it is reached:

   > `comment-name`: "Euler's constant" usually names
   > $\gamma = 0.5772\ldots$, held here as the Stieltjes constant
   > `HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}[$\gamma_0$]`, and not this number.

   That is a fact about the naming of the objects, it links at first mention,
   and the form `HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}` is already in use in T231 and
   resolves (the entry carries `id="0"`; I fetched the page).

Against the change: some older literature does call $e$ Euler's constant, and
a public table's title is an address of a kind even when the slug does not
move. That is the argument for a person deciding rather than a bot, and it is
why this is written as a finding rather than a repair.

## 2. `rigour details` is a note the migration left, not a sentence for a reader

**Worth doing, and it is small: the table next door already has the sentence.**

The page prints, under the heading *How they were obtained*:

> Checked here against exp(1) computed in ball arithmetic at 4000 bits, which
> covers every stored digit rather than the first hundred: the stored value is
> within one unit in the last place of the 301 held.

Three things a reader has to supply from outside the page:

- **"Checked here"** — here is *where*? The revision history answers it: the
  2026-08-15 and 2026-08-16 migrations describe themselves as "verified here
  against exp(1) in ball arithmetic", and "here" meant the migration. On the
  page it has no referent, and it is a fragment besides: no subject, which is
  the skill's "notes to yourself" fault exactly.
- **"rather than the first hundred"** — a reader has no first hundred in view.
  This is the 08-16 migration saying how it differed from the 08-15 one. It
  compares the present sentence with a sentence nobody can see.
- **The field is labelled *how they were obtained*, and the sentence says only
  how they were checked.** Nothing on the page says where the 301 digits came
  from. Since the repair of 2026-09-21, `Programs` does produce them, so there
  is something true and useful to say.

**Smallest change:** the sentence T7 now carries, with the function changed.
T7 is this table's structural twin — one entry, 301 digits, same shape — and
after its own critique and repair today it reads:

> Computed by Sage's `RealBallField(1024).pi()` in ball arithmetic and checked
> against $\pi$ recomputed at 4000 bits: the stored value is within one unit
> in the last place of the 301 digits held.

For T8 the honest version cannot claim the 2021 import was computed by that
call, so it should claim only what is known:

> The stored digits agree with $\exp(1)$ recomputed in ball arithmetic at 4000
> bits, at every digit written; the decimal is the truncation of that value,
> so it is within one unit of its last place.

I checked that sentence before proposing it — §5.

## 3. The definition never says what the derivative is taken in

**Small, worth doing only if the field is open for something else.**

> The number $e$ is the unique positive real number $a$ such that $a^x$ equals
> its own derivative.

This is true, it is not circular (the 2026-09-21 repair removed the $e$ that
used to sit inside its own defining condition), and two people would build the
same table from it. But $x$ is never introduced, and "its own derivative"
leaves the reader to supply both the variable and the fact that $a^x$ is meant
as a function of it rather than as a number. Six words fix it:

> The number $e$ is the unique positive real number $a$ such that the function
> $x \mapsto a^x$ is its own derivative.

## 4. Noted only because I noticed

- **`Similar tables` is empty, and I think that is right.** T7's line points at
  a table that stores $a\pi$ systematically; there is no such family for $e$
  here. What the corpus has is incidental: $\Gamma(1,1) = 1/e$ in T351 and
  $M(2;1;1) = 2e$ in T210, which are coincidences of one entry rather than a
  relation between families. I confirmed the first
  (`search_real_ball(0.36787944117144233, 1e-15)` returns T351 alone). The
  real gap — no table of $e^x$ at real or rational arguments — is already
  written up by the growth review and belongs to the ideas stage, not here.
- **`Programs` computes at 1024 bits and `rigour details` speaks of 4000.**
  Both are right (1024 bits is a radius of $2.3\times10^{-308}$, comfortably
  inside the last place of a 301-digit decimal) and a reader who notices the
  two numbers has to work that out. If §2's sentence is rewritten, saying
  "recomputed at 4000 bits" next to a program that uses 1024 is worth one
  half-clause.
- **The tags claim two things the page never says.** `transcendental` and
  `irrational` are both true and neither appears in any prose on the page.
  There is no `Comments` section at all. I am not asking for one for its own
  sake; if §1's naming comment is added, "e is transcendental" would sit
  naturally beside it, and if §1 is declined it is not worth a revision alone.
  All three tags do reach other tables (6, 3 and 5 respectively), so the
  audit's one-table-tag check has nothing to say and neither have I.
- **The 301 digits print as one unbroken run**, so a reader who wants the
  250th counts characters across wrapped lines. That is the site's display of
  every long real, not this table's doing, and T7 does the same.

## 5. The numbers, which I was not asked to check

The value is right, and I checked it myself rather than taking the growth
review's word for it, because §2 proposes a sentence that asserts something
about it.

The check is the one the written decimal actually claims. A 301-digit decimal
in this corpus denotes an interval one unit wide in its last place, so the
question is whether $e$ lies inside that interval — not whether a printed ball
looks like the stored string. Build the stored decimal as an exact rational
and subtract, which needs no formatting of a ball at all:

    q   = ZZ(digits) / 10^300            # the stored decimal, exactly
    e   = RealBallField(4000)(1).exp()   # radius 3.03e-1204
    (e - q) / 10^-300  =  0.55170276183860626...   ± 6.1e-904 ulp
    |e - q| < 10^-300  ->  True

So $e$ sits 0.5517 of a last place above the stored decimal: the 301 digits
are the true digits truncated, every one of them correct, and the decimal is
inside the interval it denotes. The true continuation is `...551702761838`.
Run under `agents/sage.sh` from `/tmp/t8_reader_check2.py`; it took one
container and no tuning.

## 6. The audit is clean, and I agree with it

`GET /api/table/T8/audit` (the key on stdin; there is no database on this
runner, which is the fallback the brief names) returns
`{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. Every check it runs is satisfied here, and
three of its silences are correct for reasons worth naming:

- **Tags.** `audit_table.py:417` complains about a tag that reaches only its
  own table. All three of T8's reach others — I counted them off the tag pages
  rather than the source: transcendental 6, irrational 3, exponential function
  5.
- **The one-table-or-several family of checks** (`:591`, `:616`, `:650`) has
  nothing to ask: there are no parameters, and the value column is headed
  `$e$` from `Display properties: number-header`, not the fallback word
  `value`.
- **Links are not fetched over the API.** `--links` is a flag on
  `manage.py audit_table` and `findings_for` is called from `api.py:1440`
  with `fetch` defaulting to off, so an audit run this way has not looked at
  the external links at all. It would not have helped: the growth review's
  finding was a link that answered 200 and pointed at the wrong article, which
  no fetch can catch either.

What no rule in it can see is §1. The audit compares a table against the
corpus's addresses, its titles and its own stored values; it has no way to
know that a *title* is the accepted name of a different constant. That took
reading the table's own link and two other tables' comments.

## 7. What I would change, in order

1. **Retitle to `Euler's number $e$`, and say once in `Comments` that
   "Euler's constant" usually means $\gamma$, linking
   `HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}`** (§1). A person's decision; the evidence is
   in §1 and none of it is my recollection.
2. **Rewrite `rigour details` as a sentence addressed to a reader** (§2), on
   the pattern T7 already uses. One field, no risk, and it removes two phrases
   that refer to things off the page.
3. **Name the variable in the definition** (§3). Six words, only worth a
   revision if one is happening anyway.

Nothing here adds an entry, changes a digit, or touches the range: the table
holds the one number its definition names, and it holds it correctly.

**I changed nothing.**
