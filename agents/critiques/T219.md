## Findings, ranked

### 1. Formula (2) does not typeset

What a reader sees, after MathJax has run:

    $\mathrm{Vol}(m003(-3,1))= \frac{3\cdot23^{3/2}}{4\pi^4} ζ_K(2)  ,where  K  isthecubicfieldgeneratedbyarootof  x^3-x+1$ [7].

The first half is raw TeX with its dollar sign, the link text
`\zeta_K(2)` is raw TeX inside the link, ", where" and "is the cubic
field generated by a root of" are set as mathematics with the spaces
squeezed out, and the polynomial is raw TeX again. Confirmed by
typesetting the rendered page with the site's vendored MathJax; the
formula block produces two math containers reading `,where` and
`isthecubicfieldgeneratedbyarootof` and no container for the volume.

Why: the document writes
`HREF{Values_of_Dedekind_zeta_functions_of_cubic_fields_at_2#-23,1}[\zeta_K(2)]`
*inside* the `$...$` of the formula. The site turns it into an `<a>`
element, and MathJax 3 ends its scan at any element it does not treat
as text, so the opening `$` never meets its closing one. The next `$`
it finds is the one before $K$, and the pairing is off by one for the
rest of the sentence. This is the fault `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`
already records under "An `HREF{...}` inside `$...$` breaks the
formula on the page"; the same construction in the `rigour details`
is written the right way round, `HREF{...}[$\zeta_K(2)$]`, and
typesets.

Smallest change: take the link out of the mathematics and put the
dollar signs inside the caption:

    $\mathrm{Vol}(m003(-3,1))= \frac{3\cdot23^{3/2}}{4\pi^4}\zeta_K(2)$,
    where HREF{Values_of_Dedekind_zeta_functions_of_cubic_fields_at_2#-23,1}[$\zeta_K(2)$]
    is the value at $2$ of the Dedekind zeta function of the cubic field
    $K$ generated by a root of $x^3-x+1$ CITE{WikiWeeks}.

Worth doing before the draft is offered; it is the formula a reader
holding the Weeks volume most wants, and at present it is unreadable.

### 2. Comment (6) writes a number into a comment that the corpus holds as a row

What a reader sees: "The next entry by volume is $m007(3,1)$, often
called Vol3, with volume $1.0149\ldots$."

Why it is wrong: that is a value in a comment, which answers no search
and cannot be cited, and it is exactly the value the reader who has
just read two rows wants next. The corpus already holds it: the
volume of Vol3 is Gieseking's constant, the volume of the regular
ideal tetrahedron, which is T175's row $s=2$, $t=1/3$ (its comment
says "Gieseking's constant, the volume of the regular ideal
tetrahedron"). SnapPy's value for $m007(3,1)$ agrees with that row to
the fifteen digits double precision gives; the equality is the fact
the name Vol3 records. The lessons file explains why the row is not
here: SnapPy's triangulation of $m007(3,1)$ has negatively oriented
tetrahedra and the generator's certificate does not apply.

Smallest change: replace the digits with the fact and the link:
"The next entry by volume is $m007(3,1)$, often called Vol3, whose
volume is
HREF{Values_of_the_Clausen_functions_at_rational_multiples_of#2,1/3}[Gieseking's constant]
$\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$, the volume of the regular ideal tetrahedron."
Better still, when a certificate for that triangulation exists, a
third row. Worth doing.

## Noted only

- Formula (3) names its field by discriminant only, "the quartic field
  of discriminant $-283$", while (2) names a polynomial. The field is
  unique for that discriminant, and it is the field of $x^4-x-1$; one
  clause makes the two formulas parallel and gives a reader something
  to type. Formula (2) is set as a fraction and (3) inline, which is
  harmless.
- The entry comments are semicolon-separated fragments, "Meyerhoff
  manifold; first homology $\mathbb{Z}/5$; Dehn filling coefficients
  $(-2,3)$ on $m003$; ...". The Dehn filling clause repeats the row
  key on both rows. Two sentences per row would read better, and the
  `+` in $\mathbb{Z}/5 + \mathbb{Z}/5$ is SnapPy's spelling where a
  reader expects $\oplus$ or $\times$.
- The page prints the keys as `m003(-3, 1)` with a space that the
  stored key does not have; that is the site's rendering of a Symbolic
  tuple, and SnapPy accepts either spelling.
- P1 prints `OrientableClosedCensus.identify(M)` rather than
  `M.name()` because a census entry's `name()` returns the unfilled
  parent, `m003`. The choice is right and undocumented; a reader who
  simplifies it gets the wrong label. Not a fault in the table.
- The title promises a census of 11031 manifolds and the table holds
  two. The `complete-note` says so honestly and the family is the
  right one to name, so this is by the book; whether a two-row table
  should carry that title until more rows can be certified is the
  reviewer's call, not a reading fault. The rigour details are the
  argument for stopping where it stopped.
- Comment (4) says the nonorientable closed census "is not part of
  this table". The parameter constraint already says orientable, and
  the title says "closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds of the Hodgson-Weeks
  census", which is the orientable census by name; the sentence is
  harmless but is a remark about the table's range.

## What reads well

The `rigour details` are the best part of the page: they say which
system was solved, why dropping one edge equation is allowed, that
every shape was checked to lie in the upper half plane, how many
digits the widest ball supports, and what each row was compared
against, and then stop. The `complete-note` is a clause that finishes
the site's sentence. The keywords put the Fomenko-Matveev-Weeks name
where the index reads it. Formula (1) tells a reader with a
triangulation how to reproduce a row.
