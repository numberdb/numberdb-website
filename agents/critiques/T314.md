# T314: Kauffman polynomials of the prime knots with at most ten crossings

Read on 2026-09-17, against the skill fetched the same day from
<https://numberdb.org/skill> (47,534 bytes). T314 is a draft of 479 `Z[]`
entries: the Kauffman polynomial $F_K(a,z)$ of the unknot, of each of the 249
prime knots with at most ten crossings as KnotInfo draws it, and of each of the
229 chiral ones' mirror images, shifted to $a^{-m}F_K(a,z)$. `rigour: exact`,
`complete: yes`. It is the fifth knot-polynomial table in the corpus and is
built to the same pattern as the four published ones.

**The table reads well.** The hardest things I checked all held: the whole
document renders, every TeX fragment typesets, and the two statements a reader
of this table has to trust -- the shift convention and the Jones specialisation
-- are exactly true of all 479 stored entries. The findings below are one
broken-looking line, one missing section, and three sentences.

## Findings, in the order I would fix them

### 1. `Size exception` puts a line on the page that says "(Unknown key)", for a limit the table does not exceed

What a reader sees, in Data properties, under "Table is complete: yes":

> Size exception: The complete Rolfsen range through ten crossings has 479
> entries when both mirror images are listed; the dry run measured a 213.7 KB
> entries block and a longest entry of 500 characters, under the 320 KB soft
> limit and matching the range of the Alexander, Jones, Conway and HOMFLY-PT
> knot tables. **(Unknown key)**

Three things are wrong with that line and they compound. The site appends
"(Unknown key)" because `Size exception` is missing from `property_names` in
`numberdb_app/views.py:1063`, which is a site bug already recorded twice in
`docs/agent-environment.md` (sections at lines 3169 and 3459) and not this
table's to fix. But this table need not meet it at all: the field exists for an
author who has *passed* a soft limit, and T314 passes none -- 479 entries
against 1200, 213.7 KB against 320 KB, and `audit_table` reports no size breach.
The sentence then says so itself ("under the 320 KB soft limit"), so the page
carries a line explaining why a limit was exceeded that ends by saying it was
not. And what it actually narrates is the build: "the dry run measured". None
of the four published sibling tables carries one.

Smallest fix: delete `Size exception` from Data properties. Nothing in it is
lost that the title, the definition and `complete: yes` do not already say.

I am ranking this first because it is the only thing on the page that looks
broken to somebody who is not reading closely.

### 2. There is no `Programs` section, and a reader cannot guess the incantation

All four published knot-polynomial tables carry one (`program-sage` on
Alexander, Conway, Jones and HOMFLY-PT), as does Hyperbolic volumes. T314 has
none, and this is the family where guessing fails: Sage's `Link` has
`alexander_polynomial`, `conway_polynomial`, `homfly_polynomial`,
`jones_polynomial`, `khovanov_polynomial` and `links_gould_polynomial`, and **no
`kauffman_polynomial`** (checked on SageMath 10.9 in a throwaway container).
The table's own `comment-range` tells the reader that KnotInfo carries Kauffman
polynomials past ten crossings, so it invites exactly the question a `Programs`
block answers.

The incantation exists and is short. In the same container:

    from sage.knots.knotinfo import KnotInfo
    KnotInfo.K3_1.kauffman_polynomial()
    # a^-2*z^2 - 2*a^-2 + a^-3*z + a^-4*z^2 - a^-4 + a^-5*z

which is, term for term, the $F$ that `comment-shift` quotes for the trefoil.
A `Programs` block of those two lines plus the shift by the lowest exponent of
$a$ gives a reader the next value, and makes the stored form reproducible from
the page alone.

### 3. The definition does not say what the shift is, and names Rolfsen without citing the reference the table declares for him

The definition reads:

> The Kauffman polynomial $F_K(a,z)$ [2] [1], shifted into ordinary polynomial
> form, for the unknot, every Rolfsen prime knot through ten crossings as
> KnotInfo draws it [3], and each distinct mirror image; the entries for $K$
> and $\bar K$ are values of the same invariant.

"Shifted into ordinary polynomial form" does not determine the shift: by the
highest exponent of $a$ rather than the lowest, or in $z$ as well, would each be
a different table, and two people building from this sentence would not agree.
The rule is in `comment-shift`, which the rendered page draws *below* Formulas,
well after the definition; the column header $a^{-m}F_K(a,z)$ is above the
fold but does not define $m$. The published Jones table states its
normalisation in the definition itself ("listed as $t^{-m}V_K(t)$ with nonzero
constant term"), which is the model to copy.

Separately, `Rolfsen` is the one reference in this table that is declared and
never cited -- the definition says "every Rolfsen prime knot" and
`comment-numbering` says "The numbering is Rolfsen's after Perko's correction
CITE{Perko}", so the book is named twice in prose and cited nowhere.
`audit_table` checks citations that resolve to nothing and not references that
nothing points at, so it says nothing here. The skill's rule is that a
reference is for citing, not for listing.

One rewrite fixes both, and "every Rolfsen prime knot" -- which reads as a kind
of knot rather than as a knot in a table -- at the same time:

> The Kauffman polynomial $F_K(a,z)$ CITE{Wiki} CITE{Kauffman}, listed as
> $a^{-m}F_K(a,z)$ with $m$ the lowest exponent of $a$, for the unknot, for
> every prime knot with at most ten crossings named as in the Rolfsen table
> CITE{Rolfsen} and drawn as KnotInfo draws it CITE{KnotInfo}, and for each
> distinct mirror image; the entries for $K$ and $\bar K$ are values of the
> same invariant.

### 4. Two pairs of rows are identical and nothing on the page says why

$9_{42}$ and $10_{71}$ each occupy two rows, one labelled $K$ and one $\bar K$,
carrying the same polynomial *and the same* $m$ -- for $9_{42}$, `$m=-2$` on
both. They are the only repeated values in the table (477 distinct values in
479 entries). A reader who meets them reads a duplication bug, because the page
has just told them, in `comment-chirality`, that an amphichiral knot has a
single entry, and neither of these knots is in the list of amphichiral ones.

The reason is a fact about the mathematics and worth a sentence: both knots are
chiral, but $F_K(a,z)=F_K(a^{-1},z)$ for each of them, so the Kauffman
polynomial does not see their chirality. The Jones table states exactly this
about its own six such knots. Smallest fix, appended to `comment-chirality`:

> The converse fails: $9_{42}$ and $10_{71}$ are chiral although
> $F_K(a,z)=F_K(a^{-1},z)$, so their two entries are equal.

(For the Jones polynomial six knots fail it and for the Kauffman polynomial
only these two, which is the sentence's own argument for being on this page
rather than inherited from the sibling.)

### 5. The comment defining $L$ leaves out the rule that introduces $a$

`comment-convention` says:

> The convention is KnotInfo's: $F_K(a,z)=a^{-w(D)}L(D)$, where $D$ is an
> unoriented diagram of $K$, $w(D)$ is its writhe after choosing an
> orientation, and $L$ is the regular-isotopy invariant with
> $L(D_+)+L(D_-)=z(L(D_0)+L(D_\infty))$ and $L(0_1)=1$.

Taken literally that determines a polynomial in $z$ alone: the skein relation
and the normalisation never produce an $a$, and the only $a$ in sight is the
$a^{-w(D)}$ out front. The missing axiom is the curl rule, which is where the
variable comes from. Smallest fix, inside the same sentence: "... and
$L(0_1)=1$, and with $L$ multiplied by $a$ or $a^{-1}$ when a positive or
negative curl is added to $D$".

A reader who already knows Kauffman's $L$ will not notice; a reader checking
their own value against the stated convention cannot get there.

### 6. The unknot's row is the only one of 479 with no comment, and a comment is what the page promised

`comment-shift` says "each entry's comment gives $m$", and the entry at
$n=0$, $k=1$ carries `equals: HREF{One}` and nothing else, so the row reads
"0 1 $K$: 1" with no note. The Jones table's unknot row carries both an
`equals` and a comment. Smallest fix: give it `$0_1$, the unknot; $m=0$`.

Small, but it is the first row of the table, which is the first thing on the
page.

## Noted only because I noticed

- **`Keywords: [Kauffman polynomial]`** repeats words already in the title,
  which the index weighs the same, so it reaches nothing new. Harmless; the
  HOMFLY-PT table's keyword ("HOMFLY polynomial", a name not in its title) is
  what the field is for.
- **No knot tag exists in the corpus at all.** `/tag/knot`, `/tag/topology`
  and `/tag/knot-theory` all answer 404, and the seven knot tables here carry
  only `polynomial` (five of them) or `volume`/`period`. T314 is tagged exactly
  as its four siblings, so this is not its fault, but it is now the seventh
  table that would carry such a tag, well past the skill's threshold of three,
  and a reader browsing sideways cannot reach any knot table by subject.
- **T314's KnotInfo link is the one that works.** It points at
  `https://knotinfo.org/`, which answers 200 from here; the four published
  siblings still point at `https://knotinfo.math.indiana.edu/`, which answers
  nothing at all. The siblings are what wants fixing, not this table.

## What I checked hardest, and what held

- **Rendering.** The SOCKS proxy on 1080 refuses every connection, and a direct
  `curl` reaches the site from this box, as `docs/agent-environment.md` records.
  `/T314` is a draft, so I read the document with the key
  (`/api/table?id=T314`, which serves `full_yaml`) and drew it through
  `/preview?table=` in sixteen pieces, each with a `Title` and at least one row,
  since a
  piece without either renders nothing or 500s (both already in
  `docs/agent-environment.md`). Every section drew: Numbers, Definition,
  Parameters, Comments, Formulas, Similar tables, Data properties, Display
  properties. Citations resolve to `[1]`, `[2]`, `[3]` in a piece carrying
  `Links` and `References`; the four `HREF`s and `HREF{One}` all name tables
  that exist and answer 200. The numbers block draws with the column headings
  $n$, $k$, knot, $a^{-m}F_K(a,z)$, the knot column showing `$K$:` and
  `$\bar K$:`; the longest entries, the 500-character ten-crossing ones, draw
  inside the value cell with their `$m=...$` comment beneath them.
- **TeX.** 94 distinct `$...$` fragments, typeset with `mathjax-full`
  (base + ams) in node: 0 errors. A control of the same run with a missing brace
  and an undefined macro reported 2.
- **The Jones specialisation, on every entry.** `formula-jones` claims
  $V_K(t)=F_K(-t^{-3/4},t^{1/4}+t^{-1/4})$ "in the convention used here". I
  substituted into each stored entry, restoring $F$ from the entry's own $m$,
  and compared with the published Jones table's entry for the same $n$, $k$ and
  mirror, restoring $V$ from *its* $m$: **479 of 479 agree exactly.** That one
  check ties together the shift convention, the sign of $m$, the mirror
  convention and the knot numbering across two tables.
- **The mirror bookkeeping.** For all 229 mirror pairs the stored $\bar K$
  entry is $a^dP(a^{-1},z)$ and its $m$ is $-m-d$, as `formula-mirror` says;
  every one of the 479 entries has lowest $a$-exponent 0, as `comment-shift`
  says; the 20 knots with no mirror row are exactly the 20 named in
  `comment-chirality`, and each of their polynomials is its own mirror.
  Row counts per crossing number are $1,1,1,2,3,7,21,49,165$, matching the
  parameter constraint and A002863, so `complete: yes` is earned.
- **"The right-handed trefoil", because I half-remembered the other
  convention.** SageMath 10.9 gives the closure of the positive braid
  $\sigma_1^3$ writhe $+3$ and $V=t+t^3-t^4$, KnotInfo's braid word for $3_1$
  is $[1,1,1]$, and its `kauffman_polynomial` is the $F$ the comment quotes. The
  comment's example is right and agrees with the stored entry; the value I
  remembered belongs to the opposite variable convention.
- **The audit.** `GET /api/table/T314/audit`, with and without
  `links=1&fetch=1`, returns one finding both times: that `knot` takes two
  names, `K` and `mirror`, each carrying entries, so the table could be two
  tables sharing a value column. It is right to ask and the answer here is the
  good kind -- $K$ and $\bar K$ are two knots, not two quantities, the value
  column is headed $a^{-m}F_K(a,z)$ rather than the word "value", and the
  definition already ends with the sentence the audit asks for. I would not
  split it. What the audit does not see is everything above: it does not read
  unknown Data-properties keys, so the `Size exception` narration passes it; it
  checks `CITE`s that resolve to nothing and not references nothing cites; and
  it has no notion of a missing `Programs` section or of two rows that are
  equal.
