## Findings, ranked

### 1. $\ell_{s,j}$ means a different polynomial in the table it links to (worth doing)

A reader sees `formula-integral`: "If $\ell_{s,j}$ is the Lagrange basis
polynomial for the nodes $0,1,\ldots,s-1$ … then
$\beta_{s,j}=\int_{s-1}^{s}\ell_{s,j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$". The first entry in
Similar tables is
[Lagrange basis polynomials for equally spaced nodes](https://numberdb.org/Lagrange_basis_polynomials_for_equally_spaced_nodes),
which defines $\ell_{d,i}$ for the nodes $0,1,\ldots,d$, so its first index is
the *degree*. The polynomial T305 calls $\ell_{s,j}$ is $\ell_{s-1,j}$ there.
A reader who follows the link to check $\beta_{3,0}=5/12$ looks up
$\ell_{3,0}$, integrates it over $[2,3]$ and gets $-3/8$, which is
$\beta_{4,0}$. Both tables are correct on their own terms, and together they
give a wrong answer. The Similar-tables relation ("integrals of these basis
polynomials over the next unit interval") does not warn about the shift
either.

Smallest fix: use the linked table's notation, and link it at the first
mention in Formulas:

> $\beta_{s,j}=\int_{s-1}^{s}\ell_{s-1,j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$, where
> $\ell_{s-1,j}$ is the HREF{Lagrange_basis_polynomials_for_equally_spaced_nodes}[Lagrange basis polynomial]
> of degree $s-1$ for the nodes $0,1,\ldots,s-1$.

In Similar tables, the relation could read "$\beta_{s,j}$ is the integral of
$\ell_{s-1,j}$ over $[s-1,s]$". The Newton–Cotes table already writes
$w_{n,j}=\int_0^n\ell_{n,j}$ with the degree as the index, so after this
change all three tables agree.

### 2. The Definition names the formula but not what determines it (worth doing)

The Definition says the coefficients are "the rational coefficients in the
explicit $s$-step formula $y_{n+s}=y_{n+s-1}+h\sum\beta_{s,j}f_{n+j}$". Read
alone, that only says where the $\beta$ appear. Any choice of $\beta$ fits
that form. What makes them *the* Adams–Bashforth coefficients is the
exactness condition, and that is in the second sentence of
`comment-notation` ("chosen so the formula is exact when $f(t,y(t))$ is a
polynomial in $t$ of degree at most $s-1$"). A reader who knows the name
does not need it. A reader who is checking whether their numbers are these
numbers does.

Smallest fix: move that clause into the Definition and delete it from the
comment:

> For $s\geq1$, the Adams–Bashforth coefficients $\beta_{s,j}$ are the
> rational numbers for which the explicit $s$-step formula
> $y_{n+s}=y_{n+s-1}+h\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\beta_{s,j}f_{n+j}$ is exact whenever
> $f(t,y(t))$ is a polynomial in $t$ of degree at most $s-1$ CITE{Wiki}.

`comment-notation` then keeps only the definition of $f_{n+j}$ and $t_{n+j}$.
That also removes its pointer "this is the moment condition in the Formulas
section", which sends the reader to count through the section.

### 3. "Nonzero" and "an absent row" describe rows that do not exist (worth doing, small)

`complete-note` renders as "complete: no (it holds every nonzero coefficient
with $1\leq s\leq12$)", and `comment-notation` ends "An absent row has
coefficient $0$." A reader then looks for the gaps. There are none. All
$\sum_{s\leq12}s=78$ coefficients are stored, and none is zero: in fact
$\beta_{s,j}$ has sign $(-1)^{s-1-j}$. Both phrases hint at a sparsity the
table does not have.

Smallest fix: write "it holds every coefficient with $1\leq s\leq 12$" and
delete the sentence about absent rows.

### 4. The completeness note does not say why the table stops at 12 (worth doing, small)

The skill asks for the reason for the range in `complete-note`, since that is
what a reader checks their own number against. Twelve is a good place to
stop: if this was the reason, it is the highest order of the Adams methods in
the standard variable-order codes (LSODE and VODE use a maximum Adams order
of 12). One clause says so: "…with $1\leq s\leq 12$, the highest order the
standard variable-order Adams codes use". If the reason was something else,
state that instead.

### 5. `comment-step`'s "when $h=1$" suggests $\beta$ depends on $h$ (note only)

"Each stored number is the coefficient multiplying one $f_{n+j}$ when
$h=1$." $\beta_{s,j}$ does not depend on $h$. The sentence means that the
coefficient of $f_{n+j}$ in the formula is $h\beta_{s,j}$, and saying that is
clearer: "The step size is factored out, so the coefficient of $f_{n+j}$ in
the formula is $h\beta_{s,j}$." The comment is optional, because the
Definition already shows the $h$.

### 6. Entry comments (note only)

- `"Euler method: $1$."` The $1$ repeats the value beside it. "Euler method"
  is also ambiguous, since the implicit (backward) Euler method is the
  one-step Adams–Moulton method. "The forward Euler method." is enough.
- The other comments write "Adams-Bashforth" with a hyphen, and the title and
  definition use an en dash. They are noun phrases rather than sentences
  ("4-step Adams-Bashforth formula, from newest to oldest: …"). That matches
  the Newton–Cotes table's "Trapezoidal rule: $\tfrac12(1,1)$.", so it is
  house style and not something a reader would stumble on. I note it only
  because I noticed it.
