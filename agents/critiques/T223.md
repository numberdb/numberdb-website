## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Kellerhals reference has a wrong arXiv number, a DOI that does not exist, and a missing author

What a reader sees, under References [2]: "Ruth Kellerhals, The minimal
growth rate of cocompact Coxeter groups in hyperbolic 3-space, Canad. J.
Math. 66 (2014), no. 2, 354-372. (arXiv) (doi)". The arXiv link is
`https://www.arxiv.org/abs/912.1384`, which answers 404. The doi link is
`https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-2012-037-8`, which also answers 404. The DOI
handle service reports that handle as not found (`responseCode 100`). A
control DOI, 10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.061102, resolves on the same service.

Why it is wrong: the reference is the only support for `comment-minimum`,
and both of its links are dead. Neither is only a typo. Crossref gives this
paper as **10.4153/CJM-2012-062-3**, by **Kellerhals and Kolpakov**, Canad.
J. Math. 66(2), 354–372, 2014. That handle resolves to Cambridge. The
arXiv number with its leading zero restored is 0912.1384, "Resonant
Coherent Phonon Generation in Single-Walled Carbon Nanotubes...". The arXiv
search for author "Kellerhals" lists no preprint of this paper. If the
audit's suggestion were followed, the link would work and go to the wrong
paper.

Smallest change:

    Kellerhals:
      bib: Ruth Kellerhals and Alexander Kolpakov, The minimal growth rate of
        cocompact Coxeter groups in hyperbolic 3-space, Canad. J. Math. 66
        (2014), no. 2, 354-372.
      doi: 10.4153/CJM-2012-062-3

Drop the `arxiv` field. Rename the key to `KellerhalsKolpakov` if you like.
Change the same lines in `table.yaml`. **Worth doing, first.**

### 2. $[5,3^1,1]$ renders as a different symbol, and $[(4,3)^2]$ reads as a square

What a reader sees: the row label is printed as plain text, "[5, 3^1, 1]",
and the comment typesets $[5,3^1,1]$. MathJax sets that as
`[5, 3¹, 1]`: a list of three items with a superscript 1 on the 3. The
standard symbol is $[5,3^{1,1}]$, where the superscript $1,1$ means that
two branches leave the node. The cited Wikipedia article writes it that
way (Coxeter notation, "$\overline{DH}_3$ [5,3<sup>1,1</sup>]"). Two more
rows are affected. The labels "[(4, 3)^2]" and "[(5, 3)^2]" typeset as
$[(4,3)^2]$, which is the exponent 2 on a parenthesis. The same source
writes these groups as $[(3,4,3,4)]$ and $[(3,5,3,5)]$, and the other
cyclic ones as $[(3,3,3,4)]$ and $[(3,3,3,5)]$, where this table has
$[(4,3,3,3)]$ and $[(5,3,3,3)]$.

Why it matters: the label is how a reader knows which group they have, and
a reader who knows the notation reads $[5,3^1,1]$ as a mistake. The page
never explains the notation either. `comment-addresses` says only "Entry
addresses use ASCII Coxeter bracket symbols and record the dimension
separately". "Entry addresses" and "ASCII" are this website's words, not
the reader's. That comment's second sentence repeats `Similar tables`.

Smallest change, while this is still a draft:

- In the generator's `DIAGRAMS`, change the keys to the cited source's
  cyclic forms, `[(3,4,3,4)]`, `[(3,5,3,5)]`, `[(3,3,3,4)]` and
  `[(3,3,3,5)]`. These read correctly as plain text and in TeX. For
  $\overline{DH}_3$, give the comment `$[5,3^{1,1}]$`. If the address may
  carry braces, use `[5,3^{1,1}]` for the key too.
- Replace `comment-addresses` with a sentence that teaches the notation:

      Rows are labelled by Coxeter symbol CITE{WikiCoxeter}: $[p,q,r]$ is the
      linear diagram with labels $p,q,r$, $[(p,q,r,s)]$ the cyclic one, and
      $[5,3^{1,1}]$ the diagram with a label $5$ followed by a branch into
      two edges labelled $3$.

  Title the parameter "Coxeter symbol" rather than "Coxeter diagram", since
  a bracket is not a diagram. This is the same point as finding 9 of the
  T222 critique.

**Worth doing.**

### 3. `Programs` computes nothing

What a reader sees: a Sage block that defines `qint` and
`finite_coxeter_growth`, and ends with the comment "# Use Steinberg's
formula with the finite special subgroups of the diagram." A reader who
wants one more value, for a paracompact tetrahedron or a simplex in
$\mathbb{H}^4$, gets two helpers and a to-do. The helpers need the degrees
of each finite subgroup, and nothing on the page supplies them.

Smallest change: a snippet that runs. The one below uses Sage's own
`CoxeterMatrix.is_finite` and `CoxeterGroup.degrees`. I ran it as written:
it gave $[3,5,3]$, $[5,3^{1,1}]$ and $[(3,5,3,5)]$ matching the stored
digits, and Lehmer's number for the triangle $\Delta(2,3,7)$.

    def growth_rate(M):
        # M: Coxeter matrix, e.g. [[1,3,2,2],[3,1,5,2],[2,5,1,3],[2,2,3,1]] for [3,5,3]
        t = polygen(QQ, 't')
        total = 0
        for T in Subsets(range(len(M))):
            T = sorted(T)
            C = CoxeterMatrix([[M[i][j] for j in T] for i in T]) if T else None
            if T and not C.is_finite():
                continue
            W_T = prod(sum(t^k for k in range(d)) for d in CoxeterGroup(C).degrees()) if T else 1
            total += (-1)^len(T) / W_T
        q = (1 / total(1/t)).denominator()
        return max(q.roots(AA, multiplicities=False)).n(digits=100)

**Worth doing.**

### 4. The page never says these are Salem numbers, or that $[3,5,3]$ is the smallest

What a reader sees: `Keywords` carries "Perron number", which is true but
weaker, and a reader never sees Keywords. `comment-minimum` says $[3,5,3]$
has the smallest growth rate. The $[3,5,3]$ row does not say so, and it is
the second row, below $[5,3,4]$.

Why it matters: somebody arriving with $1.3509803\ldots$ wants to know what
kind of number it is. The growth rate of every cocompact hyperbolic Coxeter
group in $\mathbb{H}^3$ is a Salem number, by Parry. The Kellerhals–Kolpakov
abstract opens with that sentence, and its main theorem is that $[3,5,3]$
is the unique minimum. I checked the Salem property on all nine minimal
polynomials (see above). This is the 3-dimensional analogue of Lehmer's
number at $\Delta(2,3,7)$ in T222, and the abstract says that too.

Smallest change: extend `comment-minimum`.

    Each value is a Salem number CITE{Kellerhals}. Among all cocompact
    hyperbolic Coxeter groups in $\mathbb{H}^3$, HREF{#3,[3,5,3]}[$[3,5,3]$]
    has the smallest growth rate, and it is the only group attaining it
    CITE{Kellerhals}, as HREF{Growth_rates_of_hyperbolic_Coxeter_triangle_groups#[7,3]}[Lehmer's number]
    is for cocompact triangle groups.

Check T222's key for $\Delta(2,3,7)$ before writing the second link. Also
check that `#3,[3,5,3]` resolves: an entry address separates parameters
with commas, and this key has commas inside it. If it does not resolve,
leave $[3,5,3]$ unlinked. Add
"Salem number" to Keywords. In the `[3,5,3]` entry comment, add "This is
the smallest growth rate of a cocompact Coxeter group in
$\mathbb{H}^3$". Ordering the rows by $\tau$, which puts $[3,5,3]$ first,
costs one `sorted` in `enumerate`. **Worth doing.**

### 5. `comment-range` describes the build, and repeats `complete-note`

What a reader sees: "The entries currently held are the nine compact
Coxeter tetrahedral groups in $\mathbb{H}^3$, the compact rank-$4$ list in
Humphreys [1]. Paracompact tetrahedral groups and higher-dimensional
simplex groups are not included yet." Under Data properties the reader then
reads "complete: no (it holds the nine compact Coxeter tetrahedral groups in
$\mathbb{H}^3$)".

Why it is wrong: "currently" and "not included yet" are about this
website's progress, not about the mathematics. The range has its own home,
and it already says the same thing. The reason for this range, that these
nine are the complete compact list, is the part a reader checking whether
their group belongs here needs, and it sits in the wrong field.

Smallest change: delete `comment-range`, and write the reason into the
note:

    complete-note: it holds all nine compact Coxeter tetrahedral groups in
      $\mathbb{H}^3$, which is the complete list of compact ones CITE{Humphreys},
      and none of the paracompact tetrahedra or the simplex groups in
      dimensions $4$ to $9$

**Worth doing.**

### 6. `formula-steinberg` uses $W_T$ and $W_T(t)$ without defining them

What a reader sees: "…gives
$1/W(t^{-1})=\sum_{T\subseteq S,\ W_T\text{ finite}}(-1)^{|T|}/W_T(t)$".
The Definition introduces $S$ and $W$, but nothing introduces $W_T$, the
subgroup generated by $T$, or $W_T(t)$, its growth polynomial. For a
compact tetrahedron, the condition "$W_T$ finite" means every proper
subset, and saying so is what makes the formula usable. (The formula
itself is right. I checked it by hand on the infinite dihedral group.)

Smallest change: append "where $W_T$ is the subgroup generated by
$T$ and $W_T(t)=\prod_i(1+t+\cdots+t^{d_i-1})$ its growth polynomial, $d_i$
the degrees of $W_T$". **Worth doing; small.**

### 7. The generating set is stated three times, and the third calls a different number a "convention"

What a reader sees: the Definition says "the Coxeter group generated by the
reflections in the facets … with the standard Coxeter generating set
$S$". Then `comment-generators` says "The generating set is the set of
reflections in the facets of the simplex. The orientation-preserving
subgroup has index $2$, and its growth rate with respect to its own
generators is a different convention."

Why it is wrong: the three phrases say one thing. This repetition is most
of what the audit's length rule caught. The rotation subgroup with its own
generators is a different group with a different generating set, so it
has a different number. A convention would be the same number written
another way. "Its own generators" also names no set.

Smallest change: open the Definition with "Let $S$ be the set of
reflections in the facets of a finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter simplex in
$\mathbb{H}^n$, and $W$ the Coxeter group they generate CITE{WikiCoxeter}."
Then cut `comment-generators` to "The growth rate of the index-$2$
rotation subgroup, with respect to its standard generators, is a different
number and is not held here." **Worth doing if the prose is being
touched.**

### 8. Similar tables says triangle groups are not simplex groups

What a reader sees: "Growth rates of hyperbolic Coxeter triangle groups —
the same convention for Coxeter reflection generators, in dimension $2$
rather than for simplex groups in dimensions $3$ and higher". A triangle
is the simplex of dimension 2, so "rather than for simplex groups" reads as
if it were not.

Smallest change: "the same growth rates for the simplex groups of
dimension $2$, the hyperbolic Coxeter triangles". **Worth doing; one
line.**
