## Worth doing

### 1. The link to T327 is dead, in all three published tables of this batch

`Similar tables` renders

> condition numbers for nonsingular members of these gallery families

as `<a class="HREF" href="T327">`, and `https://numberdb.org/T327` answers 404.

It is not a T331 fault to fix in isolation. T329 and T330 are published, carry

Smallest fix, and it is a decision for somebody who can publish: publish T327,
which makes all three findings disappear at once. Removing the entry from three

While the sentence is being looked at: "these gallery families" points rather
than names, and it is not accurate. The Hilbert matrix is MATLAB's `hilb` and
the Vandermonde matrix is `vander`; neither is a `gallery` matrix. "the test
matrix families listed here" costs the same.

### 2. The program on the page contradicts the table's own indexing, and the correction crashes

Comment (13) opens "Indices are $1$-based" and gives $A_{ij}=1/(i+j-1)$ for the
Hilbert matrix. The `Programs` block, four sections below, is

```python
A = matrix(QQ, 3, 3, lambda i, j: QQ(1) / QQ(i + j + 1))
A.det()
```

with no comment of any kind. It is correct — I ran it under `agents/sage.sh`
and it returns `1/2160`, which is the stored `hilbert,3` — but it is correct
for a reason the page never states: Sage's matrix constructor hands the lambda
0-based indices, so `1/(i+j+1)` is the page's `1/(i+j-1)`.

A reader who believes the page and substitutes its own formula does not get a
wrong answer, which would at least be visible. They get

    ZeroDivisionError: rational division by zero

because at `i = j = 0` the denominator is $-1+1$. I ran that too.

T330's `Programs` block is the same snippet with `charpoly()` in place of
`det()`, and it carries the line

    # Hilbert matrix, n = 3.

T331's copy lost it. Restoring that line and extending it is the whole fix:

    # Hilbert matrix, n = 3. Sage's lambda indexes from 0,
    # so 1/(i+j+1) is the A_ij = 1/(i+j-1) of comment (13).

Separately and much more weakly: the snippet covers one family of thirteen.
A reader holding a Parter determinant gets a Hilbert matrix. I would not
restructure the block for that — the comment above is what turns a trap back
into an example — but a second line building `gallery`'s Parter matrix would
cost two lines and serve the twelve other families by showing the shape.

### 3. $M(n)$ is never defined, and the two links that would define it are cited nowhere

Formula (12) ends

> ... $\det(A_n)=M(n)$ for the Redheffer matrix, and $\det(A_n)=n+1$ for the
> second-difference matrix.

$M$ appears nowhere else on the page. Comment (16) says "the Redheffer
determinants are the small-integer Mertens values", so the word is there, but
nothing ties the symbol to the word and the Mertens function is neither defined
nor linked. T330's critique raised this against T330, where the formula at
least says "where $M(n)$ is the Mertens function"; here the gloss is gone too,
so the symbol stands entirely alone. The corpus has no table of it — `text=Mertens` returns the Mertens
constants of primes in progressions and the Meissel-Mertens constant, which are
different objects — so the link has to be outward.

It already exists and is unused. The rendered `Links` list runs to eight
entries, and the in-text citation markers run `[1] [2] [3] [6] [7] [8]`:
**[4] Wikipedia: Pascal matrix and [5] Wikipedia: Redheffer matrix are cited
from nothing**, which a reader sees as two numbers missing from the sequence.
The skill's rule is that a reference is for citing and a bibliography nobody
points at is furniture.

One change fixes both. In (12), "$\det(A_n)=M(n)$, the Mertens function
CITE{WikiRedheffer}, for the Redheffer matrix"; in (16), "the Pascal
CITE{WikiPascal} ... matrices". The numbering then runs 1 to 8.

### 4. The half of `rigour details` a reader sees is the build's housekeeping

The field folds. What shows with the page closed is

> The generator builds each matrix over $\mathbb{Q}$ and computes its
> determinant exactly by fraction-free Bareiss elimination. Before the entries
> were written, `agents/table-build/dry_run.py` computed all 363 entries,
> checked exactness, and measured the longest written value as 968 characters
> at the Parter matrix with $n=30$, with an entries block of 58.8 KB.

and what is behind **more** is

> The determinants were checked against the closed forms and recurrences in the
> formulas, and every listed family with $n\leq6$ was also checked by a direct
> Leibniz determinant over permutations. The omitted small families were checked
> against (12). The relation to the characteristic polynomial table was checked
> against the stored T330 draft values on 138 overlapping rows.

The visible half names a file in a repository the reader has no access to, and
reports two measurements — 968 characters, 58.8 KB — that are facts about this
site's storage limits rather than about how the digits were obtained. The
hidden half is the evidence: three independent checks, one of them a Leibniz
determinant over permutations, which is exactly what a sceptical reader wants
to know was done. The field has it backwards.

The fold is `FOLD_ABOVE = 400` in `numberdb_app/prose.py`, cutting at the last
sentence break that fits, so "the first ~400 characters" is the whole of what
most readers read. Smallest fix: keep sentence one, drop the script name and
the two measurements, and promote the checks:

> The generator builds each matrix over $\mathbb{Q}$ and computes its
> determinant exactly by fraction-free Bareiss elimination. Every entry was
> checked against the closed forms and recurrences in the formulas, every
> family with $n\leq6$ against a direct Leibniz determinant over permutations,
> and formula (1) against HREF{T330} on the 138 rows the two tables share.

Note also "the stored T330 draft values": T330 is published, so the sentence
dates itself against the website's own state. Name the table, not its status.

### 5. The `family` parameter defines the family by what this run stored

The parameter line renders

> $A_n$ —   matrix family (one of the named matrix families listed in the
> comments whose determinant is stored as a row)

The relative clause makes the constraint circular: the admissible values are
the ones that are here. A parameter says what the family is indexed by; what
was computed belongs in `complete`/`complete-note`, which already carries it
("it holds the listed nontrivial determinant families for $2\leq n\leq30$").
The next person to add the Redheffer determinants would have to edit the
definition of the family to do it.

Smallest fix: `one of the named test matrix families defined in the comments`.

(T330's critique raised the "listed in the comments" half of this against its
own copy of the same line. The clause about rows is new here.)

### 6. Formula (5) is the one formula that is not a closed form, and leaves the substitution to the reader

> The Cauchy determinant identity
> $\det(1/(x_i+y_j))=\dfrac{\prod_{i<j}(x_j-x_i)(y_j-y_i)}{\prod_{i,j}(x_i+y_j)}$
> applies to the Cauchy and Parter matrices, and to the Ris matrix after the
> common factor $1/2$ is taken from each row.

Every other formula on the page hands a reader a number. This one hands them an
identity and three matrices, and leaves them to find $x_i$ and $y_j$ for each.
For the Cauchy matrix that is easy. For the Parter matrix, $A_{ij}=1/(i-j+1/2)$
needs $y_j=1/2-j$, and a reader matching `+y_j` against `-j` has to notice the
sign. For the Ris matrix, $A_{ij}=(1/2)/(n-i-j+3/2)$ needs $x_i=-i$ and
$y_j=n-j+3/2$, which depends on $n$ — the one substitution nobody guesses.

I checked all three against the stored entries for $2\leq n\leq30$: with those
values the identity reproduces every row.

Smallest fix: append them. "with $x_i=i$, $y_j=j$ for the Cauchy matrix;
$x_i=i$, $y_j=1/2-j$ for the Parter matrix; and $x_i=-i$, $y_j=n-j+3/2$ for
the Ris matrix."

### 7. Four tables about test matrices, all tagged `analysis`

`Tags` are `analysis`, `algebraic`. The corpus has fifty tags and not one of
them is about matrices; T327 is `analysis` alone, T329 is
`analysis, algebraic, eigenvalue`, T330 is those plus `polynomial`.

Nothing in this table is analysis: every value is an exact rational determinant
of a matrix with rational entries. The tag looks inherited from T327, where
condition numbers make it fit. And a reader browsing sideways from any one of
these four tables has no tag that leads to the other three.

This is the skill's own worked example — "six tables on percolation, the Ising
model, critical exponents, lattice entropy and self-avoiding walks were filed
under physics and combinatorics for want of this" — with four tables instead of
six. `matrix` (or `linear algebra`) would carry T327, T329, T330 and T331,
which is above the three-table threshold for proposing one, and it is the word
somebody browsing would look for.

Smallest fix: propose `matrix` and put it on all four. Dropping `analysis` from
T331 is a separate judgement and I would drop it.

### 8. Comment (16) ends with a remark about the website

> The Pascal, Minij, Frank and Moler matrices have determinant $1$ for every
> listed order; the default Chow matrices are singular for $n>1$; the Redheffer
> determinants are the small-integer Mertens values; and the second-difference
> determinants are $n+1$. **Those determinant facts are recorded in formulas
> rather than as rows, because a table of repeated or small integers would make
> search by number less useful.**

The bolded sentence is about how this site's search behaves, not about
determinants, and it reads as an apology for the table's own shape. The skill
puts that argument somewhere specific: the range you chose and why goes in the
completeness note, which is what a reader checking whether their number belongs
here actually reads.

It is also not a claim the table lives up to at the row level. Fourteen stored
entries are integers of absolute value at most ten — `grcar,2` is 2, `grcar,3`
is 4, `grcar,4` is 8, `riemann,2` and `vandermonde,2` are both 1, `fiedler,2`
and `clement,2` are both −1 — and one of them, 4, is stored three times. The
principle is a family-level one and the sentence states it as though it were
absolute.

Smallest fix: end (16) at "are $n+1$", and let `complete-note` carry the reason:
"... because a determinant that is 1, 0 or a Mertens value at every order is
not a number anybody arrives holding."

## Smaller, still worth a minute

### 9. `Similar tables` should name T329

T329, "Eigenvalues of the classical symmetric test matrices", is published, and
seven of its eleven families (Hilbert, Lehmer, Cauchy, Fiedler, Kac-Murdock-Szegő,
Wilkinson, Ris) are here. The determinant is the product of the eigenvalues —

Smallest fix: `HREF{T329}[Eigenvalues of the classical symmetric test matrices]`

### 10. $\rho$ arrives in formula (7) from nowhere

> For the Kac-Murdock-Szego matrix with $\rho=1/2$,
> $\det(A_n)=(1-\rho^2)^{n-1}=(3/4)^{n-1}$.

Comment (13) defines that matrix as $A_{ij}=2^{-|i-j|}$, with no $\rho$ in it,
so the formula's parameter is introduced by being given a value. A reader who
does not already know the family is $\rho^{|i-j|}$ cannot see where $1-\rho^2$
comes from.

Smallest fix, in (13): "The Kac-Murdock-Szego matrix is $A_{ij}=\rho^{|i-j|}$
with $\rho=1/2$, that is $A_{ij}=2^{-|i-j|}$."

### 11. A reader who spells Szegő correctly does not find this table

`Keywords` carry "Kac-Murdock-Szego matrix". The index has no `unaccent`, and
T330's `Keywords` carry both spellings. Checked live:

    text=Szego matrix  ->  T331, T329, T330
    text=Szegő matrix  ->  T329, T330

The correctly spelled query is the one that misses. The full hyphenated name
`Kac-Murdock-Szegő matrix` does still reach T331, because "Kac" and "Murdock"
are separate tokens and match on their own — which is why this is easy to test
for and conclude the wrong thing about. It is the bare surname that fails.

Smallest fix: add "Kac-Murdock-Szegő matrix" to `Keywords`.

### 12. "classical test matrix" is the title's central term and is never defined

The `Definition` reads "For a named classical test matrix $A_n$, this table
gives the determinant $\det(A_n)$ [1], where $n$ is the order of the matrix."
That is the right length and the right shape — thirteen matrix definitions
could not live there, and Comments (13) to (15) hold them in the order the
rows appear, which is good — but "classical test matrix" is the table's own
coinage, used by four tables now, and nothing on the page says what qualifies
a matrix for it. Eight words would: "one of the standard matrices used to test
numerical linear algebra software CITE{MathWorksGallery}".

The word "default" has the same shape of problem in (14), (16) and (12) — "the
default nonsymmetric tridiagonal matrix", "the default Chow matrices", "the
omitted default families" — where it means `gallery`'s default arguments and
never says so. One "with `gallery`'s default parameters" in (14) covers all
three.

- Formula (1) says "The polynomials $\chi_{A_n}(x)$ for these matrix families
  are in [T330]". True, and an understatement in both directions: T330 holds
  seven families more than this table and eighteen orders fewer (it stops at
  $n=12$). The reader who follows the link for $n=20$ finds nothing, and the
  reader who wants the omitted Pascal or Redheffer determinants could in fact
  get them from T330's constant terms — which is a consequence of (1) and (16)
  together that no sentence draws.
- `Keywords` carry six families with no rows (Pascal, Redheffer, Minij, Frank,
  Moler, second difference) and omit Chow and Mertens. Bringing a reader here
  on "Pascal matrix determinant" is arguably a service, since (12) answers
  them; it is just not applied evenly.
- The column headers read `$A_n$` | `$n$` | `$\det(A_n)$`. The first already
  carries the index whose column stands next to it. Harmless, and the value
  header is right, which is the one that matters.
- `complete-note`'s "making 363 entries" is a participle stapled to the end of
  a sentence that had finished. A full stop and "There are 363 entries." reads
  as English; nobody will be misled either way.

## What I checked hardest, and found sound

**Do the definitions on the page rebuild the numbers on the page?** Yes, all of
them. I implemented the thirteen matrices from Comments (13) to (15) alone —
1-based indices as stated, nothing taken from the generator — in plain Python
with `Fraction` and a Bareiss determinant, and compared against every stored
entry: **363 of 363 agree, no mismatches**. Every one of the thirteen prose
definitions is precise enough to rebuild its family, including the two that
lean on MATLAB notation (the Riemann matrix's $B(2:n+1,2:n+1)$ and the Clement
matrix's off-diagonals).

**Do the formulas hold?** The closed forms and recurrences in (2), (3), (4),
(6), (7), (8), (9), (10), (11) cover 334 of the 363 entries — the Riemann
family is the one with no formula — and **all 334 match**. Formula (1),
$\det(A_n)=(-1)^n\chi_{A_n}(0)$, holds on **all 138 rows** this table shares
with T330, which is the number `rigour details` claims. And formula (12)'s
seven claims about the omitted families — determinant 1 for Pascal, Minij,
Frank and Moler; 0 for Chow above $n=1$; $M(n)$ for Redheffer; $n+1$ for the
second-difference matrix — hold for **every $n$ from 2 to 30**, well past the
orders the table lists.

**Is it one table?** Yes, and of the good kind. `family` names what the number
is *of* — one determinant for each of thirteen named matrices — which is the
skill's first good answer, and the value column is headed `$\det(A_n)$`: one
symbol true of every row, not the word `value`. The audit does not ask this
(`MOST_LABELS = 4`, thirteen labels), so its silence is not an answer; the
column header is.

**Does the page render?** Yes. 235,805 bytes, all 363 rows present, all 363
entry anchors (`hilbert,2` through `riemann,30`) so any row is citable. The two
raw `<` on the page, in `$\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}$` and `$\prod_{i<j}$`, both
escape to `&lt;` and neither eats its section. All 66 TeX fragments typeset
under MathJax 3 with `\dfrac`, `\mid`, `\chi`, `\mathbb` and `\leq` intact; two
deliberately broken controls did error. The backticked `gallery("cauchy",n)`
renders as code. All eight outward links answer 200, including the GitHub
`gallery.m` source with the escaped spaces in its path — the audit does not
follow outward links, so I did.

**Entry order.** Families in the order Comments (13) to (15) define them,
orders ascending within each, Clement's odd orders absent because they are zero
and formula (10) says so. A reader scanning finds what they expect where they
expect it.
