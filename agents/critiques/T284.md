## The audit

The audit is clean, and I agree that nothing it checks is wrong. This is one
table, the header $\tau$ is true of every row, the links resolve, and no
family the corpus holds is named without a link. It cannot see findings 1 to
3 below. Each of those is about what a sentence claims, not about its form.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Five rows are Coxeter growth rates, and the rows do not say so. Worth doing.

A reader who computed the growth rate of the $(2,3,8)$ triangle group and
searches 1.2303914… lands on row `1,0,0,-1,0,-1`. The row reads "degree
$10$; minimal polynomial $x^{10} - x^{7} - x^{5} - x^{3} + 1$". Nothing on
the row connects it to what they computed. The same holds for Δ(2,3,9),
Δ(2,3,10) (also Δ(2,4,5)) and Δ(2,3,11). Lehmer's number is Δ(2,3,7), and
its row names Lehmer but not the triangle group. Search will also answer with
T222's rows, but this table's own rows should say what they are.

The Similar tables gloss is vague about the same fact: "several of its
finite-label entries are Salem numbers, including Lehmer's number". Floyd's
paper, which proves that fact, sits in `References` and nothing cites it.

Fix:
- On the five rows, add one sentence, for example "It is the growth rate of
  the Coxeter triangle group HREF{Growth_rates_of_hyperbolic_Coxeter_triangle_groups#[8,3]}[$\Delta(2,3,8)$]."
  On row `1,0,0,-1,-1`, name both Δ(2,3,10) and Δ(2,4,5). The T222 keys
  are `[7,3]`, `[8,3]`, `[9,3]`, `[10,3]`, `[5,4]` and `[11,3]`. An anchor
  holding `[` and `,` may confuse the `HREF{...}[caption]` parser, so preview
  one before writing all six. If it breaks, link the table and name the group
  in plain text.
- Gloss: "its growth rates for $\Delta(2,3,r)$ with $7\leq r\leq11$ and for
  $\Delta(2,4,5)$ are Salem numbers held here, as every finite-label
  triangle's growth rate is a Salem number CITE{Floyd}". This also gives
  Floyd a citation. If citations do not render in Similar tables, drop the
  reference instead.

### 2. The completeness evidence is in the wrong field and is vaguer than the source. Worth doing.

The page reads: "Table is complete: no (it holds Mossinghoff's 47 known Salem
numbers below $1.3$; the source certifies completeness for degree at most
$44$, and the list also includes one known degree-$46$ member)". The rigour
note ends: "Sac-Epee's later list below $49/37$ gives independent evidence
for the same small-Salem-number data".

This is the question a reader holding a number near 1.29 most needs
answered: is my number missing, or is it not a Salem number? The page
answers it badly in two ways.

- "The source certifies" refers to a web page. Mossinghoff's page claims
  completeness through degree 44, and Mossinghoff, Rhin and Wu, *Minimal
  Mahler measures*, Experiment. Math. 17 (2008) 451–458, proves it (Sac-Épée
  §1.3, which also credits degree 40 to Flammang, Grandcolas and Rhin, 1999).
- Sac-Épée's actual finding is sharper than "independent evidence for the
  same data". An integer-programming search by random sampling rediscovered
  all 47, many times each, and found no other Salem number below 1.3 (§3.2
  and §4). That result is about coverage, not about how the digits were
  obtained, so it belongs in the completeness note.

Fix. Set `complete-note` to "it holds the 47 known Salem numbers below $1.3$.
The list is proved complete for degree at most $44$ CITE{MRW}. The one
degree-$46$ entry lies outside that range, and a later search that
rediscovered all 47 found no others CITE{SacEpee}". Add MRW to `References`.
In `rigour details`, remove the Sac-Épée sentence, or cut it to the
truncation check.

### 3. "Mossinghoff marks this row as a recent discovery" is about a web page, is stale, and is missing on one row. Worth doing.

Rows 39, 43 and 46 carry this comment. "Marks this row" describes the
formatting of another website. "Recent" is relative to a page last modified
in 2009, and those numbers go back to Mossinghoff's 1998 *Math. Comp.* paper
or earlier. The source puts the same asterisk on row 40, the degree-46
number, and that row's comment does not mention it. A reader cannot tell
what distinguishes the three rows.

Fix: say the mathematical or historical fact. For example, "One of four small
Salem numbers found by Mossinghoff CITE{Mossinghoff1998}, after Boyd's
lists." Put it on all four rows, or remove it from all three. Check the
attribution against Mossinghoff (1998) and Boyd (1977, 1978) before writing
it. Sac-Épée's §1.3 dates the four to 1993 and cites the 1998 paper.

### 4. The Definition admits quadratic units, and its second sentence is the range. Worth doing, small.

"A Salem number is a real algebraic integer $\tau>1$ such that $\tau^{-1}$
is also a conjugate of $\tau$ and every other conjugate has absolute value
$1$". For $\tau=2+\sqrt3$, $\tau^{-1}$ is its only other conjugate, so
"every other conjugate" holds vacuously and the sentence makes $2+\sqrt3$ a
Salem number. The standard definitions require at least one conjugate on the
unit circle, or equivalently degree at least 4 (Sac-Épée, Definition 1.3).
No quadratic unit lies below 1.3, so no entry is affected, but the Definition
is wrong as written.

"Listed is each Salem number $\tau<1.3$ in Mossinghoff's small-Salem-number
list" defines membership by a website. The title already gives the range,
and `complete-note` says which ones are here. "Small" is also never defined.

Fix: "A Salem number is a real algebraic integer $\tau>1$ whose conjugates
other than $\tau$ all have absolute value at most $1$, at least one of them
exactly $1$ CITE{Wiki}; then $\tau^{-1}$ is a conjugate and all the others lie
on the unit circle. Listed are the Salem numbers less than $1.3$, called
small." The Mossinghoff citation moves to `complete-note`.

### 5. The coefficient convention is stated three times, and one statement is on the wrong field. Small.

The parameter title is "first half of coefficients of the minimal
polynomial". It needs an article, and $d/2+1$ of $d+1$ coefficients is not a
half. Its constraint, `comment-addresses` and `formula-reciprocal` each
explain the same convention. The formula begins "If the parameter gives
$a_0,\ldots$", which refers to the parameter instead of naming the
quantity. Separately, `comment-source-digits` ("Mossinghoff's list prints
decimal expansions truncated to $30$ places, so the entries here are
recomputed…") describes how the digits were obtained, and `rigour details`
already says that.

Fix:
- Parameter title: "leading coefficients of the minimal polynomial".
- Constraint: "the coefficients $a_0,\ldots,a_{d/2}$ of $x^d,\ldots,x^{d/2}$
  in the minimal polynomial of a Salem number of degree $d$".
- Formula: "The minimal polynomial is $a_0x^d+\cdots+a_{d/2}x^{d/2}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0$."
- Delete `comment-addresses` and `comment-source-digits`. The rigour note
  already covers the 30-place truncation.

### 6. The T223 gloss says "other" and leaves out that none of its values is below 1.3. Small.

"holds other Coxeter-group growth rates that are Salem numbers". "Other" has
nothing on this page to refer to, and a reader may expect some of T223's
values here. T223 holds nine values, and the smallest is $[3,5,3]$ at
1.3509…

Fix: "growth rates of the compact hyperbolic Coxeter tetrahedra, all Salem
numbers and all greater than $1.3$".

## Noted only

- **Entry comments are fragments.** "degree $10$; minimal polynomial …; this
  is Lehmer's number." T222 writes the same information as sentences: "The
  minimal polynomial of $\tau$ is … This is Lehmer's number." It reads well
  enough in a row. If the generator is edited for findings 1 and 3, change
  it to sentences at the same time.
- **Keywords "algebraic entropy" and tag "dynamical systems".** Nothing in
  the prose explains either. The link I have in mind is McMullen's result
  that the smallest entropy of an automorphism of a projective K3 surface is
  $\log$ of Lehmer's number. I have not checked it. With a sentence and a
  citation, both stay. Without one, drop them.
- **"Lehmer problem" is a keyword, and the prose never names it.**
  `comment-mahler` states the fact behind Lehmer's problem without the name.
  Writing "Lehmer's problem asks whether…" would give the keyword a sentence
  to match. The same comment moves from the logarithmic Mahler measure
  ($\log\tau$) to the Mahler measure ($\tau$) between its two sentences.
  "The Mahler measure of its minimal polynomial is $\tau$" would need no
  switch.
- **The author's name has lost its accents.** "Sac-Epee" appears in the bib
  and the rigour note, while the paper is by Sac-Épée. The bib year 2025 is
  v3, and v1 is from September 2024.
- **T282 and T283 are drafts.** The glosses link them by T-number. If this
  table is published first, those two links lead to a 404 for readers. Each
  gloss also describes its table only by contrast ("rather than Mahler
  measures of Salem minimal polynomials"), which is thin but true.

## What reads well

It is one table with one key, and the value header $\tau$ is true of every
row. Rows are sorted by value, so Lehmer's number, the one most readers
arrive with, is the first row on the first screen, with its polynomial and
name. Every row spells out its full minimal polynomial, so a reader does not
have to rebuild it from the key. The values agree with Mossinghoff's list and
with T222's growth rates. The Sage program runs as printed and works for any
row once `half` is changed. The page renders with no TeX or parsing errors.
