# Critique of T278, "Good examples of Hall's conjecture"

Read on 2026-09-16. The table is a private draft owned by zeta3.

## How it was read

- **Document:** `GET /api/table?id=T278` with the key (200). It has 54
  entries. Each $x$ has four parts, `x`, `y`, `k` and `r`.
- **Audit:** `GET /api/table/T278/audit` returned one finding, `clean: false`:
  "parameter part takes 4 names (k, r, x, y) ... reads as 4 tables sharing
  one title and one value column". I did not run `manage.py audit_table`,
  because this runner has no Django.
- **Rendering:** the SOCKS proxy served `/skill` once and then refused every
  connection. Nothing was listening on 1080, the same failure the T277
  critique recorded. numberdb.org answered directly without the proxy. `/T278`
  is 404 without a session, so I rendered the draft through `/preview?table=`
  in pieces. The whole document is 4.5 KB of request line, and the limit is
  4094 bytes. I sent each prose section on its own with one entry, then all 54
  entries in seven pieces of eight. Every piece returned 200. None contained
  `Math input error`, `HREF-broken` or `CITE-broken`. I read the HTML rather
  than a text dump, and that is how I found the first two findings below.
- **Source:** I compared the entries with the Wikipedia list the table
  transcribes. It has 54 entries, and it says the list "is known to contain all
  examples with $x\le10^{29}$ (the first 44 entries)". The table has 44 entries
  with $x\le10^{29}$ and 10 beyond, so the counts agree. I checked the
  non-primitive row by hand: $4x$, $8y$ and $64k$ applied to the Elkies
  example give its $x$, $y$ and $k$, and $r$ divides by 32.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The formula is printed as a Python dictionary. Worth doing.

A reader sees this under Formulas:

    (1) {'formula': '$r=\\sqrt{x}/|y^2-x^3|$', 'description': 'The Hall ratio measures how small $|y^2-x^3|$ is compared with $\\sqrt{x}$.'}

That line includes the braces, the quotes and doubled backslashes, so MathJax
cannot set it. `Formulas` is a labelled-text section
(`numberdb_app/sections_form.py`), which means a label maps to a string. The
table stores a record with `formula` and `description` keys, and the page
prints that record's `repr`. Nothing in the stored YAML looks wrong. The fault
only shows on the page.

**Fix:** `hall-ratio: $r=\sqrt{x}/|y^2-x^3|$`. Formulas has no place for the
description, and it adds little to the definition, so drop it. Since the
definition already says $r=\sqrt{x}/|k|$, the entry could also be dropped
altogether.

### 2. The Elkies link title contains raw `<`, which breaks the link. Worth doing.

The link title is `List of integers x,y with x<10^18 and 0<|x^3-y^2|<sqrt(x)`,
and it reaches the HTML unescaped:

    <a href="...hall.html">Noam D. Elkies: List of integers x,y with x<10^18 and 0<|x^3-y^2|<sqrt(x)</a>

A `<` followed by a letter starts a tag. The browser therefore reads
`<sqrt(x)</a>` as an element called `sqrt(x)`. The words "sqrt(x)" disappear
from the title, and the `</a>` is swallowed, so the link never closes where it
should. This is the same fault as the "`<` in mathematics ate the rest of a
section" case, in a field the audit does not look at. (`rigour details` is
escaped correctly: its `$x<10^{18}$` reaches the page as `&lt;`.)

**Fix:** give the link a title with no `<`, for example
`Noam D. Elkies: integers x, y with x below 10^18 and |x^3 - y^2| below sqrt(x)`,
or simply `Noam D. Elkies: Hall's conjecture examples`. Separately, the site
does not escape Links titles. That is a site bug, and a task that may write to
`docs/agent-environment.md` should record it there. This run was told to
change nothing but this report.

### 3. The completeness note makes coverage a fact about Wikipedia. Worth doing.

The page reads: "Table is complete: no (it holds every example with
$x\leq10^{29}$ in [1], together with the ten listed examples beyond that
bound)".

A reader can take this two ways: every example up to $10^{29}$, or every
example that Wikipedia happens to list. Only the first is worth saying, and it
is true, because the list is complete up to that bound. The note never states
that. It also never says whose search proves it. On Wikipedia that search is
footnote "e", Aanderaa, Kristensen and Ruud. The table has no References
section, so there is nothing to cite it with. "The ten listed examples" points
at a list the reader cannot see.

**Fix:** "it holds every $x\leq10^{29}$ with $r>1$, a range in which
CITE{AKR} showed the list complete, together with the ten larger examples
known". Add that search, with its DOI, as a reference. I have not checked
the bibliographic details, only Wikipedia's attribution.

The definition has a smaller version of the same problem. "This table holds
the cases of Hall's conjecture with $k\ne0$ and $r>1$" is loose, because a
conjecture has no cases. "the positive integers $x$ with $k\neq0$ and $r>1$,
the good examples of Hall's conjecture CITE{WikiHall}" says the same thing
precisely. `$k\ne0$` is already required for $r$ to be defined, but keeping it
does no harm.

### 4. Two entry comments talk about the source rather than the mathematics. Worth doing, small.

- On $x=5853886516781223$: "This row has the largest Hall ratio in the source
  list." "Row" and "source list" are words about the website. The comment also
  sits on the `x` part, although it is about $r$. **Fix:** move it to `r` as
  "The largest Hall ratio known, found by Elkies in 1998." If the note should
  claim only what is proven, write "the largest with $x\leq10^{29}$" instead.
- On $x=23415546067124892$: "obtained from the Elkies row
  $x=5853886516781223$ by multiplying ...". **Fix:** "from the example
  $x=5853886516781223$ by multiplying $x$, $y$ and $k$ by $4$, $8$ and $64$".

### 5. Small integers are stored as searchable values. Worth considering.

The first block stores `x: 2`, `y: 3` and `k: 1`. Further down, $k$ takes
values such as 17, 24, 225, 297 and $-207$. The skill warns that values which
are a handful of small integers "match everything and tell nobody anything".
The large $x$ and $y$ are distinctive and belong as entries, and so does $r$.
The small $k$ are the weak case. Somebody searching for 17 will get this table
among many others.

Removing the `k` part everywhere would lose the Mordell-curve reading, which
the comment makes something of. So I note this for whoever reviews the table.
I do not think it requires a change.

### 6. The audit finding: I disagree that the table needs splitting. No change needed.

The four parts are not four named quantities. They are one integral point
$(x,y)$ on $E_k$, together with its ratio. None of them means anything without
the others, and the definition already says "recording the four parts $x$, $y$,
$k$ and $r$ for each $x$". This is the "parts of one object" kind the audit
allows. The value-column header "$x$, $y$, $k$, or $r$" is honest about it.

One cost of the layout is visible on the page. Each block prints the parameter
$x$ in all four rows and then gives `$x$:` with the same number as a value, so
a 34-digit $x$ appears five times. That is the price of making $x$ searchable,
and I would accept it.

### Noted only

- **Similar tables.** "stores the refinement of $abc$ quality by merit in the
  same circle of conjectures" and "another form related to $abc$" are vague.
  Neither says what the relation is. A relation should say how merit refines
  quality, or how Szpiro's conjecture relates to $abc$, taking the wording from
  those tables' own definitions.
- **The parameter title** "integer in the Hall example" relies on the word
  "example", which the page has not yet defined. Plain `$x$` or "the integer
  whose cube is nearly a square" would read better.
- **The Mordell curve link.** The first mention of "Mordell curve" links to
  *First known Mordell curves of given rank*. That is the nearest table the
  corpus has, so the link is fine.
- **Entry type.** The page says "Entries are of type: real number" above
  columns where three of the four parts are exact integers. That comes from the
  site, and the table cannot fix it.

## What reads well

The sign convention is stated and reconciled with Elkies'. The rigour details
say exactly what was checked by integer arithmetic and what was computed in
balls. A reader holding 46.6004... or 1.41421... lands in the right place. The
first screen shows $x=2$, $y=3$, $k=1$, $r=\sqrt2$, which is the smallest
possible illustration. Findings 1 to 3 are the ones to do. Findings 1 and 2
cannot be seen in the stored YAML and were found only by reading the rendered
HTML.
