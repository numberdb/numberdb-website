## The audit

    parameter unit takes 2 names (bits, nats), each carrying entries of its
    own, so this reads as 2 tables sharing one title and one value column.

**Right in form, wrong here, and the table already answers it.** Nats and bits
are one number in two conventions, invertible by a constant in both
directions, which the skill names as the good case, and the audit's own escape
clause is "say so in the definition" — the definition says it ("nats use
natural logarithms, and bits divide by $\log 2$"). The other tell is clean
too: the value column is headed `$H(X)$`, not the word `value`, because one
symbol is true of every row. Nothing to do.

Worth saying what the audit did *not* look at. Its rule stands down above four
labels (`MOST_LABELS = 4`, `audit_table.py:504`), so the parameter with ten
names — `distribution` — was never examined. That silence is not a verdict,
and the verdict is that it is also fine, for a different reason: `distribution`
names *what the number is of*, one entropy for each of ten named families,
which is the skill's own blessed shape. One table, on both counts.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Two of the ten distribution labels do not let a reader read the shape tuple

A row of the negative binomial block renders as

    negative binomial counting failures before $r$ successes | 9, 1/10 | nats: 4.729…

The label names $r$ and never names the second component. A reader holding
4.7292 can see that 9 is the number of successes and has to guess that 1/10
is a success probability — it is not a fact the page states anywhere, in the
label, in the parameter, or in comment (7), which also names only $r$.

A Zipf row renders as

    Zipfian on $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with exponent $s$ | 3, 100 | nats: 0.677…

and here the label names $N$ first while the tuple is $(s, N)$. I confirmed
the order by recomputing: the row `2, 10` is $s=2$, $N=10$
(1.236292605723536), not $N=2$, $s=10$. A reader can only settle it by doing
that computation, which is the wrong way round.

The other eight labels get this right — `Binom(n,p)` against `2, 1/2`,
"population $N$, successes $K$, and draws $n$" against `10, 2, 5` — and so
does the whole of T241, where every multi-parameter label names its symbols in
the tuple's order (`Beta$(a,b)$`, `$F(n_1,n_2)$`). T339 breaks its own
convention twice.

**Smallest fix**, in `Parameters.distribution.values`: write the negative
binomial as "negative binomial $\operatorname{NB}(r,p)$, the number of
failures before the $r$th success", and the Zipf label as "Zipfian with
exponent $s$ on $\{1,\ldots,N\}$". Two strings, no values touched.

### 2. Six hypergeometric rows repeat a value already in the table, with nothing saying why

Twelve of the 469 entries are exact repeats of another entry of the same
table, all hypergeometric, in five groups:

    (10,2,2) = (10,2,8)
    (10,2,5) = (10,5,2) = (10,5,8)
    (20,2,5) = (20,5,2)
    (20,2,10) = (20,10,2)
    (20,5,10) = (20,10,5)

These are not coincidences and not errors: the hypergeometric law is symmetric
in $K$ and $n$, and replacing $n$ by $N-n$ maps the count of successes in the
sample to $K$ minus the count in its complement, which is a bijection and
leaves the entropy alone. The rows are worth keeping — a reader holding
$(20,10,5)$ must find it — but as the page stands a reader meets the same
hundred digits twice with no explanation, and the section that would carry it
is already there: `Formulas` states the Bernoulli and binomial $p \leftrightarrow 1-p$
symmetries, which are the two that are *not* visible in the rows, because the
table only holds $p \leq 1/2$.

**Smallest fix:** one formula,
`$H(N,K,n)=H(N,n,K)=H(N,K,N-n)$`. It explains all six rows at once. T241 does
exactly this for its own repeats, in `omitted-integers` and `lognormal`.

### 3. `Links` carries the issue that asked for the table

`RequestedIssue55`, "Requested in numberdb-data#55", renders as `[3]` in
`Links` and is cited from nowhere in the prose. The issue is titled "Entropy
of interesting probability spaces", is closed, and has an empty body: there is
no derivation, convention or source in it that a reader of the mathematics
needs. The skill is explicit that the table carries no trace of the request,
and none of T334–T338 carries such a link.

**Smallest fix:** delete the `RequestedIssue55` entry. Answer the issue in the
issue.

### 4. The Bernoulli grid stops at $p = 0.11$ and nothing says why

The Bernoulli rows are every reduced $p \leq 1/2$ with denominator at most 12
— I checked all 19 of those are present — together with the eleven two-decimal
values $0.01, 0.02, \ldots, 0.11$. So $p = 0.1$ and $p = 0.11$ are here and
$p = 0.12$ through $p = 0.49$ are not, except where they happen to be a small
fraction. A reader holding $H_2(0.35)$ or $H_2(0.15)$ finds nothing, and the
`complete-note` states the boundary without giving it a reason. The same
unexplained 0.11 turns up again as the `11/100` in the geometric and
logarithmic series sets, where it reads as a stray.

The skill's own precedent is the error-function tables, which hold every
argument of two decimal places in their range because that is what a
calculation hands back. Every two-decimal $p \leq 1/2$ would add about 80
entries to 469, on a document of 62 KB — there is a lot of room before the
160 KB the skill aims at.

**Smallest fix:** extend the Bernoulli rows to every two-decimal $p \leq 1/2$
(and drop `11/100` from the other families, or say what it is for); failing
that, put the reason for stopping at 0.11 into `complete-note`, since that
sentence is the one a reader checking whether their own number belongs here
actually reads.

### 5. The definition carries a conversion rule that Formula (1) already states

> This table gives $H(X)$ for named discrete distributions in SciPy standard
> forms; nats use natural logarithms, and bits divide by $\log 2$.

Two things. As prose, "bits divide by $\log 2$" says that a unit performs a
division; what is meant is that the bit values are the nats values divided by
$\log 2$. And that is Formula (1), `$H_{\mathrm{bits}}(X)=H_{\mathrm{nats}}(X)/\log 2$`,
word for word, in the section whose job it is. T241's sentence is the model:
"in their standard forms, in nats and in bits."

**Smallest fix:** end the definition at "…in SciPy standard forms, in nats and
in bits." Keep the units named there — that clause is what answers the audit's
one finding — and let the conversion live in `Formulas` alone.

### 6. Comment (8) says which rows are missing but not where their values went

> Rows whose bit value is an exact rational number are omitted: the
> Bernoulli$(1/2)$ row in bits, …

The claim is accurate — I checked, those seven are the only rational bit
values in the range — and omitting 1, 3/2, 2, 3, 4 is the right call under the
skill's rule about small integers matching everything. But the values are not
lost: each sits in the comment on the corresponding nats row, and comment (8)
does not say so, so a reader looking for the fair coin's one bit reads the
comment that mentions it and stops.

The per-row comments have the mirror-image problem: "The omitted bits row is
exactly $1$" points at a row that is not there, rather than stating the fact.

**Smallest fix:** add a clause to (8), "…are omitted; each is named in the
comment on the same distribution's nats row", and rewrite the seven entry
comments as "In bits this entropy is exactly $1$."

### 7. `complete: false` where the corpus writes `no`

The page renders **"Table is complete: false (it holds …)"**. The seven other
tables I checked (T288, T316, T321, T332, T335, T337, T338) write `yes` or `no`, and
`yes`/`no`/`unknown` is the closed vocabulary the metadata form offers
(`metadata_form.py:45`); anything else is printed verbatim and flagged by the
form as an answer it cannot show. T241 says `false` too, so this is inherited
rather than invented here, and the fix is one word in each table. The audit
has no rule for it.

### 8. The `shape` constraint describes a typology instead of the order

> otherwise it is the displayed integer, rational, half-integer or
> comma-separated tuple named by the distribution

A half-integer is a rational, so the list is not a classification of anything,
and "named by the distribution" points at the label rather than telling a
reader the one thing they need, which is that the components come in the order
the label names them. Mostly cured by finding 1; if it is touched, "otherwise
it is the distribution's parameters, in the order its label names them,
separated by commas" says all of it. (T241 carries almost the same sentence,
minus "rational".)

### 9. Three tables would now carry an entropy tag

T339, T241 and T153 are the three, which is exactly the threshold the skill
sets for proposing a tag, and today they share none: T339 and T241 have
`probability theory` and `special values`, T153 has `statistical mechanics`,
`combinatorics`, `physics`. `information theory` sits in the keywords of both
entropy tables instead, where it earns a search hit but does not let anybody
browse sideways. Worth proposing with the batch rather than deciding here.

## What I checked hardest, and what came back clean

*The numbers follow the conventions the table claims.* This is where a table
of named distributions goes quietly wrong, because SciPy's geometric is on
$\{1,2,\ldots\}$, its negative binomial counts failures rather than trials,
and its logarithmic series parameter is not a success probability. I wrote my
own entropy for one row of each of the ten families, from the definitions in
the labels and comment (7), and compared: Bernoulli 1/3, Binom(20,1/10),
geometric 1/5, negative binomial $(9,1/10)$, Poisson 3, uniform 30,
hypergeometric $(20,5,10)$, logarithmic series 1/2, Zipf $(1,100)$, Benford.
Every one agreed to the last float digit, worst case $10^{-14}$. The three
convention traps are all on the right side.

*The two units are consistent.* All 231 bits rows are the corresponding nats
row divided by $\log 2$ to within $10^{-12}$ relative; all 469 values carry
exactly 100 digits; none is a bare integer or a degenerate decimal.

*The `complete-note` is true of the rows.* I enumerated it clause by clause:
19 Bernoulli fractions with denominator $\leq 12$ plus 11 decimals is 33 rows,
binomial 19×4 = 76, geometric 6, negative binomial 9×4 = 36, Poisson 30,
uniform 29, hypergeometric 15, logarithmic 6, Zipf 6, Benford 1 — 238 pairs,
doubled for the units, less the seven omitted bit rows, is 469. That is the
entry count.

*The page renders whole.* Every `$` is paired, every control sequence is one
of `\sum \log \operatorname \ldots \lambda \mathrm \frac \leq \in \times
\cdot`, there is no `<` or `>` anywhere in the document, no `--`, no `CITE{`
or `HREF{` leaking through, no "Math input error", no `argument ()`, no
`href="None"`. Both `HREF{}` slugs in `Similar tables` answer 200 on the live
site, and all three `Links` URLs resolve. There is no `param-latex`, so the
`shape` column reads `9, 1/10` rather than the distribution's name once per
row.

*The `Programs` snippet runs, and reproduces a stored row.* Pasted into Sage
unchanged it gives a ball of radius $3.7\cdot10^{-129}$ whose midpoint differs
from the stored Bernoulli 1/3 nats value by $6.7\cdot10^{-102}$ — inside the
last place the hundred digits claim. (`overlaps` is the wrong test here and
answers False; see the lesson filed with this run.)

*The order a reader meets.* Bernoulli 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 at the top, Poisson from
$\lambda = 1/2$, uniform from $n = 2$: the small and common cases lead every
block, which is what the skill asks and what the rows a reader arrives holding
need.
