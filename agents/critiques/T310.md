# T310: Class polynomials of $\gamma_2=\sqrt[3]{j}$

Read on 2026-09-17, against the skill fetched that day from
<https://numberdb.org/skill> (47,534 bytes). T310 is a draft with 100 `Z[]`
entries, one for each discriminant $\Delta<0$ with $\Delta\equiv0,1\pmod4$,
$3\nmid\Delta$ and $|\Delta|\leq300$. It has `rigour: exact` and
`complete: no`.

## How it was read

- **Rendering.** The SOCKS proxy on 1080 was not listening; a direct `curl`
  reaches the site from this box, as `docs/agent-environment.md` records.
  `/T310` answers 404, which is what a draft does. I fetched the document with
  the key (`/api/table?id=T310`) and rendered it through `/preview?table=` in
  pieces under the 4094-byte request line, each piece carrying `Title`,
  `Parameters` and one entry. Every piece returned 200 and drew its section:
  Definition, Comments with Links and References, Formulas, Similar tables,
  Data properties, Programs. **The page renders.** In particular the `<` in
  `$\Delta<0$`, which occurs in the Definition, in the parameter constraint
  and in the completeness note, reaches the page as `&lt;` and eats nothing
  after it; the parameter line reads
  "$\Delta$ — discriminant ($\Delta<0$, $\Delta\equiv0,1 \pmod 4$, and
  $3\nmid\Delta$)" with no empty parentheses; the rigour note folds after its
  first sentence and its `polclass(Delta, 5)` renders as code; the three
  `CITE` and the four `HREF` all resolve, and there is no `CITE-broken` or
  `HREF-broken` span. All four Similar-tables slugs answer 200. All four tags
  exist and are populated (number theory 92 tables, polynomial 80, elliptic
  curves 41, modular function 13).
- **Audit.** `GET /api/table/T310/audit` returns `"findings": [], "clean":
  true`. I agree that nothing it looks for is wrong here. Two of my findings
  are in places it does not look: its prose checks read `Definition`,
  `Comments`, `Formulas`, `rigour details`, `complete-note` and the entry
  comments, but `Similar tables` is a list rather than a string or a mapping
  of strings, so no relation is ever read; and "the other factor" is not among
  the phrases in `POSITIONAL`, which has "the first factor" and "the second
  factor".
- **Checked, because a claim depended on it.** The Definition says the monic
  degree-$h(\Delta)$ factor is *unique*, and PARI's own manual, which this
  table cites, warns that `polclass(D, 5)` "can be the minimal polynomial of
  any of $\gamma_2(\tau)$, $\zeta_3\gamma_2(\tau)$ or $\zeta_3^2\gamma_2(\tau)$".
  So I factored $H_\Delta(x^3)$ for all 100 discriminants
  (`/tmp/crit310.py`, run through `agents/sage.sh`): every one factors into
  exactly two irreducibles, of degrees $h$ and $2h$. The uniqueness claim
  holds throughout the stored range, and PARI's caveat costs this table
  nothing. At $\Delta=-4,-23,-32,-71,-299$ I also confirmed
  $Q_\Delta(x)=P^{\gamma_2}_\Delta(\zeta_3x)\,P^{\gamma_2}_\Delta(\zeta_3^2x)$
  exactly, and that `polclass(Delta, 5)` returns the stored row, including at
  the non-fundamental $\Delta=-32$.

## What reads well

- **It is one table.** One parameter, and it is an argument rather than a
  name. The value column is headed $P_\Delta^{\gamma_2}(x)$, which is true of
  every row, not the word `value`, and there is no `param-latex`, so the rows
  read `-4:`, `-7:`, `-8:` as they should.
- **The parameters describe the family, not the run.** The constraint is
  "$\Delta<0$, $\Delta\equiv0,1 \pmod 4$, and $3\nmid\Delta$", and the range is
  in `complete-note`, where it finishes the sentence "complete: no (…)". I
  checked the note against the entries: the 100 stored discriminants are
  exactly those the note describes, and exactly the 150 discriminants of
  HREF{Hilbert_class_polynomials} less the 50 with $3\mid\Delta$, so "matching
  the range of the Hilbert class polynomials" is literally true.
- **The entries are in the order a reader wants**, by $|\Delta|$ ascending, so
  $-4$ is at the top.
- **The entry comments say what the row cannot.** "$h(\Delta)=3$; fundamental
  discriminant of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})$" names the order; the class number
  itself would be a bad row (the skill's example of a value that matches
  everything) and is in the right place here.
- **`comment-height` is true and measurable.** Over all 100 rows the largest
  coefficient of $H_\Delta$ is the cube of the largest coefficient of
  $P^{\gamma_2}_\Delta$ in size: $\log(\text{height } H)/\log(\text{height }
  P)$ is 3.00 at the minimum, the median and the maximum. At $\Delta=-83$ that
  is 21 digits against 7.
- **The program gives the row.** `polclass(-23, 5)` prints
  `x^3 + 155*x^2 + 650*x + 23375`, which is the $-23$ row exactly.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Definition uses two symbols and names neither; $j$ is not identified anywhere on the page (worth doing)

A reader meets this first:

> Let $\Delta<0$ be a discriminant with $\Delta\equiv0$ or $1 \pmod 4$ and
> $3\nmid\Delta$. The $\gamma_2$ class polynomial $P_\Delta^{\gamma_2}(x)$ is
> the unique monic factor of $H_\Delta(x^3)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ of degree
> $h(\Delta)=\deg H_\Delta$, where $\gamma_2^3=j$.

$H_\Delta$ is never said to be the Hilbert class polynomial here; that is one
section further down, in Comments (1). $j$ is worse: outside this sentence and
the title, the only $j$ in the prose is inside a linked table title under
Similar tables. Nothing on the page says $j$ is the modular $j$-invariant, and
nothing links it. So the Definition does not let two people build the same
table, which is the one thing it has to do, and a reader holding
`x^2 - 380*x + 2300` cannot tell from the first screen what they have.

Both were there one revision ago. `/revisions/T310` shows the edit "shorten
gamma2 class polynomial definition" removing "let
HREF{Hilbert_class_polynomials}[$H_\Delta(x)$] be the Hilbert class polynomial
of the imaginary quadratic order $\mathcal{O}_\Delta$" and "its roots are
class invariants $\gamma_2$ satisfying $\gamma_2^3=j$, where $j$ is
HREF{Q-expansion_of_the_j-invariant}[the modular $j$-invariant]". The first
landed in Comments (1); the second did not land anywhere. This is the same
fault the T309 critique found one table earlier: shortening a definition moved
out the part that made it one.

**Fix:** end the Definition "…, where $\gamma_2^3=j$ and $j$ is
HREF{Q-expansion_of_the_j-invariant}[the modular $j$-invariant]." That is nine
words and restores the missing identification. If the Definition may grow by
one clause more, name $H_\Delta$ there too — "the unique monic factor of
$H_\Delta(x^3)$, for $H_\Delta$ the Hilbert class polynomial of
$\mathcal{O}_\Delta$" — and drop the first sentence of `comment-factor`, which
then repeats it.

### 2. The relation given for Rational singular moduli is about a third table (worth doing)

> Rational singular moduli — the degree-one Hilbert class polynomials give the
> corresponding $j$-values

The sentence relates the Hilbert table to that one and never mentions this
one. "The corresponding" points at nothing a reader can resolve: corresponding
to what? And the fact it is reaching for is a good one, which I checked on all
ten degree-one rows against the stored Hilbert class polynomials: where
$h(\Delta)=1$ the entry is $x-\gamma_2$ with $\gamma_2^3$ equal to the
rational singular modulus for the same $\Delta$. $\Delta=-7$ stores $x+15$ and
$(-15)^3=-3375=j$.

**Fix:** "where $h(\Delta)=1$ the row here is $x-\gamma_2$ and $\gamma_2^3$ is
the rational singular modulus stored there".

### 3. The Wikipedia link is cited by nothing, and "the CM method" is the only mention of what it is about (worth doing, cheap)

`Links` carries "Wikipedia: Complex multiplication", which renders as [3] and
is pointed at by no `CITE`. Meanwhile the phrase "complex multiplication"
never appears in the prose at all: it is in `Keywords`, and in the title of the
Cox reference. The one place the subject surfaces is a Similar-tables relation,
"store another family of smaller class polynomials used in the CM method",
where the abbreviation is never expanded. A reader who does not already know
what CM stands for meets it once, unexplained, beside a link they are not sent
to.

**Fix:** write "used in the complex multiplication method" in that relation,
and put `CITE{WikiCM}` on the first mention in `comment-factor`, beside
"gives a class invariant". The Weber table cites inside a relation already, so
either place renders.

### 4. Formulas names $Q_\Delta$ and the two prose fields point at it instead (worth doing, cheap)

`comment-factor` ends "The other factor of $H_\Delta(x^3)$ has degree
$2h(\Delta)$ and corresponds to multiplying the roots by primitive cube roots
of unity", and the rigour note says "verifies that the other factor has degree
$2\deg H_\Delta$". Formula (1) has already called it $Q_\Delta$. The degree is
also stated three times over: in Formula (1), in the comment, and in the
rigour note.

The mathematics behind "corresponds to" is exact, and I checked it:
$Q_\Delta(x)=P^{\gamma_2}_\Delta(\zeta_3x)\,P^{\gamma_2}_\Delta(\zeta_3^2x)$.

**Fix:** in the comment, "$Q_\Delta$ has the roots of $P_\Delta^{\gamma_2}$
multiplied by the primitive cube roots of unity:
$Q_\Delta(x)=P_\Delta^{\gamma_2}(\zeta_3x)P_\Delta^{\gamma_2}(\zeta_3^2x)$" —
or that identity in Formulas and only the sentence in the comment. Write
"$Q_\Delta$" in the rigour note. Drop the repeated degree.

### 5. "Every discriminant is listed" is a larger claim than the table makes (worth doing, one clause)

`comment-entries` opens "Every discriminant is listed, not only the
fundamental ones." Read as English that says the table holds every negative
discriminant, which is what `complete: no` two sections below denies. What the
sentence means is that non-fundamental ones are not excluded. Its second half,
"says whether $\Delta$ is fundamental", also undersells the comments: they
name the field, or the conductor and the field.

**Fix:** "Non-fundamental discriminants are listed as well as fundamental
ones. Each entry's comment gives the class number $h(\Delta)$ and names the
order." The range stays in `complete-note`, where it already is.

### 6. "smaller class polynomials" invites the wrong reading (worth doing, one sentence)

> These are smaller class polynomials than the Hilbert class polynomials for
> the same discriminants. They define the same ring class fields but have
> coefficients of much smaller height.

The degrees are identical — both are $h(\Delta)$, which is visible in every
row — so "smaller class polynomials" can only mean the coefficients, and the
next sentence says that again. The measurement is sharp and available: the
coefficients are about a third as long, exactly, because $\gamma_2$ is a cube
root of $j$.

**Fix:** "$P_\Delta^{\gamma_2}$ has the same degree as $H_\Delta$ and defines
the same ring class field, with coefficients about a third as many digits,
since $\gamma_2^3=j$."

## Noted only

- **The invariant number 5 is opaque.** `polclass(-23, 5)` is correct and
  reproduces the row, but nothing on the page says the 5 selects
  $\gamma_2=j^{1/3}$ — the reader has to follow the manual link to find out.
  `polclass(-23, 5) \\ 5 is the invariant gamma_2 = j^(1/3)` would say it in
  place, and `\\` is GP's comment marker, which renders.
- **Each entry comment restates the degree of the polynomial beside it.**
  $h(\Delta)$ is the degree of the row, `comment-entries` says so, and the
  skill's rule about per-row comments repeating what the document says once
  for all rows applies. I would keep it anyway: it names the class number,
  which is what a reader of a CM table is looking for, and it costs 1.5 KB of
  a 16 KB entry block.
- **The definition's "unique" is a fact about the stored range.** It is true
  for all 100 discriminants, checked above, and it is the standard behaviour,
  but nothing on the page or in the attached generator cites a theorem for it.
  The generator raises if it ever fails, which is the right thing to do; a
  reader extending the table past $|\Delta|=300$ should know that is where the
  claim is enforced.
- **Nothing here claims what a search would do.** No sentence in this document
  is a remark about the website, which is worth saying because it is the fault
  that keeps recurring elsewhere.
