# T2 critique: "Integers"

**The table has no definition, and that one blank is doing more damage than it
looks.** It is the audit's only finding, and it is also why the table called
"Integers" is not among the twenty answers the site gives to somebody who types
`integer` into the search box. Everything else I found is small.

The table works by its value: a reader who arrives holding $7$ gets T2 first.
It is the arrival by name that fails.

## How I read it

Fetched <https://numberdb.org/T2> and read the rendered HTML; read the document
at `/api/table?id=T2`; ran the audit at `/api/table/T2/audit`; searched the
corpus with the published client (`numberdb.search_text`) rather than reasoning
about what search would do; and read T0, T1, T30 and T41 for comparison, since
those four are this table's immediate neighbourhood.

The audit says:

    {"tid": "T2", "title": "Integers", "findings": ["Definition is empty"],
     "clean": false}

One finding, and I agree with it. It is right in form and right here, and it is
the first thing to do. It does not see §2 to §5 below, which is fair: none of
those is a rule.

## 1. The `Definition` heading prints over blank space — worth doing

What a reader sees, in the page's own order:

    Definition
    Parameters
        $n$  —   integer

The heading renders with an empty container under it. Nothing on the page says
what an integer is, and — the part that surprised me — **the symbol
$\mathbb{Z}$ appears nowhere in the document at all.** It is not in the title,
not in the one comment, not in the parameter, not in the link caption. T30, one
click away, defines the rationals as "the field of fractions of the
HREF{Integers}[integers] $\mathbb{Z}$": the neighbour names the symbol that the
table of the thing does not.

The one comment, "The integers form a ring with their usual addition and
multiplication", is currently standing in for the definition and is not one: it
is a property, and the skill's table gives properties to `Comments`, which is
where it already sits. It is a good sentence in the right place. It just cannot
do this job.

Smallest change: one sentence in `Definition`, saying what the object is and
stopping. Something of the shape

    The ring of integers $\mathbb{Z} = \{\ldots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$.

## 2. Searching "integer" does not find the table called Integers — worth doing

This is what a reader gets from the search box at `/?q=integer`, verbatim from
the rendered page:

    22 results for integer
    Tags
      integral   2 tables, 1003 numbers
      integer    3 tables, 3 numbers
    Tables
      Values of the Fresnel sine integral $S(x)$            T207
      Values of the Fresnel cosine integral $C(x)$          T208
      Values of the Clausen functions ...                   T175
      Values of Dawson's integral $F(x)$                    T206
      ... seventeen more, every one of them an integral

T2 is not in the twenty. Nor for `integers`, nor for `Integers`. `whole
numbers` returns nothing at all.

**Why**, because the mechanism decides what the fix is. `full_text_query` makes
the last word of a term a prefix query, so `integer` stems to `integ` and
`integ:*` matches the indexed `integr` of "integral" as well as the `integ` of
"Integers". The collision is one-way — searching `integral` does *not* drag T2
in — so what is left is rank, and rank is where T2 loses: its entire indexed
text is the word "Integers" once in the title plus one comment, against twenty
tables that say "integral" in a title and again in a definition.

Three controls, so this is not a guess:

| term | result |
|---|---|
| `integer ring` | **T2 first**, then T1, T0 — so T2 is indexed and reachable |
| `integral` | T188, T349, T191, … — T2 absent, the collision is one-way |
| `rational` | T30 "Rational numbers" sixth — a one-word title normally survives |
| `additive identity` | T0 — so `Keywords` work |
| `7` | **T2 first** — the table answers by value |

T30 survives the same shape of competition because it has a definition. T2 does
not have one. So §1 is half of this fix.

The other half is cheaper and certain:

**T2 does not carry the tag `integer`.** It is tagged `ring` and `Abelian
group`. The tag `integer` exists, holds three tables — Zero, One and 42 — and is
offered to the reader *at the top of the results for this very query*.
Following it today does not reach the integers. T0 and T1 were each given that
tag; the table they are both defined in terms of was not.

Smallest change, in order of value for effort:

- add `integer` to `Tags` (one word, and it repairs the one route that already
  works for this query);
- write the `Definition` of §1, which puts "integers" into the indexed text a
  second time at definition weight;
- put `whole number` in `Keywords`, which is empty. It is the commonest English
  synonym and it currently finds nothing in the corpus.

I cannot promise the second lifts T2 into the first twenty — I did not edit the
table and could not measure it. The first works regardless of rank, because a
tag is not ranked against the tables.

## 3. Nothing points outward from the table everything points at — worth doing

`Similar tables` is empty. I walked all four hundred T-numbers to see how
one-sided that is: **four tables link to Integers and Integers links to none of
them.** T0 defines zero as "the neutral element of addition in the ring of
HREF{Integers}[integers]" and carries `Similar tables: integers — includes $0$
as its entry $n=0$`; T1 does the same for one; T30 defines $\mathbb{Q}$ from
the integers and declares `repeats: HREF{Integers}`; T42 carries `integers —
contained in`.

Zero and One are in fact reachable from T2, through the `equals: HREF{Zero}`
and `equals: HREF{One}` on rows $0$ and $1$ — both resolve, both render as
links, and that is the right way to say it, since the sentence means one
number. So the gap is narrower than "no outbound links": what is missing is the
counterpart to the relation T30 states. A reader on Integers has no way to the
rational numbers, and the corpus holds them.

Smallest change: one `Similar tables` row with the relation named, of the shape

    Rational_numbers — its field of fractions

**One thing I found while counting, which is not T2's to fix.** T42 writes its
link as `HREF{Integers#1}[integers]`, and `#1` is the address of T2's row
holding the integer $1$. The sentence means $42$, which T2 does not hold at
all, so the link lands on the wrong number and does so silently — the anchor
resolves because T2 happens to have an entry keyed `1`. It belongs in a
critique of T42; I note it here because it is a fact about how T2 is addressed
from outside, and because a whole-table link is what that sentence wanted.

## 4. "Table is complete: no", and then nothing — worth doing

The rendered `Data properties` reads:

    Table is complete: no

T30's, two tables away, reads:

    Table is complete: no (it holds every $a/b$ in lowest terms with
    $|a| \leq 5$ and $1 \leq b \leq 5$, as examples of the field rather
    than as a reference list)

T0 and T1 both have notes as well. T2 alone stops at "no", which is true of
almost every table and tells a reader nothing. The page therefore never says
anywhere that it holds $-10$ to $10$: you count the rows. And $\pm 10$ is
exactly the "which range, and why" sentence — twenty-one integers as specimens
of the ring, not as a reference list — that a reader checking whether their own
number belongs here would read.

Smallest change: a `complete-note` finishing the sentence "complete: no (...)".

## 5. The value column is headed "$n$th integer", and there is no $-10$th integer — worth doing

    $n$        $n$th integer
    -10:       -10
     -9:        -9

The column header is a claim about every value under it, and this one is false
below the middle of the table: the ordinal implies an enumeration of the
integers, and none is given or exists in the order shown. The value is just
$n$.

I checked whether the `<sup>th</sup>` spelling was the problem and it is not:
eleven tables between T0 and T59 write their header that way, and on T20 —
"$n$th root of $J_\alpha$", $n \geq 1$ — the ordinal is a real enumeration. It
is T2 that has no ordinal to claim.

Smallest change: head the column `$n$`. That also makes the page's oddest
feature honest rather than confusing: every row prints the same number twice,
which is the price of the integers being searchable by their digits at all, not
a fault to hide behind a different-looking header.

## 6. The program reproduces the stored range and not one value more — marginal

    Sage:  numbers = [-10..10]

`Programs` is the incantation for a reader who wants one more value, and this
one returns exactly the values already on the page. `numbers = [-N..N]`, or a
line naming `ZZ`, would answer the question the section asks. I would do this
while doing §4 and not on its own.

## 7. Noted only, and I would edit none of them

- **The entries run $-10$ to $10$, oldest-first for a negative index.** The
  skill's rule is to enumerate by $|a|$, positive first, so that the small
  cases are at the top and $a$ and $-a$ are adjacent. Right in form, and I
  would not apply it here: this is the one table in the corpus whose parameter
  *is* the number line, and $-10, \ldots, 10$ is that object's own order.
  Reordering it to $0, 1, -1, 2, -2$ would make the page called Integers the
  one place in the database where the integers are out of order.
- **`rigour details` says "An exact value -- an integer, a rational or a
  polynomial -- so there is no precision to choose".** The `--` reaches the
  page as two hyphens, and this page in particular is a column of minus signs,
  so it reads as mathematics for a moment. But the string is identical on T0,
  T1, T30 and T41: it is a shared default and belongs to whoever owns the
  default, not to this table.
- **`Abelian group` holds two tables** (T2 and T30) and does not appear on
  `/tags`, which lists fifty. Not wrong, just not a way through the corpus.
  `ring` holds three. I would leave both and add `integer` beside them.
- **The parameter-group header renders `$n$&nbsp `**, an entity with no
  semicolon. Browsers recover and it is invisible. It is in the site template,
  not in T2, and appears on every table with a parameter.

## 8. What I checked hardest, and what held

- **The rendering against the document.** Fetched both and compared field by
  field. No "Math input error", no section eaten by a `<`, no `argument ()`.
  The two `equals` links resolve (`/Zero` and `/One` both answer 200). The
  `<sup>` in the header survives the renderer. The empty `Definition` is in
  both the page and the stored document, so it is an authoring gap and not a
  rendering fault.
- **Whether the table is findable.** Both directions, with the published
  client rather than by reasoning about the index — which is how §2 turned out
  to be a rank problem with a one-word fix rather than "the title is wrong".
  By value it is findable and ranks first. By name it is not.
- **Whether it is one table.** It is. One parameter, an integer; the parameter
  is an argument and not a name; the value column heads with a symbol rather
  than falling back on the word `value`. Nothing here wants splitting.

I did not recheck the numbers. They are $-10$ to $10$ and the build and
`audit_table` have both looked.

## 9. What I would do, in order

1. Write the `Definition` (§1).
2. Add `integer` to `Tags`, and `whole number` to `Keywords` (§2).
3. Add the `complete-note` (§4).
4. Head the value column `$n$` (§5).
5. Add the `Similar tables` row to Rational numbers (§3).
6. If touching the document anyway, generalise the Sage line (§6).

1 and 2 are the ones that change what a reader can do. The rest are tidying.

**I changed nothing.** This is a report.
