## Worth doing

### 1. The Hirzebruch signature theorem is nowhere a reader can see it

This is the one that matters most, and it is one line.

A reader arrives holding $(7p_2-p_1^2)/45$. They met it in exactly one place:
the signature theorem, which is why anybody computes an $L$-polynomial and why
the table is called what it is called. The page never states it. The word
"signature" appears on the finished page exactly once, inside the second
sentence of `rigour details` — which finding 2 shows is folded out of sight —
and once in `Keywords`, which a reader never sees at all.

The article the table cites states it at the very section the table points at:

> Now let $M$ be a closed smooth oriented manifold of dimension $4n$ with
> Pontryagin classes $p_i=p_i(M)$. Hirzebruch showed that the $L$ genus of $M$
> in dimension $4n$ evaluated on the fundamental class of $M$ ... is equal to
> $\sigma(M)$, the signature ... $\sigma(M)=\langle L_n(p_1(M),\dots,p_n(M)),
> [M]\rangle$.

Smallest fix: one row in `Formulas`, which is where an identity goes.

    formula-signature: >
      $\sigma(M)=\langle L_n(p_1(M),\dots,p_n(M)),[M]\rangle$ for a closed
      oriented smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $4n$, where $\sigma$ is the
      signature of the intersection form on $H^{2n}(M)$ CITE{LGenusWiki}.

That also gives the rigour note's projective-space check its meaning: as the
note stands, "gives signature $1$" is the first time a reader is told the
$L$-polynomials have anything to do with signatures.

### 2. `rigour details` folds the whole case away and leaves the circular half showing

What a reader sees under **How they were obtained**, with the disclosure shut:

> The generator computes exact rational coefficients in
> $\mathbb Q[p_1,\dots,p_6]$ from Formula (1).

and behind **more**:

> It is checked against Formula (2), against the Pontryagin-root definition in
> Formula (1), and by substituting the Pontryagin classes of
> $T\mathbb P^{2m}_{\mathbb C}$ for $1\leq m\leq6$, which gives signature $1$
> for each even-dimensional complex projective space [1].

The visible sentence says the table was computed from a formula the table
itself states, which is evidence of nothing. The hidden sentence is the entire
case, including the independent one.

The measurement, for whoever fixes it: `FOLD_ABOVE = 400` in
`numberdb_app/prose.py` counts *rendered* characters; this note is 435
characters as written and 600 as rendered, and it is one paragraph, so
`_split_sentence` cuts it at the last sentence break that fits — which here is
the only one, 175 characters in. Trimming 200 rendered characters out is not
realistic when the four `CITE{}` markers cost about 45 each. Reordering is.

Smallest fix, the same one T334 already carries:

    Every stored component was checked against the terms in Formula
    CITE{formula-first}, against the Pontryagin-root definition in Formula
    CITE{formula-component}, and by substituting the Pontryagin classes of
    $T\mathbb P^{2m}_{\mathbb C}$ for $1\leq m\leq6$, which gives signature $1$
    CITE{LGenusWiki}. The generator computes exact rational coefficients from
    Formula CITE{formula-component}.

I sent that through `/preview` and it renders with the check visible and the
generator sentence behind **more**, which is the right way round. (See also
finding 9: "checked against Formula (2)" is itself circular until
`formula-first` says where its three polynomials are printed.)

This is the fifth table in the family with this fault — T331, T332 and T334
had it and were repaired, and **T335 is published with it today**: its live
page shows "The generator computes exact rational coefficients from Formula
(2) and Formula (3)" and folds away "Every stored component was checked
against the Chern-root definition ... and against
$\mathrm{ch}_n(T\mathbb{P}^m)=(m+1)h^n/n!$", which is the stronger half. Its
own critique did not catch that, so this is worth passing on beyond T336: it

### 3. Two of the three `Links` are the same Wikipedia article

`Links` shows a reader three numbered sources:

> [1] Wikipedia: L-genus
> [2] Wikipedia: Genus of a multiplicative sequence
> [3] Wikipedia: Pontryagin class

`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-genus` is a redirect:
`?action=raw` returns, in full, `#redirect[[Genus of a multiplicative
sequence#L genus]]`. It is [2]'s article, at the section [2] is about. The
Definition cites [1] and Formula (2) cites [2] for statements on one page, and
`rigour details` cites [1] for the signature fact that [2]'s article carries;
a reader has no way to tell that following either one lands in the same place.

Smallest fix: keep one row, and give it the section if the section is what is
meant.

      url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_of_a_multiplicative_sequence#L_genus

Then `CITE{LGenusWiki}` becomes `CITE{GenusWiki}` in the three places that use

arrangement, so whoever decides this should decide it for both.

### 7. `Similar tables` addresses two published tables by number, and one relation is about this website

The four rows render as

> Todd polynomials — store another universal characteristic-class polynomial
> defined by a multiplicative sequence
> Chern character polynomials — store another universal characteristic-class
> polynomial **in the same family of proposals**
> Elementary symmetric polynomials — give the elementary symmetric functions

### 9. Formula (4) restates rows 1 to 3 without saying where they come from

> $L_1=p_1/3$, $L_2=(7p_2-p_1^2)/45$, and $L_3=(62p_3-13p_1p_2+2p_1^3)/945$.

Those are the table's own first three entries, over a common denominator. As
written, that is the value column said twice, and `rigour details` then says
the entries were "checked against Formula (2)" — checked against a restatement
of themselves.

They are not a restatement: they are the forms printed in the cited article,
which gives $L_0$ through $L_4$ in exactly this shape,
$L_2=\tfrac1{45}(7p_2-p_1^2)$ and all. Say so, and the formula becomes
evidence a reader can use and the rigour note's claim becomes an outside
check:

> $L_1=p_1/3$, $L_2=(7p_2-p_1^2)/45$ and $L_3=(62p_3-13p_1p_2+2p_1^3)/945$, as
> printed in CITE{LGenusWiki}.

The article prints $L_4$ too, so a fourth term costs one line and checks one
more row against the source.

### 10. Comment (4) uses $E$, which the page never introduces

> The variables are Pontryagin classes. The convention here is
> $p_i=(-1)^i c_{2i}(E\otimes\mathbb C)$ [3].

The convention is right and is verbatim the cited article's
($p_k(E)=(-1)^kc_{2k}(E\otimes\mathbb C)$), and stating it matters, because
the other sign convention changes the stored coefficients. But $E$ appears
here and nowhere else: the Definition says "an oriented real vector bundle"
and gives it no letter, so a reader meets a bundle named halfway through the
Comments.

Smallest fix: name it once, in the Definition — "of an oriented real vector
bundle $E$" — and the comment binds.

While there: "The variables are Pontryagin classes" says what the Title and
the Definition have both already said. Dropping it leaves the comment one
sentence doing one thing.

### 11. Comment (5) gives the fact the range rests on, without the reason

> The component $L_n$ uses exactly the variables $p_1,\dots,p_n$; the first
> omitted component, $L_7$, has a nonzero $p_7$ coefficient.

The first clause is the mathematics, and it is the right thing to have here.
The second is the range argument, which `complete-note` already makes in full
and with its reason ("which is the most a stored polynomial may have"); here
it arrives before a reader has been told six is a threshold for anything. It
also asserts the coefficient is nonzero where a closed form would settle it:

> The coefficient of $p_n$ in $L_n$ is
> $2^{2n}(2^{2n-1}-1)|B_{2n}|/(2n)!$, which is never zero, so $L_n$ uses
> exactly the variables $p_1,\dots,p_n$.

I checked that closed form against my recomputation for $n=1,\dots,7$: exact
agreement at every $n$. It is the fact a reader wants when they ask why the
table stops, it uses the $B_{2k}$ that Formula (2) has already introduced, and
it makes the completeness note checkable rather than assertable.

## Noted only, because I noticed

### 12. "Even-dimensional complex projective space" is ambiguous on its own

In `rigour details`. Every complex projective space is even-dimensional as a
real manifold, which is why $\mathbb P^{2m}_{\mathbb C}$ is written out beside
it and no reader will actually be lost. "Complex projective space of even
complex dimension", or just $\mathbb{CP}^{2m}$, would not need the symbol to
rescue it. The notation $T\mathbb P^{2m}_{\mathbb C}$ is also this table's
alone: T334 and T335 write $T\mathbb P^m$ for the same object.

### 13. Nothing says in words which grading $n$ counts

$L_n$ is homogeneous of weighted degree $n$ with $\deg p_j=j$, equivalently
the component in $H^{4n}$. The Definition says "the homogeneous component" and
the parameter is titled "degree", and Formula (1) settles it, so this is not
ambiguity — only a phrase a reader would have liked in the first sentence. The
same remark was made on T335 and nothing was done, correctly.

### 14. Six entries is the right size, and the order is right

A reader might expect a table to be bigger. Six is the whole family inside the
database's cap, they share an index, and the skill's rule is one table however
few. They are enumerated $n=1$ to $6$, so the small cases anybody arrives
holding are at the top, and there are no entry comments, which is right: one
formula covers every row.
