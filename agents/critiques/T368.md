# T368: fourteen links to other people's requests, and a page that never says what a Charlier polynomial is

Read on 2026-09-20, draft `T368`, "Zeros of the Charlier polynomials
$C_n(x;a)$", slug `Zeros_of_the_Charlier_polynomials`, 780 entries over ten
rational values of $a$ and degrees $1\leq n\leq12$, 100 significant digits a
row with sixteen rows exact, `type: R`, `rigour: proven`.

The mathematics is in good order and so is most of the presentation. The page
renders, the audit is clean and I agree with its silence on the things it can
see, the three parameters are all indices of one object so this is one table
rather than two, the value column is headed `$x_k$` rather than `value`, the
constraints describe the family and the run is in `complete-note` where it
belongs, and the Sage snippet printed on the page runs, comes back in the
order the page claims for $k$, and reproduces the table.

Two things a reader meets in the first screen do not hold up. The reference
list at the bottom has sixteen entries, fourteen of them GitHub issues, and
thirteen of those fourteen are requests for other tables entirely — metric
entropy of dynamical systems, refractive indices, Kloosterman sums. And
nothing anywhere on the page says what $C_n(x;a)$ is: there is no `Formulas`
section, the definition defers to DLMF, and the recurrence that everything
here is built from is cited in `rigour details` and never written down.

## Do I agree with the audit?

Yes with what it says, and its silence is largely earned. `Definition` is one
sentence and one object. `_one_table_or_several` passes and it is right to:
$a$, $n$ and $k$ are all numbers indexing one family, not names of several
quantities, and the value column heading `$x_k$` is true of every row. No
parameter carries a `param-latex`. Both `HREF` targets resolve —
`Charlier_polynomials` is T267 and `Zeros_of_the_polygamma_functions` is T127.
Both `CITE`s resolve to declared links.

The audit cannot see finding 1, and the gap is specific: it checks that every
`CITE` resolves to a `Link` (`unresolved_citations`, line 322) and never that
every `Link` is cited by something. Fourteen orphan links pass it. That is
worth writing into `audit_table` and I have noted it in
`docs/agent-environment.md`.

## 2. Nothing on the page says what $C_n(x;a)$ is. *Worth doing.*

The definition is:

> For rational $a>0$ and integer $n\geq1$, let $x_1<\cdots<x_n$ be the real
> zeros of the Charlier polynomial $C_n(x;a)$ in the DLMF normalisation [1].
> This table lists $x_k$.

That is a good definition of the *zeros*. It is not a definition of the
polynomial, and the page has nowhere else for one: there is no `Formulas`
section, and the three comments are about normalisation, the grid and the
absence of weights. A reader who arrived holding `2.618033988749894848...`
learns that it is a zero of something called $C_2(x;1)$ and has to leave the
site to find out what that is.

The skill's rule is direct: "Define notation where you use it. A comment
saying the values are `U_n(x,-1)` is useless until something says what `U` is.
If a symbol appears in a formula or a comment, its definition belongs in the
same table." $C_n(x;a)$ appears in the title, the definition, two comments,
`Similar tables` and `rigour details`.

The gap is sharper because the fix is already written. The parent table T267
defines the polynomial in one sentence and gives the recurrence as a formula,
and this table's `rigour details` says it builds $C_n$ "from the three-term
recurrence [2]" — naming a recurrence it does not state.

**Smallest change.** Add T267's `formula-recurrence` verbatim:

    Formulas:
      formula-recurrence: "$C_0(x;a)=1$, $C_1(x;a)=1-x/a$, and
        $aC_{n+1}(x;a)=(n+a-x)C_n(x;a)-nC_{n-1}(x;a)$ for $n\\geq1$
        CITE{DLMFRecurrence}."

Three lines, and the page becomes self-contained. (Note that the site draws
`Formulas` before `Comments`, so this lands above the normalisation comment,
which is the right order.)

## 3. Sixteen rows are exact rationals and nothing explains them. *Worth doing.*

Among 780 rows of hundred-digit decimals, sixteen read `1/4`, `1/3`, `1/2`,
`2/3`, `1`, `3/2`, `2`, `5/2`, `3`, `4`, `8/3`, `1`, `4`, `2`, `1`, `1`.
Storing them exactly is right and the skill asks for exactly that. But a
reader scrolling the table sees the integer `1` four times in a column of
proven hundred-digit enclosures and has no way to tell whether those are rows
somebody truncated.

They are not, and the reasons are clean enough to state in one sentence. I
checked both in Sage rather than asserting them:

* $C_1(x;a)=1-x/a$, so the $n=1$ row is $x_1=a$ for every $a$. That is ten of
  the sixteen.
* $C_n(1;a)=1-n/a$, because every term of the explicit sum past $k=1$ carries
  the factor $x-1$. So $x=1$ is a zero exactly when $a=n$, which is the rows
  at $(a,n)=(2,2)$, $(3,3)$ and $(4,4)$.

The three left over — $4$ at $(2,2)$, $2$ at $(2,4)$, $8/3$ at $(2/3,4)$ — are
roots of the quadratic $a^2-2na+n(n-1)$ and of its cubic analogue, and need no
comment of their own.

The skill's instruction for this is "Where a value looks surprising the fact
is the explanation".

**Smallest change.** One comment: "The $n=1$ row is $x_1=a$, since
$C_1(x;a)=1-x/a$. More generally $C_n(1;a)=1-n/a$, so $x=1$ is a zero exactly
when $a=n$; those rows are stored exactly rather than as decimals."

## 4. The second `Similar tables` entry names no relation. *Worth doing.*

> Zeros of the polygamma functions —  are another table of indexed real zeros

The skill says a `Similar tables` line exists to state the *relation*, "in
words", and that "that sentence is the valuable part". This sentence is true
of at least eight other tables in this corpus — T3, T4, T20, T21, T55, T56,
T127 and T324 are all indexed real zeros — so it does not say why T127 is the
one named. Compare the parent's single line, which states the Charlier–Hermite
limit as a formula.

There is a real neighbour to name if one is wanted. T267 records that
$C_n(x;a)$ tends to $H_n$ after rescaling; the corpus also holds the other two
members of the discrete Askey family, T269 (Meixner) and T266/T270
(Krawtchouk), of which Charlier is a limit.

**Smallest change.** Drop the T127 line. The T267 line above it carries the
relation that matters ("hold the polynomials whose zeros are listed here") and
is enough on its own.

## 5. Comment (3) is an apology for what the table does not hold. *Worth doing.*

> This table stores zeros only. No standard named Charlier Gaussian quadrature
> rule is used here, so no weights are attached.

The first sentence repeats the title. The second is a statement about a
decision this table made, phrased as a defence — the skill's "what a reader
ought to conclude ... reads as apology". A reader who was not already
wondering about quadrature weights now is, and the comment names nothing they
could go and look at: the corpus's quadrature tables (T132 to T137) are not
linked, so there is no way to tell what the sentence is contrasting with.

**Smallest change.** Delete `comment-zero-only`. If the contrast with T132–T137
is worth drawing, it belongs in `Similar tables` with the relation named, not
in a comment that only says what is absent.

## 6. `rigour details` writes the Sage ring name as mathematics. *Worth doing, one character each side.*

> Sage isolates the real roots as exact algebraic numbers in $AA$

`$AA$` is set by MathJax as an italic *AA*, which is not notation any reader
of mathematics knows; it is the name of a Sage object. The skill says of
`rigour details` that "backticks mark code", and the site honours it — I
checked, and a backticked fragment renders as `<code class="prose-code">`
while `$AA$` is handed to MathJax.

**Smallest change.** Write `` `AA` `` instead of `$AA$`.

## 7. `rigour details` opens with the review workflow. *Minor.*

> Before the draft was offered, the values were checked against the parent
> polynomial table, by evaluating $C_n$ at the endpoints of each written
> decimal interval, by Vieta's relations for every degree, and by the
> interlacing of the zeros of consecutive degrees.

Everything after the first five words is exactly what `rigour details` is for,
and it is a good list. "Before the draft was offered" dates the work to a step
in numberdb's review process, which a reader of the published table has no
reason to know about. Deleting those five words costs nothing.

## 8. No `Keywords`, and "Poisson" appears nowhere a search can reach. *Noted only.*

The text index reads the title, `Keywords`, `Tags`, `Definition` and
`Comments` — not the parameter list. The word "Poisson" occurs in this
document only as the title of parameter $a$, so somebody typing
"Poisson-Charlier" reaches the parent T267, which carries
`Keywords: Poisson-Charlier polynomials, ...`, and not this table. The stem
"Charlier" does reach both, so the miss is narrow, and I would not hold up the
draft for it. If it is cheap to add, the parent's three keywords transfer
unchanged.

## 9. The definition invokes a normalisation and comment (1) says normalisation does not matter. *Noted only.*

The definition pins $C_n(x;a)$ "in the DLMF normalisation", and the first
comment then says that multiplying by any nonzero factor does not change these
zeros. Both are true and the comment is genuinely useful — a reader holding
the monic convention needs to know they are in the right place. But the word
"normalisation" is doing two jobs across two sentences: in the definition it
means DLMF's choice of the whole family, and in the comment it means a scalar
multiple. A reader can work it out. It would read better if the definition
said "as defined in HREF{Charlier_polynomials}", which is both more precise
and the link the first mention wants anyway.

## 10. The $a=1$, $n=2$ pair is $\varphi^{\pm2}$. *Noted only.*

$C_2(x;1)=x^2-3x+1$, so its zeros are $\varphi^2=2.618033988749894848\ldots$
and $\varphi^{-2}=0.381966011250105151\ldots$. Those digits are among the
most commonly met in the table, and a reader who arrives holding them is
likely to have met them as the golden ratio rather than as a Charlier zero.
T32 stores $\varphi$, $\varphi^{-1}$ and $\hat\varphi$ and not their squares,
so `equals` would be false and should not be used. An entry comment on those
two rows, linking `HREF{Golden_ratio#phi}`, would serve that reader. This is a
nicety, not a fault, and two entry comments on a 780-row table cost nothing.

## What I checked and am not reporting

* **The Sage snippet in `Programs` is the real thing.** I ran it verbatim
  through `agents/sage.sh`. It runs (one `UserWarning` about a lazy
  `SymbolicRing` import, from Sage and not from the code), gives 13 roots for
  its `charlier(13, QQ(1))` — one degree past the table, which is what a
  `Programs` block is for — and `numberdb.bits(100, losing=96)` is 429 bits,
  matching the number `rigour details` states.
* **`roots(AA, multiplicities=False)` comes back in increasing order**, so the
  snippet does produce the $k$ the page promises. This is the one claim the
  snippet leans on without saying so, and it holds for all 120 polynomials
  here.
* **All 780 written intervals bracket their zero.** Checked the way the skill
  asks — the sign of $C_n$ at the two ends of the interval the written decimal
  denotes, not the value at its midpoint — with a control (one digit of the
  $a=1,n=2,k=1$ value altered) that correctly failed. 0 disagreements, 16 of
  them exact rationals verified as equalities in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.
* **The 25 entries written in exponent form** (`1.136...e-15` and the like, all
  of them the smallest zero for small $a$ and large $n$) are correct
  `R`-type intervals and render as stored.
* **The prose reads as English.** Every field is a sentence; there are no
  fragments, no `--`, no "the former" or "as above", and nothing points up or
  down the page. `complete-note` is a clause that finishes "complete: no
  (...)" properly, and it says what the table *does* cover, which is the
  sentence a reader checking for their own $a$ actually reads.
* **The entry order** is $a$ increasing, then $n$, then $k$, which is the order
  a reader compares in, and the small cases are at the top.
* **Size.** 780 entries, longest value 107 characters, entries block about
  85 KB — comfortably inside the recommended 1000 entries and half the soft
  block limit, with room for the next person to extend $n$.
* **Tags.** `orthogonal polynomials` and `zero`, both existing tags, and `zero`
  is the corpus's singular spelling.
* **The `<` in `$x_1<\cdots<x_n$`** is escaped correctly in the rendered HTML
  (`x_1&lt;\cdots&lt;x_n`) and eats nothing.
