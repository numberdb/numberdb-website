## What reads well

It is one table. $l$ and $r$ are arguments of one threshold, the column is
headed $\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$, and there is no `param-latex`. The
parameters describe the family, and the range sits in `complete-note` as a
clause that finishes "complete: no (...)". `formula-root` says "the solution in
$0<x<1$", which is exactly right: it is unique there, and $x=0$ is always a
root as well. That avoids the fault T275's critique found in "the positive
solution". `Similar tables` names the relation to T275 in words. The rows run
$(3,4),(3,5),(3,6),\dots$, so the $(3,6)$ value $0.4881$, the one a coding
theorist most likely holds, is on the first screen with its KRU citation.

The findings are about what the prose says, not about the numbers.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The definition does not say what a MAP threshold is. Worth doing.

> For $3\leq l<r$, $\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$ is the binary erasure
> channel [4] threshold of maximum a posteriori decoding for the regular
> $(l,r)$ low-density parity-check ensemble [3] [1].

On the page this is a stack of nouns with a citation marker in the middle:
"the binary erasure channel [4] threshold of maximum a posteriori decoding". It
names the threshold without defining it. Somebody building the table from this
sentence has to know already that it means the channel parameter where
optimal decoding stops succeeding, and in which limit. T275 had the same
sentence and its repair replaced it. T276 was built afterwards from the old
wording.

**Fix:** follow the operational description KRU give on their first page:

> For $3\leq l<r$, $\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$ is the supremum of the
> erasure probabilities $\epsilon$ of the binary erasure channel CITE{WikiBEC}
> for which maximum a posteriori decoding of a code drawn from the regular
> $(l,r)$ low-density parity-check ensemble CITE{WikiLDPC} succeeds with
> probability tending to $1$ as the blocklength tends to infinity CITE{MMU}.

(MMU state this limit in terms of the
conditional entropy per bit tending to $0$. If the owner prefers that exact
form, it should be taken from MMU, which I did not read.)

### 2. `comment-root` says "a nonzero fixed point", and at this $\epsilon$ there are two. The entries call it "the Maxwell root", a name the page never introduces. Worth doing.

> At the MAP threshold, density evolution has a nonzero fixed point
> $x^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$ in $(0,1)$; each entry comment gives this root.

For every row, $\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}>\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}$, so
$\epsilon(x)=\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}$ has two solutions in $(0,1)$ (KRU Lemma
9). I counted two for all 45 pairs. $x^{\mathrm{MAP}}$ is the larger, stable
one. KRU write it $x_s(\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}})\approx0.4323$ for $(3,6)$, and
it exceeds $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ in every row. "A nonzero fixed point" is
true of both, so it does not say which one the comments give. "This root"
points back at a formula that the page prints after this comment. Every entry
comment then says "The Maxwell root is ...", but no sentence on the page gives
it that name.

**Fix:**

> At $\epsilon=\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$ the density-evolution equation
> has two fixed points in $(0,1)$, and $x^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$, the Maxwell
> root, is the larger and stable one: the erasure probability at which belief
> propagation stalls on that channel. Each entry comment gives it to twelve
> decimal places.

### 3. `comment-maxwell` names a construction without saying what it is, and opens with "The root condition". Worth doing.

> The root condition is the Maxwell construction for the extended
> belief-propagation EXIT curve [2]. Kudekar, Richardson and Urbanke give the
> regular-ensemble analytic characterization used here as Lemma 4 [1].

"The root condition" points at $p^{\mathrm{MAP}}=0$ without naming it.
"Maxwell construction" and "extended belief-propagation EXIT curve" are both
undefined. The second sentence says where the lemma comes from, which a
citation already does. What the condition means is short, and KRU state it
(Section III-A, and the computation of $T_1$ in Appendix V): integrate the
belief-propagation EXIT curve $h=(1-(1-x)^{r-1})^l$ in $\epsilon$ from $1$
down to $\epsilon(x)$. The integral is $1-l/r-p^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}(x)$. So
$p^{\mathrm{MAP}}=0$ says that the area equals the design rate. I checked this
by quadrature for three rows. It holds to working precision.

**Fix:**

> $p^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}(x)=1-\frac lr-\int_x^1(1-(1-t)^{r-1})^l\,\epsilon'(t)\,dt$,
> so $x^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$ is where the area under the belief-propagation
> EXIT curve, taken from $\epsilon(x)$ to $1$, equals the design rate $1-l/r$.
> This is the Maxwell construction CITE{MMU}, and Lemma 4 of CITE{KRU} states
> it for regular ensembles.

The first clause is a formula and could go to `Formulas` instead.

### 4. `formula-trial-entropy` is not an equivalent condition. It is $p^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$ rewritten, so the check in `rigour details` confirms nothing. Worth doing, cheap.

> Equivalently, $\epsilon(x)(1-(1-x)^{r-1})^l+lx(1-x)^{r-1}-\frac lr(1-(1-x)^r)=0$
> at $x=x^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$.

Substitute $\epsilon(x)(1-y^{r-1})^l=x(1-y^{r-1})$ with $y=1-x$. The left side
becomes $x-xy^{r-1}+\frac lr y^{r-1}(1+(r-1)x)-\frac lr$, which is
$p^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}(x)$ term for term. Numerically the two differ by less
than $2\times10^{-61}$ at 800 points. "Equivalently ... at
$x=x^{\mathrm{MAP}}$" suggests two conditions that happen to agree at one
point, when they are one polynomial everywhere. The phrase "trial entropy"
appears on the page only as "the equivalent trial-entropy equation in (4)" in
`rigour details`, and nothing explains it. That sentence also lists it as
something "the generator also checks every row against". Evaluating the same
polynomial a second way cannot disagree, so it is not a check. The other
checks in that sentence (mpmath, KRU's printed values, the ordering against
T275) are real.

**Fix:** either delete `formula-trial-entropy` and its clause in
`rigour details`, or state it as an identity: "For every $x$,
$p^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}(x)=\dots$". Then drop it from the list of checks.

### 5. `comment-conventions` and `comment-l2` were copied from T275 before T275's critique, and bring its faults with them. Worth doing, cheap.

> The value stored here is the channel erasure probability $\epsilon$, not the
> constraint density. ... The parity-check density of the ensemble is
> $m/n=l/r$, and its design rate is $1-l/r$ when the checks are independent.

This is T275's finding 4, word for word. No reader of this page expects a
constraint density. $m$ and $n$ are undefined. The condition is also the wrong
way round: $1-l/r$ is the design rate by definition, and independence of the
checks is what makes the *actual* rate equal it. T275 now carries the
corrected sentence, and T276 can take it.

The comment is also missing the one fact a reader holding a number here most
needs. $\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}<l/r$ holds in every row, and by KRU
Lemma 8 the gap closes exponentially fast in $l$. For $(11,12)$ the stored value
$0.91666666666656\ldots$ differs from $11/12$ by $1.0\times10^{-13}$, and for
$(10,11)$ it differs from $10/11$ by $3.2\times10^{-12}$. A reader who arrives
holding $0.9166666666666$ will reasonably think they are holding $11/12$. The
table can say why they are close. Today that fact appears only as a check in
`rigour details`.

**Fix:** use T275's `comment-conventions` with $\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}$ in
place of $\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}$, and add: "The gap $l/r-\epsilon^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$
tends to $0$ exponentially fast in $l$ at fixed rate CITE{KRU}, so the rows
with $r=l+1$ agree with $l/r$ to many places: $(11,12)$ to thirteen."

> There are no $l=2$ rows. The table follows the non-cycle regular ensembles
> with $l\geq3$ used in the analytic threshold characterization of [1].

"Non-cycle" is not a term anybody uses. KRU say "cycle ensembles" for $l=2$.
The sentence explains the table's choice and says nothing about the
mathematics. The fact is short: for $l=2$, $p^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{2,r}$ is
positive throughout $(0,1)$ (checked for $r\leq12$). The area condition is
therefore met only in the limit $x\to0$, and the MAP threshold equals the BP
threshold $1/(r-1)$. KRU's footnote 1 names cycle ensembles as the case where
the two thresholds coincide.

**Fix:** "For $l=2$, the cycle ensembles, the MAP and belief-propagation
thresholds coincide CITE{KRU}, and both equal $1/(r-1)$."

### 6. The MMU reference is incomplete. Cheap.

`bib` gives only the authors and title. KRU's reference [2] gives the rest:
C. Méasson, A. Montanari and R. Urbanke, IEEE Transactions on Information
Theory 54 (2008), no. 12, 5277–5307. The first author's name also lost its
accent. I did not look up the DOI, so I am not proposing one.

### 7. `Keywords` lacks what T275's repair added. Cheap.

T275 now carries "BEC, BP threshold, erasure threshold". T276 has neither
"BEC" nor "erasure threshold", and a coding theorist types both. Add "BEC,
erasure threshold, optimal threshold".

## Noted only because I noticed

- **T275 does not link back.** T275 has no `Similar tables`, so a reader there
  cannot reach this table. The fix belongs in T275, not here.
- **$x^{\mathrm{MAP}}_{l,r}$ sits in entry comments.** It is a different
  quantity from the stored one. T275 does the same, and its critique left it
  alone. I would too.
- **`HREF{T275}` in `rigour details` renders as the bare word "T275".** It
  reads acceptably in "checks ... against T275". A caption would read better,
  but it is not worth an edit on its own.
- **Why $r\leq12$?** `complete-note` gives the range and not the reason.
- **One tag, `probability theory`.** Same as T275, and the same reasoning
  applies: no coding-theory tag exists, and two tables are short of the three
  the skill asks for.

## The audit

The audit is clean, and I agree it has nothing mechanical to report. The page
renders, the escaping is right, and there is nothing in the public corpus to
link: text lookups for LDPC, erasure, belief propagation, maximum a posteriori,
coding theory and Shannon returned no tables. Findings 1 to 5 are about
meaning, and finding 4 is algebra, which no rule can see. The one pattern a rule
might catch is finding 5: prose shared word for word with a sibling draft that
has since changed. The audit has no such rule, and I would not add one for a
single case.
