## What reads well

The page renders cleanly. There is no `<` in the mathematics. Every
backslash survives into the HTML. `CITE{formula-minimiser}` and
`CITE{formula-tail}` inside `rigour details` resolve to (3) and (1). The
arXiv and DOI identifiers render as links. The folded rigour note opens on a
sentence that stands alone.

It is one table. $k$ and $r$ are both arguments, the value column is headed
$c_{k,r}$ and nothing else, and no `param-latex` is involved. The parameters
describe the family ($k\geq3$, $r\geq2$). The range computed is in
`complete-note`, as a clause that finishes "complete: no (...)". The Programs
snippet is short and works on every row.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The definition does not say which density is meant. Worth doing.

A reader sees:

> The number $c_{k,r}$ is the edge-density threshold for its appearance in
> the random $k$-uniform hypergraph.

"Edge density" is exactly where the literature disagrees, and this table's own
first comment says so: edges per vertex $m/n$, average degree $kc$, or
Dembo and Montanari's vertices per edge. The three differ by a factor of $k$
or by a reciprocal. So two people building from the definition alone could
store $0.8185$, $2.455$ or $1.222$ for $k=3$, $r=2$. The convention that
settles it is in `comment-conventions`, a screen further down. The definition
also names no random model and does not say "with probability tending to 1".
It says "minimum vertex degree $r$" where "at least $r$" is meant.

The sibling table, HREF{k-core_thresholds_of_the_Erdős_Rényi_random_graph},
already has the right shape: model, scaling, both sides of the threshold, and
the minimisation.

**Smallest fix:** rewrite the definition. For example:

> The $r$-core of a $k$-uniform hypergraph CITE{WikiHypergraph} is its largest
> subhypergraph in which every vertex has degree at least $r$. $c_{k,r}$ is
> the number such that the random $k$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices with
> $m=cn$ edges has, with probability tending to $1$ as $n\to\infty$, an empty
> $r$-core when $c<c_{k,r}$ and a nonempty one when $c>c_{k,r}$
> CITE{Molloy} CITE{CainWormald}.

The first sentence of `comment-conventions` ("The density is $c=m/n$, edges
per vertex.") then becomes redundant and can go.

### 2. The two graph-case comments point instead of naming, and say one thing twice. Worth doing.

`comment-conventions`:

> The average degree is $kc$; [$k$-core thresholds of the Erdos-Renyi random
> graph] use the graph average degree in the case $k=2$, and Dembo and
> Montanari use ...

The link caption, which is a table title, is used as the subject of "use". A
reader has to work out that the *table* uses average degree, not the
thresholds. "In the case $k=2$" is attached to a table that has no $k$.

`comment-graph-case`:

> For $r\geq3$, the same minimisation gives half of the graph $r$-core
> threshold in [the Erdos-Renyi table], because that table writes average
> degree while this table writes edges per vertex.

"The same minimisation" points back at formula (2) without naming it. The
sentence also repeats the convention difference that the previous comment has
just stated, and that `Similar tables` states a third time. The same table is
linked in both comments of one section.

**Smallest fix:** make one comment that links once and names the formula.
For example:

> The HREF{k-core_thresholds_of_the_Erdős_Rényi_random_graph}[table of $k$-core
> thresholds of the Erdős–Rényi random graph] measures density by average
> degree, which is $2c$ for a graph. So CITE{formula-threshold} taken at $k=2$
> gives half of each value stored there, for $r\geq3$. This table has no rows
> with $k=2$. For $r=2$ the graph $2$-core grows continuously from $c=1/2$,
> while for every $k\geq3$ the hypergraph $2$-core appears discontinuously.
> Dembo and Montanari use the reciprocal density $n/m$ CITE{DemboMontanari}.

### 3. `comment-peeling` quotes a value that is already a row, and restates the definition. Worth doing, small.

> In particular $c_{3,2}=0.8184691607\ldots$ is the $2$-core threshold of the
> random $3$-uniform hypergraph, and it is also the clustering threshold of
> random $3$-XORSAT.

The first half is the definition, and "in particular" follows from nothing.
The digits are a copy of the row (3, 2). Copied digits answer no search, and
the sentence could link to the row instead.

**Smallest fix:** "HREF{#3,2}[$c_{3,2}$] is also the clustering threshold of
random $3$-XORSAT CITE{DemboMontanari} CITE{Dietzfelbinger}."

### 4. Keywords promise subjects the page never mentions. Worth doing if the author can confirm the relation.

`Keywords` carries "pure literal rule" and "invertible Bloom lookup table",
so a search for either lands here. Nothing on the page then says how the
table relates to those subjects. Consider a reader who typed "pure literal
rule" holding the random 3-SAT threshold $1.63694\ldots$. That number is
$2c_{3,2}$ (I computed $2c_{3,2}=1.6369383215\ldots$). A factor of 2 is one a
reader might try, but the page should say so. Likewise, IBLT and peeling
papers quote the reciprocal $1/c_{k,2}$ cells per key, $1.222$ for $k=3$.

**Smallest fix:** one sentence each in `comment-peeling`, citing Molloy for
the pure literal rule ("the pure literal rule succeeds on random $k$-SAT up
to clause density $2c_{k,2}$"). I have not checked that statement against
the paper. The author should, before writing it. If it cannot be sourced,
drop the keyword instead.

### 5. The accents are dropped from both link captions. Cheap.

The linked table's title is "k-core thresholds of the Erdős–Rényi random
graph". Both HREF captions and the `Similar tables` caption read "Erdos-Renyi"
with a hyphen, although the slug in the same field carries the accents.

**Fix:** write "Erdős–Rényi" in the captions.

### 6. $\mathrm{Po}(\lambda)$ is used without being defined. Cheap.

`formula-tail` writes $\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Po}(\lambda)\geq s)$ and nothing in
the table says what $\mathrm{Po}$ is. The sibling table says "with
$\mathrm{Po}(\lambda)$ a Poisson variable of mean $\lambda$".

**Fix:** append that clause to `formula-tail`.

## Noted only because I noticed

- **The minimiser in the entry comments.** Every entry's comment carries an
  11-digit value of $\lambda_{k,r}$, which no search reaches. The skill's rule
  "a value belongs in a row" is about the table's own quantity in another
  normalisation, and $\lambda_{k,r}$ is a different quantity. The sibling
  graph table does the same. I would leave it.
- **Proof in `formula-minimiser`.** The formula carries its proof ("The sign
  of $g$ is ..., so $g$ decreases and then increases"), and `rigour details`
  gives the argument again. The proof also omits the two facts that make it
  one zero: $g(0)=0$ and $g\to1$. The sibling table states them. Moving the
  argument wholly into `rigour details`, with those two facts added, would
  leave the formula as a formula. It is not wrong as it stands.
- **The audit.** I agree that it has nothing mechanical to report. It cannot
  see findings 1 to 4, which are about meaning. It did not report the second
  link to the Erdős–Rényi table inside Comments, or the undefined
  $\mathrm{Po}$. That fits the earlier observation that its "undefined
  notation" check is not implemented.
