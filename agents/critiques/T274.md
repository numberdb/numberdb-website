Read on 2026-09-16, as a private draft owned by zeta3.

## What reads well

It is one table. $k$ and $\ell$ are both arguments. The value column is
headed $c^*_{k,\ell}$. There is no `param-latex`, and no row label repeats
the block heading. The parameters describe the family ($k\geq2$,
$\ell\geq1$). The computed range is in `complete-note`, as a clause that
finishes "complete: no (...)". The formulas are formulas: $Q$ is defined by
its explicit sum, so $\mathrm{Po}$ does not need a definition of its own.
The rigour note opens with a sentence that stands on its own before the
fold. The arXiv and DOI identifiers render as links. The index shift against
Dietzfelbinger et al. is stated once, in a comment, which is where a reader
holding their numbers will look.

The six findings below are about what a reader holding a hashing number
meets, and about how the definition and the entry comments read.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. "Load threshold" names the convention this table does not use. Worth doing.

`comment-orientation` says:

> In hashing language, edges are keys and vertices are buckets, so this is
> the load threshold for offline cuckoo hashing with $k$ choices and bucket
> capacity $\ell$.

`Keywords` also carries "load threshold". But for buckets that hold $\ell$
keys, the hashing literature quotes the *load factor* as keys per slot, which
is $c/\ell$. `comment-conventions` itself calls this "the fraction of bucket
capacity used". The famous numbers are in that convention: blocked cuckoo
hashing with two choices and buckets of two is quoted as $0.897$, and with
buckets of four as $0.980$. This table stores $1.7940\ldots$ and
$3.9215\ldots$. (I divided the stored rows by $\ell$: $0.8970$, $0.9592$,
$0.9804$, $0.9896$ and $0.9941$ for $k=2$, and $0.9882$ for $k=3$, $\ell=2$.)
For $\ell=1$ the two conventions agree, so the rows with $\ell=1$ are
unaffected. A reader holding $0.897$ might try doubling it. A reader holding
$0.980$ will not think of multiplying by 4. Either reader gets no hit, and
the page calls the stored number a "load threshold" as well.

**Smallest fix:** say what the stored number is. For example: "...so
$c^*_{k,\ell}$ is the threshold for the number of keys per bucket in offline
cuckoo hashing with $k$ choices and buckets of capacity $\ell$. The load
factor, keys per slot, is $c^*_{k,\ell}/\ell$." Then drop "load threshold"
from `Keywords` or replace it with "blocked cuckoo hashing".

**Larger, and a decision for the owner:** the skill says one number in two
conventions belongs in one table, with both conventions as rows. A
`normalisation` parameter with the values "keys per bucket" and "keys per
slot", holding the 30 rows with $\ell\geq2$ a second time, would make
$0.8970\ldots$ and $0.9804\ldots$ findable. Most of those 30 values are
$0.9999\ldots$ and would turn up in nobody's hands, so this may not be worth
it beyond $k=2$. I am raising it, not recommending it.

### 2. The definition does not say what "threshold" means, and leaves "$\ell$-orientability" to a comment. Worth doing.

> Let $H_{n,\lfloor cn\rfloor,k}$ be the random $k$-uniform hypergraph on $n$
> vertices. The number $c^*_{k,\ell}$ is the threshold density for
> $\ell$-orientability of $H_{n,\lfloor cn\rfloor,k}$.

The first sentence never says the hypergraph has $\lfloor cn\rfloor$ edges.
The reader has to read that off a subscript that uses a $c$ nobody has
introduced yet. "Threshold" is never given its meaning: probability tending
to 1 below and to 0 above. And the defining property, $\ell$-orientability,
is only defined three screens down, in `comment-orientation`. Both sibling
drafts state the limiting-probability condition in the definition. T273 is
"the high-probability threshold". T272 says the probability of
satisfiability "has limiting probability $1$ for $\alpha<\alpha_k$ and $0$
for $\alpha>\alpha_k$". Among these three tables T274 is the one where a
reader has to supply the meaning.

**Smallest fix:**

> A hypergraph is $\ell$-orientable if each edge can be assigned to one of
> its vertices so that no vertex receives more than $\ell$ edges. In the
> random $k$-uniform hypergraph CITE{WikiHypergraph} with $n$ vertices and
> $m=\lfloor cn\rfloor$ edges, $c^*_{k,\ell}$ is the density such that, as
> $n\to\infty$, the hypergraph is $\ell$-orientable with probability tending
> to $1$ for $c<c^*_{k,\ell}$ and to $0$ for $c>c^*_{k,\ell}$ CITE{FKP}.

The first sentence of `comment-orientation` then goes.

### 3. Every entry comment records a check, and on 35 rows it prints a different $\ell$ from the row's. Worth doing.

Every inexact row carries a comment like this one, on row $(2,2)$:

> The root is $\xi_{2,2}=2.68799934550$. Dietzfelbinger, Goerdt, Mitzenmacher,
> Montanari, Pagh and Rink tabulate this value as $1.7940237365$ to ten
> decimal places, in their row $\ell=3$ CITE{DGMMPR}.

The second sentence is a record of the check, and the skill puts that in
`rigour details`. `rigour details` already says it ("compares all rows ...
with the ten-decimal table in CITE{DGMMPR}, using the source's index shift").
`comment-conventions` already explains the shift. So the six author names
appear 35 times to say something the page states twice.

It also misleads where it is read. On the row labelled $\ell=2$ the comment
says "$\ell=3$", using this table's own symbol for the source's label, and a
reader skimming the rows sees a contradiction. On rows $(7,5)$ and $(7,6)$ it
says the source tabulates "$5.0000000000$" and "$6.0000000000$" beside
stored values of $4.99999999995\ldots$ and $5.999999999999\ldots$. That is a
rounding, but it reads like a disagreement. The skill's rule that a value
belongs in a row, not a comment, points the same way: these are ten-digit
copies of the row's own number. T272 and T273, from the same batch, do not
do this.

**Smallest fix:** cut every entry comment after its first sentence, so it
reads "The root is $\xi_{k,\ell}=\ldots$." If the source's labelling should
still be visible at a row, the sentence in `comment-conventions` already
does that.

### 4. Two families the corpus holds are named in Comments and not linked. Worth doing, cheap.

- `comment-capacity-one`: "the thresholds are equal to the satisfiability
  thresholds of random $k$-XORSAT". That phrase is T272's title, and T272
  holds exactly $c^*_{k,1}$ for $k\geq3$.
- `comment-core`: "The obstruction is the $(\ell+1)$-core". T273 holds the
  thresholds at which those cores appear.

Both are linked only from `Similar tables`, and the rule is first mention
once per section.

**Fix:** write `HREF{T272}[satisfiability thresholds of random $k$-XORSAT]`
in `comment-capacity-one`. In `comment-core`, link the core, for example
"The obstruction is the $(\ell+1)$-core, whose appearance thresholds are in
HREF{T273}[core thresholds of random $k$-uniform hypergraphs]: at the
threshold $c^*_{k,\ell}$ the core's asymptotic edge density is $\ell$
CITE{FKP}."

The same edit could fix a small misreading in `comment-capacity-one`. It
says "the ordinary two-choice cuckoo hashing threshold $1/2$, where the
cuckoo graph is a pseudoforest", and "where" reads as "at $1/2$". The graph
is a pseudoforest with high probability *below* $1/2$. Entry $(2,1)$ says
this correctly ("success is equivalent to the cuckoo graph being a
pseudoforest"), so the comment could simply stop after "$1/2$".

### 5. `formula-root` begins with an exception about the wrong symbol. Cheap.

> Except for $c^*_{2,1}=1/2$, the value $\xi_{k,\ell}$ is the positive
> solution of $k\ell=\xi Q(\xi,\ell)/Q(\xi,\ell+1)$.

The sentence is about $\xi_{k,\ell}$, but the exception names a value of
$c^*$. It also says "the positive solution" without saying that the solution
is unique. At $(2,1)$ there is no positive solution at all: the right-hand
side decreases towards 2 as $\xi\to0$ and never reaches 2.

**Fix:** "For $(k,\ell)\neq(2,1)$, $\xi_{k,\ell}$ is the unique positive
solution of ...". Uniqueness holds because the right-hand side increases
from $\ell+1$, and $k\ell>\ell+1$ exactly when $(k,\ell)\neq(2,1)$. The
argument does not need to be in the formula.

### 6. The `Similar tables` relation for T273 says a little more than T273 holds. Cheap.

> thresholds for the cores whose edge density obstructs orientability

T273 holds the densities at which $r$-cores *appear*. The obstruction in
this table is the density of that core *reaching* $\ell$, which happens
later. For $k=3$, the core appears at $c_{3,2}=0.818$ in T273, and
orientability fails at $c^*_{3,1}=0.918$ here. A reader could take the
relation to mean that T273 holds the obstruction thresholds themselves.

**Fix:** "the densities at which the $(\ell+1)$-cores appear; orientability
fails later, when the core's edge density reaches $\ell$".

## Noted only because I noticed

- **`HREF{T272}` rather than a slug.** The link renders as `href="T272"`,
  which resolves to `/T272` from `/T274`, so it works. Every other table in
  the corpus links by slug. Both targets are drafts, and the audit's
  published-to-draft check will stop a publication in the wrong order.
- **$c^*_{2,1}=1/2$ is stated five times:** in `formula-root`,
  `formula-threshold`, `comment-capacity-one`, entry $(2,1)$ and
  `rigour details`. Finding 5 removes one of them. The others are harmless.
- **Why this range?** `complete-note` gives $2\leq k\leq7$ and
  $1\leq\ell\leq6$ but not the reason. `rigour details` implies that it is
  the range of the source's printed table ("all rows in the printed range").
  Saying so in `complete-note` would be the argument the skill asks for.
- **Parameter titles use hashing words.** The parameters are "number of
  choices" and "bucket capacity", while the definition speaks of hypergraphs.
  `comment-orientation` connects the two vocabularies, and the parameter
  titles are the ones a hashing reader will recognise. I would leave them.
- **`CainSandersWormald` is cited only under `sources`.** No prose says what
  it contributes: presumably the case $k=2$. Its bib spells
  "k-orientiability". I believe that misspelling is in the published SODA
  title, so check before correcting it.
- **Entry `equals`.** Rows $(k,1)$ for $3\leq k\leq7$ are the same numbers
  as T272's rows $k$, by theorem. Two tables that agree because of a theorem
  should both answer a search, so `repeats` would be wrong. Linking the rows
  with `equals` is possible but not needed.
- **The $\xi_{k,\ell}$ values in the entry comments** are a different
  quantity from the one the table stores. T273 does the same with its
  minimiser, and the T273 critique left that alone. So would I.

## The audit

I agree that it has nothing mechanical to report on the fields it checks.
Findings 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 are about meaning, which it cannot see. It should
have found half of finding 4 and did not, for a reason that is general.

`_prose_faults` in `numberdb_app/management/commands/audit_table.py` matches
a family's title against prose after `_bare_title` removes the title's
`$...$` spans. It then searches the *unstripped* prose for the result. T272's
title "Satisfiability thresholds of random $k$-XORSAT" becomes
"Satisfiability thresholds of random -XORSAT". That string cannot occur in
prose that writes "random $k$-XORSAT", so any table whose title has
mathematics in the middle can never be reported as named and unlinked. T273's
title and many others in the corpus have the same shape. The fix would be to
strip `$...$` from the prose the same way before matching. This is a fault in
the site, not in T274. The task limited this run to this report, so I did not
append it to `docs/agent-environment.md`. It belongs there.
