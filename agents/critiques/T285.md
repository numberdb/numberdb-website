## The audit

The audit is clean. Nothing it checks is wrong: the header $\delta_g^+$ is
true of every row, the links resolve, and the page is one table. The audit
cannot see findings 1 to 4. They are about what the title and the prose claim,
and about what the corpus holds under other names.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The title names a different quantity from the one the table holds. Worth doing.

The title is "Minimum dilatations of pseudo-Anosov maps", which is $\delta_g$.
The header, the Definition and the source all say the rows are $\delta_g^+$,
the minimum over maps with orientable invariant foliations. The table's own
comment says "the unrestricted minimum $\delta_g$ is a different quantity".
For $g=3,4,5$ the unrestricted minimum is not known, and the title claims it.

The title is the first thing a reader sees and the heaviest field in search.
Somebody who searched "minimum dilatation" and landed here would read the
genus-3 row as $\delta_3$.

Fix: retitle to "Minimum dilatations of pseudo-Anosov maps with orientable
foliations". The keywords already carry "minimum dilatation" and "minimum
stretch factor", so a reader typing either still finds the table.

### 2. The comment on the convention hides the rows where the two minima agree. Worth doing.

The comment reads: "This table uses the orientable invariant-foliation
convention of Lanneau and Thiffeault. The unrestricted minimum $\delta_g$ is
a different quantity; they note evidence that $\delta_5<\delta_5^+$".

There are three problems with it.

- **"Convention" is the wrong word.** A convention is a choice of how to write
  one quantity. This is a restriction to a smaller class of maps, and it gives
  a different minimum. The second sentence says so, so the first one
  contradicts it.
- **Two rows are also $\delta_g$, and the page does not say so.** Theorem 1.1
  of the source reads $\delta_2=\delta_2^+$, the largest root of
  $x^4-x^3-x^2-x+1$, first proved by Cho and Ham. The source's introduction
  also recalls $\delta_1=(3+\sqrt5)/2$. So a reader holding the genus-2
  minimum dilatation has found exactly what they computed, but the page
  tells them it is "a different quantity". That is the most useful fact the
  table could give this reader, and it is missing.
- **"They note evidence that" leaves a reader guessing.** It could mean a
  proof, an example or a conjecture. I could not find the sentence in the
  part of the PDF I extracted. If it refers to a genus-5 map with
  non-orientable foliations and smaller dilatation, which is what Aaber–Dunfield
  in LT's bibliography suggests, then it is a strict inequality with a
  witness, and the comment should say that and cite it.

Fix: "$\delta_g^+$ is at least the minimum $\delta_g$ over all pseudo-Anosov
homeomorphisms of $\Sigma_g$. The two are equal for $g=1$ and $g=2$
CITE{LT}, so those rows are also $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$." Then add a
sentence on $g=5$ once the source has been read for the exact claim. If it
cannot be confirmed, remove that clause.

### 3. Every row is in another table, and the page names only two of them. Worth doing.

`comment-salem` says "The rows $g=4$ and $g=5$ also occur in the table of
Salem numbers less than $1.3$", and the T222 gloss says "holds several of the
same Salem numbers". In fact T222 holds every row from $g=2$ to $g=5$:

| $g$ | T222 keys with the same value |
|---|---|
| 2 | `[(3,4,8)]`, `[(3,5,5)]`, `[(4,4,4)]` |
| 3 | `[7,4]`, `[5,5]`, `[(3,3,4)]` |
| 4 | `[10,3]`, `[5,4]` |
| 5 | `[7,3]` |

The genus-1 row also has a counterpart elsewhere. T171's `cat` rows are the
Lyapunov exponents of Arnold's cat map, $\pm\log\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}$. The
cat-map matrix $\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ is the one Lanneau and
Thiffeault name as realising $\delta_1$. So `T171#cat,1` is
$\log\delta_1^+$, and the formula `formula-entropy` connects the two
directly. The T171 gloss instead says "stores logarithmic exponential growth
rates for standard maps". "Logarithmic exponential growth rates" is not a
phrase anybody uses. "Standard maps" reads as the Chirikov standard map, which
is one of T171's rows. And the gloss leaves out the one exact link between
the two tables.

A reader who arrives holding 1.40126836… from a Coxeter-group computation
reaches row $g=3$, which has no comment, and learns nothing about their
triangle group.

Fix, all small:
- **Row $g=1$:** "This is $(3+\sqrt5)/2$, the expanding eigenvalue of Arnold's
  cat map, whose Lyapunov exponent HREF{Lyapunov_exponents_of_classical_chaotic_systems#cat,1}[$\log\delta_1^+$]
  is in the table of Lyapunov exponents."
- **Rows $g=2$ to $5$:** one sentence each naming the triangle groups, for
  example "It is also the growth rate of the Coxeter triangle groups
  $\Delta(2,4,7)$ and $\Delta(2,5,5)$." For each group, either link it to
  T222 or name it in plain text. The T284 critique warned that an anchor
  holding `[` and `,` may confuse the `HREF{...}[caption]` parser. Preview
  one anchored link before writing the rest.
- **T222 gloss:** "its growth rates include every value here from $g=2$ to
  $g=5$, indexed by Coxeter diagram rather than by genus".
- **T171 gloss:** "holds the Lyapunov exponent of Arnold's cat map, which is
  $\log\delta_1^+$".
- **`comment-salem`:** extend "also occur in" to name T222, or remove that
  sentence and let the row comments and glosses carry the relation.

### 4. `complete-note` calls the values exact and does not say why the table stops at genus 5. Worth doing, small.

The page reads "Table is complete: no (it holds the exact values in the
contiguous range $1\leq g\leq5$)". The entries are 100-digit decimals of type
`R`, so "exact values" contradicts the type and the rigour line above it.
"Contiguous" adds nothing to "$1\leq g\leq5$". What a reader needs from this
sentence is whether $g=6$ is missing or unknown. The source's abstract answers
that: it finds $\delta_g^+$ for genus 3, 4 and 5 and gives only a lower bound
for genus 6 to 8.

Fix: "it holds $1\leq g\leq5$; for $g=6,7,8$ Lanneau and Thiffeault give only
lower bounds CITE{LT}". If a later paper has settled a higher genus, name it
and consider adding the row. I have not checked the later literature.

## Noted only

- **The rigour note's first sentence is not grammatical, and it is about
  mathematics rather than digits.** "The mathematical identification of these
  roots with $\delta_g^+$ is Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 and the genus-one statement
  recalled by Lanneau and Thiffeault." Theorem 1.1 is about $g=2$ and 1.2 about
  $3\leq g\leq5$, so this is right in substance. A reader would still
  understand "$\delta_2^+$ is Theorem 1.1 and $\delta_3^+,\ldots,\delta_5^+$
  are Theorem 1.2 of CITE{LT}" more easily. That fact could also sit in
  `formula-polynomials`, which cites LT already.
- **The program prints nothing when run as a script.** It ends with a bare
  dict expression, which shows in a Sage REPL but not under
  `sage -python`. Through `agents/sage.sh` it produced no output and exited 1.
  Wrapped in `print(...)` it printed the five values. It is also labelled
  `Python` while importing `sage.all`, so "Sage" would be more accurate.
- **Draft-to-draft links.** `HREF{T284}` in `comment-salem`, the Similar tables
  entry, and the row-5 link to Lehmer's number all point at T284, which is
  still a draft and gives 404 to anonymous readers. If T285 is published
  first, readers get three dead links.
- **"Lehmer problem" is a keyword that no sentence explains.** The T284
  critique noted the same thing. No other keyword or tag is a problem.
- **Row $g=1$ could link the golden ratio.** $(3+\sqrt5)/2=\varphi^2$, and T32
  holds $\varphi$ but not $\varphi^2$. The link would be to a neighbour of the
  value, not to the value itself, so I would not add it.

The Definition reads well. It says what $\delta_g^+$ is, precisely and in one
sentence, and then stops. The parameter describes the family ($g\geq1$), not
the run. The page renders cleanly.
