# T225, Volumes of the orientable cusped SnapPea census manifolds

Read 2026-09-13. Private draft. It has 301 entries of type `R`, rigour
`proven`, 100 digits: the whole `m` part of SnapPy's
`OrientableCuspedCensus`, in census order, one parameter `name`. Each entry
has a comment. Tags `volume` (4 other tables), `period`.

How it was read, and what could not be done:

- The skill was fetched from <https://numberdb.org/skill> and read in full.
- The SOCKS proxy at 127.0.0.1:1080 answered once, for the skill, and then
  refused every connection. Nothing was listening on 1080. This is already
  in `docs/agent-environment.md`. numberdb.org answered directly. `/T225`
  answers 404, as a private draft does. I read the document from
  `GET /api/table?id=T225` with the key. Its prose matches
  `generators/orientable-cusped-snappy-census-volumes/table.yaml`.
- **Rendering.** I rendered the draft through `/preview?table=` in eight
  pieces, each under 2 KB. Together they covered every section, each with
  the Links and References its `CITE`s need, plus the rows m003, m004,
  m006, m009, m010, m129 and m412. Every piece answered 200. Every `CITE`
  and `HREF` resolves. The entry link `#4,1` becomes `?entry=4,1`, and T141
  has that anchor. All four linked tables answer 200. No `<` appears in the
  prose and nothing is eaten. The one oddity is the site's, not the
  table's: a piece whose `Numbers` has no `Parameters` beside it shows the
  row names and no values.
- **MathJax.** All 47 distinct `$...$` fragments typeset without error in

  did raise an error.
- **Sage** (`agents/sage.sh`, builder image, SnapPy 3.3.2). The census has
  301 `m` names and at most 5 tetrahedra, as `complete-note` says. All 301

  gives m004 = `4_1` and m129 = `5^2_1`. The `Programs` snippet runs in Sage
  and prints the m004 volume as a `RealIntervalFieldElement`.
- **Sources.** SnapPy's own page on verified computation says they are "only
  available when using SnapPy inside SageMath" (finding 5). Crossref confirms
  the two cited references. The third reference has a wrong DOI (finding 6).
- **Data.** The 301 rows hold 219 distinct 100-digit strings. 133 rows fall
  into 51 groups that share one value, and the largest group has eight rows.

**audit_table.** It could not be run as a command. This runner has no
Django, and the checkout has no `.env` for `on-server.sh`, both already
recorded. Instead I ran the checks that need no database, using the same
local harness as the T224 critique. That covers `malformed_identifiers` and
`unresolved_citations` from `validate.py`, and the command's own rules for
editorial, positional, pointing and narration phrases. The unlinked-family
rule ran against the 222 public titles. It reports nothing. The Definition
is 225 characters with no comment phrase in it, and both tags reach other
tables. As far as its rules go I agree. But none of the findings below is a
phrase it looks for. Two of them are the kind it could catch and does not:
it never reads a Reference's `doi` (finding 6), and it never asks whether a
Reference is cited at all.

The table is well aimed. Somebody who gets a volume out of SnapPy and wants
to know which manifold it is will be served. The Definition defines and then
stops. The parameter describes the family rather than the run. The range is
in `complete-note`, in a clause that finishes its sentence. The keywords
cover both "SnapPy" and "SnapPea". The faults are in what the prose says
about particular rows: one false cross-reference, an account of repeated
volumes that is off by a factor of twenty-five, and the closed forms a reader
holding the two famous numbers would want.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The m129 comment says the prime-knot table holds the Whitehead link. It does not.

What a reader sees under m129: "This is the Whitehead link complement, also
named $5^2_1$ in [the table of prime-knot complement volumes].." The link
goes to T141, *Hyperbolic volumes of the prime knots with at most ten
crossings*.

Why it is wrong. T141 holds knots, and the Whitehead link has two
components. T141 has no $5^2_1$ row. Its only five-crossing row is $5_2$,
volume $2.8281\ldots$, so a reader who follows the link looking for
$5^2_1$ finds a different manifold under a similar-looking name. The
`SPECIAL_COMMENTS` entry in the generator was written by analogy with m004,
and m004 really is in T141.

Fix: drop the link and say where the name comes from. "It is the Whitehead
link complement, the link $5^2_1$ of Rolfsen's table." Worth doing: this is
the only false statement on the page.

### 2. The comments say two pairs of rows share a volume. 133 rows do, in 51 groups.

What a reader sees. `comment-repeats` reads: "Different census manifolds may
have the same volume. The manifolds $m003$ and $m004$ have equal volumes, and
$m009$ and $m010$ also have equal volumes." Three entry comments say "This
manifold has the same volume as ..." (m003, m009, m010). m004 does not say
it back.

Why it is wrong. The stored values say otherwise. m006 and m007 hold one
value, and so do m035 to m040. Eight rows (m125, m129, m130, m135, m136,
m137, m139, m140) hold the Whitehead link volume. The first case with no
comment is the third row of the table. Named as examples, the two pairs
suggest that coincidence is rare. It is the usual case: 133 of 301 rows
share their value with another row. The same thing happens in
`comment-arithmetic`: it says "$m129$ is twelve times its $D=-4$ entry",
and seven other rows hold that same number.

Fix: state the scale once, and drop the three per-row sentences. For
example: "Volumes repeat often in the census. The 301 rows hold 219 distinct
values, and the eight manifolds $m125$, $m129$, $m130$, $m135$, $m136$,
$m137$, $m139$ and $m140$ all have the volume of the Whitehead link
complement." Rows that agree to all 100 digits are evidence of equal volume,
not proof, so the sentence should claim no more than the source does. The
generator's `SPECIAL_COMMENTS` has to change too, or the next fill puts the
three sentences back. Worth doing.

### 3. The entry comments are notes, not sentences, and five end in "..".

What a reader sees, on every row: "1 cusp; 2 ideal tetrahedra; first
homology $\mathbb{Z}/5 + \mathbb{Z}$; This manifold has the same volume as
$m004$.." That is three fragments and a sentence, joined by semicolons, with
a capital after a semicolon and a doubled full stop. The generator builds it
as `"; ".join(pieces) + "."`, and the special pieces already end in a full
stop. So m003, m004, m009, m010 and m129 all end "..".

Why it matters. The skill's example of a note to oneself is "every rule with
$n\leq 30$ is here, nodes and weights, both halves", and this is the same
shape. It also sits on all 301 rows, under the value a reader came for. A
smaller point: `+` is how SnapPy prints a direct sum. In mathematics
$\mathbb{Z}/5 + \mathbb{Z}$ reads as a sum of sets, and $\oplus$ is the
symbol a reader expects.

Fix, in `comment()`: "It has 1 cusp, an ideal triangulation with 2
tetrahedra, and first homology $\mathbb{Z}/5\oplus\mathbb{Z}$." Put any
special sentence after that full stop. Worth doing. It is one line of the
generator and a refill.

### 4. The two volumes a reader is most likely to be holding are not named.

What a reader sees. A reader who arrives with 2.0298832 finds m003 and m004
and learns that m004 is the figure-eight knot complement. A reader with
3.6638624 finds m129 described as the Whitehead link complement. Neither
row gives the closed form. T141's own $4_1$ row does: "$\mathrm{Vol}=2\,
\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$". The Clausen table holds both constants by name. Its
row `2,1/3` is "Gieseking's constant, the volume of the regular ideal
tetrahedron", and its row `2,1/2` is "Catalan's constant $G$". Instead, the
`Similar tables` line for that table says "the Bloch-Wigner and Lobachevsky
values that give special ideal-tetrahedron volumes". It names two functions
the Clausen table does not hold under those names, and it points at no row.

A second, smaller fault in the same place: the m004 entry comment links the
whole of T141 ("the row $4_1$ in [the table ...]") where it means one row.
`comment-arithmetic` already uses `#4,1` correctly.

Fix:
- Under m003 and m004 write "$\mathrm{Vol}=2\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$, twice
  HREF{Values_of_the_Clausen_functions_at_rational_multiples_of#2,1/3}[Gieseking's
  constant]". This is twice the volume of the regular ideal tetrahedron.
- Under m129 write "$\mathrm{Vol}=4G=4\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)$", linked to
  `#2,1/2`.
- Change the Clausen relation to name those two rows.
- Point the m004 link at `#4,1`.

Worth doing. These are the values that reach people from outside SnapPy.

### 5. The `Programs` snippet is labelled Python, and it only runs in Sage.

What a reader sees: `(P1) Python`, then `from snappy import Manifold ...
M.volume(verified=True, bits_prec=400)`.

Why it is wrong. SnapPy's verified-computation page says: "Note that
verified computations are only available when using SnapPy inside
SageMath." The `rigour details` also say the method "returns a Sage
interval". A reader who runs the snippet in plain Python, as the label
tells them to, gets an error rather than the number. T141 carries the same
call and labels it `Sage`. I ran the snippet in Sage and it prints the m004
interval. I could not test plain Python, because this runner has no SnapPy
outside the Sage image. So the claim rests on SnapPy's documentation.

Fix: `language: Sage`. Worth doing; one word.

### 6. The HIKMOT reference has a DOI that does not resolve, and nothing cites it.

What a reader sees in References: Hoffman et al., "Verified computations for
hyperbolic 3-manifolds", with a doi link.

Why it is wrong. `https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2015.1022840` answers
404. Crossref gives the paper, Experimental Mathematics 25 (2016) 66-78, as
**10.1080/10586458.2015.1029599**. The arXiv id `1310.3410` is right. No
`CITE{HIKMOT}` appears anywhere either, so this is the "bibliography nobody
points at" the skill calls furniture, and a broken piece of it.

Fix: correct the DOI and cite it where it belongs. In `rigour details`,
after "verified `volume(...)` method", add "which certifies the shapes by
the interval method of CITE{HIKMOT}". Worth doing: a dead link is a visible
fault.

### 7. The formula does not say which shapes.

What a reader sees: "$\mathrm{Vol}(M)=\sum_i D(z_i)$ for an ideal
triangulation of $M$ with tetrahedron shapes $z_i$ in the upper half plane".

Why it matters. Read literally, this is true for *any* shapes in the upper
half plane. The shapes meant are the solution of the gluing and completeness
equations, the ones that give the complete hyperbolic structure. The
restriction to the upper half plane is also stronger than the formula
needs. It does hold for these 301 rows, since every census triangulation is
positively oriented.

Fix: "where $z_i$ are the shapes of the complete hyperbolic structure on an
ideal triangulation of $M$". Worth doing: it is one phrase, and without it
the formula is not a formula for $\mathrm{Vol}(M)$.

## Noted only

- **The Bianchi relation in `Similar tables`**: "arithmetic cusped orbifold
  covolumes of which some entries here are small-index multiples". The
  phrase "small-index" asserts a subgroup index that the table nowhere
  states or supports. `comment-arithmetic` says "three times" and "twelve
  times", which is all that was checked. "of which $m009$, $m010$ and
  $m129$ hold rational multiples" would say only that.
- **Census names are typeset as mathematics**: $m004$, $m004(0,0)$, $o9$,
  $s$, $v$ in italic. The Definition puts
  $\mathtt{OrientableCuspedCensus}$ in typewriter, so the names read
  differently from the census they come from. The row labels themselves
  print as plain text. Cosmetic.
- **The parameter's constraint** joins two clauses with a semicolon, and the
  second is a convention rather than a constraint. It is comprehensible as
  it stands.
- **`rigour details`** says "Before the draft was filled, all rows were
  compared...". "The draft" is a word from this site's workflow, and it
  stops being true on publication. "Every row was also compared..." says the
  same thing.
- **`comment-range`** says "the manifolds in the original SnapPea census",
  which also held the nonorientable ones. "the orientable manifolds of the
  original SnapPea census" is exact.
