Nothing was listening on `127.0.0.1:1080`, so the SOCKS proxy the prompt calls

they were the same bytes, which is not knowable in advance — my first attempt
returned `HTTP 000 size=0` while the stale file sat there looking like a

I read the document through `GET /api/table?id=T379` with the key, and rebuilt
the page with the sqlite recipe from `docs/agent-environment.md`
(`/tmp/t378_render.py` with the tid changed by one `sed`, a fresh `site.tgz`,
`agents/sage.sh` with `NUMBERDB_REMOTE=local`, both range patches and the
`_sync_tags` replacement). It worked first time: **36 records, status 200,
43,346 bytes of HTML**, tags `['groups', 'probability theory']`. Everything
quoted below as "what a reader sees" is out of that HTML.

The table holds 36 entries, $G=U,O,USp$ each over $k=1,\ldots,12$, in that
order; type `Q`, rigour `exact`. The unitary and orthogonal columns are
integers throughout and only the symplectic column is genuinely rational,
which is why `Q` is the right type.

`GET /api/table/T379/audit` returns one finding, about the three named values
of `group`. **I disagree with it**, and the last section says why — but it
fired on a real weakness in the document, and the fix for finding 1 below is
also the answer to the audit.

## What I checked hardest, and found right

* **The page's three formulas reproduce the page's own 36 rows, exactly.** I
  evaluated (2), (3) and (4) in plain `Fraction`s against every stored value:
  36 rows, 0 mismatches. Formula (2)'s prose claim, "$g_U(1),\ldots,g_U(5) =
  1,2,42,24024,701149020$", is what (2) itself produces and what the first
  five unitary rows hold. This is not a check against an outside source — the
  build did that — it is a check that the page agrees with itself, which is
  the thing a reader can do and the thing that silently rots when a formula is
  edited.

* **Nothing is eaten and no TeX is mangled.** No bare `<` or `&` inside any
  `$…$` in the body, no `--`, no `Math input error` shape, no `(Unknown key)`,
  no `argument ()`, no unresolved `CITE` or `HREF`. All 36 entry anchors are
  present and unique (`id="U,1"` … `id="USp,12"`, checked against the document's
  own `Numbers` keys); the parameter lines read `$G$ — matrix group` and
  `$k$ — moment parameter ($k\geq1$)`; the `rigour details` fold renders and
  opens.

* **The parameters describe the family and the run is in `complete-note`.**
  `k` carries `$k\geq1$`, not the twelve values computed; `group` carries all
  three symmetry types, which is the family. The run is in `complete: no (it
  holds $G=U,O,USp$ for every positive integer $1\leq k\leq12$)`, which reads
  as a clause finishing the rendered sentence. This is the fault that recurs
  most in this corpus and it is not on this page.

* **The column labels are right, and the number header is a symbol rather than
  a fallback.** There is no `param-latex`, so the group column prints `$U$`,
  `$O$`, `$USp$` — the values themselves, which is correct for a parameter
  whose values are names — and the value column heads `$g_G(k)$`, a symbol
  that is true of every row. The skill's structural tell for "this is two
  tables" (a column falling back on the word `value`) is absent.

## Findings

Findings 1, 2 and 3 are one problem seen from three sides: **the page never
says what its numbers are the leading factor *of*, and its title, its number
header and its definition each name a different thing.** Findings 4 to 8 are
small and independent.

### 1. The asymptotic these numbers are the leading coefficient of is never written down, and the Definition is Formula (1) rearranged

What a reader sees, as the first prose on the page:

> **Definition.** For positive integer $k$ and compact symmetry type
> $G\in\{U,O,USp\}$, $g_G(k)=e_G(k)!\,f_G(k)$ is the exact rational CFKRS
> random-matrix factor for characteristic-polynomial moments [2]. The
> parameter $G$ chooses which symmetry type the same factor is attached to.

and, two sections down:

> (1) $f_G(k)=g_G(k)/e_G(k)!$ is the random-matrix factor in the leading term,
> with $e_U(k)=k^2$, $e_O(k)=k(k-1)/2$, and $e_{USp}(k)=k(k+1)/2$.

Those two sentences are each other's rearrangement. The Definition defines $g$
from $f$; (1) defines $f$ from $g$. Between them they define nothing, and $f_G$
— the object the whole page is about — is never given a meaning anywhere. (1)
calls it "the random-matrix factor in the leading term", and the leading term
of what is not on the page: **no integral, no group, no power, no limit, and
no $N$ appears anywhere in T379.** The only thing that pins any value down is
the three product formulas (2)–(4), and a definition that has to be read out of
the `Formulas` section is not a definition.

Two consequences a reader meets directly:

* **For $O$ and $USp$ — 24 of the 36 rows — the page never says what $k$
  indexes.** Comment (5) is the only statement about the indexing and it is
  about $U$ alone: "The parameter is $k$, not the full unitary exponent $2k$".
  The orthogonal and symplectic moments are not taken of $|\Lambda|^{2k}$, and
  nothing says what they are taken of. Two people would not build the $O$ half
  of this table from its definition.
* **$G$ is not pinned either.** "$O$" could be $O(N)$, $SO(2N)$ or $SO(2N+1)$,
  and the CFKRS factors differ by exactly such choices. A reader has to reverse
  the question and ask which choice makes (4) come out right.

Smallest fix, and the batch already has the template: **both sibling tables
carry a `formula-moment`.** T377's is

    formula-moment: Conjecturally, $\frac1T\int_0^T |\zeta(1/2+it)|^{2k}\,dt
      \sim c_k(\log T)^{k^2}$, with $c_k=a_k f_U(k)$.

and both T377 and T378 cite it from their Definitions. T379 needs the same
field — the three group averages with their exponents $e_G(k)$ and the limit
$f_G(k)=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-e_G(k)}\langle\cdot\rangle_G$ — and the Definition
should cite that instead of citing (1)'s rearrangement. Formula (1) then
becomes what it should be: the conversion between $f$ and $g$, and nothing else.

**Worth doing, and it is the finding I would fix first.** It is one new field
and one edited sentence, and it is the difference between a table a reader can
use and a table whose numbers are defined only by the code that made them.

### 2. The title says the table holds moments; it holds the leading factor of a moment asymptotic

The title is *Moments of the characteristic polynomials of unitary, orthogonal
and symplectic matrices*. The table holds no moment. The second moment of
$|\Lambda|$ over $U(N)$ is $N+1$; the stored $U$, $k=1$ row is $1$.

The skill's rule is that three places say what a table holds — the title, the
definition and the number header — and here all three say something different:

| where | says the table holds |
|---|---|
| Title | *Moments of the characteristic polynomials* |
| Definition | *the exact rational CFKRS random-matrix factor* |
| `number-header` | $g_G(k)$, a symbol that appears in neither of the above |

Both siblings get this right and say so in their titles: *Arithmetic factors
$a_k$ in the moments of the Riemann zeta function*, *Leading constants $c_k$
of the moments of the Riemann zeta function*. Each names the quantity, gives
its symbol, and then says which asymptotic it comes from.

Smallest fix: the same shape. Something like *Random-matrix factors $g_G(k)$
in the moments of the characteristic polynomials of unitary, orthogonal and
symplectic matrices* — longer, but it keeps every word search needs (all three
group names are in the title at top index weight, which matters for finding 8
below) and it stops claiming the moments themselves.

**Worth doing.** A title is frozen only in the sense that a slug is; this one
is a draft and the cost is a sentence.

### 3. What is stored is $e_G(k)!\,f_G(k)$, and $f_G(k)$ is the number a reader will be holding

Nothing on the page says why the factorial is there. For $G=U$ it has a good
reason that the corpus itself demonstrates: $e_U(k)!f_U(k)$ is the integer
sequence $1, 2, 42, 24024, 701149020$, which is the form CFKRS quote and the
form T378 already prints in its own `formula-integer-random-matrix`. That
reason does not carry over. The same scaling applied to $USp$ gives
$1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 3/4, 143/16, \ldots$ — not an integer, not a sequence anybody
quotes, and (for the first four rows) a number that matches everything.

The reader this matters to is the one the database exists for. Somebody who
has computed a symplectic characteristic-polynomial moment and divided by
$N^{e_{USp}(k)}$ holds $f_{USp}(k)$, not $g_{USp}(k)$; at $k=3$ that is
$1/2880$, and the stored row is $1/4$. Getting from one to the other means
multiplying by $6!$, which is not among the half, the two and the $\pi$ a
reader tries before giving up. And $f_G(k)$ is held nowhere else: a text search
for "random matrix" reaches T377, T378 and T171, and T377 and T378 both *use*
$f_U(k)$ in their formulas without storing it.

I am not going to tell a reviewer which quantity to store — that is their call,
and the unitary convention is a real one. What the page owes a reader is a
sentence saying that the stored quantity is $f_G$ scaled by $e_G(k)!$, that
this is the convention in which the unitary values are the familiar integers,
and that the scaling clears denominators for $U$ and $O$ and not for $USp$
(which is itself a fact the page states nowhere and which a reader scanning the
three columns will notice). The larger option — store $f_G$ too, as entries
rather than as a formula, since $f$ and $g$ determine each other exactly — is
the skill's "one number in two conventions, one table" and would double the
table to 72 entries.

**Worth doing** as a sentence. The storage question is the reviewer's.

### 4. Three of the four numbered sources are never cited from the prose

The page prints four numbered sources. Counting `CITE{}` across the Definition,
all four formulas, both comments, both `Similar tables` relations and
`rigour details`:

| | source | cited |
|---|---|---|
| [1] | Keating and Snaith, *Random matrix theory and $\zeta(1/2+it)$* | **0 times** |
| [2] | Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein and Snaith, *Integral moments of $L$-functions* | 4 times |
| [3] | Mehta and Normand, *Moments of the characteristic polynomial in the three ensembles of random matrices* | **0 times** |
| [4] | Wikipedia: Random matrix | **0 times** |

Three quarters of the bibliography is furniture, and one of the three is the
paper the `Keywords` name ("Keating-Snaith"), so a reader who arrives by
searching that phrase finds the name nowhere on the page they land on.

This is not cosmetic here, because of what [3] is. `rigour details` says the
non-unitary rows "were checked by extracting the leading terms from the
corresponding CFKRS random-matrix moment formulas after the conductor
normalisation" — that is, against the same formulas the generator evaluates,
which is not an independent check; and "the conductor normalisation" names a
notion (matching $N$ to $\log$ conductor) that appears nowhere else on the
page. Mehta and Normand is exactly the moments of the characteristic
polynomial in all *three* ensembles. Citing it at (3) and (4) gives the
symplectic and orthogonal rows the independent source the unitary rows get from
(2)'s printed list, and gives `rigour details` something concrete to point at.

Smallest fix: `CITE{KeatingSnaith}` at the unitary formula, `CITE{MehtaNormand}`
at (3) and (4), and `CITE{WikiRMT}` beside the first "random-matrix" in the
Definition.

**Worth doing.** Note that the audit does *not* catch this — see the last
section.

### 5. There is no `HREF` in any prose field, and comment (6) points at tables it does not name

Comment (6) ends:

> The half-integer rows used by the Euler-product tables in this family are not
> included here.

Three things a reader has to guess. "This family" names nothing. "The
Euler-product tables" is plural and only one of the two is an Euler product —
T377 holds $a_k$, which is; T378 holds $c_k$, which is a Barnes $G$ quotient
times it. And a reader who has just been told that half-integer rows exist
somewhere is given no way to get to them from the sentence that says so. The
`Similar tables` block does link both, at the very bottom of the page, which
is not where the sentence is.

The document contains **no `HREF{}` at all** outside `Similar tables`. Besides
T377 and T378, T93 (*Values of the Barnes $G$-function at rational numbers*)
holds every $G(1+k)$ and $G(1+2k)$ the unitary factor needs, and both siblings
link it from their prose; T379 mentions neither it nor the function.

Smallest fix, in comment (6):

    The half-integer values of $k$ held by HREF{T377}[the table of arithmetic
    factors $a_k$] and HREF{T378}[the table of leading constants $c_k$] are not
    included here.

`HREF{T377}` is the form T378 already uses and it resolves.

**Worth doing.**

### 6. The `Programs` block raises `NameError` when it is run as a file

What a reader sees, in a block labelled `Sage`:

    from sage.arith.misc import factorial
    from sage.rings.rational_field import QQ

    k = 5
    QQ(factorial(k*k)) * prod(
        QQ(factorial(j)) / QQ(factorial(k+j))
        for j in range(k))

Two named-module imports rather than `from sage.all import *` is the skill's
own house style, and it says the block is meant to be saved and run with
`sage -python`. Run that way, exactly as printed, inside `agents/sage.sh`:

    FAILED: NameError name 'prod' is not defined

`prod` is neither of the two things imported. It is a Sage *session* global
(`sage.all` has it) and it is re-exported by `sage.arith.misc`, but importing
one name from a module does not bind the others. Pasted into an interactive
`sage`, the block works and the bare final expression echoes `701149020`;
saved to a file, it raises, and even with `prod` in scope it prints nothing,
because a bare expression is not a `print`.

Smallest fix, two lines:

    from sage.misc.misc_c import prod
    ...
    print(QQ(factorial(k*k)) * prod(...))

While there: the block computes $g_U$ only, so a reader of the orthogonal or
symplectic rows — two thirds of the table — is given no incantation at all.
The three products are four lines between them.

**Worth doing.** It fails loudly rather than printing a wrong number, which is
why I have it sixth rather than first.

### 7. Formula (2) writes `$k^2!$` where the sibling writes `$(k^2)!$`

> (2) $g_U(k)=k^2!\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{j!}{(k+j)!}$

Rendered, that is $k^2!$ — which is $(k^2)!$ or $k^{2!}$ depending on the
reader. It is the only factorial on the page whose argument is not bracketed:
(3) and (4) both write `\left(k(k+1)/2\right)!`. T378's
`formula-integer-random-matrix` writes the same quantity as `$(k^2)!f_U(k)$`,
with the parentheses.

Smallest fix: two characters, `$(k^2)!$`.

**Worth doing**, and it is the cheapest thing on this list.

### 8. The three tables of one batch share no tag

T377 and T378 are tagged `number theory`, `L-function`. T379 is tagged
`groups`, `probability theory`. Nothing but `Similar tables` connects the
three, and the skill counts tags as one of the four ways a split family stays
joined — the one that works sideways, for a reader browsing rather than
following a name.

A reader on `/tags/L-function` reading about $c_k=a_k f_U(k)$ has no way to
reach the table of the random-matrix factor, and `groups` is a thin description
of a table whose subject is random matrices rather than group theory. There is
no random-matrix tag to use: `/tags/random-matrix`, `/tags/random_matrix` and
`/tags/random matrix` all answer 404, while `/tags/L-function`,
`/tags/groups` and `/tags/probability theory` answer 200. A new one needs three
tables and I have not counted three.

Smallest fix: add `L-function`, which is what the two tables this one exists to
serve already carry.

**Worth a decision** rather than worth doing — it is a judgement about what the
table is about, and the reviewer is better placed to make it than I am.

## Noted only, because I noticed them

* **`Parameters` calls $G$ a "matrix group"; the `Definition` calls it a
  "compact symmetry type".** Two words for one thing, in the two places a
  reader meets it first. The skill asks for the table's own word. One of them
  should go.

* **The formulas run $U$, $USp$, $O$; everything else runs $U$, $O$, $USp$.**
  The title, the definition's $G\in\{U,O,USp\}$, the rows and `complete-note`
  all use the second order. A reader scrolling from the orthogonal block to
  check it against a formula hits (3), which is symplectic. Swapping two fields
  fixes it; the `CITE` labels are already named rather than numbered, so
  nothing else moves.

* **`$USp$` sets as math-italic $U\!S\!p$**, which reads as a product of three
  variables. `\mathrm{USp}` is the usual setting. $U$ and $O$ are fine as they
  are.

* **`complete-note` says what is held and not why $k$ stops at 12.** Both
  siblings stop at 12 too, so this is a house habit rather than this table's
  fault — but $g_U(12)$ is 103 characters and $g_U(15)$ would be very much
  longer, which is a real reason and one clause long.

* **Eight rows are numbers that match everything**: $O$ at $k=1,2,3,4$ is
  $1,1,1,2$ and $USp$ at $k=1,2,3,4$ is $1/2,1/4,1/4,3/4$. The skill warns
  about tables of such values. These are the honest first terms of a real
  family and the large rows are as distinctive as anything in the corpus; I
  would not remove them. Worth knowing when reading finding 3, since the
  $f_G$ normalisation would not have this shape.

* **"the exact rational … factor" in the Definition** is a claim about the
  type, which `Data properties` already carries as `type: Q`, `rigour: exact`.

* **Nothing says the random-matrix side is a theorem.** T377 and T378 both
  carry a `comment-conjecture` saying their asymptotic is known for $k=1,2$ and
  conjectural otherwise. T379 rightly carries no such caveat, because these
  values are theorems — but that contrast is the first thing a reader crossing
  from T378 will want, and it is nowhere. One sentence, low value.

* **I could not see the attachment list.** `/files/T379` answers 200 to a
  bearer token but renders the anonymous page (it shows "Login"), and
  `/api/table/T379/files`, `/api/files?id=T379` and
  `/api/table/T379/attachments` all 404. So I cannot say whether a generator is
  attached. The audit's rule for that did not fire, which means either one is
  attached or the entries were not recorded as computed by the client.

## On the audit

`GET /api/table/T379/audit` returns one finding:

> parameter group takes 3 names (O, U, USp), each carrying entries of its own,
> so this reads as 3 tables sharing one title and one value column. If those
> are 3 named quantities, make 3 tables and relate them in Similar tables; if
> they are one object in several parts, or one number in several conventions,
> say so in the definition and leave them together

**The rule is right and its conclusion is wrong here.** Four reasons, in the
order I weighted them:

1. **The skill's own test points the other way.** "Does the parameter name
   *what the number is of*, or *which quantity is taken of it*?" $G$ names what
   the number is of — the symmetry type the average is taken over. That is the
   first kind, and it belongs in one table, like one entropy for each of
   twenty-two distributions. The quantity is the same functional of each group:
   the leading coefficient of $N^{-e_G(k)}$ times a characteristic-polynomial
   moment.
2. **The structural tell is absent.** The rule's own evidence is a value column
   that falls back on the word `value` because no symbol is true of every row.
   This column heads `$g_G(k)$`, and there is no `param-latex`.
3. **The findability argument that forces the $\operatorname{Ei}$/$\operatorname{li}$/$\operatorname{Si}$
   split does not apply.** There, two of six functions were not findable by
   name inside the joint table. Here "unitary", "orthogonal" and "symplectic"
   are all in the title, at the index's top weight, so each of the three is
   reachable by its own name without splitting anything.
4. **The split would be expensive and buy nothing.** Three tables of twelve
   entries, each restating the same definition, the same three references and
   the same conversion to $f_G$.

But the audit fired on something real. The skill says that where the answer is
the good kind you "say so in the definition", and the sentence this table
offers is

> The parameter $G$ chooses which symmetry type the same factor is attached to.

which is not true as written: $g_U(2)=2$, $g_O(2)=1$ and $g_{USp}(2)=1/4$ are
not the same factor. What makes this one table is that $f_G$ is defined by *one
limit* for each $G$ — and that limit is exactly what finding 1 says is missing.
**Fix finding 1 and the audit's question is answered properly rather than
waved at.**

Two things the audit does not look at, from the eight findings above:

* **It does not check a Reference or a Link that nothing cites.** I expected it
  to — the T378 critique of yesterday states that it does — so I read
  `numberdb_app/management/commands/audit_table.py`. The citation rule is
  `unresolved_citations`, which checks `CITE{x}` for an `x` that exists. There
  is no rule in the other direction, and T379's three uncited sources (finding
  4) pass silently. That is a cheap mechanical rule and worth having: a
  `References` or `Links` key that no prose field cites.
* **It does not parse a `Programs` block.** Finding 6 is a `NameError` in a
  field the audit treats as opaque text. This is the second batch running where
  the printed program does not run as printed — T378's PARI block had an
  unescaped line continuation — and both were caught by running the bytes the
  document holds.

Findings 1, 2, 3 and 5 are judgements about what the prose owes a reader, and I
would not expect a rule to make them.

## What I did not do

I did not re-verify the stored values against an independent source; the build
did that, `verify` does it again, and the prompt is right that repeating it is
the least useful thing available. The two runs I made in the Sage container
were about the page rather than the values: whether the formulas printed on it
reproduce the rows printed on it (they do, all 36), and whether the program
printed on it runs (it does not).

I changed nothing in the table.
