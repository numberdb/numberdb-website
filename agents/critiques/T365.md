# T365: a completeness note that never says the table is complete, and an index nobody can reproduce

Read on 2026-09-20, draft `T365`, "Residues of Dedekind zeta functions of
quartic fields", slug `Residues_of_Dedekind_zeta_functions_of_quartic_fields`,
456 entries over 405 discriminants with $|D|\leq 5000$, 100 digits a row,
`type: R`, `rigour: proven`.

The table is in good shape. It renders, the audit is clean, the entries are
the right size and in the order the document claims, every count the prose
states is true of the data, and the PARI program in it runs and reproduces a
stored value. Two sentences a reader leans on do not carry their weight: the
completeness note, which is the sentence somebody checks to see whether their
field is here, and the comment defining $k$, which is the sentence somebody
reads to find out *which* field a row is. Both were inherited from the elder
sibling table and lost something on the way.

## How I read it

* The SOCKS proxy on `127.0.0.1:1080` served `/skill` once and then refused
  every connection (`curl` exit 7, six tries; `ALL_PROXY` empty). Direct
  `curl https://numberdb.org/...` worked for everything after that, as the
  notes of 2026-09-16 and 2026-09-17 in `docs/agent-environment.md` say.
  Nothing new to add there.
* `/T365` is 404 anonymously. The document came from `GET /api/table?id=T365`
  with the key on stdin.
* The rendering came from `GET /preview?table=<yaml>` in nine slices, each
  carrying `Title`, `Parameters` and at least one entry, each under the 4094
  byte request line. All answered 200 except one 4.3 KB slice, which I split.
* The audit came from `GET /api/table/T365/audit`: `findings: []`,
  `clean: true`.
* TeX: every `$...$` fragment in the document and in all 456 entry comments,
  501 distinct, typeset with `mathjax-full@3` in node. 0 errors; a control
  fragment `\frac{1}{` was flagged.
* One Sage run through `agents/sage.sh`, to run the table's own PARI block.
* Everything else is plain Python over the stored document.

## Do I agree with the audit?

Yes with what it says. Its silence on this table is mostly earned: the
`Definition` is one object and two sentences, the value header `$\kappa_K$` is
true of every row, no parameter carries a `param-latex`, the parameter
constraints describe the family rather than the run ("$D$ the discriminant of
a quartic field", "$k\geq 1$" -- not a list of the 405 discriminants that
happen to be here), and all three `HREF` targets resolve (200). The checks it
does not make are the ones below: nothing in it can see that a clause has lost
its antecedent, or that a notation used on every row of the table is defined
nowhere in it.

I also checked, and am **not** reporting, the things the audit cannot reach:

* Every count in the prose is true of the entries. 456 entries; signatures 20
  of $(4,0)$, 180 of $(2,1)$, 256 of $(0,2)$; $w_K=2$ for 338, $4$ for 44, $6$
  for 71, and one each of $8$, $10$, $12$. The order is by $|D|$ with positive
  $D$ first, exactly as `rigour details` says, with no violations; $k$ runs
  $1,2,\ldots$ contiguously under every $D$; $\min|D|=117$, $\max|D|=4999$.
  The smallest $|D|$ at each signature is $117$, $-275$ and $725$, which are
  the right three.
* The LMFDB label in every one of the 456 entry comments agrees with that
  entry's own $D$, signature and $k$ -- all four components, all 456 rows.
* The PARI block runs. Under PARI 2.17.1 it returns
  `0.33983727824052353546427878115897748532897723332738514842937970878880772711223324`
  from both of its last two lines, matching the stored $D=125$ value to the 80
  digits it asks for, and `bnfcertify` returns 1.
* Size: the entries block is 103 KB, under the 160 KB the skill aims at, and
  456 entries of 101 characters is a reference rather than a dump.

## 1. The completeness note says "the same" and there is nothing for it to mean (worth doing)

What a reader sees under **Data properties**:

> Table is complete: no (it holds the same 456 quartic fields with
> $|D|\leq 5000$ under the same $D$ and $k$)

The same as *what*? Nothing on the page names another set of 456 fields, and
nothing names another $D$ and $k$ to be the same as. The sentence is the elder
sibling's, where it had a referent: T159, the cubic table, has a `Similar
tables` entry for the regulators table reading "the other factor of the class
number formula, for the same 515 fields under the same $D$ and $k$", and there
"the same" points at the table being related to. Carried into `complete-note`, where it renders
alone inside a parenthesis, it points at nothing. There is no
`Regulators of quartic fields` (404), so a reader cannot even guess the
missing half.

Worse than the dangling reference: the note never says the thing it is for.
It does not claim the table holds *every* quartic field of that size, so a
reader who has a field with $|D|=3000$ and cannot find it does not learn
whether the table is exhaustive or merely has 456 of them. The table is
exhaustive, and `rigour details` proves it in passing: the enumeration runs
`nflist` over `C4`, `V4`, `D4`, `A4` and `S4`, which is every transitive group
of degree four, so every quartic field with $|D|\leq 5000$ was offered.

The cubic table's own note is the model, and says it in one clause: "it holds
every cubic field with $|D|\leq 3000$, the 419 complex and the 96 totally real
ones".

Smallest fix, one field, `Data properties: complete-note`:

    it holds every quartic field with $|D|\leq 5000$: 20 totally real, 180 of
    signature $(2,1)$ and 256 totally complex

Those three counts are the ones the data has.

## 2. The rule that defines $k$ does not determine $k$ (worth doing)

Comment (1), the only place the second parameter is pinned down:

> The fields of discriminant $D$ are numbered $k=1,2,\ldots$ by reducing their
> defining polynomials with PARI's `polredabs` and sorting the reduced
> polynomials lexicographically by coefficient vector.

"Coefficient vector" has two readings and PARI ships both: `Vec` gives
$(1,a_3,a_2,a_1,a_0)$, highest degree first, and `Vecrev` gives
$(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,1)$, lowest first. They are different orders, and they
disagree here. 45 of the 405 discriminants carry more than one field (40 carry
two, four carry three, one carries four), and on **18 of those 45** the two
readings give different numbering. A reader who takes the other one reads the
wrong row: at $D=1040$, $k=1$ is $a^4-3a^2-2a+5$ under the stored order and
$a^4+a^2-4a+4$ under the other; at $D=2304$ the stored order is
$a^4-4a^2+1$, $a^4+9$, $a^4+4a^2+1$, and the other reading moves $a^4+9$ from
$k=2$ to $k=3$.

The generator sorts on `(a_3, a_2, a_1, a_0)` (`_coeff_key`, highest degree
first, leading coefficient dropped). The cubic table states exactly that,
which is where this sentence came from and what it dropped: "in lexicographic
order of the coefficient vectors $(a_2,a_1,a_0)$ of their reduced defining
polynomials $x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0$".

Smallest fix, in comment (1):

    ... and sorting the reduced polynomials lexicographically by the
    coefficient vector $(a_3,a_2,a_1,a_0)$ of $x^4+a_3x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0$.

Worth adding while that sentence is open, because it is free and because the
cubic table had to say the opposite: **$k$ agrees with the last component of
the LMFDB label in every one of the 456 entries**. A reader holding
`4.0.2304.2` can go straight to $k=2$. The cubic table warns that the LMFDB's
index is *not* its order; a reader who knows that table will assume the same
caveat here unless told otherwise.

## 3. Every entry carries a 4T label and nothing says what a 4T label is (worth doing)

Comment (3) says the entry comment gives "the Galois group of the Galois
closure as a 4T label", and each of the 456 rows then reads `4T1`, `4T2`,
`4T3`, `4T4` or `4T5`. The table never says which group any of those is. A
reader who does not already know the transitive-group numbering learns only
that two fields have the same something.

This is the skill's "define notation where you use it", and the table already
knows the answer: `rigour details` enumerates by `nflist("C4", ...)`,
`nflist("V4", ...)`, `nflist("D4", ...)`, `nflist("A4", ...)`,
`nflist("S4", ...)`, in the order that is 4T1 to 4T5. The counts here are 7
cyclic, 30 with group $V_4$, 195 with $D_4$, 2 with $A_4$ and 222 with $S_4$.

Smallest fix, one clause at the end of comment (3):

    ... as a 4T label: 4T1 is $C_4$, 4T2 is $V_4$, 4T3 is $D_4$, 4T4 is $A_4$
    and 4T5 is $S_4$.

Naming them also earns the table a sentence it currently cannot write: the
seven 4T1 fields are the cyclic quartic fields, for which $\zeta_K/\zeta$
factors into Dirichlet $L$-functions, which is the relation its own third
`Similar tables` entry claims.

## 4. Three exceptional fields are counted and two are named, in an order that pairs them wrongly (worth doing, small)

Comment (2):

> Among these 456 fields, $w_K=2$ for 338 fields, $w_K=4$ for 44 fields,
> $w_K=6$ for 71 fields, and the three remaining fields have $w_K=8$, $10$ and
> $12$. This factor is visible in the class number formula (1); the entries
> with $D=125$ and $D=144$ are the cyclotomic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$ and
> $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})$.

A reader who has just been handed the list $8,10,12$ and is then handed two
entries reads them in parallel and takes $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$ for the
$w_K=8$ field. It is the $w_K=10$ one. The third is never named at all, and
it is the interesting one to omit: $D=256$, $a^4+1=0$, which is
$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$ with $w_K=8$. All three exceptional fields are
cyclotomic, and the sentence can say so completely and in matching order:

    ... and the three remaining fields are the cyclotomic fields
    $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$ at $D=256$, $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$ at $D=125$ and
    $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})$ at $D=144$, with $w_K=8$, $10$ and $12$.

"This factor" in the same comment points backwards rather than naming: write
"$w_K$ is a factor in the class number formula (1)". The symbol is shorter
than the phrase pointing at it.

## 5. The `Links` list carries the request that produced the table (worth doing, small)

Rendered, the Links block ends:

> [6] Requested in numberdb-data#42

The skill is explicit: a "table wanted" issue is answered in the issue, and
"the table carries no trace of the request: it is encyclopedic, and who asked
for it is not a fact about the mathematics", with an exception for an issue
that carries a derivation, a convention or a source. Issue 42 is titled
"Residue at s=1 of Dedekind zeta functions", is closed, and has an empty body,
so there is nothing in it for a reader of the mathematics. It is also the
issue that produced the quadratic and cubic tables, and neither of those links
it.

The corpus is split on this and I would not pretend otherwise: T340 is
published and still carries `Request43`; T360 was published without the two
request links its `generators/.../table.yaml` still has. Somebody should
decide once. My reading of the skill is that the link comes out.

Smallest fix: delete the `Request42` entry from `Links`. It is cited from no
prose, so nothing else changes.

## Noted only because I noticed

* **The program computes 80 digits for a table that stores 100.**
  `default(realprecision, 80)` means a reader who runs the block to check a
  row can check four fifths of it. `110` costs nothing. While that file is
  open: its last two lines are two independent routes to the same number and
  nothing says so, where the cubic table's block glosses each line with a `\\`
  comment and the expected first digits. I ran both lines and they agree to
  all 80 digits, so this is presentation, not correctness.
* **Nothing on the page says what $\kappa_K$ counts.** A reader who met this
  number as the constant in an ideal-count asymptotic, $\#\{\mathfrak a:
  N\mathfrak a\leq x\}\sim\kappa_K x$, has no sentence here to recognise it
  by; the cubic table has one, with a worked count. This is an addition rather
  than a fault, and it is the kind of sentence that makes a search hit legible
  to the person who arrived holding the number.
* **The entry comments are semicolon-separated fragments** ("$a^4 - a^3 - a^2
  + a + 1=0$; signature $(0,2)$; 4T3; $h_K=1$; $w_K=6$; LMFDB 4.0.117.1").
  Comment (3) declares the layout and the sibling tables do the same, so this
  is a house convention for a six-field record rather than telegraphic prose,
  and I would leave it. I mention it only so the next reader does not spend
  the afternoon on it that I nearly did.

## What I could not check

The count of 456 is the generator's own, from `nflist`, and `rigour details`
says the generator refuses to run unless it finds exactly that. An independent
count from the LMFDB was not available: `www.lmfdb.org/api/nf_fields/` answered
this runner with a reCAPTCHA page under HTTP 200, which is the behaviour
already recorded in `docs/agent-environment.md`. So the claim that these are
*all* quartic fields with $|D|\leq 5000$ rests on `nflist` covering the five
transitive groups of degree four, which it does, and on `nflist` itself.
