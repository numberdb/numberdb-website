## Findings, ranked

### 1. Rows are sorted as strings, so the smallest curve in a class comes last

What a reader sees: conductor 394 lists 394.a.3152.1 above 394.a.394.1;
464 lists 464.a.29696.1 and 464.a.29696.2 above 464.a.464.1; 472 lists
472.a.60416.1 above 472.a.944.1; 708 runs 181248, 19116, 2832. Of the
31 classes in the table with more than one discriminant, 12 are in this
order (394.a, 464.a, 472.a, 708.a, 800.a, 847.d, 862.a, 882.a, 909.a,
925.a, 961.a, 990.a).

Why it is wrong: the third part of an LMFDB label is the absolute
discriminant, and the LMFDB lists a class by it numerically, so the
curve of minimal discriminant, the one the LMFDB shows first and the one
a reader most likely has, comes first. `curve_data.py` says its rows are
"sorted by conductor and curve label", and a label sorts as text, so
"3152" precedes "394". The skill's rule is that the order the generator
enumerates in is the order a reader gets, and this one puts the row
anybody arrives wanting under a larger one.

Fix: in `enumerate`, sort the records by
`(conductor, class letter, int(discriminant), int(number))` before
yielding, then republish. One line. Worth doing.

### 2. The definition rests on "a Neron basis on a regular model", which names nothing

What a reader sees: "If $\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}^2$ is the period
lattice of a Neron basis on a regular model of $C$, this table gives
$\Omega_A=\#\pi_0(A(\mathbb{R}))\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda\cap\mathbb{R}^2)$
[1]."

Why it is wrong: "Néron basis" is not a phrase the cited knowl or the
literature uses, and it is the whole normalisation. The knowl says two
things that this compresses into one: the differentials are a basis of
the global sections of the canonical sheaf of a regular model of $C$
over $\mathbb{Z}$, and the lattice is generated by their integrals over
a basis of $H_1(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})$. A reader who does not know
which of those is meant cannot rebuild the table, and one who does still
has to guess that "basis" means differentials and not homology. The
accent is also missing from Néron, and "If ... this table gives" makes
the definition conditional on a sentence that defines nothing.

Fix: "Let $C/\mathbb{Q}$ be a genus $2$ curve with Jacobian
$A=\operatorname{Jac}(C)$, let $\omega_1,\omega_2$ be a basis of the
global sections of the canonical sheaf of a regular model of $C$ over
$\mathbb{Z}$, and let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}^2$ be the lattice of the
vectors $(\int_\gamma\omega_1,\int_\gamma\omega_2)$ for $\gamma\in
H_1(C(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})$. The real period is
$\Omega_A=\#\pi_0(A(\mathbb{R}))\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda\cap\mathbb{R}^2)$,
the covolume taken in $\mathbb{R}^2$ [1]." Two sentences, and the last
sentence of comment (3) can then go. Worth doing.

### 4. Comment (5) says "not only to its isogeny class", which is the wrong logic

What a reader sees: "The real period is attached to the curve's
Jacobian, not only to its isogeny class. The curves 249.a.249.1 and
249.a.6723.1 have the same period in the source data, while 277.a.277.1
and 277.a.277.2 are in one isogeny class and have different periods."

Why it is wrong: the point is that the period is not an isogeny
invariant, and "not only" says the opposite, that it is one and
something more. "In the source data" hedges a fact about the mathematics
as if it were a fact about a database. The two examples are the right
ones and could be links to their rows.

Fix: "The real period is an invariant of the Jacobian and not of its
isogeny class: HREF{#277.a.277.1}[277.a.277.1] and
HREF{#277.a.277.2}[277.a.277.2] lie in one class and have different
periods, while HREF{#249.a.249.1}[249.a.249.1] and
HREF{#249.a.6723.1}[249.a.6723.1] have the same period." One sentence.
Small, worth doing.

## Noted only

* **The complete-note** says the table holds every LMFDB curve with
  $N\leq1000$ "whose real period is recorded in the LMFDB genus $2$
  curve data". The LMFDB has 159 curves in that range and all 159 are
  here, so the clause excludes nothing and makes a reader wonder what
  was left out. "it holds every LMFDB genus $2$ curve with conductor
  $N\leq1000$" is the sentence.
* **The fold on the rigour note** shows one sentence before "more", and
  it is "The generator uses the $159$ LMFDB genus $2$ curve rows with
  conductor $N\leq1000$, fetched from `g2c_curves` and frozen in
  `curve_data.py`." A reader of the page has no `curve_data.py`; the
  second sentence, which says what was transcribed and to how many
  digits, is the one they want first.
* **The `heuristic` rigour** is honest and the note explains it: the odd
  conductors were checked through the BSD quotient against PARI, the
  even ones were not. That is more than most transcribed tables say. A
  reader may still be surprised that a value copied from a reviewed
  database is at the weakest computed level; the note answers that, but
  only after the fold.
* **Entry comments** link the LMFDB with `HREF{https://…}[LMFDB
  169.a.169.1]`. It renders as a link and there is no `CITE` form that
  would give 159 different targets, so this is right; it is just not
  what the skill describes `HREF` as for.
* **Formula (1)** names $L^{(r)}(A,1)/r!$, which T212 stores for the
  same Jacobians, without linking it; Similar tables makes the
  connection, so a reader gets there in one more step.
* **The long url** is `/Real_periods_of_genus_2_curves_over`, the
  slugger having dropped `$\mathbb{Q}$` and left a dangling "over". That
  is the site's slugging, as with T212, and anybody writing `HREF{…}` to
  this table must copy it rather than guess it.

  family; the range is in the complete-note. This is the split the skill
