## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Golden ratio gloss is not a relation, and its source does not say it

Similar tables reads: "Golden ratio — the omega constant is the fixed
point for exponentiation that is often compared with the golden ratio's
algebraic fixed-point equation." A reader asks: compared by whom, and in
what respect? The only source the page cites for the omega constant is
the Wikipedia article, and that article mentions the golden ratio
nowhere in its text; it appears only in the navigation box of irrational
numbers at the foot, beside Champernowne's and Cahen's. The proposal
this table was built from lists the golden ratio "as the other famous
one-constant fixed point; loosely", and the word "loosely" is the
proposal saying the link does not hold. A Similar-tables entry states a
relation in the mathematics; "often compared with" is a remark about
the literature, and here an unsupported one. Smallest change: delete
the entry. There is nothing in the corpus that holds $W$, $\Omega$ or
an exponential fixed point, so the section would keep one row.

### 2. The `branch` parameter line reads "branch — branch"

The parameters section prints

    branch —   branch
    $x$ —   argument ($x\in\mathbb{Q}$; for branch $0$, $x>-e^{-1}$; …)

The first line is the key, the site's em dash, and the title, which is
the same word. A reader learns from it neither what the two values are
nor which is which; that is left to the second line, which names the
branches only by their numbers, and to comment (4). The parameter is
the one place the skill asks for each parameter's type and constraint.
Smallest change: give `branch` a `constraints` text such as "$0$ is
the principal branch $W_0$ and $-1$ the lower real branch $W_{-1}$",
which is what the values' displays already say row by row. Retitling it
"branch of $W$" would also stop the line repeating itself.

### 3. The gloss on Euler's constant $e$ restates the definition

"Euler's constant $e$ — the Lambert $W$ function is the inverse of
$w\mapsto we^w$, and $W_0(1)$ is the fixed point of $x\mapsto e^{-x}$."
The first clause is the definition's first sentence again, and the
second is formula (3) again; neither says how the table of $e$ and this
table are related. The relation the page already contains, split over
comment (6) and the constraint, is that $e$ marks the two special
arguments: the real domain ends at $x=-e^{-1}$, where both real branches
equal $-1$, and $W_0(e)=1$. Smallest change: make the gloss that
sentence, and let the omega constant stay in formula (3).

### 4. Branch is the outer parameter, so the two real roots at the same $x$ are 120 rows apart

The rows come in the document's order: 120 rows of $W_0$ at positive
$x$, 11 rows of $W_0$ at negative $x$, and then 11 rows of $W_{-1}$ at
the same negative $x$. A reader with a negative argument has two real
solutions of $we^w=x$ and this is the table that holds both, yet
$W_0(-1/4)$ is on row 128 and $W_{-1}(-1/4)$ on row 139, with nothing
between them that concerns that reader. The generator interleaves the
branches at each negative $x$; the nested `Numbers` block, keyed by
`branch` first, cannot keep that order. Putting `x` first would set the
two roots side by side and lose nothing on the positive side, where
there is one row per argument anyway. Parameter order is frozen when a
table is published and a citation resolves on it, so this is the last
moment it can be chosen, which is why I am recording it as a decision
rather than a fault. If the order is kept, the page still reads: the
positive rows begin at the top and $\Omega$ is the twelfth row, with
its comment.

### 5. Comment (6) opens with a sentence that belongs to the complete-note

"The exact value $W_0(0)=0$ is not listed." is a fact about the run,
and the complete-note already ends "the exact value $W_0(0)=0$ is
omitted". The rest of comment (6), that $W=-1$ at the branch point and
$W_0(e)=1$ and neither argument is rational, is the mathematics and is
worth keeping. Smallest change: drop the first sentence, or turn the
comment round so that it lists the three exact values and says which
one is rational.

### 6. Small things, noted because I noticed

* Formula (3) names $\Omega=W_0(1)$, and the row is on this page with
  anchor `0,1`. `HREF{#0,1}[$\Omega$]` would land a reader on it. The
  entry comment "This is the omega constant." already does the job in
  the other direction, so this is a convenience, not a gap.
* Both programs show $W_0(1)$ only. A reader wanting a $W_{-1}$ value
  has to guess that the branch is the method's argument, and one who
  reaches for `RealBallField(...)(x).lambert_w()` gets no branch
  argument at all and always the principal branch, which the
  generator's docstring knows and the page does not say. One more line,
  `CBF(QQ(-1)/4).lambert_w(-1).real()`, would show both.
* The definition cites "[2] [1]" in that order because `Links` lists
  the DLMF first and the sentence cites Wikipedia first. Swapping the
  two `CITE`s makes the numbers ascend. Cosmetic.
* "$W_0(x)$" and "$W_{-1}(x)$" are printed as the branch label on every
  row, beside a column that already shows $x$. They are labels rather
  than formulas, so the skill's rule is not broken; "$W_0$" and
  "$W_{-1}$" would be shorter and say the same.
