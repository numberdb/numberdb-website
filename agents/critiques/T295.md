## The audit

It reports one thing: `normalisation` takes two names, so the table "reads as
2 tables sharing one title and one value column".

**I disagree with the finding and agree with how the table already answers
it.** $k_r$ and $m_r=k_r^2$ are one number in two conventions, and the map
between them is invertible on positive reals. That is exactly the case the
skill keeps in one table. The Definition says so ("one number in two
invertible conventions"), as the audit asks. The detector does not read that
sentence, so it will keep firing. Nothing to change.

What the audit does not see is below. Findings 1 and 2 are exactly the kind of
thing it misses.

## Findings, most worth doing first

### 1. A formula is printed as a label on a hundred rows. Worth doing.

Every entry block reads

    2   $k_r$:        0.41421356…
    2   $m_r=k_r^2$:  0.17157287…

`Parameters.normalisation.values.parameter` is `$m_r=k_r^2$`. The skill says
"A value's display is a label, not a formula", and gives almost this case,
`$c=-r(r-2)/4$` repeated on every row. The relation $m_r=k_r^2$ is already
stated in the Definition, in comment (6), in formula (1) and in the
complete-note.

**Fix:** `parameter: $m_r$`.

### 2. The parameter list repeats itself. Worth doing.

The Parameters section renders as

    $r$          —  positive integer (positive integer)
    convention   —  convention

For `r`, `title` and `constraints` are both "positive integer", so the reader
gets the same phrase twice and nothing about what $r$ indexes. For
`normalisation`, `display` and `title` are both "convention", so the line
glosses a word with itself.

**Fix:** give each title a phrase that says what the parameter is. For
example, `r` could have title `index, with $\tau=i\sqrt r$` and constraints
`positive integer`. `normalisation` could have title
`whether the row holds the modulus $k_r$ or the parameter $m_r$`.

### 3. Formula (2) is in the modulus convention, and the table it links uses the parameter. Worth doing.

Formula (2) renders as: "$q=\exp(-\pi K'(k_r)/K(k_r))$, so
$K'(k_r)/K(k_r)=\sqrt r$ [1]."

This is DLMF's notation, where $K$ takes the modulus $k$. But the one table
this page links for $K$ is T25, "Complete elliptic integral of the first kind
$K(m)$", which takes the parameter $m=k^2$. The `Similar tables` gloss even
says "the defining quotient uses $K$ at the complementary parameters $m_r$ and
$1-m_r$".

A reader who follows that link and evaluates $K(k_r)$ with the stored $k_r$
gets the wrong ratio. That is the modulus/parameter trap this very table
exists to help with. Three smaller faults sit in the same line:

- $K'$ is never defined.
- $q$ is defined only inside formula (1).
- The gloss's "the defining quotient" points at a quotient the Definition does
  not contain. The Definition defines $k_r$ through $\lambda$.

**Fix:** write formula (2) in T25's convention and name $q$ there too:
"$K(1-m_r)/K(m_r)=\sqrt r$, with $K(m)$ as in
HREF{Complete_elliptic_integral_of_the_first_kind_K}[the complete elliptic
integral of the first kind]; equivalently the nome is
$q=\exp(-\pi K(1-m_r)/K(m_r))=e^{-\pi\sqrt r}$." That also gives $K$ its
first-mention link in Formulas.

In `Similar tables`, change the relation to "$m_r$ is the parameter at which
$K(1-m)/K(m)=\sqrt r$".

### 4. Comment (6) names value keys, gives a wrong reason, and repeats the Definition. Worth doing.

It renders as: "The modulus rows store $k_r$. The parameter rows store
$m_r=k_r^2=\lambda(i\sqrt r)$, the elliptic parameter used by many software
libraries. These are two invertible conventions for the same singular value
because $0<k_r<1$."

- `modulus` and `parameter` are the keys of `Numbers`, and they reach the page
  in code font. The rows themselves are labelled $k_r$ and $m_r$, so the words
  appear nowhere a reader can match them. "The parameter rows" also collides
  with the page's own "Parameters" section.
- "Many software libraries" leaves the reader to guess which. The fact worth
  stating is that mpmath's `ellipk`, Sage's `elliptic_kc` and arb's
  `elliptic_k` take $m$, while DLMF and much of the older literature write
  $K(k)$.
- Squaring is invertible here because $k_r>0$, not because $k_r<1$.
- The first two sentences restate the Definition.

**Fix:** replace the comment with one sentence: "$k_r$ is the elliptic modulus
and $m_r$ the elliptic parameter; DLMF writes $K(k)$, while mpmath, Sage and
arb take $m$."

Apply the same change in `rigour details`, which ends a sentence with "at the
row $r=1$, `modulus`" and elsewhere says "the `parameter` row". Write "for
$k_1$" and "the $m_r$ row".

### 5. Formula (3) points at its own left side and calls an evaluation a continuation. Worth doing.

It renders as: "$k_{1/r}=\sqrt{1-k_r^2}$, where the left side is the analytic
continuation of the same singular-value function to positive real indices."

"The left side" makes the reader look back. "Analytic continuation" is the
wrong word: $\sqrt{\lambda(i\sqrt s)}$ is defined directly for every real
$s>0$, and nothing is continued.

**Fix:** "$\sqrt{1-k_r^2}=k_{1/r}$, where $k_s=\sqrt{\lambda(i\sqrt s)}$ for
any real $s>0$; this is $\lambda(-1/\tau)=1-\lambda(\tau)$."

### 6. Comment (5) opens with the Definition and leaves "the order" unexplained. Worth doing; small.

It renders as: "The CM point is $\tau=i\sqrt r$. The order is
$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-r}]$, of discriminant $-4r$."

The first sentence repeats the Definition. The second does not say what the
order belongs to, or why a reader of these numbers should care.

**Fix:** "The lattice $\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\,i\sqrt r$ has complex
multiplication by the order $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-r}]$, of discriminant $-4r$, so
$k_r$ is an algebraic number." That sentence also explains why this table sits
beside HREF{Hilbert_class_polynomials}[the Hilbert class polynomials].

### 7. The Definition uses $\lambda$ without naming it. Worth doing; one phrase.

"…the positive square root of $\lambda(\tau)$…". A reader learns what
$\lambda$ is only from the title of link [2] or from formula (1).

**Fix:** "…the positive square root of $\lambda(\tau)$, where $\lambda$ is the
elliptic modular function CITE{DLMFModularFunctions}…".

## Noted only because I noticed

8. **A reader holding one of the first few values is not told what it is.**
   $k_1=1/\sqrt2$, $k_2=\sqrt2-1$, $k_3=(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)/4=\sin 15^\circ$,
   $k_4=3-2\sqrt2$. Somebody arriving with 0.2588190… would be served by those
   closed forms in entry comments for the handful of $r$ where they are short.
   Optional.
9. **The same digits appear twice**: $k_4=m_2=3-2\sqrt2$. That is Landen's
   $k_{4r}=(1-k_r')/(1+k_r')$ at $r=1$. It is not wrong, but a reader
   scanning the column may wonder. A Landen formula in `Formulas` would
   explain it, and it is a standard identity for singular values anyway.
10. **Keyword "singular moduli" collides with T83.** In T83, "Rational singular
    moduli", the phrase means values of $j$. Borwein's usage for $k_r$ is real,
    so the keyword is defensible, and a search answering with both tables is
    arguably right. No change needed.
11. **The complete-note says what is here but not why $r\leq100$ was chosen.**
    The skill asks for that sentence. A reader is unlikely to miss it, since
    nearly every $k_r$ quoted in the literature has small $r$.

## Summary

Nothing is wrong with the numbers or the one-table shape, and the page renders
without faults. The work worth doing is findings 1–7, all small edits to prose
and labels. The most consequential is 3: the page's only formula for $K$ uses
the opposite argument convention from the $K$ table it links.

Possible lesson, not filed because this task allows no other file: the skill
says `elliptic_k` takes $m$, but not that a formula should be written in the
argument convention of the corpus table it links. Otherwise a reader who
follows the link plugs in the wrong argument.
