## Findings, ranked

### 1. Four supersingular surfaces are called "not simple", and they are simple

What a reader sees: the row 2.2.a_ae, $t^4-4t^2+4$, carries the comment
"LMFDB 2.2.a_ae. The class is not simple, has p-rank 0, and is not
ordinary." The same sentence stands on 2.3.a_ag ($t^4-6t^2+9$), 2.5.a_ak
($t^4-10t^2+25$) and 2.7.a_ao ($t^4-14t^2+49$).

Why it is wrong: these are $(t^2-p)^2$, the Weil polynomial of the
simple supersingular abelian surface over $\mathbb{F}_p$ attached to the
real Weil number $\sqrt p$. By Honda–Tate the class of a simple variety
has Weil polynomial $m(t)^e$ with $m$ irreducible, and for $\sqrt p$ the
endomorphism algebra is a quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt p)$,
so $e=2$ and the variety is a surface. The LMFDB page for 2.2.a_ae says
"This isogeny class is simple but not geometrically simple, primitive,
not ordinary, and supersingular", and names the quaternion algebra; the
page for 2.3.a_ag says the same. The generator's `_is_simple_class`
decides simplicity by whether the quartic factors over $\mathbb{Q}$,
which is the wrong criterion in exactly this case. Over a prime field it
is the only such case: every $t^2+at+p$ with $a^2<4p$ is an
elliptic-curve polynomial, so its square is $E\times E$, and $t^2-p$
alone is not a Weil polynomial of any variety. I recomputed all three
comment fields for every one of the 434 surface rows from the stored
polynomial, deciding simplicity by whether $t^4+a_1t^3+a_2t^2+qa_1t+q^2$
splits as $(t^2+\alpha t+q)(t^2+\beta t+q)$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ with the
$(t^2-q)^2$ exception, and the $p$-rank by formula (4): the $p$-rank
and ordinary flags agree on every row, and simplicity disagrees on
these four rows and no others.

It matters more than four rows: the row a reader arrives at holding a
supersingular Weil polynomial is precisely this one, and comment (10)
promises that "each entry comment says whether the isogeny class is
simple". The `rigour details` also say the generator "requires the
polynomial ... to have the LMFDB label and the $p$-rank stated in the
entry comment", which reads as if the comment data were checked against
something; it was checked against the function that produced it, and
simplicity was not checked at all.

Fix: in `_is_simple_class`, return `True` when `g == 2` and the
polynomial equals `(t**2 - q)**2`, with a line saying why, then
republish; and either verify the three comment fields against the LMFDB
for each row or say in `rigour details` that simplicity is decided by
irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$ together with that one exception. Worth
doing first.

### 2. Rows are in label-string order, which is neither the LMFDB's nor $\mathbb{Z}$'s

What a reader sees: for $g=1$, $q=13$ the rows run 1.13.a, ab, ac, ...,
ah, b, c, ..., h, that is $a_1=0,-1,-2,\ldots,-7,1,2,\ldots,7$. For
$g=2$ each $a_1$ block runs $a_1=0,-1,-2,-3,-4,1,2,3,4$ and inside a
block $a_2$ runs $0,-1,-2,\ldots$ then $1,2,\ldots$.

Why it is wrong: `_build_records` sorts by `record["label"]`, and the
label's base-26 encoding puts every negative (leading "a") between 0 and
1. The LMFDB lists the same classes in numerical order of the
coefficients: for $g=1,q=2$ it shows ac, ab, a, b, c, and for $g=2,q=2$
it begins ae_i, ad_f, ad_g, ac_c, ..., which is ascending $(a_1,a_2)$.
The skill asks that a reader comparing $a$ and $-a$ find them adjacent,
and here $t^2-7t+13$ and $t^2+7t+13$ are fourteen rows apart with the
trace-zero row between them.

Fix: sort by `record["coefficients"]` instead of by label, which is the
LMFDB's order, or by $(|a_1|, \ldots)$ with the positive first as the
skill suggests. One word in two `sorted` calls, then republish. Worth
doing, and cheap to do in the same republish as finding 1.

### 3. Comment (9) calls a theorem a convention and explains the restriction by the wrong genus

What a reader sees: "The table is restricted to prime fields. For prime
powers the root-size inequalities for a Weil polynomial do not by
themselves give exactly the elliptic-curve traces that occur;
Waterhouse's occurrence conditions are then an extra part of the
convention [1]."

Why it is wrong: which traces occur over $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is
Waterhouse's theorem, not a convention anybody chose, and the sentence
gives the reason for the restriction only for elliptic curves, while 434
of the 494 rows are surfaces. A reader is left to guess whether the
enumeration by (6) is claimed to be exhaustive over prime fields, which
is the fact the table rests on and which `rigour details` does support
by count. "Root-size inequalities" is also a phrase the rest of the
table never uses; it means the Hasse bound and formula (6).

Fix: "The table is restricted to prime fields, where every integer $a$
with $a^2\leq4q$ is the trace of an elliptic curve [1], and where the
enumeration of surfaces by (6) agrees with the LMFDB's count of isogeny
classes for each $q$ in the table. Over a proper prime power some
polynomials satisfying these bounds are not Weil polynomials of any
abelian variety [1]." Worth doing.

### 4. Comment (8) gives an odd reason for the label being in the index

What a reader sees: "It is part of the index because the same base
field and dimension have many isogeny classes with nearby coefficients."

Why it is wrong: this is a remark about the table's design rather than
about the mathematics, and the reason it gives is not the reason. The
label is a parameter because $g$ and $q$ do not determine the class;
"nearby coefficients" has nothing to do with it.

Fix: "It identifies the isogeny class, which $g$ and $q$ alone do not."
Or drop the sentence.

### 6. Program P1 serves the sixty rows, not the four hundred

What a reader sees: one Sage line producing $t^2+at+q$ for $q=17$, which
is the next elliptic prime beyond the table and nothing for surfaces.

Why it is wrong: `Programs` is the incantation for a reader who wants
one more value, and the reader of this table most likely wants the
surfaces for $q=11$. Formula (6) is already the recipe; three more
lines of Sage would turn it into one.

Fix: add a second program that loops $a_1$ over $[-4\sqrt q,4\sqrt q]$
and $a_2$ over the interval in (6), or say in the existing one that it
is the $g=1$ case.

## Noted only because I noticed

- The entry comments write "p-rank" in plain text where formula (4) and
  comment (10) write "$p$-rank"; and for every $g=2$ row "has p-rank 2,
  and is ordinary" says one fact twice, since ordinary means $p$-rank
  $g$. Comment (10) promises all three, so a reader is not misled.
- The definition does not say that $P_A$ depends only on the isogeny
  class, which is why a class rather than a variety indexes the table; a
  clause would do, "and depends only on the isogeny class of $A$".
- The corpus has an `elliptic curves` tag and sixty of these rows are
  Weil polynomials of elliptic curves, with formula (5) written for
  them. Whether a table that is mostly surfaces should carry it is a
  judgement; I would.
- The four `Similar tables` glosses are thin. "Stores another family of
  Frobenius trace sums over finite fields" for Kloosterman sums and
  "Frobenius eigenvalue sums in related point-counting problems" for
  Gauss sums name no relation a reader can use; the Jacobi-sum gloss is
  the one real one, and the Roots of unity gloss is true but the table
  it points at holds $e^{2\pi ik/n}$, not $\alpha/\sqrt q$. The corpus
  has nothing on Frobenius, abelian varieties or point counts (searched
  by all three), so there is nothing better to point at yet.
- Formula (3) says "an initial $a$ marking a negative integer" right
  after saying $a=0$; a reader may pause at "ab" before working out that
  it is $-1$. The LMFDB knowl is cited beside it, which is enough.
- The `rigour details` open with "The generator enumerates the
  coefficient tuples in (2) exactly", which is a sentence about the
  program, not about how the values were obtained; the counts that
  follow are the useful part.
