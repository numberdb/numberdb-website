## Findings, ranked

### 1. A comment on every row, saying what the row already shows

What a reader sees: under every one of the 160 rows with $k\geq16$,
"The degree is $2=\dim S_{24}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))$", with the
row's own degree and weight filled in. Under the four weight-12 rows,
"This is $x-\tau(2)$, where $\tau(n)$ is the coefficient of $q^n$ in
the modular discriminant $\Delta$", with $\Delta$ linked, four times in
a row.

Why it is wrong: the degree of $x^2-1080x-20468736$ is visible in the
row, and formula (4) already says the degree is $\dim S_k$ for every
row at once. The weight-12 sentence is formula (3) verbatim, and the
second sentence of comment (5) says it a third time. The skill's rule is
that a comment on every entry earns its place when it says something the
row cannot, and is left out when it repeats what a formula in the
document says for all rows. Here the comments are the entire comment
load of the table and none of them says anything the page does not.
The repeated link is also against "first mention, once per section".

Smallest change: drop all 164 entry comments. Formulas (3) and (4), with
the change in finding 3, carry everything they said. If a comment is
wanted on the weight-12 rows, one sentence on the $k=12$, $p=2$ row is
enough.

### 2. "Maeda conjecture" is a keyword, and the page never mentions Maeda

What a reader sees: `Keywords` carries "Maeda conjecture", so a search
for Maeda lands here. Nothing on the page says what the conjecture is or
what it has to do with these polynomials.

Why it is wrong: the conjecture of Hida and Maeda is *about these
polynomials*: that $\chi_{k,2}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ with
Galois group the full symmetric group $S_d$, $d=\dim S_k$, for every
$k$, with the same expected for every $p$. It is the single most
interesting fact about the rows a reader arrives holding, and a reader
who has a degree-7 polynomial from a computation wants to know whether
it factors. A keyword promises a page will answer a query, and this one
does not. It also weakens the table's own claim: a keyword that stands
for nothing on the page is the kind of thing a reviewer reads as
padding for search.

Smallest change: one comment, roughly "Maeda's conjecture CITE{...}
states that $\chi_{k,2}(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ with
Galois group $S_{\dim S_k}$ for every $k$, and the same is expected for
every $p$. Every polynomial in this table is irreducible over
$\mathbb{Q}$." with a `References` entry for Hida and Maeda, *Non-abelian
base change for totally real fields*, Pacific J. Math. 181 (1997),
189–217, and, if the numerical range is quoted, Ghitza and McAndrew,
*Experimental evidence for Maeda's conjecture on modular forms*, Tbilisi
Math. J. 5 (2012), 55–69, who verify it for $T_2$ up to weight 14000.
The citation details and the verified range should be checked against
the papers before they are written in; I have not fetched them. The
irreducibility of the 164 stored rows I did check, with `sympy`'s
`factor_list` on the stored strings: all 164 are irreducible over
$\mathbb{Q}$. The Galois groups I did not check. Alternatively drop the
keyword, but the comment is the better table.

While that comment is being written, two further facts a reader of a
row wants and the page does not state: the roots are real, since $T_p$
is self-adjoint for the Petersson inner product, and they lie in
$[-2p^{(k-1)/2},\,2p^{(k-1)/2}]$ by Deligne's bound. Either belongs in
the same comment or beside formula (4). Optional.

### 3. Formula (4) says the degree is a dimension, and never says the dimension

What a reader sees: "(4) $\deg\chi_{k,p}=\dim S_k(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))$."

Why it is wrong: as it stands it restates the definition, since a
characteristic polynomial on a $d$-dimensional space has degree $d$. What
a reader looking at a degree-7 row at $k=94$, or wondering why weight 26
has degree 1 while weight 24 has degree 2, actually wants is the
dimension formula, and the page never gives it. Comment (6), explaining
that weight 14 is absent, and the 160 row comments of finding 1 are both
consequences of the formula that is missing.

Smallest change: replace (4) by
"$\deg\chi_{k,p}=\dim S_k(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))=\lfloor k/12\rfloor-1$
if $k\equiv2\pmod{12}$ and $\lfloor k/12\rfloor$ otherwise, for even
$k\geq4$." Then comment (6) can read "There are no cusp forms of level
one below weight 12, and none in weight 14, by (4)", which also answers
the reader who wonders why the table starts at 12.

### 4. Formula (2) uses five symbols it does not define, and the tables that define three of them are linked only later

What a reader sees: "(2) The level-one cusp-form space is
$S_k(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))=\Delta\,M_{k-12}$, and
$M_*=\mathbb{C}[E_4,E_6]$. Thus the products $\Delta E_4^aE_6^b$ with

Why it is wrong: $M_{k-12}$, $M_*$, $E_4$, $E_6$ and $\Delta$ appear here

$\Delta$ each have a table in this corpus, and the rule is to link a
family at its first mention in a section; the first mention in `Formulas`
is here, and the link to $\Delta$ comes one formula later in (3). A
reader who does not already know the notation gets it from the `Similar
tables` glosses, at the bottom of the page.

Smallest change: "where $M_j$ is the space of modular forms of weight
$j$ for $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, $M_*=\bigoplus_j M_j$, and
$E_4$, $E_6$ HREF{...}[the Eisenstein series] and $\Delta$
HREF{Q-expansion_of_the_modular_discriminant}[the modular discriminant]
are its generators"; then the link in (3) becomes plain text.
