## Worth doing

### 1. The conductor ideal in 428 entry comments renders as `(5∗w−2)`

**What a reader sees.** A reader sees "has conductor ideal (5∗w−2) and rank 0". The
comment stores `$(5*w-2)$`, which is LMFDB's machine syntax. Inside math,
MathJax sets `*` as the asterisk operator ∗, a binary operator with space
around it. 428 of the 572 comments contain one.

**Why it is wrong.** It is a program's string printed as mathematics. Nobody
writes an ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[w]$ that way, and a reader has to guess
whether ∗ means something, such as a convolution or a dual.

**Smallest fix.** In the generator, drop the `*` before it goes into the
comment: `$(5w-2)$`. Every label here has an integer coefficient directly
before `w`, so deleting the character is safe for this table. (The general
lesson, that an asterisk in `$...$` renders as ∗, is already proposed in
`agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md` from T169 and T222.)

### 2. Half the rows repeat another row, and nothing says why

**What a reader sees.** Under $D=5$ the first two rows, `31.1 a` and `31.2 a`,
hold the same 70 digits, and so do `41.1`/`41.2`, `55.1`/`55.2`, and so on.
Across the table, 572 rows hold 322 distinct numbers. Every repeated value
appears exactly twice, and both rows always share a conductor norm. For
$D=12$, seven pairs even share a conductor label (`128.1 b` and `128.1 d`),
because there the ideal is its own conjugate.

**Why it is wrong.** A reader who searches for one of these numbers gets two
curves back and cannot tell from the page whether that is a coincidence, a
duplicate, or an error. The explanation is a fact about the mathematics: the
nontrivial automorphism of $K$ takes $E$ to its conjugate $E^\sigma$, and
$L(E^\sigma/K,s)=L(E/K,s)$. It is one sentence, and it is missing.

**Smallest fix.** Add one sentence to `comment-index`: "An isogeny class and
its Galois conjugate have the same $L$-function, so they share a value: for
$D=5$ the rows $31.1$ and $31.2$ are such a pair." I checked the pairing
against the stored conductor ideals only for $31.1/31.2$ and for the $D=12$
labels $54.1$, $96.1$ and $128.1$, not for all 250 pairs, so whoever makes the
edit should confirm it in the generator. Linking each pair with `equals` would
also tell search, but that is optional.

### 3. `comment-convention` contradicts the Definition

**What a reader sees.** The Definition says the table gives "the leading
Taylor coefficient $L^*(E/K,1)=L^{(r)}(E/K,1)/r!$". The comment then says:
"The stored value is the leading coefficient divided by $r!$."

**Why it is wrong.** Read literally, against the Definition's own terms, that
is $L^{(r)}(E/K,1)/(r!)^2$. The leading coefficient already includes the
$1/r!$. For $r=0$ and $r=1$ the two agree, which is every row here, but the
sentence is wrong for the family, and it is exactly what a reader comparing
with T70 (which stores $L''(E,1)$ undivided) will read twice.

**Smallest fix.** "The stored value is the $r$-th derivative divided by $r!$,
which is the number on the left of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer formula
CITE{WikiBSD} in CITE{formula-BSD}." This also removes the "Thus", which joins
two sentences that do not follow from one another.

### 4. The column headers read $conductor$ and $class$ in math italics

**What a reader sees.** The head of the table and the parameter list show
`$conductor$` and `$class$`. Neither parameter has a `display`, and the view
falls back to `"$%s$" % key` (`numberdb_app/views.py:846`), so MathJax sets
each word as a product of single-letter variables, spaced like one. The first
parameter, `D`, has `display: "$D$"` and looks right beside them.

**Why it is wrong.** These are labels whose values are words and numbers such
as `31.1`, not symbols. The header is the first thing on the page, and it is
the one thing on it that reads like broken typesetting.

**Smallest fix.** Give both parameters a display that is plain text:
`display: "conductor"` and `display: "class"`. (Already proposed as a lesson:
"A `Symbolic` parameter without `display` is shown as `$name$`, in math
italics".)

### 5. The Definition defines $r$ as the rank, and the leading coefficient needs the order of vanishing

**What a reader sees.** "...the leading Taylor coefficient
$L^*(E/K,1)=L^{(r)}(E/K,1)/r!$, where $r=\operatorname{rank}E(K)$".

**Why it is wrong.** The leading Taylor coefficient is taken at the order of
vanishing of $L(E/K,s)$ at $s=1$. Setting that equal to the Mordell-Weil rank
is the rank part of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, which this table's own
`formula-BSD` states as a formula rather than a definition. For these rows the
two presumably agree: every stored value is visibly nonzero, and the
generator checks the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer quotient. But a definition should not need that argument to say
what it stores. The rank also belongs where it already is, in each entry
comment.

**Smallest fix.** "...where $r$ is the order of vanishing of $L(E/K,s)$ at
$s=1$." If the equality with the rank is worth saying, put it in one sentence
under Comments.

## Noted only

### 6. Some prose in `formula-BSD` and `rigour details` is left as notes

* "The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer formula in these normalisations" points at
  something without naming it. "In these normalisations" should be "with the
  $L$-function normalised as in the Definition".
* "$\Omega_{E/K}$ is the product over both real embeddings with real
  components counted" does not say what is multiplied. A reader has to supply
  "the real period of $E$ at each embedding, times the number of connected
  components of its real points". $c_{\mathfrak p}$ is never named: these are
  the Tamagawa numbers.
* `rigour details`: "gives the square integer recorded by ecnf-data" should be
  "gives the analytic order of Sha, the square integer recorded by
  ecnf-data". As written, a reader has to work out which integer is meant.

### 7. `complete-note` says which fields are here but not why

The clause is well formed. Adding "the five real quadratic fields of
smallest discriminant" before $D\in\{5,8,12,13,17\}$ would turn a list into a
reason, which is what the skill asks this sentence to carry.

### 8. The LMFDB link is never cited, and LMFDB's convention is cited to GitHub

Link [2], "LMFDB: elliptic curves over number fields", is listed but nothing
`CITE`s it. `comment-index` mentions LMFDB first and has no citation.
`comment-field-generator` says "the one used by LMFDB CITE{ecnf-data}", which
sends a reader to a GitHub repository for LMFDB's convention. Cite `LMFDB` at
the first mention in `comment-index`. Also, `$2.2.5.1-31.1-a$` in that same
comment is a label inside math, so it renders with its hyphens set as minus
signs: "2.2.5.1 − 31.1 − a". The entry comments already write labels outside
math, and this one should too.

### 9. Smaller things

* `Keywords` carries "Q(sqrt 5)" and none of the other four fields. Either
  list all five or drop it.
* In `Similar tables`, the T70 and T71 relations begin "the same derivative
  over $\mathbb{Q}$". This table has no rank 2 or rank 3 rows, so "the same"
  has nothing to point back to. "The leading coefficient over $\mathbb{Q}$ in
  rank $2$, stored there as $L''(E,1)$ without dividing by $2!$" says it.
* `formula-base-change` could link $L^*(E_0,1)$ to
  HREF{Special_value_of_L-function_of_elliptic_curves_over_Q_of_rank_1} at
  first mention, since one factor of a rank-1 base change has rank 1 over
  $\mathbb{Q}$. The corpus has no rank 0 table over $\mathbb{Q}$, so only that
  factor has a table to link to.
