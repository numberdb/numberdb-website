# T315: Khovanov polynomials of the prime knots with at most ten crossings

Read on 2026-09-17, against the skill fetched the same day from
<https://numberdb.org/skill> (47,534 bytes; fetched directly, since this
machine has no SOCKS proxy). T315 is a draft of 479 `Z[]` entries: the
unreduced integral Khovanov polynomial $Kh_K(q,t,T)$ of the unknot, of each of
the 249 prime knots with at most ten crossings as KnotInfo draws it, and of
each of the 229 chiral ones' mirror images, shifted to
$q^{-a}t^{-b}Kh_K(q,t,T)$. `rigour: exact`, `complete: yes`. It is the sixth
knot table in the corpus and is built to the pattern of T138, T139, T140, T312
and T314.

**How I read it.** The site authenticates its HTML routes by session, so
`GET /T315` answers 404 here even with the key, and so does `/preview/T315`;
that is already recorded in `docs/agent-environment.md`, and every earlier
critique on this machine had to work from the document alone. Rather than
review a page nobody had seen, I rebuilt it: the site's own code and
templates, the document as `/api/table?id=T315` serves it, a throwaway sqlite
database inside the `agents/sage.sh` container, and `views.table_by_tid`
called with the draft's owner as `request.user`. 479 entries, 497 KB of HTML.
Every quotation below is from that page. The method is written up in
`docs/agent-environment.md` so the next critique need not work it out again.

**The table reads well.** The page renders whole, the three claims a reader of
this table has to trust about the shape of the family are all true of the 479
rows, and the `Programs` snippet runs verbatim and reproduces a stored row.
The findings below are one Definition that is weaker than its own siblings',
an uncited reference, and two phrases that point instead of naming.

## Findings, in the order I would fix them

### 1. The Definition never says what the shift is, and the shift is the first thing a reader meets

The numbers block is at the top of the page. What a reader sees first is the
column header

> $q^{-a}t^{-b}Kh_K(q,t,T)$

and under it

> 0  1  $K$:  q^2 + 1  comment: $a=-1$

Nothing there says what $a$ and $b$ are. The Definition, the next thing down,
says only

> The unreduced integral Khovanov polynomial $Kh_K(q,t,T)$ [4] [1], shifted
> into ordinary polynomial form, for the unknot and each Rolfsen prime knot
> through ten crossings as KnotInfo draws it [8].

"shifted into ordinary polynomial form" is less than the column header already
says. The sentence that defines $a$ and $b$ is comment (5), the fifth block of
prose on the page, below three formulas: a reader who has scrolled past 479
rows each carrying `$a=\ldots$` has met the symbol several hundred times
before meeting its definition.

Both siblings put it in the Definition, in one clause. T139:

> ... and of its mirror image $\bar K$, listed as $t^{-m}V_K(t)$ with nonzero
> constant term.

T314, built by this campaign the day before:

> The Kauffman polynomial $F_K(a,z)$ ..., listed as $a^{-m}F_K(a,z)$ with $m$
> the lowest exponent of $a$, ...

Smallest fix: replace "shifted into ordinary polynomial form" with "listed as
$q^{-a}t^{-b}Kh_K(q,t,T)$, with $a$ and $b$ the lowest exponents of $q$ and
$t$". Comment (5) then repeats itself slightly and can keep its worked
examples, which are the part of it that earns its place.

### 2. "The parameter $K$ or $\bar K$ chooses the mirror image of the same named knot"

That is the Definition's second sentence, and it says the wrong thing. $K$
does not choose a mirror image; the parameter chooses *between* the knot and
its mirror, and which value means which is not stated until comment (6). A
reader meeting the rows `$K$:` and `$\bar K$:` under the same $n,k$ has to
guess, and the sentence that should settle it tells them both values are the
mirror image.

It also names the parameter by two of its values. The parameter's label on the
page is the word `knot`; "the parameter $K$ or $\bar K$" is a phrase a reader
cannot match against anything they can see.

Smallest fix: delete the sentence and put the mirror into the noun phrase, as
both siblings do. T314: "... as drawn by KnotInfo CITE{KnotInfo}, and each
distinct mirror image." Comment (6) already says the rest, and says it well.

Findings 1 and 2 are a sentence and a half of the same field, so one rewrite
settles both. On the model of the two siblings, that is:

> The unreduced integral Khovanov polynomial $Kh_K(q,t,T)$ CITE{Wiki}
> CITE{Khovanov}, listed as $q^{-a}t^{-b}Kh_K(q,t,T)$ with $a$ and $b$ the
> lowest exponents of $q$ and $t$, for the unknot, each prime knot $K=n_k$ of
> the Rolfsen table CITE{Rolfsen} through ten crossings as KnotInfo draws it
> CITE{KnotInfo}, and each distinct mirror image $\bar K$.

### 3. Reference [2] is cited by nothing, and the sentence that needs it cites Perko instead

The page's reference list runs [1] Khovanov, [2] Rolfsen, [3] Perko. The prose
uses [1] and [3]. Nothing on the page points at [2], while the name Rolfsen
appears three times unlinked: "each Rolfsen prime knot" in the Definition,
"index in the Rolfsen table" in the parameter list, and

> The numbering is Rolfsen's after Perko's correction [3].

where the one citation is for the correction rather than for the numbering.
The skill's rule is that a reference is for citing and a bibliography nobody
points at is furniture; T138, T139 and T314 all cite `Rolfsen` in their
definitions.

Smallest fix: `CITE{Rolfsen}` at the first naming, which the rewrite in
finding 2 already carries. Otherwise put it in comment (8): "The numbering is
Rolfsen's CITE{Rolfsen} after Perko's correction CITE{Perko}."

### 4. "the convention used here" appears twice, and names nothing either time

Formula (1):

> ... where $V_K(t)$ is the Jones polynomial in the convention used here.

and the `Similar tables` relation for HOMFLY-PT:

> the same knots; both refine or specialise to the Jones-polynomial convention
> used here

This table states no Jones convention, so "here" has nothing to refer to, and
a reader who follows the link to check finds a further problem: T139 stores
$t^{-m}V_K(t)$, not $V_K(t)$ — its column header says so — so the identity in
formula (1) relates two things neither table holds a row of, and a reader
testing it against the two tables' numbers finds it off by a monomial on each
side. The identity itself is right: I checked it by hand on the right-handed
trefoil, where the free part $q+q^3+t^2q^5+t^3q^9$ gives
$q+q^3+q^5-q^9=(q+q^{-1})V(q^2)$.

The second sentence has its own trouble: "both refine or specialise" leaves
the reader to work out which verb belongs to which polynomial. T314's relation
line is the model of what this should read like: "the same knots; the Kauffman
polynomial specialises to the Jones polynomial".

Smallest fix, in formula (1): "... where $V_K(t)$ is the Jones polynomial of
the same knot $K$, which
HREF{Jones_polynomials_of_the_prime_knots_with_at_most_ten_crossings} stores
as $t^{-m}V_K(t)$." And in the relation: "the same knots; the Jones polynomial
is the graded Euler characteristic of Khovanov homology and a specialisation
of the HOMFLY-PT polynomial".

## Noted only because I noticed

- **The column header reads $Kh_K$ over the 229 rows labelled $\bar K$.** The
  table's own comment (6) makes $K$ and $\bar K$ two different names, so the
  header claims something untrue of just under half the rows. I am not
  proposing it: T139 ($t^{-m}V_K(t)$) and T312 ($v^{-a}P_K(v,z)$ or
  $l^{-a}P_K(l,m)$) head their columns the same way, and a table that fixed it alone would be the only one
  of four that reads differently. It is a decision for whoever holds all of
  them.
- **Comment (5)'s worked example is written backwards from the rows it
  explains.** It gives the trefoil's entry as
  $1+q^2+t^2q^4+t^3q^8+t^3q^6T^2$; the row itself reads
  `q^8*t^3 + q^6*t^3*T^2 + q^4*t^2 + q^2 + 1`. Same polynomial, opposite term
  order and $t$ before $q$ inside each term. A reader checking the example
  against the row sorts it out by hand. Reversing the example costs nothing,
  but it is the last edit I would spend.
- **One tag, `polynomial`.** So have all five sibling knot tables, and no tag
  in the corpus names knots or topology, though eight tables would now carry
  one. That is a corpus decision rather than this table's, and the skill's bar
  for proposing a tag (three tables) is comfortably passed.
- **T314 is the one knot-polynomial table the corpus holds that T315 does not
  name**, and T314 does not name T315 either. Both are drafts, so nothing is
  broken today; when both are published each should name the other.

## Where I disagree with the audit

`GET /api/table/T315/audit` returns one finding:

> parameter knot takes 2 names (K, mirror), each carrying entries of its own,
> so this reads as 2 tables sharing one title and one value column. If those
> are 2 named quantities, make 2 tables and relate them in Similar tables; if
> they are one object in several parts, or one number in several conventions,
> say so in the definition and leave them together

The rule fires correctly and the answer here is the good kind. `knot` names
*what the number is of*, not which quantity is taken of it: $K$ and $\bar K$
are two knots, and the whole point of the table is that Khovanov homology
tells them apart, which is what comment (6) says. The structural tell the
skill gives for the bad case is absent — the value column is headed with a
symbol, $q^{-a}t^{-b}Kh_K(q,t,T)$, not with the word `value` — and T139 and
T312, both reviewed and public, carry this parameter block verbatim. Splitting
would also cost the reader the thing they came to compare: $K$ and $\bar K$
are adjacent rows.

The audit is worth taking seriously on one point, which it does not quite
make: if the two mirror images are one table, the column header has to be true
of both, and $Kh_K$ is not. That is the first note above.

## What I checked hardest, and found right

- **The page renders.** No "(Unknown key)", no `HREF{` or `CITE{` left
  unconverted — every one became an anchor — no `<` inside any mathematics
  (the fault that ate a section of another table this year), and no parameter
  rendered as "argument ()". All three parameters render with their
  constraints in parentheses, and all 479 entry comments render under their
  rows. MathJax runs in the browser and I have none, so instead of looking for
  "Math input error" I took the control sequences out of every `$...$` span:
  they are `\bar`, `\ldots`, `\leq`, `\mathbb` and `\sum`, and each of the
  five is in the served `static/vendor/mathjax/tex-svg.js`, where `\Sha` — the
  one that printed in red on T212 — is not.
- **`Programs` works as printed.** I ran the snippet verbatim through Sage's
  preparser in the throwaway container, SageMath 10.9. `Link.khovanov_polynomial()`
  exists, the inline comment `q^13*t^5 + q^11*t^5*T^2 + ...` is exactly what
  Sage prints for that braid closure, and the snippet's `shifted` equals the
  stored $5_2$, $K$ row character for character. `Kh(0_1)` came back
  `q + q^-1`, which is formula (3).
- **The prose claims about the family are true of the rows.** The 21 knots
  with no mirror entry are exactly the unknot and the 20 amphichiral knots
  comment (7) lists, in that order; the entries per crossing number are
  $1,1,1,2,3,7,21,49,165$, the $N(n)$ the parameter states; 250 + 229 = 479;
  the eleven-crossing counts in comment (9) add to the 552 it claims; the
  amphichiral counts $1,1,5,13$ add to the 20 listed.
- **The shift convention is stated exactly and holds.** Comment (5)'s trefoil
  example is the stored $3_1$, $K$ row. Formula (2) takes that row back to
  $Kh_{3_1}$ and forward to $Kh_{\overline{3_1}}$, whose lowest exponents are
  the $a=-9, b=-3$ the stored mirror row's comment gives, and shifting it
  gives that row term for term. All 479 comments are of the form `$a=\ldots$`
  or `$a=\ldots, b=\ldots$`; none is empty.
- **Size and readability.** The longest entry is 399 characters ($10_{121}$,
  $K$) and the entries block is about 158 KB, half the soft limit, so the
  table can be extended and the largest entry is still something a person
  reads.
- **Links.** All four slugs in `HREF{}` answer 200 anonymously.

I did not recompute the values. The build checked them against KnotInfo and
against the stored Jones table, and the one row I did recompute, as a
by-product of the `Programs` check, agreed.
