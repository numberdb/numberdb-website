## Findings, ranked

Findings 1 to 6 are worth doing, and each is a small edit. Finding 7 is for
whoever reviews the draft, not for the document. The rest I note only
because I noticed them.

### 1. The rows at $x=\tfrac14,\tfrac34,\tfrac13,\tfrac23,\tfrac16,\tfrac56$ have short closed forms, and the page gives only the one at $\tfrac12$

A reader who evaluated $K(1/4)$ or $\zeta'(-1,1/4)$ along the way holds
$1.2956195693\ldots$ or its logarithm $0.2589890126\ldots$. They find the
row with no comment, while the rows at $x=1/2$, a little higher on the same
screen, have one. These six arguments are where $K$ is
classically known in closed form, and each form needs one constant besides
$A$. Every relation below was found by PSLQ at 90 digits on the stored rows,
with integer coefficients of at most 144:

| row | closed form of $\log K(x)$ |
|---|---|
| $x=\tfrac14$ | $\tfrac98\log A-\tfrac3{32}+\dfrac{\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)}{4\pi}$ |
| $x=\tfrac34$ | $\tfrac98\log A-\tfrac3{32}-\dfrac{\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)}{4\pi}$ |
| $x=\tfrac13$ | $\tfrac43\log A-\tfrac1{72}\log3-\tfrac19+\dfrac{\mathrm{Cl}_2(2\pi/3)}{4\pi}$ |
| $x=\tfrac23$ | $\tfrac43\log A-\tfrac1{72}\log3-\tfrac19-\dfrac{\mathrm{Cl}_2(2\pi/3)}{4\pi}$ |
| $x=\tfrac16$ | $\tfrac56\log A+\tfrac1{72}\log2+\tfrac1{144}\log3-\tfrac5{72}+\dfrac{3\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(2\pi/3)}{8\pi}$ |
| $x=\tfrac56$ | $\tfrac56\log A+\tfrac1{72}\log2+\tfrac1{144}\log3-\tfrac5{72}-\dfrac{3\,\mathrm{Cl}_2(2\pi/3)}{8\pi}$ |

$\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)$ is Catalan's constant. As a cross-check, each
denominator class multiplies out to the page's formula (6) for $n=3,4,6$:
the Clausen terms cancel in pairs and the rest matches
$A^{(n^2-1)/n}n^{-1/(12n)}e^{(1-n^2)/(12n)}$.

**Fix.** Give the `logK` entry at each of the six arguments a comment with
its row of the table above, in the same style as the existing
"$\frac32\log A-\frac1{24}\log2-\frac18$." at $x=1/2$. Give the `K` entry the
exponentiated form, for example
`$A^{9/8}e^{-3/32}\exp(\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)/(4\pi))$`. Link each Clausen value
to its row, at first mention in each comment:
`HREF{Values_of_the_Clausen_functions_at_rational_multiples_of#2,1/2}[$\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)$]`
and `...#2,2/3`. Those rows exist, and the first is labelled "Catalan's
constant $G$" there. **Do not write Catalan's constant as $G$ on this page**,
where $G$ is already the Barnes function in formula (2). This finding is
the one most worth doing, because it serves exactly the reader who arrives
with a number.

### 2. The Definition uses $\zeta'(-1)$ and the prime without saying what they are

> Listed are Kinkelin's hyperfactorial $K$-function
> $K(x)=\exp(\zeta'(-1,x)-\zeta'(-1))$ [1] and its real logarithm at positive
> rational arguments $x$, where $\zeta(s,x)$ is the Hurwitz zeta function.

The sentence defines $\zeta(s,x)$. It does not define the prime, which is
the derivative in $s$ rather than in $x$, and it does not define
$\zeta'(-1)$, which is the Riemann zeta function's derivative. A reader has
to supply both. Formula (1) and the T228 relation use the same notation, so
the gap carries through the page. The "where" clause also comes after the
arguments clause, so the gloss on the formula sits a clause away from the
formula it explains.

**Fix.** Keep one sentence per job, for example:

> The hyperfactorial $K$-function is $K(x)=\exp(\zeta'(-1,x)-\zeta'(-1))$
> CITE{WikiKFunction}, where $\zeta(s,x)$ is the Hurwitz zeta function,
> $\zeta'(s,x)$ its derivative in $s$, and $\zeta'(-1)=\zeta'(-1,1)$. Listed
> are $K(x)$ and $\log K(x)$ at positive rational $x$.

### 4. The integers are said three times, and the rows that say them say little

- `formula-integers` gives "$K(n)=\prod_{m=1}^{n-1}m^m$". `comment-shift`
  repeats that formula word for word, then adds "with empty product
  $K(1)=1$", which is a fragment, and "Thus the hyperfactorial $H(n)$ is
  $K(n+1)$". The last part is the only thing the comment adds, and "Thus"
  presents a definition as if it were a deduction.
- `formula-log` is the Definition's formula with the exponential removed.
- The entry comments on $\log K(1)$ and $\log K(2)$ are "$0$.", which repeats
  the value in the row. $K(3)$ and $K(4)$ carry "$1^1 2^2=4$." and
  "$1^1 2^2 3^3=108$.", which repeat formula (4). $K(5)=27648$ and
  $K(6)=86400000$ carry nothing. A reader who arrived holding 27648 has
  computed the hyperfactorial $H(4)$. They find it at $x=5$, and nothing on
  that row names the hyperfactorial or the shift by one.

**Fix.** Make `comment-shift` say the shift and nothing else: "The
hyperfactorial $H(n)=1^1 2^2\cdots n^n$ is $K(n+1)$, so $H(n)$ is the row
$x=n+1$." Delete the two "$0$." comments. On $K(2)$ to $K(6)$, either give
each row the comment "the hyperfactorial $H(n-1)$", with $n-1$ written out,
or delete the two product comments so that the integer rows are consistent.
The first option serves the reader holding 27648. `formula-log` can stay,
since a formula list normally opens with the defining one. Keep only one of
formula (4) and the product in `comment-shift`.

### 5. `comment-domain` describes a branch choice where none is made

> The table uses the real positive branch on $x>0$. No values at
> non-positive rational arguments are listed, since continuing
> $\Gamma(x)^{x-1}/G(x)$ there requires a branch choice.

On $x>0$, $K(x)$ is real and positive, and $\log K(x)$ is real without any
choice. The first sentence makes a reader wonder which other branch was on
offer. The second sentence is a fact about the mathematics, and it is right.

**Fix.** "For $x>0$, $K(x)$ is real and positive, so $\log K(x)$ is real. The
table holds no values at $x\leq0$: there $\Gamma(x)^{x-1}$ is not real for
non-integer $x$, and a value would depend on a choice of branch."

### 6. The complete-note says what range is here but not why

**Fix.** Append a clause, for example: "..., which contains every product
in the multiplication formula for $n\leq12$ and ends at $K(6)=86400000$."
