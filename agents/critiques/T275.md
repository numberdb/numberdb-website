## What reads well

It is one table: $l$ and $r$ are both arguments of one threshold. The value
column is headed $\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$, with no `param-latex` and no
row labels repeating a block heading. The parameters describe the family
($l\geq3$, $r>l$). What was computed is in `complete-note`, as a clause that
finishes "complete: no (...)". The rows run $l$ first and then $r$, so
$(3,6)$, the pair a coding theorist is most likely to be holding, is the third
row, and its comment names the source. The rigour note says what was bounded
and how. Nothing is named that the corpus holds and is left unlinked.

Most of what follows is about the formulas and the prose around them.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. `formula-root` calls $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ "the positive solution", and for every even $r$ there are two. Worth doing.

> The root $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ is the positive solution of
> $p^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}(x)=0$, where
> $p^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}(x)=((l-1)(r-1)-1)(1-x)^{r-2}-\sum_{i=0}^{r-3}(1-x)^i$.

For even $r$ the leading term $(1-x)^{r-2}$ is positive again once $x>1$, so
$p$ has a second positive root in $(1,2)$. This happens for 25 of the 45
pairs: $(3,4)$ has roots $0.442$ and $1.358$, and $(3,6)$ has $0.261$ and
$1.512$. A reader who builds the table from this sentence can land on the
wrong root. The generator, the program and `rigour details` all bisect on
$(0,1)$, so the stored values are right. Only the sentence is wrong.

A second gap sits next to it. `formula-threshold-curve` introduces
$\epsilon(x)$, and nothing on the page says why. The reader is never told that
the threshold is the minimum of $\epsilon(x)$, or that $p^{\mathrm{BP}}$ is
where its derivative vanishes. The only hint is "an mpmath minimisation of
$\epsilon(x)$" in `rigour details`. The computation is easy to confirm:
with $y=1-x$ and $g=1-y^{r-1}$,
$\epsilon'(x)=-x\,p^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}(x)/g^{\,l}$. So $p>0$ before the root
and $p<0$ after it, and the root in $(0,1)$ is the minimiser.

**Smallest fix:** in `formula-root`, "is the unique solution in $(0,1)$ of".
**Better,** because it also answers the second gap: replace
`formula-threshold` with
"$\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}=\min_{0<x\leq1}\epsilon(x)=\epsilon(x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r})$",
and say in `formula-root` that $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ is the minimiser, which
is the unique zero of $p^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ in $(0,1)$.

### 2. The definition does not say what a threshold is. Worth doing.

> The number $\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ is the binary erasure channel
> [4] erasure probability at the belief-propagation decoding threshold of
> this ensemble [1].

On the page this is a stack of five nouns with a citation marker in the
middle ("the binary erasure channel [4] erasure probability at the ...
threshold"). It says the number is the value *at* the threshold, but never
what the threshold is. Two people could not build the same table from it. One
might use the blocklength limit of BP decoding and the other the
density-evolution fixed point. Those agree, but only by a theorem the page
does not state. T272 and T273, from the same batch, both write out the
limiting condition.

**Smallest fix:**

> ... The belief-propagation threshold $\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ is the
> supremum of the erasure probabilities $\epsilon$ of the binary erasure
> channel CITE{WikiBEC} for which the erasure probability of belief-propagation
> decoding on this ensemble tends to $0$ as the blocklength tends to infinity
> CITE{RichardsonUrbanke}.

This also cites `RichardsonUrbanke` from the prose. Today it appears only
under `sources` (see finding 5).

### 3. `comment-l2` and `comment-threshold` are written as notes, and each leaves a term undefined. Worth doing, cheap.

> There are no $l=2$ rows. In that degree-two variable-node case the
> density-evolution threshold is the stability value $1/(r-1)$, so these exact
> values are left to the comment rather than repeated as entries.

"In that degree-two variable-node case" is a fragment standing in for "when
$l=2$". "The stability value" is never defined. The clause "left to the
comment rather than repeated as entries" is about this website, not about the
codes. The fact itself is right: for $l=2$, $\epsilon(x)$ increases on
$(0,1]$, so its infimum is its limit $1/(r-1)$ as $x\to0$.

**Fix:** "For $l=2$ the threshold is $\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}_{2,r}=1/(r-1)$,
the limit of $\epsilon(x)$ as $x\to0$, since $\epsilon(x)$ is increasing
there."

> At the threshold with $l\geq3$, density evolution has a nonzero double
> fixed point $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$; each entry comment gives this root.

"With $l\geq3$" repeats the definition. "Double fixed point" is not defined.
It means that the curve $\epsilon(x)$ is tangent to the channel parameter
there, which is the minimum from finding 1. "This root" points back at a
formula that, on the page, comes after this comment.

**Fix:** "At the threshold, the density-evolution equation has a nonzero
fixed point $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ at which $\epsilon(x)$ is minimal. Each
entry comment gives $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ to twelve decimal places."

### 4. `comment-conventions` answers a confusion no reader of this page has, and gets the rate condition slightly wrong. Worth doing, cheap.

> The value stored here is the channel erasure probability $\epsilon$, not the
> constraint density. The parity-check density of the ensemble is
> $m/n=l/r$, and its design rate is $1-l/r$ when the checks are independent.

"Not the constraint density" makes sense to someone who has just read T272 to
T274, which store densities. A coding theorist arriving here has no reason to
expect one. $m$ and $n$ are not introduced. The design rate is $1-l/r$ by
definition. What depends on the checks being linearly independent is whether
the *actual* rate equals it. Otherwise the actual rate is higher.

The fact a reader holding $0.4294$ would find useful is missing: the Shannon
limit for rate $1-l/r$ on the erasure channel is $\epsilon=l/r$, so each row
can be compared with $l/r$. For $(3,6)$ that is $0.4294$ against $0.5$.

**Fix:** "With $n$ variable nodes and $m$ check nodes, $m/n=l/r$, and the
design rate is $1-l/r$. The actual rate is at least this, with equality when
the parity checks are linearly independent. A code of rate $1-l/r$ can
correct erasures with probability at most $l/r$ on this channel, the Shannon
limit, and $\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}<l/r$ for every row." (The last
clause holds for all 45 stored rows. I compared each one with $l/r$.) Drop
the first sentence.

### 5. `RichardsonUrbanke` is listed and never cited. Cheap.

It appears only in `sources`. Density evolution comes from that paper, and
`formula-density-evolution` cites KRU for it instead. The fix in finding 2
cites it once. Alternatively, add `CITE{RichardsonUrbanke}` to
`formula-density-evolution`.

### 6. `Keywords` lacks what a coding theorist types. Cheap.

The title carries "LDPC" and "binary erasure channel", but the everyday forms
are "BEC" and "BP threshold", and the text search will not reach either from
the title. Add "BEC, BP threshold, erasure threshold" to `Keywords`.

## Noted only because I noticed

- **The $x^{\mathrm{BP}}_{l,r}$ values sit in entry comments.** The skill says
  a value belongs in a row. But these belong to a different quantity from the
  one the table stores. T273 and T274 do the same with their minimisers, and
  those critiques left it alone. I would too.
- **Entry $(3,6)$ repeats the check against KRU.** "Kudekar, Richardson and
  Urbanke give $\epsilon^{\mathrm{BP}}_{3,6}\approx0.42944$" is the same check
  `rigour details` records. It is only one row, and it tells a reader holding
  $0.42944$ that they are in the right place, so it can stay.
- **Why $r\leq12$?** `complete-note` gives the range but not the reason, and
  the skill asks for the reason in that sentence.
- **Overloaded $\epsilon$.** $\epsilon$ is the channel parameter in
  `formula-density-evolution` and a function $\epsilon(x)$ in the next formula.
  KRU does the same, and the text makes clear which is which.
- **One tag, `probability theory`.** There is no `coding theory` or
  `information theory` tag in the corpus. The skill asks for three tables
  before proposing a tag, and this is the only coding table in the batch.
  Leave it.
- **No `Similar tables`.** The peeling decoder on the erasure channel is the
  same process as the core computation in T273, but for a hypergraph with
  Poisson vertex degrees rather than regular ones. I have not checked a
  precise statement of that relation, so I am not proposing wording. If the
  owner can state it, a `Similar tables` entry to T273 would be the right
  place.
