# T321: Merit factors of the Legendre sequences

Read on 2026-09-18, against the skill fetched the same day from
<https://numberdb.org/skill> (47,534 bytes, fetched directly: nothing is
listening on `127.0.0.1:1080` at all today, which is a step worse than the
refusing-proxy notes already in `docs/agent-environment.md`).

T321 is a draft of 167 exact rationals, the aperiodic merit factor $F_p$ of the
length-$p$ Legendre sequence for every odd prime $p<1000$. `type: Q`,
`rigour: exact`, `complete: no`. It is the fourth table of the
Fekete/Shapiro/Legendre batch: T318 (Fekete polynomials) is public, T319
(Shapiro polynomials) is public, T320 (complementary Shapiro polynomials) is a
draft, and this one is the only one of the four that holds numbers rather than
polynomials.

**How I read it.** The HTML routes authenticate by session, so `GET /T321`
answers 404 to the zeta3 key. I rebuilt the page by the recipe in
`docs/agent-environment.md` -- the site's own code and templates, the document
as `/api/table?id=T321` serves it, a throwaway sqlite database inside the
`agents/sage.sh` container, with the range patches and the corrected
`_sync_tags` signature. Status 200, **85,252 bytes**, 167 rows, tags
`number theory` and `combinatorics` as the document says. Every quotation below
is from that page. The mathematics was checked in plain exact arithmetic
(`/tmp/t321_checks.py`), and the `Programs` block was run as stored under
`sage -python` in one `agents/sage.sh` run (`/tmp/t321_programs.py`).

`manage.py audit_table` cannot run here: there is no Django and no database on
this machine. `GET /api/table/T321/audit` answers
`{"findings": [], "clean": true}`, and **I agree with it** on everything it
looks at: every `CITE` resolves, all three `HREF` slugs name tables that exist,
the value column is headed `$F_p$` rather than the word `value`, the Definition
is 275 characters against a median of 195 and a threshold of 320, no parameter
takes a handful of names, the generator is attached and carries the install and
run commands, and no limit is anywhere near. Two of its rules are worth a word,
because both stand aside exactly here and both matter below:

* its draft-link rule (`HREF{...} points at a draft, which answers 404 to
  everybody`) is guarded by `table.published`, so on a draft linking a draft it
  says nothing -- finding 3;
* its "notation used in a formula and defined nowhere in the table" rule is
  satisfied by a definition anywhere in the document, and its
  `Formulas...below` rule fires only on the words. A symbol defined in a
  comment that the page draws *after* the formula using it passes both --
  finding 1.

**The table reads well.** The page renders whole, every stored value and every
identity on it is right, and the prose is in sentences with no fragments, no
dashes and no editorial. What is left is two clauses missing from the Formulas
block, a link that will break if the batch is published in the wrong order, a
relation written as a negation, a completeness note that says what and not why,
and a generator paragraph inherited from a sibling table.

## What I checked hardest, and found nothing wrong with

* **The page renders.** 85,252 bytes, status 200. Zero `CITE-broken` spans,
  zero "(Unknown key)", zero "Math input error", no `argument ()`, no
  unbalanced `$`, no `&` and no non-ASCII in the prose. Six labelled blocks (1)
  to (6), (P1), references [1] to [3]. The two hazards in the document are the
  bare `<` in the Definition's $1\leq j<p$ and in `complete-note`'s $p<1000$,
  and the renderer escapes both: the HTML holds `j&lt;p` and `p&lt;1000`, which
  is what MathJax needs. The `Programs` block reaches the page inside
  `<pre><code class="table-code language-python">` with its indentation intact,
  so the loop bodies are still inside their loops.
* **Every stored value comes back out of the Definition.** All 167, rebuilt
  from a Legendre sequence computed by Euler's criterion rather than by a
  library symbol, and reduced as exact fractions: 167 of 167 identical, from
  `9/2` to `994009/663668`. The keys are exactly the odd primes below 1000, and
  there are 167 of them, which is what `complete-note` claims.
* **Every formula holds.** (1) reproduces every stored value, and its
  parenthetical $c_0=p$ is true at every entry (this is the repaired form of
  the fault T320's critique found in its formula (3): here $u_j=\pm1$ is fixed
  by the Definition, so the claim follows). (2) was checked *numerically
  against the actual integral*, not against the same sum rearranged: sampling
  $|U_p(z)|^4$ at $8p$ roots of unity gives $\|U_p\|_4^4 = 63, 293, 1487,
  17101$ at $p=7,13,31,101$, and $p^2/(\|U_p\|_4^4-p^2)$ is the stored value
  each time. (3) is the right limit and the right attribution: the largest
  $|F_p-3/2|$ over $p>500$ is 0.0504, and the values oscillate around 3/2
  rather than approaching it monotonically, so the stated limit is the only
  thing that could have been stated.
* **The `Programs` block runs as stored and returns what it claims.**
  Preparsed and executed in the Sage image, `legendre_merit_factor(1009)`
  returns `1018081/679728` in `Rational Field`, equal to an independent
  Euler-criterion computation of the same quantity, and 1009 is indeed the next
  prime after 997. The code is also the shortest honest statement of the
  definition: no preparser dependency, no float division, `QQ(p * p) / QQ(2 *
  denominator)` written the way the skill asks.
* **The attached generator is the repository's, and its checks are real.**
  `/files/T321/generate.py` answers 200 to the key, 5,265 bytes, byte-identical
  to `generators/merit-factors-legendre-sequences/generate.py`. Its
  `check_identities` re-derives every value three ways -- Sage's
  `kronecker_symbol` against Euler's criterion, the known values at
  $p=3,5,7,11,13$, and $\|U\|_4^4-p^2$ summed over all ordered coefficient
  pairs instead of over positive shifts -- and it runs before every publish,
  not once.
* **Both references are real and are quoted correctly.** Crossref resolves
  `10.1109/TIT.1983.1056744` to Golay, IEEE Trans. IT **29** (1983), no. 6,
  934-936, and `10.1109/18.2620` to Hoholdt and Jensen, **34** (1988), no. 1,
  161-164: volume, issue, pages and year match the stored `bib` in both cases.
* **Comment (6)'s identity is true of the table it points at.** T318 defines
  $f_p(x)=\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)x^a$ with
  $\left(\frac{0}{p}\right)=0$, so its constant term is 0 and $U_p=1+f_p$ is
  exactly right. T318's own comment (3) says the same thing from the other
  side, which is the corpus agreeing with itself.
* **It is one table.** One parameter, an integer index, no parameter whose
  values are names, a value column headed with the quantity's symbol, no
  `param-latex`, no entry comments, and the parameter states the family ("$p$
  is an odd prime") rather than this afternoon's run. The batch's split is the
  good kind: Fekete polynomials, Shapiro polynomials and merit factors are
  three named objects, each findable by its own name, and the merit factor is

* **Nothing is named that the corpus holds and is not linked.** I harvested 318
  title/slug pairs from `/tables` and matched each against the prose with the

## Findings

### 1. Formula (2) uses $U_p$, and the page defines it two sections later

The Formulas block reads, as (2):

> If $\|U_p\|_4^4$ denotes the integral of $|U_p(z)|^4$ over $|z|=1$ with
> normalized Haar measure, then $F_p=p^2/(\|U_p\|_4^4-p^2)$.

Nothing above it on the page says what $U_p$ is. The definition is in comment
(6), two sections down:

> If $U_p(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}u_jx^j$, then $U_p(x)=1+f_p(x)$ ...

where it arrives as scaffolding for the Fekete relation rather than as the
definition of the symbol the formula above already used.

This is the page order trap, and the document walked straight into it: the YAML
writes `Comments` before `Formulas`, so in the document $U_p$ is introduced
before it is used, and the page draws Formulas first whatever the document
says. The audit knows this shape -- its own message for a formula that says
"see the comment below" is "the page draws Formulas before Comments, whatever
order the document is written in, so a reader who looks where they are told may
not find it" -- and it fires on the words "comment below", which (2) does not
say. The reader who is not told anything is worse off than the one who is sent
to the wrong place.

**Smallest fix**: define it where it is first used, in (2)'s own "If" clause,
and let (6) refer back.

> If $U_p(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}u_jx^j$ and $\|U_p\|_4^4$ denotes the integral of
> $|U_p(z)|^4$ over $|z|=1$ with normalized Haar measure, then
> $F_p=p^2/(\|U_p\|_4^4-p^2)$.

Then (6) opens "The polynomial $U_p$ of (2) satisfies $U_p(x)=1+f_p(x)$, where
..." and the Fekete comment is about Fekete.

Worth doing.

### 2. "and $c_0=p$" is stated where nothing uses it, and the identity that would use it is missing

Formula (1):

> $F_p=p^2/(2\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}c_k^2)$, where $c_k=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1-k}u_ju_{j+k}$
> and $c_0=p$.

The sum runs from $k=1$, so $c_0$ appears nowhere in (1). It is true -- I
checked it at every entry -- and it is there because (2) subtracts $p^2$. But
the step that connects them, $\|U_p\|_4^4=\sum_{k=1-p}^{p-1}c_k^2=c_0^2+2\sum_{k\geq1}c_k^2$,
is on neither formula, so a reader meets a fact in (1) that (1) does not use
and a subtraction in (2) that (2) does not explain, and has to reconstruct
Parseval to see that the two formulas are the same formula.

**Smallest fix**: move the clause to where it does something. Drop "and
$c_0=p$" from (1), and end (2) with it:

> ... then $F_p=p^2/(\|U_p\|_4^4-p^2)$, since
> $\|U_p\|_4^4=\sum_{k=1-p}^{p-1}c_k^2$ and $c_0=p$.

Worth doing; it is one clause moved and one identity added, and it turns two
formulas that look independent into one that is checkable.

### 3. `Similar tables` links a table that answers 404, and nothing will warn anybody

The third entry is
`HREF{Complementary_Shapiro_polynomials}[Complementary Shapiro polynomials $Q_n$]`.
That slug is T320, which is still a draft: `curl https://numberdb.org/Complementary_Shapiro_polynomials`
answers **404** today, while the other two answer 200. The link renders as an
ordinary anchor -- the renderer makes no existence check, and in my rebuilt
page, where none of the three targets existed, all three came out as plain
`<a class="HREF" href="...">` -- so a reader cannot tell it is dead until they
click it.

The audit has exactly this rule and it is switched off here: the check is
`elif table.published and public is not None and target not in public`, so it
speaks only about a *published* table. T321 is a draft, so it is silent; and it
will stay silent through the review, because the reviewer's act of publishing
is what makes the condition true. If T321 is published before T320, the page
goes public with a dead link that nothing has flagged and nobody has seen.

**Smallest fix**: publish T320 first, or publish the two together. Failing
that, drop the third `Similar tables` entry and add it back when T320 is
public. This is a note for whoever reviews the batch rather than an edit to
make now.

Worth doing, and it has to be done by the person who publishes.

### 4. The relation to T320 says what that table holds instead of how it relates

> Complementary Shapiro polynomials $Q_n$ — store the second polynomial in the
> Rudin-Shapiro recursion rather than a Legendre-symbol sequence merit factor

Read straight down the section, the reader has just been told the same thing
about T318 ("...rather than the merit factor of the length-$p$ sequence with
$u_0=1$"), and this sentence's only content beyond that negation is which
polynomial of the pair T320 holds -- which its title already says. The middle
relation is the one that works, because it names what the two families have in
common: "use the Rudin-Shapiro sign rule for another family of binary sequences
with small aperiodic autocorrelations". The skill's rule for this field is that
the relation is the valuable part, and "not the thing this table holds" is not
a relation.

The relation worth naming is the one a reader of *this* table would want, and
it is quantitative: the Shapiro coefficient sequences have merit factors of
their own, and they tend to 3 against 3/2 here. I checked that rather than
quoting it -- the length-$2^n$ Rudin-Shapiro sequence has merit factor 3.0476,
3.0118, 3.0029, 3.0007 at $n=6,8,10,12$.

**Smallest fix**: "the second sequence of the same Rudin-Shapiro pair, another
family of binary sequences with small aperiodic autocorrelations, whose merit
factors tend to 3 rather than to 3/2." If nobody wants a second limit on the
page, the first clause alone is still a relation and the present sentence is
not.

Worth doing; one line.

### 5. `complete-note` says what is here and not why it stops there

> Table is complete: no (it holds every odd prime $p<1000$)

The clause finishes the sentence, names the family and the bound, and matches
the entries exactly. What it does not carry is the argument: 1000 is a round
number, and nothing on the page says whether it was chosen because the values
get expensive, because the entries get unreadable, or because that is where
somebody stopped. None of those is true here in the usual way -- an $F_p$ is a
few characters at any $p$, and the whole table is 167 entries -- so the real
reason is about which lengths anybody arrives holding, which is the reason the
skill most wants written down.

Compare T320's, which the batch already knows how to write: "stopping before
the printed polynomials reach about two thousand characters; $Q_8$ has 1,931
characters."

**Smallest fix**: one clause saying why. If the reason is that the tabulations
in this literature stop around there, say so; if it is the $O(p^2)$ cost of the
autocorrelations, say that.

Worth doing.

### 6. The attached generator explains how to publish from a build machine the reader does not have, and states a convention about coefficients this table does not have

`/files/T321/generate.py` is what a contributor downloads from the table page.
Its docstring gives the three `sage -python` lines the skill asks for, and then

>     On the NumberDB build machine, where arguments are not passed through
>     agents/sage.sh, publish with:
>
>         $ cat "$NUMBERDB_KEY_FILE" | NUMBERDB_KEY_FROM_STDIN=1 NUMBERDB_PUBLISH=1 \
>               agents/sage.sh generate.py

`agents/sage.sh` is in a repository the reader does not have and
`$NUMBERDB_KEY_FILE` is set on one box. All four generators of this batch carry
it word for word -- T318's and T319's are already public -- and T320's critique
already asked for it to go; it is one edit in four files.

The second half is this table's own. The docstring continues:

> The family convention from numberdb-data#160 is used here: coefficient and
> sequence indices run low degree first, the Legendre sequence has u_0 = 1, and
> the merit factor is aperiodic.

T321 holds no coefficients and no polynomials. "Coefficient ... indices run low
degree first" is the batch's listing convention for its three polynomial
tables, which say "the variable is x, coefficients run low degree first" in the
same place, carried onto a table of rational numbers where it is true of
nothing on the page. It is the same sentence, from the same issue, that T320's
critique found had reached T320's *table* as a comment. The other two clauses
are load-bearing and should stay. Naming
the issue is also a trace of the request in a file served to the public, and it
buys nothing, since the sentence states the convention anyway.

**Smallest fix**: delete the build-machine paragraph, delete "coefficient and"
and the issue reference, and keep "The Legendre sequence has u_0 = 1 and the
merit factor is aperiodic. The periodic merit factor and the merit factors of
cyclically rotated Legendre sequences are different quantities."

Worth doing, in the batch.

## Noted because I noticed, not because they are worth an afternoon

* **The Definition defines the sequence and not the merit factor, and I think
  that is fine.** It ends "This table holds its aperiodic merit factor $F_p$",
  and $F_p$ itself is defined in formula (1). I considered calling this a
  finding -- the object the table holds is defined outside the Definition --
  and decided against it: (1) is the very next thing on the rendered page,
  putting the sum into the Definition would take it past the audit's length
  threshold, and two people reading the page would build the same table.
  Written down so the next reader does not "fix" it by moving the formula up.
* **`prime length` says "prime" twice with the constraint beside it.** The
  parameter line renders as "$p$ — prime length ($p$ is an odd prime)". The
  constraint is the field that has to carry "odd prime", so the title is free
  to say what $p$ measures: "length of the sequence". Half a minute, and only
  if somebody is editing the table anyway.
* **The `Programs` block's first line is not load-bearing here.**
  `import numberdb.sage as numberdb` is never used by the snippet, and unlike
  T320's case the three `from sage...` imports below it succeed without it: in
  a fresh interpreter in the deployed image -- which is what `sage -python
  file.py` gives -- both the stored four lines and the same four with the first
  removed import cleanly and evaluate `QQ(9)/QQ(2)` and
  `kronecker_symbol(2, 7)`. So a reader who has not run
  `sage -pip install numberdb` gets an ImportError from a program that does not
  need the package. It is the batch's house style -- T318's and T319's
  `Programs` blocks open with the same line, and theirs go on to import
  `PolynomialRing`, which is where T320's critique found the line earning its
  place -- and I did not test a modular passagemath, where the initialisation
  it does may still matter. Not worth an edit on its own.
* **The generator takes its table from the environment.**
  `table = os.environ.get("NUMBERDB_TABLE", "T321")` in a file readers
  download: a reader with that variable left set from something else would
  publish this table's numbers somewhere else. The skill's example hardcodes
  the T-number. Nobody has been bitten and the default is right.
* **A reader arriving with one of these numbers is served on the page, and
  search will want all their digits.** The rows are the first thing under the
  title, indexed by $p$ and headed $F_p$. Search by number is the part worth
  knowing about: in `/api/search` a searched decimal is a point, not the
  interval its last place denotes, and it has to fall inside the stored value's
  enclosure, which for an exact value is about as wide as a double. Measured on
  public tables -- `-691/2730` finds T13 and so does `-0.2531135531135531`,
  while `-0.2531135531` finds nothing; `3.141592653589793` finds seven tables
  and `3.14` finds none; `4.5` does find the stored `9/2` in T130, because 4.5
  is exactly 9/2. So a reader who pastes the full output of their own
  floating-point merit factor will land here once the table is public, and one
  who types `1.3629` will not. Nothing
  for this table to change -- exact rationals are the right thing to store --
  but it is a fact about what the table can answer, and I have written it up as
  a lesson proposal.
* **The rotated family is next door and is not mentioned.** Comment (4) says
  "the merit factor is the aperiodic one" and that the periodic one is a
  different quantity, which is the caveat that matters. The other convention a
  reader may be holding is a rotation: rotating the sequence by a quarter of
  its length gives merit factors near 6 rather than near 3/2 (I get 5.98 at
  $p=997$ against 1.4978 unrotated), and 6 is the number this literature is
  best known for. The corpus holds no table of them, so there is
  nothing to link; one clause in (4) naming the rotated family as the other
  thing a reader might have would serve the reader who arrives with a 6. An
  addition, not a fault.
* **"Hoholdt" is what IEEE's own record says.** The author is Tom Høholdt, and
  the unaccented spelling looks like a fault. It is not: Crossref returns
  `Hoholdt` for `10.1109/18.2620`, so the `bib` matches the publisher's record,
  and the ASCII form is also what a reader typing the name will search for.
  Noted only to stop the next reader "correcting" it.
* **Nothing in the corpus points back here.** T318 has no `Similar tables`
  section at all, and T319's names only T318, so a reader of the two public
  tables in this batch cannot reach T321. Nothing can be done today -- a
  published table may not link a draft -- but it is the same note T320's
  critique left: the act of publishing T321 is also the moment to add the
  relation to T318, whose comment (3) already tells its readers that the
  Legendre sequence with $u_0=1$ is a different object and now has somewhere to
  send them.
