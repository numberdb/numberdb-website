## Findings, ranked

### 1. The rigour note names a guard the generator did not use

"How they were obtained" says each value was computed "at
`numberdb.bits(digits, losing=64)` bits" and that "the widest returned
ball had radius less than $10^{-119}$". The attached generator says
`WORKING_GUARD = 80`, with a comment that at that guard the widest
radius is below $10^{-119}$. Both cannot be true, and measurement says
which is: over all 270 entries, the widest ball at a guard of 64 bits
has radius $1.6\cdot10^{-115}$ (at $\nu=1/3$, $x=5$) and at 80 bits
$2.5\cdot10^{-120}$, at the same entry. So the values were made at 80
and the sentence was carried over from the $I_\nu$ table, whose note
reads the same with $10^{-115}$. The digits are unaffected either way;
what is wrong is the account of how they were obtained, which is the
one field whose whole job is to be accurate about that. Smallest
change: write `losing=80` in the rigour note. The Sage program on the
page also says 64, which is fine for a program, since 64 still
supports a hundred digits, but matching it to the generator would
cost nothing.

### 2. Comment (5) says the $I_\nu$ table has what it does not have

"The point $x=0$ is excluded because $K_\nu$ has a branch point there,
which distinguishes it from the table of $I_\nu$." A reader takes this
to mean the $I_\nu$ table has a row at $x=0$ and this one does not. It
does not: T196 starts at $x=1/4$ too, and its own comment says it
"begins above $x=0$ so that it uses the same argument range as" the
$J_\nu$ and $Y_\nu$ tables. The two tables hold exactly the same
thirty arguments. There is also a pronoun with no clear owner, "it",
which could be the exclusion, the branch point or the function. The
reason a reader wants is the plain one: $K_\nu(x)\to\infty$ as
$x\to0^+$, for every order. Smallest change: "The point $x=0$ is
excluded because $K_\nu(x)\to\infty$ as $x\to0^+$." and drop the
comparison.

### 3. Thirty rows carry the same formula as a comment

Every row at $\nu=1/2$ has the entry comment
"$K_{1/2}(x)=\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}e^{-x}$." It is formula (3), printed
thirty-one times on the page. The skill's rule is that an entry comment
earns its place by saying something the row cannot, and is left out when
it repeats what a formula already says for every row at once. The
$I_\nu$ table does the same with its own half-order formula, so this is
a batch habit rather than one table's slip, and the person deciding may
want to fix both. Smallest change: remove `_comment` from the generator
and republish; comment (8) already says half-integer orders are
elementary and can point at the formula.

### 4. The $I_\nu$ table it links to is a draft

The page links "the table of $I_\nu$" in comment (5) and again first in
Similar tables. The slug
`Values_of_modified_Bessel_functions_of_the_first_kind` answers Not
Found to an anonymous reader, and so does `/T196`: it is still a draft.
`audit_table --links` accepts a link from one draft to another, which
is why it says nothing. If T199 is published first, its two most
important links are dead for everybody who is not logged in. Not a
change to the table, but a fact about the order of publishing that the
reviewer should know.

### 5. Comment (8) alludes to a formula the page could state

"Half-integer orders are elementary. The Bessel polynomials occur in
explicit formulas for the associated spherical and modified spherical
functions." A reader cannot tell what "associated" attaches to, and the
link to the Bessel polynomials is a promise the page does not keep,
since no formula on it contains a $y_n$. For $K$ the formula is short,
exact and finite:
$K_{n+1/2}(x)=\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}\,e^{-x}\,y_n(1/x)$ for $n\geq0$, with
$y_n$ the Bessel polynomial in the corpus's convention ($y_0=1$,
$y_1=x+1$, $y_2=3x^2+3x+1$). I checked it in ball arithmetic on the
stored rows at $\nu=1/2,3/2,5/2$ for $x=1$, $1/4$, $19/4$ and $5$: the
closed form and arb's $K_\nu$ differ by a ball containing zero in every
case, and both round to the stored digits. It is DLMF 10.49.12 in the
notation of 10.47.9. Smallest change: add it to Formulas, where it
subsumes formula (3) as the case $n=0$, and let comment (8) cite it.
That is the sentence which makes the Bessel-polynomials link and the
last Similar-tables gloss mean something on this page.

### 6. The definition names the function rather than defining it

"This table gives the real principal values of the modified Bessel
function of the second kind $K_\nu(x)$, Macdonald's function, [1] [2]
[8], for rational order $\nu\geq0$ and rational argument $x>0$." The
modified Bessel equation appears nowhere on the page, although Similar
tables calls $I_\nu$ "the other standard solution of the modified
Bessel equation", and the relation
$K_\nu=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,\frac{I_{-\nu}-I_\nu}{\sin\nu\pi}$ that ties this
table to its sibling is not stated either. The same wording is on the
three published sibling tables, T187, T190 and T196, so this is the
batch's convention and changing one table alone would make it the odd
one out; I am recording it rather than pressing it. If it is changed,
one sentence does the work: "$K_\nu$ is the solution of
$x^2w''+xw'-(x^2+\nu^2)w=0$ that decays as $x\to\infty$, normalised by
$K_\nu(x)\sim\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}\,e^{-x}$." The decaying solution is unique
up to a constant and the asymptotic fixes the constant, so two people
would build the same table from it. "Real principal values" on the
positive axis says the same thing twice; "real" is enough there.

### 7. Two keywords the page never explains

`Keywords` carries "Matern covariance" and "Yukawa potential". Neither
phrase, nor anything about covariance functions or screened potentials,
appears anywhere a reader can see. The same fault was named on T197 and
T198 in this batch. Drop the two, or write one sentence in a comment
saying where $K_\nu$ arises and let the keywords point at it. Dropping
is smaller. "Cylinder functions" is shared with the $J$ and $Y$ tables
and is a name for the family, which is fine.

### 8. Small things, noted because I noticed

* Comment (6) and formula (2) both say $K_{-\nu}=K_\nu$. The comment
  is the reason negative orders are absent and the formula is the
  identity, so both have a home, but the comment could say "by (2)"
  instead of restating it.
* Four of the eight Similar tables are the zeros and extrema of $J$ and
  $Y$, glossed as "stores zeros of the ordinary Bessel function
  $J_\alpha$", which describes that table rather than relating it to
  this one. $K_\nu$ has no zeros for $x>0$. The list is copied from the
  siblings; a person may prefer the batch to look alike.
* "The principal branch is that of [2]" in comment (5) is moot on the
  positive real axis, where every branch agrees; harmless.

## What reads well

The parameters describe the family and the complete-note describes the
run, in a clause that finishes the sentence the site starts. Every
formula is a relation and the checking is under "How they were
obtained", where it belongs. The recurrence, the Tricomi identity and
the half-order formula are cited to the DLMF sections that hold them.
Both programs run as printed and give the stored digits, and the
comment in the Sage program about arb taking the order as the method's
argument is the one line a reader would otherwise get wrong. The glosses
"over the same orders and arguments" on the $I$, $J$ and $Y$ tables are
true: all four tables hold the same nine orders and thirty arguments.
