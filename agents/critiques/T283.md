# Critique of T283, "Mahler measures of $1+x_1+\dots+x_{n-1}$ (short random walks)"

Read on 2026-09-16. The table is a private draft. It holds four real values,
$\mu_3$ to $\mu_6$, to 50 decimals, with `rigour: heuristic (agreement-checked)`.

## The audit

The audit is clean, and nothing it checks for is wrong here. This is one
table with one integer parameter, the column headings say what the columns
hold, and the links resolve. It misses two things below. Finding 2 is
notation that is used but never defined; the audit's docstring claims that
check but no code makes it, as the T221 note says. Finding 4 is a Similar
tables gloss that is false about this table.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The complete-note does not say why the table stops at $n=6$. Worth doing, one clause.

The page reads: "Table is complete: no (it holds the classical short-walk
cases $3\leq n\leq6$; the cases $n=1$ and $n=2$ are zero and are stated in
the comments)". "Classical" is the only reason given, and a reader cannot
tell what it means. The actual reason is visible in the Formulas: $n=3$ and
$n=4$ have proved closed forms, $n=5$ and $n=6$ have conjectured ones, and
nothing is quoted for $n\geq7$. A reader who arrives holding
$\mu_7=0.70292\ldots$ (from the same Bessel integral) should learn from this
note that it is missing on purpose, not by oversight.

Fix: "it holds $3\leq n\leq6$, the values of $n$ for which an evaluation is
proved ($n=3,4$) or conjectured ($n=5,6$); $\mu_1=\mu_2=0$". If the reason is
something else, write that instead. Check against CITE{StraubZudilin} before
adding any claim that no evaluation is known for $n\geq7$.

### 2. $m(\cdot)$ is used throughout and defined nowhere. Worth doing.

The Definition says "$\mu_n$ is the logarithmic Mahler measure CITE{Wiki} of
$1+x_1+\cdots+x_{n-1}$". It never writes the integral and never introduces a
symbol. The comments then use $m(1)=m(1+x)=0$, $\mu_3=m(1+x+y)$ and
$\mu_4=m(1+x+y+z)$, and the keywords carry $m(1+x+y)$. The skill says "If a
symbol appears in a formula or a comment, its definition belongs in the same
table". A reader of the Definition also has to leave the page to learn what
is being computed, although the whole integral fits in one line.

Fix: "For an integer $n\geq1$, $\mu_n=m(1+x_1+\cdots+x_{n-1})$, where
$m(P)=\int_{[0,1]^k}\log|P(e^{2\pi it_1},\dots,e^{2\pi it_k})|\,dt_1\cdots dt_k$
is the logarithmic Mahler measure CITE{Wiki} of a polynomial $P$ in $k$
variables." Keep the second sentence about $W_n'(0)$.

### 3. `comment-indexing` points at things instead of naming them. Worth doing.

It reads: "The index $n$ is the number of unit steps, or equivalently the
number of terms in $x_1+\cdots+x_n$; the displayed polynomial has $n-1$
variables after dividing by the first term."

A reader has to guess three things. What is $x_1+\cdots+x_n$? It appears
nowhere else on the page. Which polynomial is "the displayed polynomial"? It
is the one in the title. Why is dividing by "the first term" allowed? It is
allowed because $|x_1|=1$ on the torus, and the sentence never says so.

Fix: "The index $n$ is the number of steps of the walk, and the number of
terms of $1+x_1+\cdots+x_{n-1}$. Since $|x_n|=1$ on the torus,
$m(x_1+\cdots+x_n)=m(1+x_1+\cdots+x_{n-1})$, so one variable can be removed."
The second sentence of the comment (the zeros for $n=1,2$) is fine.

### 4. The Pólya gloss says this table is indexed by a dimension. Worth doing, one line.

Similar tables reads: "Pólya's random walk constants: gives return
probabilities for lattice random walks, another family of constants indexed
by a walk dimension". T19 is indexed by the lattice dimension $d$. This table
is indexed by the number of steps of a walk in the plane. "Another" makes the
sentence claim that this table is indexed by a dimension too, which is false.

Fix: "gives the return probabilities of simple random walks on
$\mathbb Z^d$, indexed by the dimension; the walks here are in the plane and
indexed by their number of steps". Deleting the entry is also reasonable,
since the tables have only the words "random walk" in common.

### 5. `rigour details` describes the run, not the digits. Worth doing, small.

The second paragraph starts "Before the draft was filled". It checks
$\mu_5$ and $\mu_6$ "to the 18 digits printed in the screening report". It
ends with a series "rejected for this first draft". A reader has no
screening report, and "draft" will be wrong the day the table is published.

The paragraph also leaves out the fact that actually carries digits 19 to 50
of $\mu_5$ and $\mu_6$. The stored digits are digits of the eta-integrals
(5) and (6). They are digits of $\mu_5$ and $\mu_6$ because Borwein, Straub,
Wan and Zudilin confirmed those identities to 600 and 80 places, not because
of an 18-digit check. The Comments say this, but the rigour note is where a
reader asks the question.

Fix: "$\mu_3$ and $\mu_4$ were also computed from the Bessel integral CITE{formula-bessel},
and $\mu_5$ and $\mu_6$ agree with it to 18 digits. The eta-integrals were
evaluated at 50, 80 and 120 working digits, and the 50 digits kept here
agree. The eta-integrals equal $\mu_5$ and $\mu_6$ conjecturally, and BSWZ
confirmed the identities numerically to 600 and 80 decimal places
CITE{BSWZ}, which covers the 50 kept here." The sentence about the rejected
moment series can go. If it stays, drop "for this first draft".

### 6. The comments on rows 5 and 6 call the value a conjecture. Small.

"Rodriguez-Villegas' eta-integral conjecture for $\mu_5$, confirmed
numerically by Borwein, Straub, Wan and Zudilin." The row holds $\mu_5$,
which is not conjectural. The conjecture is that $\mu_5$ equals an integral.
The name is also spelled two ways: "Rodriguez Villegas" in
`comment-known-values` and "Rodriguez-Villegas" in these rows and in the
rigour note. The name is published without the hyphen.

Fix: "Conjecturally equal to the eta-integral CITE{formula-rv5} of Rodriguez
Villegas; confirmed numerically by Borwein, Straub, Wan and Zudilin
CITE{BSWZ}." Do the same for row 6, and remove the hyphen in the rigour
note.

## Noted only

- **Keyword "Lehmer problem".** Nothing in the prose connects these values to
  Lehmer's problem. Drop it, or add the sentence that justifies it. T282 has
  the same keyword and the same issue.
- **Citation for $\mu_4$.** Formula (4) cites only Smyth's 1981 *Bull.
  Austral. Math. Soc.* paper, while `comment-known-values` cites Smyth and
  Boyd together. As I recall, $m(1+x+y+z)=7\zeta(3)/(2\pi^2)$ was first
  printed in Boyd's *Speculations* paper, credited to Smyth. I have not
  checked this. If it is right, formula (4) should cite CITE{Boyd} too.
- **Programs.** The program reproduces the two closed forms, so a reader who
  wants one more value, $\mu_7$, gets nothing. A general program is not a
  one-liner. The Bessel formula (2) with plain `mp.quad` on $[0,1]$ and
  `mp.quadosc(..., omega=n)` on $[1,\infty)$ at `dps=20` gave $\mu_3$ and
  $\mu_5$ to 19 or more digits. It gave $\mu_2=3.6\times10^{-5}$ instead of
  $0$, $\mu_4$ wrong in the 8th place and $\mu_6$ in the 10th, because the
  tail of $J_0^n/x$ does not oscillate for even $n$. Leave the program as it
  is unless somebody writes and checks a better one.
- **Links into T153 could name the rows.** The T153 relation could link
  `HREF{Entropy_constants_of_lattice_models#dimer,honeycomb,entropy}` and
  `#spanning-tree,triangular,entropy`, since it names two numbers. It reads
  well as it stands.
- **For other tables, not this one.** T153's `dimer,honeycomb,entropy`
  comment says "half the Mahler measure of $1+x+y$" and could link to
  `T283#3` once this table is published. The sibling draft T282 (Mahler
  measures of $x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}+k$) and this table should list each other
  in Similar tables once both are public.

## What reads well

It is one table. It has one integer parameter whose constraint $n\geq1$
describes the family, and the range is in `complete-note`. The column heading
$\mu_n$ is true of every row. The conjectural evaluations are written with
$\stackrel{?}{=}$ and called conjectures in the Comments, so nothing claims
more than is known. Formulas (3) and (4) link the constants they use to the
rows that hold them, not just to the tables. The four entry comments give a
reader who arrives holding $0.3230659\ldots$ or $0.4262783\ldots$ the closed
form on the row they land on. The page renders cleanly, with no TeX errors.
