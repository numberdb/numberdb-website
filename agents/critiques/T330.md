## Worth doing

### 1. It is a table of polynomials and is not tagged `polynomial`

Tags are `analysis`, `algebraic`, `eigenvalue`. The corpus has a `polynomial`
tag with fifty tables on it, and T237 — "Characteristic polynomials of
connected graphs", which this table names in `Similar tables` as its nearest
relative — carries `polynomial`, `combinatorics`, `eigenvalue`, `chemistry`.
A reader browsing sideways from T237 through `polynomial`, which is how a tag
is meant to be used, never reaches T330.

`analysis` looks inherited rather than chosen: T327 is condition numbers, where
it fits, and T329 and T330 carry the same triple. Nothing here is analysis —
every value is an exact element of $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ obtained from an integer or
rational matrix.

Smallest fix: add `polynomial`. Dropping `analysis` is a separate judgement and
I would drop it.

### 2. The second-difference polynomials are Chebyshev $U_n$, and the corpus holds those

`formula-second-difference` gives the recurrence $p_n(x)=(x-2)p_{n-1}(x)-p_{n-2}(x)$
with $p_0=1$, $p_1=x-2$. That is exactly the Chebyshev recurrence composed with
$t=(x-2)/2$, so

$$\chi_{A_n}(x) = U_n\!\left(\tfrac{x-2}{2}\right).$$

I checked this against the stored entries for every $n$ from 2 to 12: equal in
all eleven. T99 is "Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind $U_n$", at
`Chebyshev_polynomials_of_the_second_kind`.

This is the finding that changes what a reader gets. Somebody who arrives
holding `x^3 - 6*x^2 + 10*x - 4` currently learns it is the characteristic
polynomial of a 3×3 second-difference matrix and stops there; one clause more
tells them it is a Chebyshev polynomial in disguise and hands them the table of
those. Of twenty families this is the one whose polynomials have a name
elsewhere in the corpus.

Smallest fix: end `formula-second-difference` with "equivalently
$\chi_{A_n}(x)=U_n((x-2)/2)$, where $U_n$ is the Chebyshev polynomial of the
second kind, HREF{Chebyshev_polynomials_of_the_second_kind}".

### 3. The `family` parameter points at the comments instead of naming its source

The constraint is `one of the named matrix families listed in the comments`,
and the parameter line renders as

> $A_n$ —   matrix family (one of the named matrix families listed in the comments)

which says a matrix family is one of the named matrix families. A reader who
wants to know *which* twenty, and on whose authority, has to scroll to the
second comment and find "MATLAB gallery documentation or the Test Matrix
Toolbox source" applied there to nine of them. "Classical test matrix" is the
phrase in the title and the definition and is never anchored to anything.

T329, the sibling, already does this right: its `family` constraint reads "one
of the real symmetric matrix families from MATLAB's `gallery`
CITE{MathWorksGallery} or the Test Matrix Toolbox source CITE{TMTGallery}".

Smallest fix: adopt T329's sentence, dropping "real symmetric". Both links are
declared here already.

### 4. The Mertens function is named twice and linked nowhere

`formula-redheffer` says "where $M(n)$ is the Mertens function", and `rigour
details` says "the Redheffer determinant against the Mertens function". Neither
carries a link. The corpus has no table of the Mertens function — the word
search returns "Meissel-Mertens constant $M$" and "Mertens constants of primes
in arithmetic progressions", which are different objects, so the rule that
applies is the second half of it: a reference the table already declares.
`WikiRedheffer` is declared, and the Redheffer article is precisely where
$\det = M(n)$ is explained.

Smallest fix: `CITE{WikiRedheffer}` at the first mention, in
`formula-redheffer`.

## Noting only

- **Two of the three `Similar tables` are drafts.** T327 and T329 answer 404 to
  anybody without a key; T237 is public. If the three are published together
  this resolves itself, and the audit deliberately permits drafts to link to
  each other. Worth a check at review time and nothing before it.

- **The definition describes the table rather than the object.** "For a named
  classical test matrix $A_n$, this table gives the characteristic polynomial
  …". Compare T237: "The characteristic polynomial $\phi(G,x)$ of a graph $G$
  is $\det(xI-A(G))$ …". It is a register difference, not a loss of
  information, and it costs a rewrite: "The characteristic polynomial of a
  classical test matrix $A_n$ of order $n$ is $\chi_{A_n}(x)=\det(xI_n-A_n)$."

- **Kac-Murdock-Szegő is reachable only unaccented.** The name appears as
  "Kac-Murdock-Szego" in the parameter values, the comment and `Keywords`, and
  the index has no `unaccent`, so a reader typing "Szegő" finds nothing. The
  skill's own remedy is to carry both forms; here only one exists. One extra
  keyword.

- **`Programs` shows one family.** The snippet builds the 3×3 Hilbert matrix and
  calls `charpoly()`. For a reader who wants the Riemann matrix at $n=13$ it is
  a template to adapt from the comments rather than an incantation to run. Fair
  for a twenty-family table; a parenthesis saying "(Hilbert matrix, $n=3$)"
  would stop it reading as *the* matrix of this table.

- **The $n=2$ rows collide with each other.** `x^2 - 3*x + 1` is the entry for
  six different families (Frank, Minij, Moler, Pascal, Riemann, Vandermonde),
  `x^2 - 1` for two and `x^2 - 2*x + 3/4` for two more — every 2×2 here with
  trace 3 and determinant 1 gives the same answer. This is not a fault: they
  really are the characteristic polynomials, and a reader holding
  $x^2-3x+1$ is told six true things. I note it because `complete-note` calls
  $2\le n\le 12$ "the nontrivial small orders", and $n=2$ is the order at which
  these families stop being distinguishable. Nothing to change.

## What I checked hardest, and what it says

**Do the definitions define?** This is the question a twenty-family table lives
or dies on, because the definition proper says only "a named classical test
matrix" and the whole content is in three comments. I rebuilt all twenty
matrices in plain Python from the comment text alone — nothing from the
generator — and computed $\det(xI_n-A_n)$ by interpolation over
`fractions.Fraction`, for $n = 2,3,4,5$: **80 polynomials, all twenty families,
zero mismatches.** Every sentence in `comment-families`,
`comment-toolbox-families` and `comment-more-toolbox-families` is precise
enough that a second person building from it gets this table back, including
the ones most easily read two ways: the Frank matrix ("unreflected upper
Hessenberg"), the Riemann matrix's shift to $B(2{:}n+1,2{:}n+1)$, the Moler
matrix's $\min(i,j)-2$ off the diagonal, and the Ris matrix. The "Indices are
$1$-based" sentence at the head of the first comment is load-bearing and is
there.

The three `Formulas` also hold across the whole stored range, not just where
they were checked: Clement for $n=2..12$, second-difference for $n=2..12$, and
$\chi_{A_n}(0)=(-1)^nM(n)$ for the Redheffer matrix for $n=2..12$.

**Is it one table?** Twenty named families under one value column is the shape
the skill warns about, so I asked it properly rather than trusting the clean
audit — and the audit does not ask here at all: `MOST_LABELS = 4` in
`audit_table.py`, so a parameter with twenty values is taken to be indexing
something and the check stands down before it looks. Reading it as a reader I
agree with the outcome. `family` names *what the polynomial is of*, not *which
quantity is taken*; it is the same shape as one Ehrhart polynomial for each of
seventy-eight root systems. The column header is the evidence the skill points
at: `$\chi_{A_n}(x)$`, one symbol true of every one of the 220 rows, not the
word `value`. T329, which stores $\lambda_k(A_n)$ over the symmetric subset, is
a different quantity in a different table, and that is the right split.

**Does it render?** All eleven pieces returned 200 and rendered in full: three
comments, three formulas, `Data properties` with the fold under "How they were
obtained", `Programs`, `Similar tables`, the parameter list, and entries at the
smallest and largest sizes. No `<`, `>` or `&` appears anywhere in the prose.
All 48 distinct `$…$` fragments typeset without error under MathJax 3 with the
site's own package set (`tex-svg`: base, ams, newcommand, noundefined, require,
autoload, configmacros), and again with `noundefined` removed so an undefined
macro would raise; controls with an unclosed brace and an unknown macro did
raise. The parameter list renders as two named groups with no `argument ()`,
and entry anchors come out as `hilbert,2` — the citable form.

**Two things the skill is strict about, done right.** The parameters describe
the family ($n\geq 1$) and not the run, with $2\leq n\leq 12$ in
`complete-note` where it belongs — and that note goes on to say *why* it stops,
in characters of the largest entry rather than in a count. And every "we
checked it" sentence is in `rigour details`, leaving the three formulas as
formulas. The largest entry, the Ris matrix at $n=12$, is 1139 characters,
which is just at the line the skill draws with $F_{100}$ at 1107; the note's
argument for stopping is exactly that, and it is the right argument.

**The audit.** Clean, and I agree with it. Nothing it reports is wrong here and
nothing it missed is large; findings 1 to 4 above are all outside what it
looks for.
