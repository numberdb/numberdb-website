## Findings, ranked

### 1. Three links on the page go to a draft, and the rigour note leans on it

A reader sees "Stirling polynomial" linked in formula (2), in Similar
tables, and in the rigour note. The link is to `Stirling_polynomials`,
which is T180: zeta3's draft, not published. Fetched anonymously it is
Not Found. If T186 is published before T180, the three links are dead,
and the rigour note's sentence "using the stored values of Stirling
polynomials for the last comparison" cites values a reader cannot see.

Nothing in the text is wrong; formula (2) is right against T180's
definition (checked: $S_1=(x+1)/2$ and $S_2=x^2/4+5x/12+1/6$ both come
out of $n!(x+1)\sigma_n(x+1)$). The fix is an order, not an edit:
publish T180 first, or together. Worth knowing for whoever reviews.

One thing to notice on the way through the link. T180's comment says
Knuth's $\sigma_n(x)$ "are different families" from its $S_k(x)$;
T186's Similar-tables gloss says they "are the same Stirling family in
the Sheffer-sequence normalisation". T186 has the better of it, since
(2) is a shift and a factor, and a reader who follows the link is told
the opposite of what they just read. That is a sentence in T180, not
here, and is noted so the two drafts can be made to agree.

### 2. Comment (9) uses notation the page never defines, for numbers the page writes another way

A reader sees: "This title includes "convolution" to distinguish them
from Stirling polynomials $S_k(x)$ and from the Gessel-Stanley
polynomials $S(n+k,n)$ and $c(n,n-k)$."

$S(\cdot,\cdot)$ and $c(\cdot,\cdot)$ are defined nowhere on the page.
Formulas (4) and (5), two screens up, write the same numbers as
$\left[{a\atop b}\right]$ and $\left\{{a\atop b}\right\}$, so a reader
has to guess that $S$ is the braces and $c$ is the brackets, and then
guess that $n$ is the variable and $k$ the index. "This title includes"
is also a sentence about this table's name rather than about the
polynomials, which the skill asks to leave out.

Smallest fix, keeping every fact: "Knuth CITE{Knuth} calls these
convolution polynomials, and Concrete Mathematics CITE{Concrete} calls
them Stirling polynomials. That name is also used for the Sheffer
sequence $S_n(x)$ of HREF{Stirling_polynomials}[Stirling polynomials],
related by (2), and for the Gessel–Stanley polynomials, which are
$\left\{{n+k\atop n}\right\}$ and $\left[{n\atop n-k}\right]$ as
polynomials in $n$ for fixed $k$." Worth doing; it is one comment.

### 3. Program (P1) is the generator, forty-five lines of it

A reader who wants $\sigma_{26}$ is given a series multiplier, a
base-series routine, a falling-factorial routine and a binomial
expansion. That is `generate.py`, and the skill keeps the two apart:
`Programs` is the incantation, and the generator is attached to the
table.

Five lines do the same in Sage, tested in the throwaway against all
25 stored polynomials and P1's own output for $n\leq25$, with no
disagreement:

    R.<x> = QQ[]
    S.<z> = PowerSeriesRing(R, 30)
    f = (x * (z / (1 - exp(-z))).log()).exp()   # (z e^z/(e^z-1))^x
    sigma = lambda n: R(f[n] / x)
    sigma(26)

Two things a reader would hit, worth one comment line each if this is
adopted: `(z/(1-exp(-z)))**x` is refused ("exponent must be a rational
number or power series"), which is why it goes through `exp` and `log`;
and the `.exp()` of a series over $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ needs `sage.all`
loaded, since with only the named ring imports it dies in a circular
import inside `sage.symbolic`. Worth doing if the page is being edited;
the present program is correct, only long.

### 4. Two of the five Links are cited from nowhere

[6] OEIS A008275 and [7] OEIS A008277 sit in Links and no sentence

`CITE{OEISFirstKind}` after "unsigned Stirling number of the first kind"

### 5. The Similar-tables glosses do not quite parse

"Stirling polynomials — are the same Stirling family in the
Sheffer-sequence normalisation of (2)": (2) is a formula, not a
normalisation. "Touchard polynomials — have Stirling numbers of the
second kind as coefficients, which give (5)": coefficients do not give
a formula. Suggest "hold the same polynomials in the Sheffer-sequence
normalisation, related by (2)" and "have as coefficients the Stirling
numbers of the second kind in (5)". Marginal; do it with 2.

## Noted only

* **$\sigma_0(x)=1/x$ is stated three times**, in (1), (3) and (8).
  (3) needs it, since the recurrence at $n=1$ uses $\sigma_0$; (8) is
  the caveat and can stay; the clause in (1) is the one to drop.
* **"Noerlund"** in the rigour note is a transliteration; the site
  renders "Lévy" and would render "Nørlund". The captions of [4] and
  [5] say "Bernoulli polynomials", following OEIS, where the note calls
  the same relation Nørlund's, so a reader has to work out that the
  "Bernoulli polynomials $B^{(n)}(x)$" of A001898 are the
  $B_n^{(a)}$ of (7) and not the table of Bernoulli polynomials this
  corpus holds. A caption such as "OEIS A100655: coefficients of the
  Nørlund polynomials $B_n^{(x)}$, times their common denominators"
  would say so.
* **The rigour note writes the generating function as
  $(z/(1-e^{-z}))^x$** where the definition has $ze^z/(e^z-1)$. They
  are equal, and a reader has to check that they are. $b(z)$ in
  "$(1+b(z))^x$" is undefined but clear from the shape.
* **Comment (9) writes $S_k(x)$, formula (2) writes $S_n(x)$**, for the
  same polynomials.
* **Keywords carry "Stirling polynomial" and "Stirling polynomials"**,
  so this table and T180 answer that search with equal weight. Given
  Knuth's usage that is defensible, and a reader gets both, which is
  what they should get.

  above. I derived each rather than trusting it.

* **First screen.** A reader holding $x/8-1/24$ sees it as the second
