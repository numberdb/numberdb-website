## Findings, most worth doing first

### 1. The $r=10$ comment has a stray `$`, and its formula falls apart on the page. Worth doing; one character.

The stored comment is

    MathWorld gives $K(k_{10})=\sqrt{2+3\sqrt2+\sqrt5}$ \sqrt{\Gamma(1/40)…/(2560\pi^3)}$ CITE{MathWorldSingularValue}.

It has three dollars. The math closes after `\sqrt5}`, so the whole Gamma
product prints as raw TeX (`\sqrt{\Gamma(1/40)\Gamma(7/40)…`), and a lone `$`
is left before "[4]". The other nine comments have two dollars each. The
closed form itself is correct: it matches the stored value to below
$10^{-60}$.

**Fix:** replace `$ \sqrt{` with `\,\sqrt{`, so the formula is one piece of
mathematics.

### 2. The page writes the stored number as $K(k_r)$ in some places and $K(m_r)$ in others, and the Definition states a false-looking equation. Worth doing.

The Definition renders: "The entry is $K(k_r)=K(m_r)$, the complete
elliptic integral of the first kind in the parameter convention." Under any
single convention for $K$, that equation is false, since $m_r=k_r^2\neq k_r$.
A reader has to read comment (1) to learn that the left side is the modulus
notation and the right side the parameter notation.

The Formulas section then uses both notations, one line apart:

- (1) "$K(k_r)=\frac{\pi}{2}\theta_3(0,q)^2$" (modulus notation)
- (2) "$K(1-m_r)=\sqrt r\,K(m_r)$" (parameter notation)
- (3) "$K(k_r)=\frac{\pi}{2}\,{}_2F_1(1/2,1/2;1;m_r)$" (modulus notation,
  with the parameter as the argument)

`Similar tables` links T25, "$K(m)$". A reader who takes (1) or (3) to T25
and looks up $K$ at $k_r$ gets a different number. This is the trap the T295
repair fixed for that table (see
`agents/lessons/proposals/20260917T092055Z-repair.md`), and here it sits in
the table about $K$ itself.

**Fix:** keep the title and the column header as $K(k_r)$. That is the
classical name, and it is what a reader holding one of these values types.
Comment (1) already reconciles it. Everywhere else, write one convention:

- Definition: "The entry is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind
  at the singular modulus $k_r$, written $K(k_r)$ classically and $K(m_r)$ in
  the parameter convention."
- Formulas (1) and (3): write $K(m_r)$ on the left side.

### 3. Most rows are $\pi/2$ to many digits, and nothing says why $r$ runs to 100. Worth deciding.

$K(m_r)=\frac{\pi}{2}\theta_3(q)^2=\frac{\pi}{2}(1+4q+\dots)$ with
$q=e^{-\pi\sqrt r}$, so the values collapse onto $\pi/2$ as $r$ grows.
Measured on the stored values:

| agrees with $\pi/2$ to a relative | rows |
|---|---|
| $10^{-6}$ | 77 of 100 |
| $10^{-8}$ | 61 |
| $10^{-10}$ | 40 |

A reader who types $\pi/2$ to six or eight places is holding T25's
$K(0)$, or $\pi/2$ itself. Once T296 is published, up to 77 rows of it will
answer that search too, none of them what the reader holds. From the other
side, it is hard to think of anyone arriving with $K(k_{73})$ in hand. The
skill's test is whether anybody will arrive holding a number, and a count
that matches another table is not a reason.

The `complete-note` gives only that reason: "the same compact CM-point range
as the companion singular-value table". "Compact" means nothing here, and
"the companion singular-value table" points at T295 without naming or linking
it.

**Fix:** the owner has to choose. The smallest honest change keeps the range
and gives a reason in the note, if there is one. For example, if the
Borwein–Borwein tables or Ramanujan's series use these $r$ up to 100 (58 is
the famous case), say so. Otherwise, cut to the $r$ for which singular values
are actually quoted, which is roughly the small $r$ and the idoneal and
Ramanujan cases. Either way, rewrite the note as a clause that names its
neighbour: "it holds $K(k_r)$ for every integer $1\leq r\leq 100$, the range
of HREF{T295}[the singular moduli $k_r$]".

### 4. Comment (1) cites DLMF for the wrong convention, and describes the website. Worth doing.

It renders: "…which is the convention used by arb's `elliptic_k` and by [the
NumberDB table for $K(m)$] [1]." Citation [1] is DLMF 19.2, which writes
$K(k)$ with the modulus. So the citation, placed there, credits DLMF with the
opposite convention. "The NumberDB table for" is a phrase about the site. The
mathematical object is the complete elliptic integral $K(m)$.

**Fix:** "The classical notation, used by DLMF CITE{DLMFEllipticIntegrals},
writes $K(k_r)$ with the elliptic modulus $k_r$. The value stored is $K(m_r)$
at the parameter $m_r=k_r^2$, the convention of arb's `elliptic_k` and of
HREF{Complete_elliptic_integral_of_the_first_kind_K}[the complete elliptic
integral $K(m)$]."

### 5. The Pólya relation misstates the factor, and names a quantity that table does not store. Worth doing.

It renders: "Pólya's random walk constants — Watson's cubic-lattice integral
uses $K(k_6)^2$ times an algebraic factor."

That factor is not algebraic. Checked in Sage to 40 digits:
$u(3)=\frac{12}{\pi^2}(18+12\sqrt2-10\sqrt3-7\sqrt6)\,K(k_6)^2=1.5163860592\ldots$,
which agrees with T19's Gamma form of $u(3)$. The factor carries
$1/\pi^2$. T19 also stores the return probability $p(3)=1-1/u(3)$, not the
integral, so a reader who follows the link finds neither "Watson" nor
$K$ there.

**Fix:** "the return probability $p(3)=1-1/u(3)$, where Watson's integral is
$u(3)=\frac{12}{\pi^2}(18+12\sqrt2-10\sqrt3-7\sqrt6)K(k_6)^2$".

### 6. $\lambda$ and $\theta_3$ are used without being named, and $q$ is defined in the wrong place. Worth doing; small.

- The Definition writes "$m_r=k_r^2=\lambda(\tau)$", and only comment (2)
  names $\lambda$ ("the elliptic modular lambda value").
- The Definition also sets "$q=e^{-\pi\sqrt r}=e^{\pi i\tau}$", but nothing
  in the Definition uses $q$. Formula (1) defines it a second time.
- $\theta_3(0,q)$ is never defined.

**Fix:**

- Definition: "…and $m_r=k_r^2=\lambda(i\sqrt r)$, where $\lambda$ is the
  elliptic modular function CITE{DLMFModularFunctions}." Drop $q$ and $\tau$
  from the Definition.
- Comment (2): its first sentence then repeats the Definition, so start at
  "The lattice…".
- Formula (1): add "where $\theta_3(0,q)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{n^2}$".

### 7. Two self-referring glosses. Worth doing if the fields are being edited anyway.

- **The T295 relation.** "$m_r=k_r^2$ is the parameter used in this table".
  On T296's page, glossing T295, "this table" could mean either one. Write:
  "holds $k_r$ and $m_r=k_r^2$; the values here are $K$ at those $m_r$".
- **Formula (2).** "…where $m_r=k_r^2$ and $K(1-m_r)$ is the complete
  elliptic integral at the complementary parameter" defines $K(1-m_r)$ as
  itself. Once finding 2 is fixed, the formula needs no gloss:
  "$K(1-m_r)=\sqrt r\,K(m_r)$."
