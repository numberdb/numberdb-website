## Findings, ranked

### 1. `$a_13$` renders as $a_1 3$ on 44 rows

What a reader sees: under 23.2.a.a at $p=13$, "The characteristic
polynomial is reducible over $\mathbb{Q}$; $a_13$ does not generate the
full coefficient field $K_f$", where the mathematics prints as $a_1$
followed by a full-size $3$. The same on every commented row with
$p\in\{11,13,17,19\}$: 44 of the 89 reducible-row comments. The rows
with $p\leq7$ are right, since a one-digit subscript needs no braces.

Why it is wrong: the stored text is `$a_13$`, and LaTeX takes one token
after `_`. The reader is told about $a_1$, which is $1$ for every
normalised newform. This lives only in the rendering and the audit does
not see it.

Smallest change: line 276 of `generate.py` writes `"$a_%s$"`; write
`"$a_{%s}$"` and republish the entries. Nothing else changes.

### 2. "The usual Atkin-Lehner coefficient" is not what the row holds

What a reader sees: comment (6) ends "When $p\mid N$, the row records
the usual Atkin-Lehner coefficient." The same sentence, as "Here $p$
divides the level $N$; the polynomial records the usual Atkin-Lehner
coefficient $a_p$ on $K_f$", heads the comment of all 47 rows with
$p\mid N$. Five of those rows are $x^2$: 63.2.a.b at $p=3$, 68.2.a.a at
$p=2$, 81.2.a.a at $p=3$, 88.2.a.b at $p=2$ and 98.2.a.b at $p=7$.

Why it is wrong: what the row holds is the $U_p$-eigenvalue $a_p$, as
formula (1) correctly says. That is not the Atkin-Lehner eigenvalue
$w_p$: for $p\,\|\,N$ in weight 2 one has $a_p=-w_p$, and for $p^2\mid N$
one has $a_p=0$ while $w_p=\pm1$. Checked in the throwaway Sage with
`atkin_lehner_eigenvalue`: 35.2.a.b has $a_5=1$, $w_5=-1$ and $a_7=-1$,
$w_7=1$; 63.2.a.b has $a_3=0$, $w_3=1$; 98.2.a.b has $a_7=0$, $w_7=1$;
the same sign flip on every row tried. A reader holding $(x+1)^2$ from
35.2.a.b at $p=7$ and looking up the LMFDB page, where the Atkin-Lehner
sign at 7 is $+1$, will think the table has the sign wrong. On the five
$x^2$ rows the sentence says a row of zeros records a coefficient that
is $\pm1$. Formula (1) and comment (6) also contradict each other on the
page as it stands.

Smallest change: replace the second sentence of comment (6) by "When
$p\mid N$, $a_p$ is the eigenvalue of $U_p$, which is $-w_p$ for the
Atkin-Lehner sign $w_p$ of $f$ at $p$ if $p$ exactly divides $N$, and
$0$ if $p^2\mid N$." Then the first sentence of the 47 row comments is
formula (1) restated and can go; where $p^2\mid N$ the comment could say
"$a_p=0$ because $p^2\mid N$", which is the one thing a reader of an
$x^2$ row wants to know. The second sentence on those rows, that the
polynomial is reducible, is then a consequence of $a_p\in\{0,\pm1\}$ and
reads oddly as a separate observation, but it is true and can stay.

### 3. The definition uses "coefficient field" without defining it

What a reader sees: "let $K_f$ be its coefficient field", and then
$\chi_{f,p}$ is defined on it. Nothing on the page says
$K_f=\mathbb{Q}(a_n:n\geq1)$, that it is a totally real number field, or
that its degree is the dimension of the newform orbit, which is what
makes the degree of every row what it is.

Why it is wrong: the definition should let two people build the same
table, and "coefficient field" is a term a reader outside the subject
has to guess at. The degree of the rows is the first thing a reader
holding a cubic from 41.2.a.a asks about.

Smallest change: "let $K_f=\mathbb{Q}(a_n:n\geq1)$ be its coefficient
field, a totally real number field whose degree is the dimension of the
newform orbit". Optional, but cheap: a formula
"$\deg\chi_{f,p}=[K_f:\mathbb{Q}]$, the roots are real, and
$|\sigma(a_p)|\leq2\sqrt p$ for $p\nmid N$" gives the reader of a row a
check they can do by hand.

  $\prod_\sigma(x^2-\sigma(a_p)x+p)$, so each row here determines a row

  before it is written.

  site's rendering of two honest fields.

  Plain `N.2.a.o` in backticks reads as a label.

## What reads well

order, the row, the definition, formula (1) fixing which operator, and
comment (5) explaining with a worked row why $(x-3)^2$ and not $x-3$
is stored, which is the question a reader of a reducible row asks
first. Comment (6)'s first sentence, that the eigenvalues are $a_p$ and
not $a_p/\sqrt p$, is right and answers the other question. The
`complete-note` finishes the sentence the site starts and says exactly
what the 400 rows cover. Row order is level ascending, then prime
ascending, so the smallest level is at the top. The `rigour details`
say what was checked against PARI and LMFDB and nothing about the
website.
