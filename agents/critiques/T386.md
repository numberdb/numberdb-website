# T386 — Zeros of the Hurwitz zeta function $\zeta(s,a)$

Read on 2026-09-21 as a reader would meet it. 60 entries, six values of $a$
($1$, $1/2$, $1/3$, $2/3$, $1/4$, $3/4$), type `C`, 30 significant digits,
`rigour: heuristic (agreement-checked)`.

The table is in good shape. It is one table, the value column carries a symbol
that is true of every row, the mathematics typesets, every link resolves, and
the entry comments on $a=1$ and $a=1/2$ do the thing the skill asks for and

## Worth doing

### 1. The `Programs` snippet raises a `TypeError`, and would not give a zero if it ran

The one program the table offers a reader is

    C = ComplexBallField(200)
    s = C(QQ(1)/2) + C.gen() * C(14)
    C(s).zeta(C(QQ(1)/3))

Run under Sage 10.9 (`agents/sage.sh`, `/tmp/prog386.py`):

    TypeError: gen() takes exactly 2 positional arguments (1 given)

`ComplexBallField.gen` has no default index. A reader who copies the five
lines gets a traceback on the second one. Nothing on the site runs a
`Programs` snippet — the files page says of the attachments "These are
recorded, not executed" — so nothing but a person catches this.

The second fault is worse than the first, because fixing the first exposes it.
With `C.gen(0)` the snippet runs and prints

    [-1.9355468431725488538649197720285216053308255114987637691143 +/- 3.03e-59]
      + [1.8984526472317339276251300891200558879871024321060818183345 +/- 4.19e-59]*I

which is a *value* of $\zeta(s,a)$, not a zero, and not an entry of this
table. The point chosen, $1/2+14\mathrm{i}$, is the first Riemann zero worn as
a disguise: at $a=1/3$ it is nothing in particular. This is the program the
companion table of *values* wants. A reader of a table of zeros who wants one
more zero is given a recipe for something else.

Smallest fix: a snippet that finds a zero. The generator already does it with
mpmath, and three lines of `mp.findroot` starting from a stored ordinate is
the honest incantation. Failing that, at the very least `C.gen(0)`, and
evaluate at a stored $\rho$ rather than at an arbitrary point, so the snippet
shows the entry vanishing rather than showing an unrelated value. Read the
stored digits into the ball field as rationals, not as Python floats, or the
200 bits are spent on 53.

(I ran that form: `rho.zeta(C(a))` returns balls of size $3\cdot10^{-31}$ and
smaller at four stored entries across four values of $a$, which is what 30
stored digits should give. The numbers are fine; it is the snippet that is
not.)

### 2. The Hurwitz zeta function is never defined

The Definition names $\zeta(s,a)$, cites Wikipedia and moves on. No comment
says what the function is. The convention that matters is exactly the one left
out: whether the series starts at $n=0$ or $n=1$ — the two differ by $a^{-s}$
and have different zeros, and the table's whole content is those zeros. A
reader can deduce $n=0$ from `formula-shift`, $\zeta(s,a+1)=\zeta(s,a)-a^{-s}$,
by working backwards; the skill's test is that two people would build the same
table from the definition, and here the second person has to reverse-engineer
a formula to do it.

The corpus already has the sentence. `Values of the Hurwitz zeta function at
pairs of rational numbers` carries

> The Hurwitz zeta function $\zeta(s,x)$ is the meromorphic continuation (with
> respect to $s$) of the series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty (n+x)^{-s}$, $\Re(s)>1$,
> $\Re(x)>0$.

Smallest fix: that comment, with $x$ written as $a$. It is also what the
`CITE{Spira}` review states, so it agrees with the reference.

### 3. $t_n$ is used twelve times and defined nowhere

Every $a=1$ row and six of the ten $a=1/2$ rows say "its imaginary part is
$t_1$", "$t_2$", and so on. $t$ appears in no definition, comment, formula or
parameter of this table. The link carries the meaning, which means the meaning
is on another page.

Worse, the page it is on prints those same numbers under the header
$\rho_n$ — `Zeros of the Riemann zeta function` stores ordinates and heads its
value column `$\rho_n$`. So a reader told here that $\rho_1(1) = 1/2 +
14.13\ldots\mathrm{i}$ and that "the imaginary part is $t_1$" clicks through
and is shown $\rho_1 = 14.13\ldots$. The same symbol, two different objects,
one click apart.

Smallest fix: one clause in `comment-special-a`, "write $t_n$ for the ordinate
of the $n$th zero of $\zeta(s)$". The symbol collision is mostly the other
table's to answer for and I would not hold this one up over it, but defining
$t_n$ here costs six words.

### 4. The reference has no title

`References` renders as

> [1] Robert Spira, Math. Comp. 30 (1976), no. 136, 863-866. (doi)

The paper is *Zeros of Hurwitz zeta-functions* (Zbl 0341.10034). A reader
deciding whether to follow the link has the author, the journal and the page
range, and no way to tell whether the paper is about this table's subject —
which, in this case, it exactly is. Smallest fix: put the title in the `bib`
string. `zbl: 0341.10034` beside the `doi` costs another line and renders as a
second link; the AMS page answers 403 to a plain fetch, zbMATH does not.

### 5. `comment-strip` throws away the number the theorem gives

> Spira proved that, for fixed $a$, the complex zeros of $\zeta(s,a)$ lie in a
> vertical strip [1]. The table therefore uses a height bound, not a real-part
> bound, for the computed range.

What Spira proves is that all complex zeros lie in $-1<\Re s<1+a$. That is a
fact a reader holding one of these numbers can use — a real part of $0.57$ or
$-0.23$ is inside it, a real part of $3$ is not this function's zero — and
"a vertical strip" is the one version of it that helps nobody. Smallest fix:
name the strip.

The second sentence is about this run rather than about the mathematics, and
`complete-note` already says what range is here. It can go.

### 6. `Parameters: n: comments:` is dead text

    n:
      comments: For fixed $a$, non-real zeros with positive imaginary part are
        ordered by increasing imaginary part, with ties broken by increasing
        real part.

The renderer's parameter loop reads `display`, `title`, `type`, `constraints`,
`values` and `show-in-parameter-list`, and drops everything else in silence. I
checked the rendered HTML of all ten pieces: "ties broken" appears once
outside the echoed source, in the Definition, and never from this field.

No harm is done here — the sentence is a near-copy of the Definition's second
sentence, which *does* render — so the fix is to delete the key rather than to
move anything. It is worth doing anyway because the next person to edit this
table will read that field and believe it is on the page.

This is the second table in this family to do it: the same finding was
written up against T324 on 2026-09-18
(`agents/lessons/proposals/20260918T074121Z-critique.md`), and again against
T142/T143 in `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`. The lesson is proposed and not yet
in the skill, which is why it keeps arriving.

### 7. The caveat in `rigour details` is behind the fold

The rendered field reads, with the page closed:

> How they were obtained: The generator first finds roots with mpmath at 50
> and 65 decimal working digits and stores only digits that agree between the
> two runs. The listed values were then checked by evaluating $\zeta(s,a)$ in
> Sage's arb-backed complex ball field, and after filling the stored centres
> were checked against the special cases $a=1$ and $a=1/2$.   *more*

and inside the `more`:

> The zero search is not a certified enclosure method, so the table does not
> claim proven zeros.

Two sentences of what was checked are visible and the one sentence saying what
is *not* guaranteed is hidden. `prose.py` folds above 400 rendered characters.
Smallest fix: put the caveat first. This too is the same finding T324 got
three days ago, in the same file.

## Noted only because I noticed

* **`$2\pi\mathrm{i}1/\log 2$`** on the first $a=1/2$ row reads as "2πi1/log 2"
  — the $k=1$ case of a comment written with `k` substituted. Rows 3, 6 and 9
  are fine. One special case in the generator, or drop the numeral for $k=1$.

* **The fifth `Similar tables` relation is about method, not mathematics.**
  "uses the same agreement-checked convention for another zeta-zero family"
  tells a reader something about how both tables were computed and nothing
  about how the objects relate. The other four relations are genuinely
  mathematical — the Dirichlet one ("zeros of finite linear combinations of
  rational-parameter Hurwitz zeta functions") is the best sentence in the
  table.

* **`complete-note` says which range and not why.** "it holds the non-real
  zeros with $0<\operatorname{Im}\rho\leq40$ for rational $a\in(0,1]$ in
  lowest terms with denominator at most $4$" is a well-formed sentence and
  finishes "complete: no (...)" properly. It does not tell a reader holding a
  zero of $\zeta(s,1/5)$ why their $a$ is absent. One clause would.

* **"a separate one-real-variable question studied in the literature"** in
  `comment-real-zeros` is vague, and the paper the table already cites
  describes those real zeros concretely (one in each interval
  $(-2n-4a-1,\,-2n-4a+1)$). Either cite `CITE{Spira}` there or say what they
  are.

* **The `Similar tables` caption renames a table.** "Zeros of Dirichlet
  $L$-functions" is captioning a table actually titled "Zeros of Dirichlet
  L-series". The slug resolves, so nothing breaks; a reader following it lands
  on a different name from the one they clicked.

## A judgement I looked at and would leave alone

**Exact real parts, and whether that outruns the rigour level.** The $a=1$ rows
store `1/2 + i * 14.13...` and three $a=1/2$ rows store `0 + i * 9.06...`. Both
are exact claims — that the zero is *on* the critical line, that it is *on* the
imaginary axis — from a table whose declared rigour is the weaker "heuristic
(agreement-checked)". For $a=1/2$ this is clean and the entry comment gives the
argument: $2^s=1$ puts those zeros at $2\pi\mathrm{i}k/\log 2$ exactly. For
$a=1$ the argument is a theorem plus somebody's rigorous computation, and the
table does not make it. It is not wrong — every zero with $0<t\leq40$ is known
to lie on $\Re s = 1/2$ — but the grounds are not on the page, and the skill's
rule is that exactness is decided from the definition and never from the
digits. A clause in `comment-special-a`, citing what the Riemann zeros table
already cites, would close it. I put it here rather than above because the
values are right and the omission is a sentence.

**Whether `repeats:` is wanted.** It is not, and the absence is correct. At
$a=1$ this table holds $\rho_n$ and the Riemann table holds $\Im\rho_n$: a
reader typing either number matches exactly one of them, so there is nothing
for search to deduplicate. The ordinates were also recomputed rather than
transcribed (`rigour details` says they were checked against the special case
afterwards), and they agree with the published ones to the last stored place —
T386's `14.1347251417346937904572519836` is the correct 30-digit rounding of
the Riemann table's `14.1347251417346937904572519835625 +/- 2.5e-31`.
