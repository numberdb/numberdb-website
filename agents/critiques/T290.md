**Audit:** one finding: "Definition is 809 characters (median here is 195)".
I agree that the Definition needs work (finding 3), but not with the fix the
finding suggests. Most of those characters are the list of normal forms, and
that list is definitional: without it two people would not build the same
table. What is wrong is the prose around the list. The audit cannot see
findings 1, 2, 4 and 5.

**What I checked hardest, and found sound:**

* **Rendering.** Every backslash survives: `\frac`, `\left(`,
  `\operatorname{lct}`, `\tilde b_f`, `\mathbb{Q}`. No field has a `<` in its
  mathematics. `CITE{AGV}`, `CITE{WikiBFunction}` and `CITE{SingularBfct}`
  resolve. The value column is headed $b_f(s)$. The $n$ column reads `1`, `2`,
  `3`. The `HREF` to `Poincaré_polynomials_of_the_finite_Coxeter_groups`
  resolves (200). The Singular program keeps its line breaks.
* **It is one table.** `singularity` has 41 name-like values, but they name
  *what the polynomial is of*, one object per label. That is the good kind of
  name parameter, and the header is a real symbol, not `value`. `n` is an
  argument (stabilisation by squares).
* **The mathematical claims in the prose.** For $E_6$ at $n=3$, the reduced
  roots $13/12,4/3,17/12,19/12,5/3,23/12$ give $e^{2\pi i m/12}$ with
  $m=1,4,5,7,8,11$, which are the exponents of $E_6$. So the Similar-tables
  relation holds there. The weighted-homogeneous formula agrees with $D_4$
  and $P_8$ by hand. Singular's `bfct` agrees with the stored $E_{12}$,
  $Z_{11}$ and $J_{10}$ rows at modulus zero.

On the whole the table is sound and the entries are readable, because each one
carries its factored form. Findings 1 to 6 are worth doing, and each is a
one-field edit. Findings 7 to 10 are noted only.

## Worth doing

### 1. The comment on the full b-function puts the root at $+1$

Rendered, Comments (1):

> … when $1$ is a root of $\tilde b_f(s)$, the full polynomial has $(s+1)^2$
> as a factor.

Roots of a b-function are negative. $(s+1)^2$ appears when $-1$ is a root of
$\tilde b_f$, as on the $A_1$, $n=2$ row, $b_f=(s+1)^2$. A reader who checks
the sentence against any row finds that it is wrong.

Fix: `when $-1$ is a root of $\tilde b_f(s)$`.

### 2. The title says "unimodal singularities", but the hyperbolic ones are absent and nothing says so

Arnold's unimodal singularities are the three parabolic families, the
hyperbolic series $T_{p,q,r}$ and the fourteen exceptional families. The table
holds the parabolic and exceptional families only. The parameter constraint
does say "parabolic or exceptional". The title, the first sentence of the
Definition ("a simple or unimodal hypersurface singularity") and the
completeness note do not. A reader who arrives from $T_{3,3,4}$, the most
common hyperbolic case, gets no answer to "is it here?". The reason is a fact
about the mathematics, and it is worth saying: $T_{p,q,r}$ has no
quasi-homogeneous member, so it has no modulus-zero normal form.

Smallest fix: add to `complete-note`
"…with $n\leq3$; the hyperbolic unimodal singularities $T_{p,q,r}$, which have
no quasi-homogeneous normal form, are not included". In the Definition, say
"simple, parabolic or exceptional unimodal". The title can stay.

### 3. The Definition does not say cleanly what $n$ does, and its last sentence reads wrongly

Rendered:

> For each listed modulus-zero normal form $f$ of a simple or unimodal
> hypersurface singularity in Arnold's list [1], this table stores the local
> Bernstein-Sato polynomial $b_f(s)\in\mathbb{Q}[s]$ [2] at the origin, with
> $n$ variables. The normal forms are … For $n$ above the corank of the
> displayed form, a square in the next free variable is added until $n=3$.

* "until $n=3$" reads as: always pad to three variables. What is meant is:
  add squares until there are $n$ variables.
* "with $n$ variables" dangles. A reader cannot tell whether it modifies the
  polynomial, the origin or the table.
* "listed" points forward to a list the reader has not reached yet.
  "Modulus-zero" means nothing for $A_k$, $D_k$ and $E_{6,7,8}$, which have no
  modulus.
* "this table stores" is a remark about the website. The Definition should
  say what $b_f$ is.

Suggested text, which keeps the list:

> $b_f(s)\in\mathbb{Q}[s]$ is the local Bernstein-Sato polynomial CITE{WikiBFunction}
> at the origin of $f+x_{c+1}^2+\cdots+x_n^2$, where $f$ is the normal form in
> $c$ variables $x,y,z$ of a simple, parabolic or exceptional unimodal
> singularity in Arnold's classification CITE{AGV}, with the modulus set to
> $0$: $A_k:x^{k+1}$; $D_k:x^2y+y^{k-1}$; … ; $U_{12}:x^3+y^3+z^4$.

That also answers the audit. Only the list stays long, and the list is the
definition.

### 4. $\mu$ and $\operatorname{lct}$ are used on every row and defined nowhere, and the entry comments are fragments

Every entry comment reads like

> Normal form $f=x^3+y^4$; $\mu=6$; $\operatorname{lct}(f)=\frac{7}{12}$.
> Factored form: $b_f(s)=\left(s+\frac{7}{12}\right)\cdots$.

Nothing in the table says that $\mu$ is the Milnor number or what
$\operatorname{lct}$ is ("Define notation where you use it"). The comment is
also three noun fragments joined by semicolons. The factored form is the most
useful thing on the page, because the expanded polynomials run to
denominators like $23298085122481$, and it should stay.

Smallest fix: add one table comment:
"$\mu$ is the Milnor number $\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathbb{C}[x]/(\partial f)$, and
$\operatorname{lct}(f)$ is the log canonical threshold, which is minus the
largest root of $b_f$." Then make the entry template one sentence:
"$f=x^3+y^4$, with $\mu=6$ and $\operatorname{lct}(f)=7/12$; $b_f(s)=\ldots$".

### 5. The comment on the modulus is muddled, and a concrete fact is available

Rendered, Comments (3):

> The b-function is an analytic invariant of the chosen polynomial, not only a
> topological invariant of the singularity label.

The b-function is not a topological invariant "of the label" at all.
"Chosen polynomial" and "label" are the website's words, not the
mathematics'. The reader needs to know *which* rows would change with the
modulus. I checked with Singular's `bfct`:

* $J_{10}$: $x^3+y^6$ and $x^3+y^6+x^2y^2$ have the same roots. The parabolic
  families stay quasi-homogeneous.
* $E_{12}$: $x^3+y^7$ has the root $-32/21$. $x^3+y^7+xy^5$ has $-11/21$
  instead, and every other root is the same.
* $Z_{11}$: $x^3y+y^5$ has the root $-23/15$. $x^3y+y^5+xy^4$ has $-8/15$
  instead.

Suggested text: "For the exceptional unimodal families the b-function depends
on the modulus: $x^3+y^7$ has the root $-32/21$ where $x^3+y^7+xy^5$ has
$-11/21$. The simple and parabolic singularities are quasi-homogeneous at
every value of the modulus, so the rows for them hold for every member."
Include the second sentence only if the author confirms it for $P_8$ and
$X_9$ as well; I checked $J_{10}$ only.

### 6. The $n$ parameter describes the run

> the number of variables after adding squares to the displayed normal form,
> from the corank of the singularity to $3$

"to $3$" is this run's range. It is already stated in `complete-note`
("with $n\leq3$"). The family goes on to every $n$ at least the corank.

Fix: "the number of variables, at least the corank of the singularity".

## Noted only

### 7. The Similar-tables relation leaves the reader one step short

The relation is true, but T257 holds Poincaré polynomials, not exponents or
eigenvalues. A reader who follows the link has to know that the degrees $d_i$
of the Coxeter group give the exponents $d_i-1$. Adding "…of the corresponding
finite Coxeter group, whose degrees $d_i$ are read off its Poincaré polynomial
as $m_i+1$" closes the gap. The caption says "Poincare"; the table's title is
"Poincaré". A bare `HREF{…}` or the accented caption would match.

### 8. `rigour details` describes a mode, not what was done

> In self-check mode it compares every row with Singular's `bfct` after the
> control $x^2+y^3$ …

A reader does not know what "self-check mode" is, and cannot tell whether that
comparison was run on these rows or agreed. If it was run, say that: "Every
row was also computed with Singular's `bfct` and agreed; the control
$x^2+y^3$, whose roots are $-5/6,-1,-7/6$, was run first."

### 9. The suspension formula is a sentence where an identity would do

"Replacing $f$ by $f+u^2$ adds the weight $1/2$ to every value of $\alpha$ in
the reduced b-function" could be written
$\tilde b_{f+u^2}(s)=\tilde b_f\!\left(s+\tfrac12\right)$, which says the same
thing and is what `Formulas` is for.

### 10. One tag

`Tags` is just `polynomial`. The commit history shows that `singularity theory`
was removed because it would be a one-table tag, which is the skill's rule. No
existing tag fits better. I mention this only so that nobody reads the
single tag as an oversight.
