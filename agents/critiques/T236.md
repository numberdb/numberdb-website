# T236, Volumes of the Birkhoff polytopes

Read 2026-09-13. This is a private draft owned by zeta3. It has 30 entries of
type `Q` and rigour `exact`: three normalisations of the volume of $B_n$ for
each $1\leq n\leq10$. No entry carries a comment. The entries block is 3.8 KB.

**Summary.** Every row agrees with its formulas, and nearly all of the page
renders cleanly. Finding 1 is a rendering fault a reader will see: the three
$n=10$ rows are labelled $B_1$ followed by a 0. It is a two-character fix.
Findings 2 and 3 are the substance. The page never defines the three
normalisations precisely enough to rebuild them, never explains the row
notation, and gives the conversion between rows only in terms of an Ehrhart
polynomial the page does not hold. Both are fixed by rewriting one comment and
three formulas. Findings 4 to 7 are worth doing and small. Section 8 is noted
only.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The $n=10$ rows are labelled "$B_1$ 0" (worth doing; first)

**What a reader sees.** The labels are
`$\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{rel}}(B_10)$`, `$\operatorname{Vol}(B_10)$` and
`$\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{Euc}}(B_10)$`. In TeX a subscript without braces
takes one token. MathJax parses the first label as
`<msub><mi>B</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><mn>0</mn>`: a subscript 1 followed by a
full-size 0. A reader arrives holding the most sought-after value on the page,
the volume of $B_{10}$, which took Beck and Pixton fourteen months of
computing. They see it labelled as a volume of $B_1$. No typesetting error is
raised, so a check that the page "renders" passes it.

**Where it comes from.** `generators/birkhoff-polytope-volumes/generate.py`
lines 160, 165 and 170 write `B_%d`.

**Smallest fix.** Use `B_{%d}` in those three lines and in the three stored
labels.

### 2. The three normalisations are named, not defined, and the row notation is explained nowhere (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

- The Definition: "stores its volume in three normalisations: the relative
  lattice volume, the normalised lattice volume, and the Euclidean volume".
- Comment (6): "The relative volume is measured with respect to the lattice
  $\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}\cap\operatorname{aff}(B_n)$, translated to the
  zero-margin subspace."
- Comment (7): "…It differs from the relative volume by the covolume of the
  zero-margin lattice."

This is what a reader would have to guess:

- $\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}\cap\operatorname{aff}(B_n)$ is a coset of a lattice,
  not a lattice.
- "translated to the zero-margin subspace" has no clear subject.
- "Zero-margin" is defined nowhere. Formula (5), which a reader meets first
  because Formulas are drawn before Comments, says only that it "is the
  integral cycle lattice of $K_{n,n}$". That needs a further identification
  of matrices with edge weights.
- "Differs by" reads as a difference. It is a factor.
- The dimension $d=(n-1)^2$ appears only inside formulas.

The rows compound this. Each row shows only its label, and the three labels
are $\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{rel}}$, $\operatorname{Vol}$ and
$\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{Euc}}$. That a capital V means the normalised
volume is written nowhere on the page. Somebody holding 352 sees
"$\operatorname{Vol}(B_4)$: 352" and cannot tell which of the three it is
without counting rows.

**Smallest fix.** Replace Comments (6) and (7) with one comment. It defines
the lattice, the dimension, all three volumes and the three symbols:

> Let $d=(n-1)^2$ be the dimension of $B_n$, and let $\Lambda_n$ be the
> lattice of integer $n\times n$ matrices whose rows and columns all sum to
> $0$. The affine hull of $B_n$ is a translate of
> $\Lambda_n\otimes\mathbb{R}$. The relative volume
> $\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{rel}}(B_n)$ is the $d$-dimensional volume in
> which a fundamental domain of $\Lambda_n$ has volume $1$. The normalised
> volume is $\operatorname{Vol}(B_n)=d!\,\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{rel}}(B_n)$.
> The Euclidean volume $\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{Euc}}(B_n)$ is the
> $d$-dimensional volume for the Frobenius inner product
> $\langle X,Y\rangle=\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}Y_{ij}$.

The Definition can stay as it is. If the author prefers, "of dimension
$(n-1)^2$" could be added after "doubly stochastic matrices".

### 3. The formulas convert between rows only through $H_n(t)$, which the page does not hold (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Formulas (2) and (4) give the normalised and the
Euclidean volume as multiples of $[t^d]H_n(t)$. Formula (1) says that is the
relative volume. So a reader holding a Euclidean volume must chain two
formulas through the leading coefficient of a polynomial that is in another
table. The direct relations are what somebody converting their number needs,
and they appear nowhere: normalised $=d!\cdot$relative, and Euclidean
$=n^{n-1}\cdot$relative. Formula (5) also uses $\tau(K_{n,n})$ without saying
what $\tau$ is. Formula (2) repeats Comment (6).

**Smallest fix.** With finding 2 applied, Formulas become:

- `formula-leading-coefficient`: "$\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{rel}}(B_n)=[t^d]H_n(t)$,
  the leading coefficient of the HREF{T232}[Ehrhart polynomial] $H_n(t)$ of
  $B_n$."
- `formula-h-star` stays as it is.
- `formula-euclidean`:
  "$\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{Euc}}(B_n)=n^{n-1}\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{rel}}(B_n)$."
- `formula-covolume`: "The covolume of $\Lambda_n$ in the Frobenius inner
  product is $n^{n-1}$. Identified with the integral cycle lattice of
  $K_{n,n}$, its squared covolume is the number of spanning trees of
  $K_{n,n}$, which is $n^{2n-2}$ by the matrix-tree theorem
  CITE{WikiMatrixTree}."
- `formula-normalised` is then the definition in the comment. Drop it, or
  keep it as "$\operatorname{Vol}(B_n)=d!\,[t^d]H_n(t)$".

The labels `formula-euclidean` and `formula-covolume` are cited from
`rigour details`, so keep those two keys.

### 4. Two Similar-tables relations name no relation (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** "Volume of the Platonic solids — stores Euclidean
volumes of another family of polytopes". "Volumes of the hyperbolic Coxeter
simplices — stores volumes of Coxeter simplices". Neither says how that table
bears on this one. Having volumes in common is a shared subject, and the
`volume` tag already says that. The T232 relation is a real relation and
reads well.

**Smallest fix.** Delete both entries.

### 5. The Beck–Pixton paper is listed and cited nowhere (worth doing; small)

**What a reader sees.** Reference [1], Beck and Pixton's paper, sits in
References with no `CITE` on the page. `rigour details` cites their website's
polynomial list and not the paper. The page never says where the values come
from, and the answer is Beck and Pixton: $B_9$ in the paper, $B_{10}$ in the
computation announced on their site.

**Smallest fix.** Cite the paper in `complete-note`, which also says why the
range stops: "it holds all three normalisations for every matrix size $n$
with $1\leq n\leq10$; Beck and Pixton computed $B_9$ CITE{BeckPixton} and
later $B_{10}$ CITE{BeckPixtonVolumes}". This needs a new Link, below. I did
not find a statement that no $n\geq11$ is known, so I would not claim that.

### 6. The $n=9$ sentence in `rigour details` records a discrepancy without its explanation (worth doing; small)

**What a reader sees.** "At $n=9$, the A078525 b-file denominator gives the
relative volume denominator instead; the stored Euclidean value is the one
given by (4)." A reader comparing with OEIS finds the two disagree. The page
says so but leaves them to decide which is wrong. The explanation is
published: Beck and Pixton's first $n=9$ Euclidean volume omitted the factor
$9^8$, they corrected it, and A078525 still carries the old denominator. Their
volumes page lists all ten Euclidean values, and every one matches this table
verbatim. That is a better independent check than the OEIS pair, and the page
does not link it.

**Smallest fix.** Add a Link `BeckPixtonVolumes`, "Beck and Pixton: volumes of
the Birkhoff polytopes", <https://matthbeck.github.io/birkhoff/volumes.html>.
Replace the two A078524/A078525 sentences with: "All ten Euclidean volumes
agree with Beck and Pixton's list CITE{BeckPixtonVolumes}. OEIS A078525
CITE{OEISEuclideanDenominators} agrees except at $n=9$. There it keeps the
denominator Beck and Pixton first published, which lacked the factor $9^8$
they later corrected."

### 7. `Programs` computes no volume (worth doing; small)

**What a reader sees.** `birkhoff_volumes(n, normalised_volume)` takes the
normalised volume as an argument and rescales it. A reader who wants to check
a row, or compute one, gets nothing they did not already have. It also imports
`numberdb.sage` and never uses it.

**Smallest fix.** Sage computes the Euclidean volume directly with no optional
packages. I ran this for $n=2,3,4$ and it printed the stored values. At $n=5$
it was killed, presumably for memory on this small container, so a sentence
should say it is for small $n$:

    B = polytopes.Birkhoff_polytope(4)
    B.volume(measure='induced', engine='internal')    # 176/2835, Euclidean

With `latte_int` or `pynormaliz` installed,
`B.volume(measure='induced_lattice')` gives the relative volume. I could not
run that here. The other option is to drop `Programs`: nobody can compute a
new row.

### 8. Noted only

- **The $n=1$ and $n=2$ rows are 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2.** The skill's warning about
  handfuls of small integers applies, and T232 leaves out $n=1,2$ for the same
  reason. Only two distinct values are involved, so the harm to search is
  small, and a volume table that starts at $B_1$ is natural. This is the
  author's call. If they are dropped, `complete-note` should say why, as
  T232's does.
- **`Keywords`** lacks "assignment polytope" and "perfect matching polytope",
  which are Wikipedia's other names. "magic squares" is loose, because magic
  squares have diagonal conditions and nothing here involves them. T232 does
  not carry it.
- **`HREF{T232}` links a draft.** T232 must be published no later than T236.
- **Formula (1)'s "Ehrhart polynomial"** is the first mention of what T232
  holds. The link in finding 3 covers it. The audit cannot see this, because
  the phrase differs from T232's title.
- **T232's Comment** says the Beck–Pixton paper "gives the volume of
  $B_{10}$". The abstract says $B_9$, and the site presents $B_{10}$ as a
  later announcement. That is a question for T232, not this table. I did not
  read the paper's body.

## What reads well

- **The parameters describe the family.** $n$ is "an integer with $n\geq1$",
  and the computed range is in `complete-note`, written as a clause that
  finishes "complete: no (…)". `normalisation` is a specific key.
- **The values are right and their provenance is honest.** The formulas hold
  on every row. The Euclidean column matches Beck and Pixton's own list
  verbatim, and Sage reproduces it for $n\leq4$. `rigour details` reports
  the one discrepancy it met instead of hiding it.
- **Apart from finding 1 it renders.** There are no math errors, and every
  `CITE`, formula label and link resolves. Nothing on the page apologises or
  points backwards.
