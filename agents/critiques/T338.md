## 1. A `Similar tables` relation says this table holds something it does not

**What a reader sees.** In `Similar tables`:

> Complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials —  give the $h_n$ whose signed
> Chern-root expressions are stored here

**Why it is wrong.** Read plainly, that sentence claims T338 stores *signed
Chern-root expressions* of $h_n$. T338 stores expressions in the Chern
**classes**: `-c1`, `c1^2 - c2`, `-c1^3 + 2*c1*c2 - c3`. The Chern-root
expressions are precisely what the *linked* table holds — its rows are
`x1 + x2`, `x1^2 + x1*x2 + x2^2`, and so on in $x_1,\dots,x_n$. So the
relation swaps the two tables' contents, and it does so in the one sentence
whose whole job is to tell a reader which is which. A reader who clicks
through to check lands on a page full of root expressions and has to work out
that the gloss was describing that page, not this one.

The sibling shows the intended shape. `Chern_character_polynomials` writes, for
the same kind of relation, "give the power sums $p_n$ whose **Chern-class
expressions** determine these polynomials" — same sentence frame, and correct
about which table holds what.

**Smallest fix.** One word, or a clause if the sign is worth keeping:

    give the $h_n$ in the Chern roots; the entries here are $(-1)^n h_n$
    rewritten in the Chern classes

**Worth doing.** Yes. It is the only statement on the page that is false about
the table's own contents.

## 2. `$h$` appears undefined in `rigour details`, where `$h_n$` already means something else

**What a reader sees.** Under "How they were obtained", behind the *more* fold:

> ... by substituting the Chern classes of $T\mathbb P^m$ for
> $1\leq n\leq m\leq 6$, which gives $s_n(T\mathbb P^m)=(-1)^n\binom{m+n}{n}h^n$.

**Why it is wrong.** $h$ is the hyperplane class, and nothing on the page says
so. The skill's rule is "Define notation where you use it. ... If a symbol
appears in a formula or a comment, its definition belongs in the same table."
Here it is worse than a bare undefined symbol, because the page has *already*
defined $h$-with-a-subscript as something else: Formula (2) says "$h_n$ is the
complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial". A reader meeting $h^n$ a few
paragraphs later reads it as the $n$-th power of that symmetric polynomial,
which is not a thing that could sit in $H^*(\mathbb P^m)$ and makes the
identity nonsense.

The bare $h$ is inherited from the sibling —
`Chern_character_polynomials` writes $\mathrm{ch}_n(T\mathbb{P}^m)=(m+1)h^n/n!$
with $h$ equally undefined — but there it is only terse, because that table
never defines an $h_n$. T338 is the one table in the family where the two
collide, so the habit that is merely terse elsewhere becomes a genuine
misreading here.

(The identity itself is right: $c(T\mathbb P^m)=(1+h)^{m+1}$ gives
$s(T\mathbb P^m)=(1+h)^{-(m+1)}=\sum_n(-1)^n\binom{m+n}{n}h^n$, and the
$n\leq m$ constraint is what keeps $h^n$ from vanishing.)

**Smallest fix.** Four words: "... $=(-1)^n\binom{m+n}{n}h^n$, where $h$ is the
hyperplane class."

**Worth doing.** Yes.

## 3. $s_0=1$ is said three times, and comment (6) is about the page rather than the mathematics

**What a reader sees.** Formula (1) ends "with $s_0=1$". Formula (3), on its
own line, is:

> (3) $s_0=1$.

and comment (6) is:

> (6) The constant component is $s_0=1$ and is stated in Formula (3), rather
> than stored as a row.

**Why it is wrong.** Two things at once. The triviality gets three of the
page's nine numbered items, having already been stated as part of the
recurrence that needs it. And comment (6) spends its main clause on where the
fact is *printed on this page* — "is stated in Formula (3)" — which is a
remark about the document, not a fact about Segre classes; the skill's rule is
"A comment states a fact about the mathematics." The thing a reader actually
wants from that comment, that the table begins at $n=1$, is left implicit.

Both siblings do this in one item and get it right. Todd: "The constant
component $\mathrm{Td}_0=1$ is not stored as a row; the table begins at
$n=1$." Chern character: "The degree-zero component is the rank of the vector
bundle, ... so the table begins at $n=1$."

**Smallest fix.** Delete Formula (3); rewrite comment (6) as the family does:

    The constant component is $s_0=1$ and is not stored as a row; the table
    begins at $n=1$.

Formulas then renumber to (1)–(3) and comments to (4)–(8). Nothing else on the
page cites `formula-zero`, so the `CITE` in comment (6) is the only reference
to remove.

**Worth doing.** Yes, and it is a deletion, which is cheap.

## 4. Formula (4) reprints rows 1 to 3

**What a reader sees.**

> (4) $s_1=-c_1$, $s_2=c_1^2-c_2$, and $s_3=-c_1^3+2c_1c_2-c_3$.

sitting about four inches below the rows `1: -c1`, `2: c1^2 - c2`,
`3: -c1^3 + 2*c1*c2 - c3`.

**Why it is wrong.** `Formulas` holds "identities, closed forms, generating
functions, recurrences"; three particular stored values are none of those.
They are the data, restated in a second notation that nothing keeps in step
with the first — if a row were ever corrected, this formula would not be, and
the page would contradict itself in the space of one screen. Neither Todd nor
Chern character carries an analogue, and Formula (1)'s recurrence already
generates these three in one line.

**The counter-argument, which is real.** The rows render in the corpus's ASCII
polynomial form (`c1^2 - c2`) and Formula (4) is the only place a reader sees
the typeset form (`$c_1^2-c_2$`). If that is the purpose, though, the formula
does not say so, three is an arbitrary number of rows to do it for, and comment
(8) plus the title already establish that `c1` means $c_1$.

**Smallest fix.** Delete Formula (4).

**Worth doing.** Yes, but it is the weakest of the four I would do — take it
after 1 to 3.

## 5. Two `Similar tables` rows carry the same sentence

**What a reader sees.**

> Todd polynomials —  store another universal characteristic-class polynomial
> in the same Chern-class variables
>
> Chern character polynomials —  store another universal characteristic-class
> polynomial in the same Chern-class variables

**Why it is wrong.** The skill's whole case for `Similar tables` is that the
relation sentence "is the valuable part, and one table cannot hold it". Two
identical sentences hold half as much as two different ones: a reader gets
nothing telling Todd from the Chern character, and no reason to click either
rather than both. The phrase is house style — `Chern_character_polynomials`
uses it verbatim for Todd — but using it twice in one section is T338's own.

**Smallest fix.** Keep it on one and distinguish the other, e.g. the Chern
character row as "store the components of $\operatorname{ch}(E)=\sum_i
e^{x_i}$ in the same Chern-class variables", which is what that table's own
Formula (1) says.

**Worth doing.** Marginal, but it is one sentence.

## 6. The `Programs` snippet prints a row the table already has

**What a reader sees.** The snippet ends `print(segre_component(6))`, and
prints row 6.

**Why it is worth a thought.** The skill calls `Programs` "the standard
incantation for a reader who wants one more value", and the one more value
here is $s_7$ — the component the table demonstrably cannot store, named in
`complete-note`, and the first thing a reader who has read the page will want.
`Todd_polynomials` does exactly this: its table stops at $n=7$ and its snippet
prints `todd_component(8)`. `Chern_character_polynomials` does not, so there is
no settled house rule, and printing a stored row does have the virtue that a
reader can check the snippet against the page — which is how I checked it.

**Smallest fix.** `print(segre_component(7))`.

**Worth doing.** Your call; I lean yes because Todd sets the precedent, but
nothing is wrong as it stands.

## Noted only because I noticed

* **Comment (9)'s semicolon hides an implication.** It reads "The component
  $s_n$ uses exactly the variables $c_1,\dots,c_n$; the coefficient of $c_n$ is
  $-1$." Those are not two facts side by side — the second is the *reason* for
  the first, and the reason `complete-note` can assert that $s_7$ needs a
  seventh variable. `Chern_character_polynomials` writes the same thought with
  a colon and a clause: "uses exactly the variables $c_1,\dots,c_n$: the
  coefficient of $c_n$ is $(-1)^{n-1}/(n-1)!$, which is never zero." Changing
  the semicolon to a colon and adding "which is never zero" would make the
  range note land on something visible.

* **The most distinctive property of Segre classes is not on the page.**
  Comment (7) tells a reader to set $c_j=0$ for $j>r$ on a rank-$r$ bundle and
  stops there, which is exactly where the surprise is: unlike $c_n$, the
  component $s_n$ does *not* vanish for $n>r$ — the Segre series of a
  finite-rank bundle is infinite, which is why this table can run to any $n$ at
  all and why Segre classes are the ones that extend to cones. A reader
  arriving from Chern classes will expect vanishing. This is an addition rather
  than a fault, and the skill's Comments row ("conventions, caveats") is where
  it would go.

* **`complete-note`'s trailing "which" dangles** — "needing more than six
  variables, which is the most a stored polynomial may have" attaches "which"
  to "six variables" where the sense wants "six". It is the exact wording of
  both published siblings, so it is a corpus-wide sentence rather than T338's,
  and not worth changing here alone.
