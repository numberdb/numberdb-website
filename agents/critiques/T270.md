# T270 critique: Krawtchouk polynomials $K_n(x;p,N)$ in the Askey normalisation

Read 2026-09-16. T270 is a private draft. As in the T269 run, the SOCKS proxy
on 127.0.0.1:1080 refused the connection (`curl` returned 000), but
numberdb.org answered `curl` directly. `/T270` answers 404, as a draft
should. I read the document through `GET /api/table?id=T270` with the key.
I rendered it through `/preview?table=` in four pieces, each carrying
`Parameters` and one to four entries, and all four answered 200 with no
"Error while parsing". The table holds 760 entries of type `Q[]` with rigour
`exact`: $p\in\{\tfrac14,\tfrac13,\tfrac12,\tfrac23,\tfrac34\}$, every
$1\leq N\leq16$ and every $0\leq n\leq N$. There are no entry comments. The
longest entry is 549 characters, and $K_{16}(x;\tfrac34,16)$ renders as one
long polynomial that a reader can still scan.

`GET /api/table/T270/audit` reports `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. I agree
that the form is sound. The two `HREF` slugs
(`Krawtchouk_polynomials_of_the_Hamming_scheme`, `Meixner_polynomials`)
answer 200, every `CITE` resolves, and each section holds what it should. The
audit cannot see finding 1, which is about what a formula claims, or findings
2 to 5, which are about what the prose says and leaves out.

**What I checked hardest, and found right:**

* *The page renders.* All 35 `$...$` fragments typeset in MathJax 3 (node,
  all packages) with no error. A control fragment with an unclosed brace did
  fail. The `\begin{matrix}-n,-x\\-N\end{matrix}` kept its row break through
  storage. Every `<` in the mathematics ($0<p<1$ in the definition, the
  parameter list and the Meixner relation) is escaped, and the text after it
  renders.
* *It is one table, and the columns say so.* $p$, $N$ and $n$ are arguments
  of one polynomial, and the header $K_n(x;p,N)$ is true of every row. The
  row labels print `1/2`, `2`, `1:`, which are the values themselves. The
  parameters describe the family ($0<p<1$, $N\geq1$, $0\leq n\leq N$), and
  the computed grid is in `complete-note`.
* *The stated mathematics holds, apart from finding 1.* A direct evaluation
  of the ${}_2F_1$ in Python `Fraction`s agrees with every stored row at
  $x=0,1,7/3,5,-2$ (3800 evaluations, 0 mismatches). The Hamming relation
  holds exactly on every row with $p=1-1/q$ (2280 checks). The Meixner
  relation $K_n(x;p,N)=M_n(x;-N,p/(p-1))$ holds on every row, while a control
  with $p/(1-p)$ fails 2710 times. Orthogonality, including the norm
  $((1-p)/p)^n/\binom Nn$, holds exactly for every $p$, $N$, $m$ and $n$
  stored.

Ranking: finding 1 should be fixed before publication, because the formula
as printed is false for the variable the table stores. Findings 2 and 3 are
worth doing. Finding 4 is worth doing and cheap, and finding 5 is a
cheap deletion. The last section lists
things I only noticed.

## 1. The generating function is stated for every $x$, but holds only on $x=0,\ldots,N$ (must fix)

**What a reader sees.** Formula (2):

> $\sum_{n=0}^{N}\binom{N}{n}K_n(x;p,N)t^n=(1-\frac{1-p}{p}t)^x(1+t)^{N-x}$ [2]

**Why it is wrong.** The left side is a polynomial in $t$ of degree at most
$N$. The right side is a polynomial only when $x$ is an integer with
$0\leq x\leq N$. DLMF 18.23.3, the cited source, says
"$x=0,1,\ldots,N$". The table dropped that condition, and comment (1) then
says the table stores polynomials in the *indeterminate* $x$. A reader is
therefore invited to read (2) as an identity in $x$, and it is false. Take
$N=1$, $p=\tfrac12$, $x=2$, $t=\tfrac25$: the left side is
$1+K_1(2;\tfrac12,1)\,t=-\tfrac15$, and the right side is
$(1-t)^2/(1+t)=\tfrac{9}{35}$. With the stored values, it fails at $x=2$,
$N=1$ for all five $p$, and holds at every $x\in\{0,1,2\}$ with $x\leq N$.

**Fix.** Append "for $x=0,1,\ldots,N$" to formula (2), as the duality formula
(5) already does for its own range.

## 2. `Similar tables` omits the two limits that connect this table to Hahn and Charlier (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** `Similar tables` lists the Hamming-scheme Krawtchouk
polynomials and the Meixner polynomials. The corpus also holds
the Hahn polynomials (T268, `Hahn_polynomials`) and the Charlier polynomials
(T267, `Charlier_polynomials`), and neither is mentioned anywhere on the
page.

**Why it is wrong.** In the Askey scheme, Krawtchouk sits between those two,
and DLMF states both limits:

* 18.21.3: $\lim_{t\to\infty}Q_n(x;pt,(1-p)t,N)=K_n(x;p,N)$;
* 18.21.6: $\lim_{N\to\infty}K_n(x;a/N,N)=C_n(x;a)$.

The second one is the Poisson limit of the binomial distribution, written for
the orthogonal polynomials. It is the relation a reader who knows the
probability behind this table expects to find. I checked both numerically in
exact arithmetic at $x=\tfrac52$, $n=2,4$. The Hahn difference is
$-2.2\times10^{-6}$ and $4.3\times10^{-6}$ at $t=10^6$, down from
$-2.2\times10^{-3}$ and $4.3\times10^{-3}$ at $t=10^3$. The Charlier
difference is $1.7\times10^{-6}$ and $2.2\times10^{-6}$ at $N=10^6$, down
from $1.7\times10^{-3}$ at $N=10^3$. Both shrink like $1/t$ and $1/N$.

**Fix.** Add two entries, each citing a new `Links` key for its DLMF
equation:

* `HREF{Hahn_polynomials}[Hahn polynomials]`: "tend to these,
  $Q_n(x;pt,(1-p)t,N)\to K_n(x;p,N)$ as $t\to\infty$";
* `HREF{Charlier_polynomials}[Charlier polynomials]`: "are the limit
  $K_n(x;a/N,N)\to C_n(x;a)$ as $N\to\infty$".

## 3. `complete-note` ends in a noun phrase and leaves out the real reason for the choice of $p$ (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** "complete: no (it holds $p\in\{\tfrac14,\tfrac13,
\tfrac12,\tfrac23,\tfrac34\}$, every support endpoint $1\leq N\leq16$ and
every degree $0\leq n\leq N$, a symmetric set of common binomial
probabilities with short support sizes)"

**Why it is wrong.** The last phrase is tacked on after the list and is not a
clause. A reader has to guess what "symmetric" means and what "short support
sizes" adds to "$1\leq N\leq16$". The skill says this sentence is the
argument for the range, and the table has a better argument than
"common". $p=\tfrac12,\tfrac23,\tfrac34$ are exactly $p=1-1/q$ for
$q=2,3,4$, so these rows are the binary, ternary and quaternary Hamming
schemes that the comment and T266 relate to. "Symmetric" presumably means
the set is closed under $p\mapsto1-p$, and that matters because of the
reflection

$$K_n(N-x;p,N)=\Bigl(-\frac{1-p}{p}\Bigr)^nK_n(x;1-p,N),$$

which holds exactly on every pair of stored rows (3800 checks). That
reflection is not in `Formulas`, so the word points at something the page
never states.

**Fix.** End the note after "$0\leq n\leq N$", then add: "; $p=\tfrac12,
\tfrac23,\tfrac34$ are the Hamming schemes with $q=2,3,4$, and the set is
closed under $p\mapsto1-p$". Add the reflection to `Formulas`, as T268 does for
Hahn in `formula-reflection`, or drop the word "symmetric". I verified the
reflection only on the stored rows and did not look up a source for it.

## 4. The definition names the object instead of defining it (worth doing, cheap)

**What a reader sees.**

> For rational $0<p<1$, integer $N\geq1$ and $0\leq n\leq N$, $K_n(x;p,N)$ is
> the degree-$n$ Krawtchouk polynomial in the Askey normalisation, defined by
> the terminating hypergeometric sum (1) of DLMF [1].

**Why it is wrong.** The sentence says that $K_n$ is the Krawtchouk
polynomial in the Askey normalisation, which is what the title already says.
The definition itself is "(1)", a number the reader has to look up further
down the page. It is short enough to state: the Charlier (T267) and Hamming
Krawtchouk (T266) definitions write their sums inline, and T266 is the table
a reader is most likely to compare this one with. T268 and T269 have the same
pattern, so this is a house habit rather than a slip in this table.

**Fix.** "For rational $0<p<1$, integer $N\geq1$ and integer $0\leq n\leq N$,
the Krawtchouk polynomial is
$K_n(x;p,N)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_j(-x)_j}{(-N)_j\,j!}p^{-j}$, the
terminating ${}_2F_1(-n,-x;-N;1/p)$ of DLMF CITE{DLMFExplicit}." The
"Askey normalisation" is already in the title and in comment (2).

## 5. `rigour details` ends on the size of a dry run (cheap, minor)

**What a reader sees.** "The dry run measured 760 entries, longest written
entry 549 characters, and an entries block of 112.4 KB."

**Why it is wrong.** This field says how the values were obtained. The entry
count, character count and block size are facts about a run and a size
limit, not about the polynomials. The sentence is also a list without verbs
after "measured".

**Fix.** Delete the sentence. The two sentences before it are good.

## Noticed only

* **The Hamming relation is printed twice.** Comment (2) and the first
  `Similar tables` entry both give
  $\mathcal{K}_n(x;N,q)=\binom Nn(q-1)^nK_n(x;1-1/q,N)$. In the same way,
  $K_n(0;p,N)=1$ is in comment (2) and again as formula (4). Neither copy is
  wrong. If a person wants one copy of each, the comment could keep only
  "This is the Askey-scheme normalisation of DLMF, with $K_n(0;p,N)=1$".
* **The Meixner relation cites only half of itself.** `CITE{DLMFExplicit}`
  is DLMF 18.20.6, the Krawtchouk ${}_2F_1$. The Meixner ${}_2F_1$ it is
  compared with is 18.20.7.
* **The index letter differs from T266.** T266 writes $\mathcal{K}_k$ with
  degree $k$, and this page writes $\mathcal{K}_n$. The relation is still
  correct, but a reader who follows the link meets a different letter.
* **Repeated values.** 80 of the 760 entries are the constant polynomial
  `1` ($n=0$), and $K_1=1-x/(pN)$ repeats wherever $pN$ agrees (for example
  `-1/2*x + 1` four times). Degree 0 is held by every polynomial table in the
  corpus, so I am not recommending a change. I note it only because 80
  identical rows is more than usual.
* **The keyword `Askey scheme`** is indexed at title weight, so a search for
  "Askey scheme" will rank this table above Hahn, Meixner and Charlier,
  which belong to the scheme just as much and do not carry the keyword.
  The title already contains "Askey".
* **`Programs`** ends with a bare expression, which prints nothing under
  `sage -python` but shows the result in a Sage session. The term ratio it
  multiplies by is that of formula (1), and the stored
  $K_2(x;\tfrac12,5)$ is `1/5*x^2 - x + 1`.
