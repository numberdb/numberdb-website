Read on 2026-09-17 against the skill as served at <https://numberdb.org/skill>
that day (fetched fresh).

**How it was read.** The SOCKS proxy served `/skill` and then stopped
listening on 1080, as `docs/agent-environment.md` already records, so
everything after that went direct. The document came from
`GET /api/table?id=T293` with the zeta3 key, and from `numberdb.table('T293')`
inside `agents/sage.sh`. The two agree. `/T293` answers 404 to a draft, so

**Audit:** `GET /api/table/T293/audit` returned **HTTP 500**, twice, with the
generic error page. The same keyed request for T292 returned
`"findings": [], "clean": true` a few seconds later. So the endpoint works,
and something about this document makes it crash. This is the crash already
recorded at the end of `docs/agent-environment.md`. I have no audit to agree
or disagree with. The one field in T293 that is unlike T292 is
`References: {reference_1: "null"}` (finding 6). The audit code I read handles
that shape, though, so that suspicion is unconfirmed. Somebody with server
logs should run `manage.py audit_table T293`.

**What I checked, and found sound:**

* **It is one table.** $D$ is a number, `conductor`, `class` and `curve` make
  up an LMFDB curve label, and the column is headed $R_{E/K}$, which is true
  of every row.
* **The parameters describe the family.** $D$ is "positive fundamental
  discriminant". The five fields and the norm bound are in `complete-note`.
* **Order.** Rows run by $D$, then by conductor norm and index, then by class
  and curve, as LMFDB lists them.
* **Identification.** Every entry comment gives the full LMFDB label, the
  conductor ideal, the rank and a Weierstrass equation. A reader who lands on
  a row knows which curve they have.

The prose is in reasonable shape, but the table has one real problem
(finding 1) and two smaller ones in the numbers (findings 2 and 3). Findings 4
to 8 are one-line fixes, most of them already made in T292 and not carried
across. Findings 9 to 12 are noted only.

## Worth doing

### 1. 553 of the 585 entries are the number 1

**What a reader sees.** The page opens on rows reading `5 / 31.1 / a / 1: 1`,
`1`, `1`, `1`... All 48 rows for $D=5$ and all 82 rows for $D=8$ are `1`. The
first regulator that is not 1 is row 225, `12 / 47.1 / b / 1`. Across the
table, 32 rows hold a real regulator and 553 hold `1`, the regulator of a
rank-0 curve.

**Why it is wrong.** The skill says this directly. Values that are a handful
of small integers "match everything and tell nobody anything, and putting
them in a table makes search by number worse for everyone. Those belong in
the comment on the entry they explain". Nobody holding a `1` from a
calculation will learn anything from 553 elliptic curves. And for the reader
this table is for, who holds 0.0985688415..., the useful rows are buried
under 224 rows of the same integer. The fact behind those rows is already
stated once, in `comment-rank-zero`, and once is where it belongs. The
corpus's own precedent is the same: the three tables over $\mathbb{Q}$ that
this one lists under `Similar tables` are by rank 1, 2 and 3, and none holds
rank 0.

**Smallest fix.** Drop the rank-0 rows from the generator. Keep
`comment-rank-zero` as the one sentence saying what their regulator would be.
Rewrite `complete-note` to "it holds every curve of positive rank in
ecnf-data with ...". With 32 rows left, the conductor-norm bound of 60 is
worth raising. T292 already goes to 150 over the same five fields, so the
source clearly covers that range. I did not count how many positive-rank
curves that would give. The generator can report the count before anybody
settles on a bound.

### 2. Rows that must be equal disagree in their last digits

**What a reader sees.** Two neighbouring rows:

    17 / 17.1 / a / 1:   6.0705123554606643216641072065434946721
    17 / 17.1 / a / 8:   6.0705123554606643216641072065434946201

and, under `38.2`, `a1` ends `...536608` while `a4` ends `...536611`.

**Why it is wrong.** The conductor ideal $(-2w+1)$ is its own Galois
conjugate, since $\sigma(w)=1-w$ gives $\sigma(-2w+1)=-(-2w+1)$. Applying
$\sigma$ to the coefficients of `a1`, $330w-936\mapsto-330w-606$ and
$4996w-13110\mapsto-4996w-8114$, gives exactly the equation stored for `a8`.
So the two curves are conjugate, and their regulators are equal. A stored
decimal claims plus or minus one unit in its last place, and these two
differ by 520 units. At least one of them, and in practice both, carries two
or three digits the source does not support. `38.2 a1` and `a4` are
isogenous and agree to 37 digits, so they differ by 3 units in the last
place, and the same explanation applies. The rigour is `heuristic`, which is
honest. But the digits still make a claim that the table's own rows
contradict.

**Smallest fix.** In the generator, cut every transcribed regulator to the
number of digits on which pairs that are equal in theory actually agree:
Galois-conjugate curves, and isogenous curves whose ratio is 1. Judging by
the `17.1 a1/a8` pair, that is about 35 significant digits rather than 38.
Say so in `rigour details`. Or ask the source why its `reg` field carries
more digits than it supports.

### 3. Repeated values are not explained

**What a reader sees.** Among the 32 real regulators, `47.1 b` and
`47.2 b` under $D=12$ hold the same two numbers, and so do `38.2 a` and
`38.3 a`, `47.1 a` and `47.2 a`, and `52.3 b` and `52.6 b` under $D=17$. Under
$D=13$ the pairs cross: `51.2 a1` equals `51.3 a2`, and `51.2 a2` equals
`51.3 a1`. Inside one class, `17.1 a` holds $6.0705\ldots$, $3.0352\ldots$,
$1.5176\ldots$, $0.7588\ldots$ and $0.3794\ldots$, each half the one before it.

**Why it is wrong.** A reader who searches one of these numbers gets two or
more curves back and cannot tell from the page whether that is a duplicate,
an error or mathematics. It is mathematics, and two sentences would say so.
The conductor ideals $(-2w+9)$ and $(2w+7)$ for $D=13$ are Galois conjugate,
and conjugate curves have equal regulators. `comment-index` already says why
there is one row per curve. It does not say that isogenous curves have
regulators in a rational ratio, which is why `17.1 a` is a column of halvings.
T292 had the same gap (its finding 2) and now has the sentence.

**Smallest fix.** Add to `comment-index`: "A curve and its Galois conjugate
have the same regulator, so the rows for conductors $51.2$ and $51.3$ over
$D=13$ repeat one another, with the curve numbers exchanged. Curves in one
isogeny class have regulators in a rational ratio: in class $17.1$-a over
$D=17$ the ratios are powers of $2$."

### 4. The conductor ideal in 343 entry comments renders as `(5∗w−2)`

**What a reader sees.** "has conductor ideal $(5*w-2)$", where MathJax sets
`*` as the binary operator ∗. 343 of the 585 comments contain one.

**Why it is wrong.** The same fault was finding 1 on T292 and has been fixed
there. It is LMFDB's machine syntax printed as mathematics.

**Smallest fix.** Share T292's repaired comment builder, or delete the `*`
before `w` the same way. Every occurrence has an integer coefficient directly
before `w`.

### 5. The column headers read `$conductor$`, `$class$`, `$curve$` in math italics

**What a reader sees.** The first line of the page, and the parameter list,
show three words typeset as products of single-letter variables beside a
correctly set $D$ and $R_{E/K}$.

**Why it is wrong.** None of the three has a `display`, so the view falls
back to `$key$`. T292 was given `display: conductor` and `display: class`
(its finding 4). T293 has a third such parameter.

**Smallest fix.** `display: "conductor"`, `display: "class"`,
`display: "curve"`.

### 6. The References section renders one reference reading "null"

**What a reader sees.** Under References: "[1] null". Because that entry
takes number [1], the Definition's `CITE{ecnf-data}` reads [2] where every
other section's reads [1].

**Why it is wrong.** It is an empty field serialised as a string. The table
cites no paper.

**Smallest fix.** Remove `References` (T292 has `References: {}`). It is also
the first thing to try against the audit crash above.

### 7. `formula-BSD` has the double $r!$ and the unfinished glosses that T292 fixed

**What a reader sees.** "Here $L^*(E/K,1)$ is the leading Taylor coefficient
divided by $r!$, $\Omega_{E/K}$ is the product over both real embeddings with
real components counted, and $R_{E/K}$ is the regulator for the absolute
Neron-Tate height." Earlier in the formula: "in these normalisations".

**Why it is wrong.** The leading Taylor coefficient already includes the
$1/r!$, so read literally this divides twice (T292 finding 3). "The product
over both real embeddings with real components counted" does not say what is
multiplied. $c_{\mathfrak p}$ and Sha are never named. "These normalisations"
points at nothing. Here $r$ is the Mordell-Weil rank from the Definition,
while $L^*$ is taken at the order of vanishing. The formula is the place
where the two are asserted to be equal, so it should not assume that
equality in its own gloss.

**Smallest fix.** Copy T292's repaired `formula-BSD`, which now reads "with
the $L$-function normalised as in the Definition" and names the real
periods, the component counts and the Tamagawa numbers. Then add "where
$L^*(E/K,1)$ is $L^{(r)}(E/K,1)/r!$ and the order of vanishing is $r$, as the
conjecture predicts." The two sibling tables should carry the same sentence
for the same formula.

### 8. The Definition names its source's basis, and spells Néron without its accent

**What a reader sees.** "...the regulator $R_{E/K}=\det(\langle
P_i,P_j\rangle)$ of the free part of $E(K)$, using the absolute Neron-Tate
height pairing and the ecnf-data Mordell-Weil basis [2]."

**Why it is wrong.** The determinant does not depend on which basis is
chosen, since a change of basis is in $\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{Z})$. Which
basis the source used is a fact about how the numbers were transcribed, so
it belongs in `rigour details`, not in the definition. Meanwhile $P_i$ is
never introduced. The name is Néron. The ASCII spelling belongs in
`Keywords`, which already has it.

**Smallest fix.** "This table gives the regulator
$R_{E/K}=\det(\langle P_i,P_j\rangle)_{i,j}$, where $P_1,\dots,P_r$ is a basis
of $E(K)$ modulo torsion and $\langle\,,\rangle$ is the absolute Néron-Tate
height pairing." Do the same in `formula-BSD`.

## Noted only

### 9. `Similar tables`: a note-to-self relation, and a link that waits on T292

The T292 relation reads "the $L^*(E/K,1)$ factor in the BSD formula for
overlapping ecnf-data curves". "Overlapping ecnf-data curves" is shorthand.
T292 covers every isogeny class here, so "the left side of the Birch and
Swinnerton-Dyer formula (1), for the same curves" says it. The link 404s
until T292 is published.

### 10. `complete-note` gives no reason for the fields or the bound

T292's note now says "the five real quadratic fields of smallest
discriminant" and uses norm 150. This one uses 60 with no reason. A reader
comparing the two sibling tables will wonder why. If finding 1 is done, the
sentence is rewritten anyway.

### 11. Keywords and citations

* `Keywords` carries "Hilbert modular form, base change", neither of which
  the table mentions, and "Q(sqrt 5)" but none of the other four fields.
  T292 now lists all five.
* The `LMFDB` link is never cited. `comment-field-generator` cites the GitHub
  repository for "the one used by LMFDB". Cite `LMFDB` there and at the first
  mention in `comment-index` (T292 finding 8).

### 12. `rigour details`: "the square integer recorded by ecnf-data"

A reader has to work out that this means the analytic order of Sha. T292's
repaired wording names it.
