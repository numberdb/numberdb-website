## Findings, ranked

### 1. The page never says why the range is $3\leq n\leq6$ (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** "Table is complete: no (it holds both forms for every
matrix size $n$ with $3\leq n\leq6$)". Comment (7) says Beck and Pixton give
$H_n(t)$ for $1\leq n\leq9$. A reader who compares the two wonders why 7, 8
and 9 are missing, and has no answer. Comment (6) begins "The omitted cases
are $H_1(t)=1$, …". Omitted from what, and why, is left to the reader. The
parameter meanwhile says "$n\geq3$". That is where this table starts, not
what the family is: the family is $n\geq1$, and comment (6) gives its first
two members.

**Why it matters.** The skill asks for the range and the reason to be stated
in `complete-note`: "That sentence is the argument". There are two reasons
here, and both are good. $H_7(t)$ is 2252 characters, past where an entry is
still read (the build measured this). $H_1=h^*_{B_1}=h^*_{B_2}=1$ would
answer every search for 1, and `api/lookup` for "1" already returns 100
results. Neither reason is on the page.

**Smallest fix.**
- Set the `n` constraint to "$n$ is a positive integer".
- Set `complete-note` to "it holds both forms for $3\leq n\leq6$; $H_1$,
  $H_2$ and their $h^*$-polynomials are trivial and are given in a comment,
  and from $n=7$ the Ehrhart polynomial runs to thousands of characters".
- Rewrite comment (6) to name what it gives: "For $n=1$ and $n=2$ the
  polytope is a point and a segment: $H_1(t)=1$, $H_2(t)=t+1$, and
  $h^*_{B_1}(z)=h^*_{B_2}(z)=1$."

### 2. Comment (7) ends on a remark about a web page, and the paper it comes from is never cited (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** "The full polynomial $H_{10}(t)$ is not included in
that source." That is a fact about a web page, not about $B_{10}$. The fact
about the mathematics is more useful: the same paper computes the volume of
$B_{10}$, which is the leading coefficient of $H_{10}(t)$ up to $81!$. OEIS
A037302 lists it. Separately, `References` holds Beck and Pixton's *Discrete
Comput. Geom.* paper with doi and arXiv, and nothing cites it. Under
"References" it is furniture.

**Smallest fix.** Replace comment (7) with "Beck and Pixton computed
$H_n(t)$ for $n\leq9$ CITE{BeckPixton}, and their polynomials are listed at
CITE{BeckPixtonData}. For $n=10$ only the leading coefficient is known, as
the normalized volume CITE{OEISVolume}." If "only … is known" cannot be
confirmed beyond 2003, write "the same paper gives the volume of $B_{10}$"
instead. The paper's abstract says that much.

### 3. Formula (4) reads as though $d$ were the normalized volume (worth doing; one sentence)

**What a reader sees.** "$h^*_{B_n}(1)=d!\,[t^d]H_n(t)$ for $d=(n-1)^2$, the
normalized volume of $B_n$ [5]." The appositive sits right after
"$d=(n-1)^2$", so the natural reading is that $(n-1)^2$ is the normalized
volume. $[t^d]$, coefficient extraction, is not defined anywhere on the
page.

**Smallest fix.** "$h^*_{B_n}(1)=d!\,[t^d]H_n(t)$, $d!$ times the leading
coefficient of $H_n(t)$, is the normalized volume of $B_n$ CITE{OEISVolume}."

### 4. Formulas (1) and (2) begin "If $d=(n-1)^2$", and nothing says $d$ is the dimension or that (1) defines $h^*$ (worth doing; small)

**What a reader sees.** "If $d=(n-1)^2$, then $\sum_{t\geq0}H_n(t)z^t=\dots$".
"If" reads as a hypothesis, as though the identity held only for some $d$.
But $d$ is always $(n-1)^2$, the dimension of $B_n$, and the page never uses
the word dimension. Formula (1) is also how the $h^*$-polynomial is defined.
The Definition names $h^*$ and gives no definition of it, so that is the
sentence a reader of the Definition needs.

**Smallest fix.**
- (1): "The $h^*$-polynomial is defined by
  $\sum_{t\geq0}H_n(t)z^t=h^*_{B_n}(z)/(1-z)^{d+1}$, where $d=(n-1)^2$ is the
  dimension of $B_n$ CITE{WikiEhrhart}."
- (2): "Writing $h^*_{B_n}(z)=\sum_{i}h_i^*z^i$,
  $H_n(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_i^*\binom{t+d-i}{d}$."

### 5. The symmetry a reader sees in every $h^*$ row is not stated (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** Anyone who arrives holding
$1,14,87,148,87,14,1$ sees at once that it is a palindrome, and that its
degree, 6, is less than $d=9$. The page explains neither. The explanation is
the standard structural fact about $B_n$, and it is also what makes these
polynomials computable by interpolation.

**Smallest fix.** Add `formula-reciprocity`: "$H_n(-n-t)=(-1)^{n-1}H_n(t)$
and $H_n(-1)=\dots=H_n(-n+1)=0$ CITE{BeckPixton}. Equivalently,
$h^*_{B_n}(z)$ has degree $(n-1)(n-2)$ and palindromic coefficients." The two
identities are quoted from the paper, and all three statements hold on every
stored row (see "How it was read").

### 6. The Sage program recomputes a stored row from another stored row (worth considering)

**What a reader sees.** (P1) turns $h^*_{B_4}$, typed in by hand, into
$H_4(t)$. It runs, and it gives the stored value. But the input and the
output are both on the page already, and formula (2) says the same thing. The
skill says `Programs` is "the standard incantation … for a reader who wants
one more value", and this one cannot produce a value the table lacks.

**Smallest fix.** Add the incantation that starts from the polytope:

    from sage.geometry.polyhedron.library import polytopes
    polytopes.Birkhoff_polytope(4, backend='normaliz').ehrhart_polynomial()

Say beside it that it needs PyNormaliz (`sage -pip install pynormaliz`) or
LattE. **I have not run this.** The Sage image here has neither engine. It
also counts in $\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}$, which is the lattice the Definition
fixes, but the output should be checked against $H_3$ and $H_4$ before it
goes in. Keep (P1) if the conversion is wanted, since it is correct.

### 7. Formula (3) ends with a remark about the site (small)

**What a reader sees.** "$H_n(1)=n!$, the number of permutation matrices in
$B_n$, so these values are given by the factorial table." "These values"
points instead of naming. "Are given by the factorial table" describes this
database, not the mathematics. The Similar tables entry says the same thing
again, in almost the same words.

**Smallest fix.** "$H_n(1)=$HREF{Factorial}[$n!$], since the lattice points
of $B_n$ are the $n\times n$ permutation matrices." Or leave the link to
Similar tables and write $n!$ plainly.

### 8. Comment (5) points at "the count", and a keyword names the objects it excludes (small)

**What a reader sees.** "The count is for semi-magic squares: it fixes all
row and column sums and imposes no diagonal condition." No count has been
named in Comments. The Definition's participle is two sections up. `Keywords`
also carries `magic squares` on its own, at title weight. Magic squares with
diagonal conditions are exactly what this comment says the table is not.
Deleting the keyword costs no reach, because `semi-magic squares` already
contains both words.

**Smallest fix.** "$H_n(t)$ counts semi-magic squares: $n\times n$
nonnegative integer matrices whose rows and columns all sum to $t$, with no
condition on the diagonals." Delete the keyword `magic squares`.

### 9. Noted only

- **Definition**: "$H_n(t)=L_{B_n}(t)$" introduces $L_{B_n}$ and never uses
  it again. "its Ehrhart polynomial $H_n(t)$" is enough. Otherwise the
  Definition does its job; see below.
- **`rigour details`** opens "Before the draft was filled". Once the table is
  published that describes a stage nobody sees, and "The entries were
  compared with …" says the same. "each stored row with $3\leq n\leq6$
  satisfied $H_n(1)=n!$" is true only of the `ehrhart` rows, and every stored
  row has $3\leq n\leq6$ anyway.
- **Link titles** (6)–(8) say "with all line sums $n$". On this page $n$ is
  the matrix size and $t$ is the line sum, so `$t$` would match.
- **$h^*_{B_3}(z)=z^2+z+1$** is also $\Phi_3$ in T95, and `api/lookup`
  already matches across the variable name. Two tables answering is the
  database working, not a fault. It is not `repeats` or `equals`.
- **Ehrhart polynomials print in descending powers**, which is the client's
  order. Beck and Pixton print ascending powers, which put the small-$t$
  counts first. I do not suggest changing it.
- **Issue #94** spells it "Erhart". A keyword `Erhart polynomial` would
  catch the misspelling. It is a judgement call, and I do not press it.

## What reads well

- **The Definition defines.** It fixes the polytope, the ambient space, and,
  through "counting nonnegative integer matrices with all row and column sums
  $t$", the lattice that both the Ehrhart and the $h^*$-polynomial depend on.
  Two people would build the same table from it.
- **Rendering is clean.** Every fragment typesets, both parameter keys render
  as code, and every CITE and HREF resolves on the full page. The row labels
  $H_n(t)$ and $h^*_{B_n}(z)$ tell a reader holding a polynomial which form
  they have, in the first screen.
- **No sentence apologises**, and nothing is pointed at by position except
  "the count" and "these values" (findings 7 and 8). The one link the corpus
  allows is made.
