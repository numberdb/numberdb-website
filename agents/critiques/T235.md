type `Q[]` and rigour `exact`: both forms for every $\Pi_n$ with
$3\leq n\leq20$. No entry carries a comment. The longest entry is
$h^*_{\Pi_{20}}(z)$ at 816 characters, and the entries block is about 11 KB.

**Summary.** The page renders cleanly, and every formula holds on every row.
Nothing below stops it being published. Findings 1 to 3 are worth doing:
a wrong parameter title, a definition that hands its convention to Wikipedia

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The parameter $n$ is titled "rank", and the rank is $n-1$ (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** "$n$ — rank ($n$ is a positive integer)". $\Pi_n$ is
$(n-1)$-dimensional. It is the permutohedron of the root system $A_{n-1}$,
whose rank is $n-1$. T233, in the same series, indexes $A_n$ by its rank. A
reader who takes "rank" at its word and wants the rank-3 permutohedron (the
truncated octahedron) looks up $n=3$ and gets the hexagon. Wikipedia and Sage
both call $n$ the order: "the permutohedron of order 4 … is the truncated
octahedron", and `polytopes.permutahedron(4)`.

**Smallest fix.** Change the title to `order`, or to `number of coordinates`.
The key `n` does not change.

### 2. The Definition hands the $h^*$ convention to Wikipedia, and the page never gives the dimension it depends on (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** "…the numerator $h^*_{\Pi_n}(z)$ using the
Ehrhart-series convention that [1] describes." The linked article writes the
denominator as $(1-z)^{d+1}$ with $d$ the dimension. Nothing on the page says
that $\Pi_n$ has dimension $n-1$. The Definition places $\Pi_n$ "in
$\mathbb{R}^n$", and the parameter is called its rank. So a careful reader
could take $d=n$ and expect a numerator over $(1-z)^{n+1}$. Formula (1) does
say $(1-z)^n$, but the Definition sends the reader to Wikipedia and not to
Formula (1). The Definition should define both polynomials itself, precisely
enough that two people build the same table.

**Smallest fix.** This version is 338 characters:

> For a positive integer $n$, $\Pi_n$ is the $(n-1)$-dimensional
> permutohedron $\operatorname{conv}\{(\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(n)):\sigma\in S_n\}$
> in $\mathbb{R}^n$. This table stores its Ehrhart polynomial
> $L_{\Pi_n}(t)=\#(t\Pi_n\cap\mathbb{Z}^n)$ and its $h^*$-polynomial, defined
> by $\sum_{t\geq0}L_{\Pi_n}(t)z^t=h^*_{\Pi_n}(z)/(1-z)^n$.

`CITE{WikiEhrhart}` is then cited nowhere. It can move to the first mention of
"Ehrhart polynomial" in a comment, or stay in `Links` uncited.

### 3. `Keywords` lacks "permutahedron" (worth doing; one word)

**What a reader sees.** Nothing, and that is the fault. Wikipedia gives
"permutahedron" as the other spelling, and Sage's constructor is
`polytopes.permutahedron`. So somebody who built one in Sage and wants the
polynomial types "permutahedron". The index stems words but does not bridge
spellings, and the page contains "permutohedron" only.

**Smallest fix.** Add "permutahedron" and "permutahedra" to `Keywords`.

### 4. Comments (6) and (8) do not say what they mean (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

- (6): "[2] describes the permutohedron. Translating the vertices, for example
  to $(0,1,\ldots,n-1)$, does not change the Ehrhart polynomial." The first
  sentence is a remark about a link, not a fact about the mathematics. The
  second says "translating the vertices to" a single point. What is meant is
  translating the polytope so that its vertices are the permutations of
  $(0,1,\ldots,n-1)$. It is true only because the translation vector is
  integral, and the comment does not say so.
- (8): "Stanley's graphical-zonotope formula [1] gives the forest identity.
  Formula (2) identifies $L_{\Pi_n}(t)$ with the forest polynomial of $K_n$."
  "The forest identity" is named before anything has said what it is.
  "Graphical zonotope" and "forest polynomial" are defined nowhere, and the
  two sentences say the same thing twice. The keyword "graphical zonotope" is
  backed only by this comment.

**Smallest fix.** Replace (6) and (8) with one comment:

> Up to translation by $(1,\ldots,1)$, $\Pi_n$ is the graphical zonotope of
> the complete graph $K_n$ CITE{WikiPermutohedron}, the Minkowski sum of the
> segments $[e_i,e_j]$ for $1\leq i<j\leq n$. An integer translation does not
> change the Ehrhart polynomial. Stanley CITE{StanleyZonotope} showed that the
> Ehrhart polynomial of a graphical zonotope counts the forests of its graph
> by their number of edges, which for $K_n$ is CITE{formula-forests}.

I checked the Minkowski-sum statement for $n=5$ (see above), and it is the
statement in the Wikipedia article. I did not read Stanley's paper. The claim
attributed to it is the one the table already makes.

### 5. Two Similar-tables relations do not state a relation (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.**

- T125: "gives another specialization of graph polynomials for the same graph
  census". This table has no graph census, and "another specialization of
  graph polynomials" relates the chromatic polynomial to the Tutte polynomial,
  not to this table. A reader cannot tell why the link is here. There is a
  real connection. The vertices of a graphical zonotope correspond to the
  acyclic orientations of its graph, and those number $|P(G,-1)|$. For $K_n$
  that is $n!$, the number of vertices of $\Pi_n$.
- T234: "records the Ehrhart data of the hypersimplex summands of the
  permutohedron". The relation is true:
  $\Pi_n=\Delta(1,n)+\Delta(2,n)+\cdots+\Delta(n-1,n)+(1,\ldots,1)$, which I
  checked for $n=5$. But "summands" leaves the reader to reconstruct the sum.
  And "Ehrhart data of the summands" suggests that $L_{\Pi_n}$ can be built
  from those rows. It cannot, because the Ehrhart polynomial of a Minkowski
  sum is not determined by the Ehrhart polynomials of its summands.

**Smallest fix.**

- T125: "holds $P(K_n,x)$, and $|P(K_n,-1)|=n!$ counts the acyclic
  orientations of $K_n$, which correspond to the vertices of $\Pi_n$".
  Otherwise drop the entry.
- T234: "holds the hypersimplices $\Delta(k,n)$, and
  $\Pi_n=\Delta(1,n)+\cdots+\Delta(n-1,n)+(1,\ldots,1)$ as a Minkowski sum".

The Tutte relation reads "whose specialization at $(1+1/t,1)$ gives
$L_{\Pi_n}(t)$". It drops the factor $t^{n-1}$, and T126 holds $K_n$ only for
$n\leq7$. "holds $T_{K_n}$ for $n\leq7$, and
$L_{\Pi_n}(t)=t^{n-1}T_{K_n}(1+1/t,1)$" says both.

### 6. The normalized volume is in `rigour details` and not in `Formulas` (small)

**What a reader sees.** $h^*_{\Pi_n}(1)=(n-1)!\,n^{n-2}$ appears only as
"For every stored $n$, … $h^*_{\Pi_n}(1)$ equalled $(n-1)!n^{n-2}$", a fact
about the run. It is the one identity about the `h-star` column. A reader
holding an $h^*$-polynomial checks its coefficient sum first. It is also the
only formula that ties the $h^*$ column to Formula (4). The skill puts
identities in `Formulas`.

**Smallest fix.** Add a formula: "$h^*_{\Pi_n}(1)=(n-1)!\,n^{n-2}$ is the
normalized volume of $\Pi_n$, $(n-1)!$ times the leading coefficient in
CITE{formula-leading}". The sentence in `rigour details` can stay as the
record of the check.

### 7. `Programs` is the generator's recurrence, not an incantation (small)

**What a reader sees.** Twenty-two lines: an unneeded `import numberdb.sage`,
two named ring imports, a hand-written forest recurrence, and no
$h^*$-polynomial, which is half of what the table holds. It runs and is right.
But a reader in Sage who wants $n=21$ can use Sage's own Tutte polynomial and
Formula (3), which the page already states. The same finding was made on T234
and repaired there in this form.

**Smallest fix.** Use this code. I ran it through `agents/sage.sh` for
$n=3,5,7$, with `**` in place of `^` because scripts are not preparsed, and
it printed both stored rows each time:

    n = 5
    t = polygen(QQ, 't'); z = polygen(QQ, 'z')
    T = graphs.CompleteGraph(n).tutte_polynomial(); x, y = T.parent().gens()
    L = QQ['t'](t^(n-1) * T.subs({x: 1 + 1/t, y: 1}))
    h = (sum(L(j)*z^j for j in range(n)) * (1 - z)^n).truncate(n)
    L, h
