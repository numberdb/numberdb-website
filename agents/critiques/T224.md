## Findings, ranked

### 1. Three row symbols name a different group from the one stored, and one of them names a Euclidean group

What a reader sees, in the Coxeter symbol column: `[4, 3^[1, 1]]`,
`[3, 4^[1, 1]]` and `[4^[1, 1, 1]]`.

Why it is wrong. In this notation a bracketed superscript is a ring and a
plain one is a branch: $[6,3^{[3]}]$ ends in a triangle, $[4^{[4]}]$ is a
square, and $[3,4^{1,1}]$ forks into two edges labelled $4$. The generator
turns every `^{...}` into `^[...]`, so the branch symbols of the source,
$[3,4^{1,1}]$ and $[4^{1,1,1}]$, now read as rings.

The row `[4,3^[1,1]]` has a second fault. It is $\overline{DV}_3$, and the
diagram that Kozma and Szirmai draw beside it has a $6$-edge followed by a
fork. That is $[6,3^{1,1}]$, which is how Wikipedia's "Paracompact uniform
honeycombs" (already in `Links`) writes it. The source's own label,
$[4,3^{[1,1]}]$, is a misprint, and the table copied it.
$[4,3^{1,1}]$ is the affine group $\tilde B_3$, which acts on Euclidean
space. The misprint is hidden because the stored value, $\frac58\Lambda(\pi/3)$,
is right for $[6,3^{1,1}]$, and it happens to equal the row above.

`comment-notation` says the symbols "are written as in" Table 1. A reader
who checks that finds $[3^{[\,]\times[\,]}]$ where the page prints
`[3^[]x[]]`. The page never says what a superscript means.

These are entry addresses, and addresses freeze when the table is
published. **Fix this before it is offered for review.**

Smallest change:

- In the generator, key the rows by the symbol with its braces kept:
  `[6,3^{1,1}]`, `[3,4^{1,1}]`, `[4^{1,1,1}]`, `[6,3^{[3]}]`,
  `[3^{[3,3]}]`, `[3^{[]x[]}]`, `[(3,6)^{[2]}]`, `[4^{[4]}]` and so on. If
  braces cannot go in an address, keep `^[...]` for rings only and write
  branches as `^1,1`, so that `[6,3^1,1]` and `[4,3^[3]]` stay distinct.
- Replace `comment-notation` with a sentence that explains the notation and
  owns the correction:

      Rows are labelled by Coxeter symbol CITE{WikiParacompact}: $[p,q,r]$ is a
      linear diagram, $[(p,q,r,s)]$ a cycle, a superscript $1,1$ a fork into two
      edges, and a bracketed superscript such as $[3]$ a ring. Each entry comment
      gives the Witt symbol of CITE{KozmaSzirmai}, Table 1, whose label for
      $\overline{DV}_3$ is misprinted as $[4,3^{[1,1]}]$; its diagram is
      $[6,3^{1,1}]$.

**Worth doing, first.**

### 2. The entry comments give a check decimal where the source gives the closed form, and the page never says that one row is Catalan's constant

What a reader sees, under the row `[4^[4]]`, whose value is
0.9159655941…: "Kozma and Szirmai write this simplex as
$\widehat{RR}_3$ and give the check decimal $0.9159655942\ldots$ in their
Table 1." All 23 comments have this shape.

Why it is wrong. A check decimal is a fact about the build, and
`rigour details` already says the rows were checked against the source.
Under a row it says nothing a reader can use, and it repeats ten digits of
the value printed just above it. Meanwhile the same Table 1 gives the
volume in closed form for 17 rows. I checked all 17 against the stored
values:

| rows | volume |
|---|---|
| `[3,3,6]` `[3,3^[3]]` `[4,3,6]` `[3,6,3]` `[4,3^[3]]` `[4,3^[1,1]]` `[6,3,6]` `[3^[]x[]]` `[6,3^[3]]` `[(3,6)^[2]]` `[3^[3,3]]` | $\frac18, \frac14, \frac5{16}, \frac12, \frac58, \frac58, \frac34, \frac54, \frac32, \frac52, 3$ times $\Lambda(\pi/3)$ |
| `[3,4,4]` `[3,4^[1,1]]` `[4,4,4]` `[(3^2,4^2)]` `[4^[1,1,1]]` `[4^[4]]` | $\frac16, \frac13, \frac12, \frac23, 1, 2$ times $\Lambda(\pi/4)$ |

Since $\Lambda(\pi/4)=G/2$, `[4^[4]]` is exactly Catalan's constant. That
is the number T175 stores as $\mathrm{Cl}_2(\pi/2)$, and a reader who
arrives holding it is the reader this page most needs to serve.
`[3^[3,3]]` gets this treatment in `comment-regular`, but `[4^[4]]` gets
nothing. `[3,6,3]` and `[(3^2,4^2)]` are also exactly the $D=-3$ and
$D=-4$ covolumes stored in T221, and the page says so only for the rows a
quarter of those.

Smallest change: in the generator, give each comment the closed form from
Table 1 in place of the decimal. For example:

    Kozma and Szirmai write this simplex as $\widehat{RR}_3$. Its volume is
    $2\Lambda(\pi/4)$, which is HREF{Values_of_the_Clausen_functions_at_rational_multiples_of#2,1/2}[Catalan's constant $G$].

For `[3,6,3]` and `[(3^2,4^2)]`, add "the covolume of
$\mathrm{PSL}_2$ of the Eisenstein integers" or "of the Gaussian integers",
linked to `Covolumes_of_the_Bianchi_groups#-3` or `#-4`. The six rows with
no closed form (`[5,3,6]`, `[5,3^[3]]`, and the four cyclic symbols with
$5$ or $6$) keep only the Witt symbol. Drop the second sentence of
`comment-notation`, or reword it to "Each entry comment gives the Witt
symbol and, where there is one, the closed form." **Worth doing.**

### 3. `comment-range` repeats `complete-note`, in the vocabulary of the build

What a reader sees: "This fill holds the $23$ noncompact rank-$4$ Coxeter
simplices in $\mathbb{H}^3$, also called Koszul-type tetrahedra [1]. The
nine compact Coxeter tetrahedra and the higher-dimensional simplices are
not listed yet." Under Data properties the page then says "complete: no (it
holds the $23$ noncompact Coxeter simplices in $\mathbb{H}^3$ and no compact
tetrahedra or simplices in dimensions $4$ to $9$)".

Why it is wrong: "fill" is this website's word for a generator run, and
"yet" is a promise. The range is said twice, and `complete-note` is the
right home for it. The mathematical content of the comment is what
"noncompact" means and the other name for it, and that is worth keeping.

Smallest change:

    comment-range: A Coxeter simplex is noncompact when it has at least one
      ideal vertex. Such simplices are also called Koszul-type CITE{KozmaSzirmai},
      and in $\mathbb{H}^3$ there are $23$ of them.

**Worth doing; small.**

### 4. `Similar tables` says the growth-rate table holds "the same reflection groups", and the two tables share none

What a reader sees: "Growth rates of hyperbolic Coxeter simplex groups —
the same reflection groups, storing growth rates rather than covolumes".

Why it is wrong: T223 holds the nine compact tetrahedral groups, and this
table holds the 23 noncompact ones. A reader who follows the link for the
growth rate of a group on this page will not find it there. The link is
also a 404 while T223 is a draft. Publishing the two tables together
removes that problem, and `audit_table` would flag it after publication.

Smallest change:

    relation: growth rates of the same family of reflection groups; it holds the
      nine compact tetrahedral groups, which this table does not yet

Either publish T223 first or publish the two together. **Worth doing.**

## Noted only

6. **The parameter list prints "Coxeter symbol — Coxeter symbol (a Coxeter
   symbol of …)".** The `display` is used as a label, not a symbol, so the
   page prints the title twice. The key `diagram` does not match either,
   because a bracket is not a diagram. While the table is a draft, the key
   could become `symbol`. Otherwise, a constraint of "a finite-volume
   hyperbolic Coxeter simplex" avoids the third repetition.

7. **The `Programs` $\Lambda$ returns nan wherever its argument is a ball
   around a multiple of $\pi$.** Examples are `Lambda(pi)`, and in
   `formula-orthoscheme` for $[3,3,6]$ the term $\Lambda(\gamma-\theta)$
   with $\gamma=\theta=\pi/6$. There $e^{2i\theta}$ is a ball containing
   the branch point $1$ of $\mathrm{Li}_2$. `Lambda(RB(0))` is fine. A reader
   who combines the snippet with the formula gets nan on the first row.
   One line fixes it:
   `if (theta / pi).contains_integer(): return RB(0)`.

8. **`rigour details` begins its second paragraph "Before the draft was
   filled".** The words are about the build process, and they will read
   oddly once the table is published. The same paragraph uses "ideal
   octahedral values" without saying which rows those are. "All $23$
   values were checked…" says the same thing.

9. **`WikiUniform` and `WikiParacompact` are never cited.** Finding 1's
   rewrite cites the second. The first could go.

10. **Row order follows the commensurability classes of Table 1**
    ($[3,3,6]$, $[3,4,4]$, $[5,3,6]$, then the rest), and the page never
    says so. This is a sensible order, but it looks arbitrary. A clause in
    `comment-notation` would explain it.

11. "Listed is the hyperbolic volume of $P$" in the Definition is inverted
    but grammatical. I would leave it.
