# T255 critique: Degrees of the irreducible characters of the sporadic simple groups

Read 2026-09-16. T255 is a private draft: `/T255` answers 404 anonymously. It
holds 1370 entries, the degrees $\chi_i(1)$ for $i\geq2$ of each of the 26
sporadic simple groups, two parameters (`G`, a named group; `chi`, an integer
index), type `Z`, rigour `exact`, `complete: yes`, no entry comments. The
audit reports nothing.

**It is one table, every fragment of it typesets, and the two cross-table
claims it makes are true of the tables it points at.** Those are the three
things I checked hardest and all three held; the measurements are in "How it
was read" at the end.

What is left is mostly prose, plus one thing a reader cannot see and one thing
they will see before anything else.

- **1 is what a reader meets first and is not the table's to fix.** The one
  field that exists to justify this table's size renders with `(Unknown key)`
  after it, because the site's renderer does not know the key the site's own
  limits module defines. It needs a one-line change in `numberdb_app/views.py`.
- **2 is the reason to open the file.** The `Programs` snippet plus the
  table's own label for one group sends a reader to a different, non-simple
  group, silently.
- **3 to 5 are worth doing**: the size argument is right and has no numbers in
  it, the definition does not link the corpus table it names, and 19 of the 26
  groups cannot be found by their own name.
- **6 to 9 are small** and worth doing while the file is open.
- **The notes at the end are there only because I noticed them.**
- **I agree with the audit, which found nothing.** The last section says what
  it could not have seen.

---

## 1. `Size exception` prints "(Unknown key)" at the reader

What a reader sees, under *Data properties*:

> Size exception: The table has one row for each nontrivial irreducible
> character of each sporadic simple group, because the repeated degrees and
> the shared character index are part of the data; the integer entries are
> short. **(Unknown key)**

Why it is wrong. `numberdb_app/limits.py:93` defines
`EXCEPTION_KEY = 'Size exception'` as the place an author states why a table
is over a soft limit, and `stated_reason` reads it from `Data properties`. The
page renderer's `property_names` map (`numberdb_app/views.py:1063`) does not
list it, so the `else` branch at line 1152 fires and appends `(Unknown key)`.
The field is doing exactly what the system asks of it, and the page tells the
reader it is unrecognised — on the one line whose whole job is to be read and
judged by a person.

The smallest change. Not in the table: add `'Size exception': 'Size
exception'` to `property_names`. T255 is the only table in this repository
carrying the key — no other `generators/*/table.yaml` has one — so the line
may never have been rendered before; it will be rendered on every table that
carries one from now on. I have not made the change; it is outside this table
and outside what a critique may touch.

## 2. `CharacterTable("Fi24")` is a different group from the one stored

What a reader sees. The `Programs` block is

    LoadPackage("ctbllib");
    degrees := List(Irr(CharacterTable("M24")), chi -> chi[1]);

and the *Numbers* column of group labels includes `Fi24`. A reader who wants
one more degree substitutes their group's label for `"M24"`, which works for
25 of the 26 and is the obvious thing to do.

Why it is wrong. In CTblLib 1.3.11 the simple group is stored as `F3+`, and
`Fi24` is registered by `ALN("F3+.2",["fi24","fi24'.2","f24"])` in
`data/ctofisc2.tbl` — so `CharacterTable("Fi24")` returns `F3+.2`, the full
Fischer group $\mathrm{Fi}_{24}=\mathrm{Fi}_{24}'.2$, of order
2510411418381323442585600 with 183 irreducible characters, against the simple
group's 1255205709190661721292800 and 108. No error, no warning: the reader
gets a longer list of different integers and no reason to doubt it. I checked
all 26 labels against the library's `MOT` and `ALN` declarations; `Fi24` is
the only one that resolves to something other than the group stored under it.

The comment already says the mathematics — "The label `Fi24` denotes the
Fischer group $\mathrm{Fi}_{24}'$" — but a reader following the program needs
the library's name for it, which appears nowhere on the page.

The smallest change. In `comment-labels`, after the existing sentence: "In the
GAP Character Table Library that group is `F3+`; `CharacterTable("Fi24")` is
the larger group $\mathrm{Fi}_{24}'.2$." The generator already knows this —
`generate.py` maps `("Fi24", "F3+", "ctofisc2.tbl")` — so this is moving a
fact the build had into the page the reader gets.

## 3. The size argument is the right argument with the numbers left out

What a reader sees: the `Size exception` quoted in 1 above.

Why it is wrong. A `Size exception` is read by a person deciding whether to
accept it, and this one gives them nothing to weigh. Two of the three
sentences argue about size — "the integer entries are short" — and size is not
the limit that was passed. Measured with `limits.measure`'s own walk:

| | this table | soft limit |
|---|---|---|
| entries | **1370** | 1200 |
| entries block | 27.9 KB | 320 KB |
| digits | 27 | does not apply (`type: Z`, exact) |

Only the entry count is over, by 14%. And the note's real argument — that the
repeated degrees are kept because equal degrees are different characters — is
both correct and decisive, and checkable in one line: dropping the repeats
within each group would give 1115 entries, under the limit. The 255 rows the
exception is actually asking for are exactly those repeats. None of that is on
the page.

"the shared character index" in the same sentence is the other half of the
problem: the index $i$ is not shared between anything, and a reader has to
guess what it means.

The smallest change. Replace the field with the numbers:

> The table holds 1370 entries, above the soft limit of 1200. Keeping the
> repeated degrees is what puts it over: 255 rows repeat a degree already
> listed for the same group, and dropping them would give 1115 entries and
> lose the distinction between a character and its complex or Galois
> conjugate.

## 4. The definition names a table this corpus holds and does not link it

What a reader sees, as the first sentence of the page:

> For a sporadic finite simple group $G$, this table lists the degrees
> $\chi_i(1)$ of the nontrivial irreducible complex characters $\chi_i$ in the
> ordering used by the GAP Character Table Library [2].

Why it is wrong. T77, *Orders of sporadic finite simple groups*, is the table
that says what those 26 groups are and holds one row for each. `Comments` and
`Formulas` both link it — correctly, once per section — and the `Definition`,
which is where a reader meets the phrase first, does not. The skill's rule is
first mention, once per section, and the Definition is a section.

The audit does not catch it because the phrase in the definition is "a
sporadic finite simple group" and the title is "Orders of sporadic finite
simple groups"; matching prose against the bare title is what the audit does,
and this is the case the critique is for.

The smallest change:
`HREF{Orders_of_sporadic_finite_simple_groups}[sporadic finite simple group]`
on the definition's first four words. The slug resolves (200, checked).

## 5. Nineteen of the twenty-six groups cannot be found by name

What a reader sees. `Keywords` names Mathieu, Conway, Fischer and Janko groups,
the Monster and the Baby Monster; `comment-labels` names the O'Nan group.
Nothing on the page contains "Rudvalis", "Lyons", "Held", "Thompson",
"Harada", "Higman-Sims", "McLaughlin" or "Suzuki".

Why it is wrong. The text index weights title and `Keywords` first, `Tags`,
the `Definition`, and the table's `Comments` — and an entry's own comment is in
none of them. So a reader who computed 118784, knows it came from the Rudvalis
group, and types "Rudvalis" gets nothing. I checked what the corpus answers
today:

    Rudvalis        []
    Lyons           []
    McLaughlin      []
    Harada          []
    Thompson group  []
    Monster         []
    Mathieu         []
    sporadic        ['Orders_of_sporadic_finite_simple_groups']

Not one of the group names is answered by anything in the corpus — T77 carries
"Mathieu group", "Janko group" and the rest as *entry comments*, which the
index does not read. ("Held group" and "Higman-Sims" do return rows, from
`Growth_rates_of_hyperbolic_Coxeter_simplex_groups` and three others; that is
the stemmer matching "group" and "sims", not the Held or Higman-Sims group.)
T255 is the first table in a position to answer these queries, and it answers
seven of twenty-six.

The smallest change. Add the missing names to `Keywords`: Rudvalis, Lyons,
Held, Thompson, Harada-Norton, Higman-Sims, McLaughlin, Suzuki, O'Nan. That is
nine words in a list that already has eleven, and it is the same weight as the
title.

## 6. The `chi` constraint is a second copy of the definition, in fragments

What a reader sees, in the Parameters list:

> $i$ —  character index ($i\geq2$; $\chi_i$ is the $i$th irreducible complex
> character in the GAP Character Table Library ordering for $G$.)

Why it is wrong. Two clauses stapled with a semicolon, the second a restatement
of the definition three screens above, and a full stop inside a parenthesis the
site opened. The field holds the constraint the entries satisfy; the constraint
is $i\geq2$, and the reason is that $\chi_1$ is omitted.

The smallest change: `$i\geq2$, the trivial character $\chi_1$ being omitted.`

## 7. "rather than the same character" denies something the reader has to reconstruct

What a reader sees, comment (2):

> Repeated degrees are kept. Equal-degree rows may be complex-conjugate or
> Galois-conjugate characters rather than the same character.

Why it is wrong. Two rows of this table are never the same character — their
indices differ — so the thing being denied is "the same character written
twice", and a reader has to work that out. "Repeated degrees are kept" is also
a note about what the build did rather than a fact about the groups.

The smallest change:

> Two rows may share a degree without being the same character: complex
> conjugates and Galois conjugates have equal degrees, and both are listed.

## 8. The moonshine formula points at a row and links a table where it means one number

What a reader sees, formula (2):

> For the Monster group $M$, the first nontrivial degree is $196883$, and
> $196884=1+196883$ is the coefficient of $q$ in *the $q$-expansion of the
> $j$-invariant*.

Why it is wrong, twice. "the first nontrivial degree" makes a reader go and
find which row that is, when the row has a name: it is $\chi_2(1)$. And the
sentence means one coefficient, not the whole expansion; T79's entries are
keyed by $n$ and its $q^1$ row has id `1` (read off the rendered page), so the
link can land on 196884 itself.

The smallest change:
`$\chi_2(1)=196883$ for the Monster group, and $196884=1+196883$ is
HREF{Q-expansion_of_the_j-invariant#1}[the coefficient of $q$ in the
$q$-expansion of the $j$-invariant]`.

## 9. The Monster is upright in the rows and italic in the formula

What a reader sees. Every row of the Monster prints $\mathrm{M}$ upright, from
the parameter value `$\rm{M}$`. Formula (2) writes "the Monster group $M$",
which MathJax sets as a math-italic $M$ — the same letter in a different font,
three sections apart, for the same group.

Why it is wrong. The table has a symbol for each group and this is the one
place it does not use it. The neighbouring $G$ *is* a variable and is rightly
italic, which makes the clash worse: the page now uses upright and italic M
for the group and italic $G$ for a group, and a reader cannot tell which
distinction is meant.

The smallest change: `$\rm{M}$` in `formula-moonshine`, matching the
parameter value.

Related, and smaller: `comment-labels` writes `$\mathrm{Fi}_{24}'$` where the
other 26 occurrences of a group name on the page use `\rm{...}`. T77 uses
`\rm{...}` too, so `\rm` is this corpus's house spelling and the `\mathrm` is
the odd one. Both typeset identically; this is consistency, not correctness.

## Noted only because I noticed

- **The same fact twice.** `formula-moonshine` and the second `Similar tables`
  relation both say that 196884 differs by 1 from the smallest nontrivial
  Monster degree. `Similar tables` has to name a relation, so it cannot simply
  be cut; but the two sentences could differ.
- **The least durable link.** `CTblLibArchive` is an 18 MB versioned tarball
  on a personal university homepage. It answers 200 today (I downloaded it to
  check finding 2), and it is the right citation for "the entries came from
  version 1.3.11" — but it is the one link here that will rot, and the
  package's own page, already cited as `CTblLib`, will outlive it.
- **A reader landing on a row gets the symbol, not the name.** `$\rm{Ru}$`,
  `36`, `118784` tells somebody the group is called Ru. The name is a click
  away in T77, and putting it on all 1370 rows as an entry comment would be
  exactly what the skill warns against. Finding 5 is the version of this that
  is worth acting on.
- **`ON` as a parameter key is safe here, and only here.** The key is a YAML
  1.1 boolean, so `yaml.safe_load` on
  `generators/sporadic-character-degrees/table.yaml` turns it into `True` and
  the O'Nan group loses its display symbol. Nothing in this project does that:
  the API carries JSON, and `data_pipeline.utils.load_yaml_recursively` and
  `views.preview` both use `yaml.BaseLoader`, which keeps every scalar a
  string. I hit it myself building a preview and it cost a render. It is not a
  fault in the table; it is written up as a lesson proposal.
- **The `rigour details` fold.** The note is 601 characters of prose in a
  single paragraph, so its rendered HTML is well over `prose.py`'s
  `FOLD_ABOVE = 400` with no second block to fold, and it is cut at a sentence
  instead: the provenance shows and
  "The generator checks that..." sits behind *more*. The cut landed where an
  author would have put a paragraph break. Nothing to change.

## How it was read

- **The skill** came from <https://numberdb.org/skill>, fetched this run
  (47,044 bytes). The rules that bite here are "Link the first mention of a
  thing to what explains it", "Name the thing, do not point at it", "Link the
  number, not the table, when the sentence means one number", "A parameter
  says what the family is indexed by, not how much of it you computed", the
  `Parameters` row of the section table, and the paragraph on `Keywords` and
  what somebody holding a number would type.
- **The SOCKS proxy on 127.0.0.1:1080 is still gone** — connection refused,
  curl exit 7, nothing listening — and still not needed: this machine reaches
  numberdb.org directly and every fetch below went out that way. Already
  recorded in `docs/agent-environment.md`.
- **The document** came from the Python client with the zeta3 key
  (`numberdb.table('T255')`, run from `/tmp` so the Django `numberdb` package
  does not shadow the client). Apart from `Numbers` it matches
  `generators/sporadic-character-degrees/table.yaml` exactly.
- **The audit** came from `GET /api/table/T255/audit`:
  `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. There is no Django here, so
  `manage.py audit_table` was not available; that endpoint runs the same
  `findings_for`.
- **Rendering.** The document is 4,829 bytes and `/preview?table=` refuses a
  request line over 4,094 ("Request Line is too large (6029 > 4094)"), so it
  was read in six pieces, each valid YAML with a handful of real entries. All
  six returned 200. A seventh piece carried M11, O'N, Fi24, B and M with their
  first, second and last characters, to read the group labels and the
  27-digit Monster degree as a reader gets them; the full
  258823477531055064045234375 reaches the page intact.
- **Typesetting.** All 53 `$...$` fragments (42 distinct) were converted with
  MathJax 3 under the package set `static/vendor/mathjax/tex-svg.js` actually
  loads — base, ams, newcommand, noundefined, require, autoload, configmacros.
  None produced an `merror`, and none emitted a literal backslash glyph, which
  is how `noundefined` shows an unknown macro; a control, `\frac{1`, failed.
  `\rm{M}_{11}` and `\mathrm{M}_{11}` produce byte-identical SVG, so `\rm` is
  defined here and is not a hazard. `$\rm{Fi}_{24}'$` sets the prime as a
  superscript over the subscript, which is the ATLAS convention; `$\rm{O'N}$`
  gives O′N. No `<` anywhere, no `argument ()`, no `CITE-broken` or
  `HREF-broken` in any of the seven renders.
- **Links.** `Orders_of_sporadic_finite_simple_groups` and
  `Q-expansion_of_the_j-invariant` both answer 200. The two CTblLib URLs
  answer 200. `https://oeis.org/A001379` answers 403 to this machine —
  Cloudflare, not a dead link; `A001379/b001379.txt` serves fine.
- **It is one table, and I agree with the audit for a reason of my own.** `G`
  has 26 values, past the audit's `MOST_LABELS = 4`, so the split check never
  ran. Independently: `G` names *what the number is of* — one character-degree
  list per group, as T233 holds one Ehrhart polynomial per root system — and
  the value column is headed `$\chi_i(1)$`, a symbol true of every row, not the
  word `value` a table of two quantities falls back on. Splitting this into 26
  tables is the "Values of the Bessel functions" mistake inverted.
- **The two cross-table claims hold.** `formula-squares` as written, trivial
  character contributing 1: $1+\sum_{i\geq2}\chi_i(1)^2$ equals T77's stored
  order for all 26 groups, no mismatches. The Monster's 193 stored degrees,
  with 1 prepended, equal all 194 terms of OEIS A001379's b-file.
- **Search is not made worse.** Of 1370 entries, 3 are at most 10 and 57 are at
  most 100 — 4% — so this is not the "handful of small integers that match
  everything" the skill warns about. `search_integer` finds none of 196883,
  21296876, 336033532800, 258823477531055064045234375, 4371 or 118784 anywhere
  in the corpus today, so these rows answer questions nothing else answers.
- **The parameters describe the family, not the run.** All 26 sporadic groups,
  every $i\geq2$, `complete: yes` — there is no afternoon's range hiding in
  either parameter, which is the commonest fault in this slot and is absent.

## What the audit could not have seen

Nothing it reports is wrong, and four of the nine findings above are outside
what any set of rules could check from the document alone:

- finding 1 is in the renderer, not the document;
- finding 2 needed the CTblLib archive downloaded and its `ALN` declarations
  read;
- finding 3 needed the entry count measured against `limits.py` and the
  per-group duplicate count computed;
- finding 5 needed the corpus searched for eight names that are not in it.

Findings 4 and 6 to 9 are the kind a rule could reach — an unlinked first
mention whose phrase differs from the title, a constraint that repeats the
definition, a font used inconsistently for one name — and none of them is
worth a rule that would also fire on tables where the same shape is right.
