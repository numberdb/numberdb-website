# T351 — Values of the upper incomplete gamma function $\Gamma(a,x)$

Read 2026-09-20 as a reader, not as its builder. 700 entries, type `R`,
rigour `proven`, 100 digits on every value, still a private draft.

**What I read.**

* **The document**: `GET /api/table?id=T351` with the zeta3 key, 85,155 bytes.
  Seven parameter values of $a$, one hundred of $x$ under each.
* **The page**: `https://numberdb.org/T351` answers 404 to everybody but a
  logged-in owner, so the page was rebuilt whole by the sqlite draft-render
  recipe in `docs/agent-environment.md` — `/tmp/t351_render.py` (the previous
  run's script with one `sed`), `NUMBERDB_SAGE_MEMORY=1200m
  NUMBERDB_SAGE_PYTHONPATH= agents/sage.sh /tmp/t351_render.py /tmp/site.tgz
  /tmp/T351.json` — giving **700 records, status 200, 405,089 bytes** of HTML.
  Where I say "renders as" below it is off that HTML, at
  `/tmp/T351_page.html`.
* **The audit**: `GET /api/table/T351/audit` with the key. `manage.py
  audit_table` cannot run here — no Django, no database — which is the
  standing note in `docs/agent-environment.md`.
* **The four tables T351 links**, T9, T188, T204 and T209, read through
  `api/table` and `api/lookup`.
* **The generator**, `generators/upper-incomplete-gamma-values/generate.py`.

**What I checked hardest and found right.** The definition contains three
unescaped `>` inside mathematics (`$x>0$` twice, and once in the `x`
parameter's constraint); all three reach the browser as bare `>` in text
content and neither truncates its section, so the fault that ate a section
this year is not here. `CITE{formula-recurrence}` and the two others in
`rigour details` are keys of `Formulas`, not of `Links` — the site resolves
them, and they render as `(2)`, `(4)`, `(5)`, so they are cross-references and
not broken citations. All 700 entry anchors are present and unique, `0,1/10`
through `5/2,10`. The value column is headed `$\Gamma(a,x)$` and not the word
`value` (its two published cousins, T190 and T199, both fall back on `value`).
No `param-latex`, so the parameter columns read `0` and `1/10` rather than the
column header once per row. The four `HREF{}` slugs in `Similar tables` all
answer 200 anonymously. And the entries are enumerated by $|a|$ with the
positive first — `0, 1/2, -1/2, 1, 3/2, 2, 5/2` — which is exactly the order
the skill asks for and which most tables with a negative index get wrong.

It is one table on every test the skill offers: both parameters are numbers
rather than names, both say *what the number is of* rather than *which
quantity is taken*, and the one quantity is $\Gamma(a,x)$.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The $a=0$ row is fifty of T188's published values, written out again, and nothing says so

`Similar tables` says "the row $a=0$ is the exponential integral $E_1(x)$", and
that is literally true of the stored text. T188, *Values of the exponential
integral*, holds $E_1(x)$ at every $x$ of two decimal places with $0<x\leq5$.
T351's $a=0$ row holds $\Gamma(0,x)$ at $x=k/10$, $1\leq k\leq100$. The fifty
arguments they share carry **fifty identical hundred-digit strings** — I
compared all fifty and not a character differs. Searching the first
twenty-five digits of T351's own $a=0$, $x=1/10$ value through `api/lookup`
returns T188's entry `E1,1/10` and nothing else, which is what a reader typing
those digits will get once T351 is public: two tables, in no stated order,
saying the same thing.

That is what `Data properties: repeats` exists for, and this is the good case
for it rather than the interesting one: $\Gamma(0,x)$ and $E_1(x)$ are not two
constructions meeting by a theorem, they are the same integral under two
names, which is why the page can state the relation as an identity of
definitions. `fold_repeats` in `numberdb_app/search.py` folds **per value**,
comparing stored text, so the declaration costs the other 650 entries nothing:
the fifty colliding rows fold into T188 with T351 attached as `also_in`, and
everything else answers as it does now.

Smallest change: add `repeats: HREF{Values_of_the_exponential_integral}` to
`Data properties`. T188 declares no `repeats` of its own, so the one-hop rule
is satisfied.

The honest argument against, which is why this is a person's call and not
mine: `repeats` is a table-level field and only 50 of 700 entries are
repetitions, so a reader of `Data properties` may read it as a claim about the
whole table. If that is judged too strong, the fallback is a sentence in the
relation saying where the two meet, because the relation as written implies
more than the overlap: **T351's $a=0$ row runs to $x=10$ and T188 stops at
$x=5$**, so half of that row is new and half is a copy, and neither page says
which half. Either way the overlap is a fact about the corpus that ought to be
written down before this is published, since it is much cheaper to declare now
than to retrofit onto a public table.

### 2. "every half-integer parameter $a$" does not say which seven values are here

The completeness note renders as

> Table is complete: no (it holds every half-integer parameter $a$ with
> $-1/2\leq a\leq5/2$ and every argument $x=k/10$ with $1\leq k\leq100$)

and this is the one sentence a reader checks their own number against. "Half
-integer" is read both ways in ordinary use: as $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$, which is
what the table means and which gives seven rows, and as an odd multiple of
$\tfrac12$, which gives four and excludes $a=0,1,2$ — that is, excludes the
exponential-integral row and both elementary rows. A reader holding
$\Gamma(2,x)$ and reading this sentence concludes the table does not have it.

The corpus has already met this and disambiguates by writing the set out.
T190's note says "at every half-integer order $\nu=0,\tfrac12,1,\ldots,15$",
and T199's says "$\nu\in\{0,1/3,2/3\}\cup\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$".

Smallest change: "it holds $\Gamma(a,x)$ at every
$a\in\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}$ with $-1/2\leq a\leq5/2$, that is at
$a=-1/2,0,1/2,1,3/2,2,5/2$, and at every argument $x=k/10$ with
$1\leq k\leq100$". Worth doing.

### 3. Formula (1) uses $\gamma(a,x)$ and nothing on the page says what it is

Formula (1) renders as

> If $a\ne0,-1,-2,\ldots$, then $\gamma(a,x)+\Gamma(a,x)=\Gamma(a)$ [1].

$\gamma(a,x)$ appears here and nowhere else on the page. The `Similar tables`
line for T9 names "the lower and upper incomplete gamma functions" in words
but never attaches the symbol to the words, and the corpus holds no table of
the lower incomplete gamma function to link, so the definition has to be in
this table — "Define notation where you use it" is the skill's rule and this is
the plainest breach of it here.

The collision makes it worse rather than merely untidy. $\gamma$ is the
Euler–Mascheroni constant three lines from here on the very table T351 links
for its $a=0$ row: T188's formula (2) reads
"$\operatorname{Ei}(x)=\gamma+\log x+\sum_{k\geq1}x^k/(k\,k!)$, where $\gamma$
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant". A reader moving between the two pages meets
one letter as a constant on one and as a two-argument function on the other,
with neither page acknowledging the other's use.

Smallest change: finish formula (1) with the definition, and mind that the
integral form does not cover the whole range the formula claims —
$\int_0^x t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t$ diverges at $a=-1/2$, which is a row this
table holds. Something like: "where $\gamma(a,x)$ is the lower incomplete
gamma function, equal to $\int_0^x t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t$ for $a>0$ and
continued analytically in $a$ otherwise". Worth doing.

### 4. "the principal real values" raises a doubt the definition has already settled, in T188's words for a different thing

The definition renders as

> For real $x>0$, the upper incomplete gamma function is
> $\Gamma(a,x)=\int_x^\infty t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t$ [1]. This table gives
> the principal real values of $\Gamma(a,x)$ at rational parameters $a$ and
> positive rational arguments $x$.

and comment (7) then spends a sentence taking it back: "For $x>0$ and real $a$
the defining integral is real, and the principal value agrees with that
integral." For rational $a$ and rational $x>0$ — everything this table holds —
the integral in the first sentence is a single real number and there is nothing
to choose, so "principal" names a distinction that cannot arise on the domain
the same sentence states. A reader who does not already know that the
multivalued $\Gamma(a,z)$ has a cut in $z$ reads it as a warning and goes
looking for what else there might be.

And "principal value" is the phrase T188's own definition uses for the Cauchy
principal value of a divergent integral: "the exponential integral is the
principal value $\operatorname{Ei}(x)=\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^x
e^t\,\mathrm{d}t/t$". T188 is the table T351 sends its $a=0$ readers to.

Smallest change: drop the word from the definition — "This table gives
$\Gamma(a,x)$ at rational parameters $a$ and positive rational arguments $x$" —
and let comment (7) carry the branch, saying what it is about: that $x>0$ keeps
the argument off the cut of the multivalued $\Gamma(a,z)$, so these are the
values of its principal branch. Worth doing; it is one word out of the
definition and one clause into a comment.

### 5. The completeness note says what is here and not why, and the word that explains it is a Keyword that appears nowhere on the page

The skill asks the completeness note to carry the argument for the range,
because that is the sentence a reader checking whether their own number
belongs here actually reads. This one is an inventory: a set of $a$ and a set
of $x$, with nothing saying why those.

There is an argument available, and the document half-states it in a place no
reader sees. `Keywords` carries "chi-squared distribution", and the word
**chi-squared occurs nowhere in the rendered page** — not in the definition,
not in a comment, not in a formula, not in a link title. The connection is
real: $Q(a,x)=\Gamma(a,x)/\Gamma(a)$ is the survival function of the
chi-squared distribution with $2a$ degrees of freedom at $2x$, so the rows
$a=1/2,1,3/2,2,5/2$ are exactly one to five degrees of freedom — which is a
reason for that grid and not another. Keywords weigh the same as the title in
the text index, so this word is what brings a statistician to the page, and
what they find is a table whose only sentence about the regularized function
says it is not stored here.

Smallest change: one clause in comment (8), which already introduces $Q(a,x)$,
naming the distribution and the degrees of freedom; and a "why" in the
completeness note, which then has one to point at. It also answers the row
nothing on the page accounts for: $a=-1/2$ is the only negative parameter, it
is what makes the recurrence testable downwards and the "any rational $a$"
parameter non-vacuous, and it is not a number of degrees of freedom. Worth
doing — two clauses, and they retire both halves of this finding.

### 6. `proven` at a hundred digits, with the guard stated and nothing measured behind it

`rigour details` renders as

> How they were obtained: Values are computed as real parts of Sage complex
> balls with arb at `numberdb.bits(digits, losing=64)` bits from exact rational
> parameters and arguments. The imaginary part is required to contain zero.
> *(folded)* The entries are checked against (2), against the exponential
> integral table on the $a=0$ row, against the erfc identity in (4), and
> against the elementary integer formula (5).

Two small things, in a field where the whole claim of the table lives.

The guard is a number with no measurement attached. The skill asks for the
guard "as a constant with the measurement behind it: how many digits the worst
entry actually retained", and T188 — the same shape of table, the same arb, the
same 64-bit guard — closes its note with "The widest returned ball had radius
less than $10^{-117}$ for 100 requested digits." That sentence is what makes
`proven` at a hundred digits checkable by a reader instead of asserted. The
generator does not measure it either, so this needs a run rather than an edit:
the worst radius over the 700 entries, which `RealBall.rad()` gives.

And "checked against (2)" names nothing. The three items beside it say what
they are — "the erfc identity in (4)", "the elementary integer formula (5)" —
so the first reads as the one the author could not be bothered to name, and a
reader has to scroll seven hundred rows up to find out that (2) is the
recurrence. Write "checked against the recurrence CITE{formula-recurrence}".

Worth doing, the second part trivially.

### 7. Tricomi's confluent hypergeometric function is named in Formulas and linked only in Similar tables

Formula (6) renders as "$\Gamma(a,x)=x^ae^{-x}U(1,a+1,x)$, where $U$ is
Tricomi's confluent hypergeometric function [4]", pointing at DLMF 13.6. The
corpus holds that family: T209, *Values of Tricomi's confluent hypergeometric
function $U(a,b,z)$*, at `Values_of_Tricomi's_confluent_hypergeometric_function`,
and T351 already links it from `Similar tables`. The skill's rule is first
mention, once per section, and `Formulas` is a section — T188 does exactly this,
linking `HREF{Stieltjes_constants#0}[the Euler-Mascheroni constant]` from
inside a formula.

Smallest change:
`HREF{Values_of_Tricomi's_confluent_hypergeometric_function}[Tricomi's
confluent hypergeometric function]` in formula (6), keeping the DLMF citation
for the identity. Worth doing while the table is open; it is one link.

One thing to watch when writing it: in $U(1,a+1,x)$ the letter $a$ is *this*
table's parameter while T209's own first parameter is the 1, so a reader
following the link with $a=3/2$ in hand needs T209's row $a=1,b=5/2$. The
relation sentence in `Similar tables` repeats the formula and inherits the
same trap. Not worth its own finding, but a clause naming which is which would
earn its place.

## Noted only because I noticed

* **The parameter constraints restate the types.** The parameters render as
  "$a$ — parameter ($a\in\mathbb{Q}$)" and "$x$ — argument ($x\in\mathbb{Q}$
  and $x>0$)". The first says the same thing three times: the group heading
  gives the symbol, the title gives DLMF's word for it, and the constraint
  repeats the declared type `Q`. It is not wrong — and the division the skill
  cares about is right here, the family in `Parameters` and the run in
  `complete-note` — so this is only an empty slot, not a false one. If it were
  filled, the fact worth putting there is that $a$ may be zero or negative,
  where the integral still converges because $x>0$.
* **Comments (7) and (8) both say the regularized function is not stored**,
  once as "The table stores the unregularized upper incomplete gamma function"
  and once as "The regularized value is not stored here". One of the two is
  enough, and (8) is the one that also defines $Q$.
* **Two hundred of the seven hundred entries are elementary** — $\Gamma(1,x)
  =e^{-x}$ and $\Gamma(2,x)=(1+x)e^{-x}$ — and formula (5) says so for every
  row at once, which is the right place for it. Worth knowing that the sibling
  T209 went the other way and excluded elementary entries by policy ("it holds
  every non-elementary entry with …"). I think T351's choice is the better one
  here: these two rows are the Erlang and Poisson tails and a reader does
  arrive holding $e^{-3/2}$, and no table of the exponential function answers
  a corpus lookup, so they are findable nowhere else. It is a choice the range
  sentence could say out loud, along with finding 5.
* **The $a=1/2$ row meets T204 at three arguments.** The relation
  "$\Gamma(1/2,x)=\sqrt{\pi}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{x})$" is exact
  mathematics, but T204 holds $\operatorname{erfc}$ at two decimal places, so a
  reader can pair rows only where $\sqrt{x}$ has two decimals — $x=1,4,9$ of
  the hundred. Nothing is wrong on either page; it is simply a smaller bridge
  than the sentence suggests, and unlike finding 1 no digits coincide, since
  the $\sqrt\pi$ makes them different numbers.
* **No entry carries a comment**, and a reader who lands on an anchor deep in
  the $a=0$ block has nothing beside their row saying it is $E_1(x)$; the page
  draws 700 rows before the definition. I am not recommending a fix, because
  the fix is a comment on a hundred rows repeating what formula (3) says once,
  which the skill warns against by name.

## Nothing new for the skill or the environment from the reading itself

`docs/agent-environment.md`: the draft page that answers 404 to a bearer
token, the sqlite draft-render recipe (this is a two-parameter real table, 700
records, and it needed the tid changed and nothing else), and the anonymous

when the skill tells them to search for the family and explicitly not for the
values.
