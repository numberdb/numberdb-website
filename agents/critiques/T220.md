The table reads well in its bones. Each row names its group in the
convention a reader is likely to hold, $\operatorname{PSL}_{n+1}(q)$,
$\operatorname{PSU}_{n+1}(q)$, $\operatorname{PSp}_{2n}(q)$ or
$\operatorname{P\Omega}^\pm_{2n}(q)$, and that is what someone arriving from a
calculation needs. The exclusions are listed, the B/C same-order rows say so
on both sides, and the `complete-note` gives the actual range. The faults are
in the details a reader meets first: one row label that will not typeset, two
rows that say less than their twins, a notation clash, and a page about orders
with no order formula on it.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Tits group's row label is unclosed TeX (inferred)

What a reader sees: in the family column of the last row, the raw text
`${}^2F_4(2)':` with the dollar sign still showing, where every other row
shows a typeset label.

Why: `Parameters.family.values.Tits` is `"${}^2F_4(2)'"`, with no closing
`$`. The renderer puts each row's family label in its own
`<div class="table-param-group">`, and MathJax does not pair a `$` across
elements, so that `$` is left as text. The entry comment, `the Tits group
${}^2F_4(2)'$.`, is closed and typesets correctly. The Parameters list does not
show this value, so the fault is only in the row. This is the first thing to
confirm when a render is possible.

Smallest change: `Tits: "${}^2F_4(2)'$"`. Worth doing before the draft is
offered; it costs one character.

### 2. The rows for 20160 and 168 tell a reader less than their twins

What a reader sees, holding $20160$: two rows.
- $A_2(4)$ says only "$\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(4)$."
- $A_3(2)$ says "$\operatorname{PSL}_{4}(2)$. This group is isomorphic to $A_8$."

Nothing says that these two groups are *not* isomorphic, and a reader
comparing the rows can easily conclude that $\operatorname{PSL}_3(4)\cong A_8$
as well. This is the famous exception: the smallest pair of non-isomorphic
simple groups with the same order.

Holding $168$, the reader again gets two rows. $A_1(7)$ says "isomorphic to
$A_2(2)$", and $A_2(2)$ says nothing back. The other coincidences are stated on
both sides: $A_1(4)$ and $A_1(5)$, $B_2(3)$ and ${}^2A_3(2^2)$, and every
$B_n(q)$/$C_n(q)$ pair.

I counted repeated values in the stored rows: 87 values occur more than once.
All but four are $B_n(q)$/$C_n(q)$ pairs. The four are 60, 168, 20160 and
25920, exactly the cases above.

That result is itself the fact a reader holding a number wants: two rows share
an order only when the groups are isomorphic, except $A_2(4)$ and $A_3(2)$,
and $B_n(q)$ and $C_n(q)$ for odd $q$ and $n\geq3$. This is Artin's theorem on
the orders of the classical simple groups (1955), extended to all finite
simple groups by Kimmerle, Lyons, Sandling and Teague (1990). Check the
citation before using it.

Smallest change:
- On $A_2(4)$ (generator `comment`, beside the `n == 3 and q == 2` case):
  "It has the same order as $A_3(2)$ and is not isomorphic to it."
- On $A_3(2)$, add "It has the same order as $A_2(4)$ and is not isomorphic
  to it."
- On $A_2(2)$: "This group is isomorphic to $A_1(7)$."
- Better still, one Comment stating the theorem, with a Reference to cite.

Worth doing. It is the reason somebody holding 20160 came.

### 3. A table of orders has no order formula, and two fields refer to one

What a reader sees: Definition, Parameters and Comments, then 893 integers.
There is no `Formulas` section. Yet `comment-notation` says "the parameter $q$
is the one in the order formula", and `rigour details` says "computed from the
order formulas". Neither formula appears anywhere on the page. A reader who
wants to check a row, or extend to $q=37$, has to go to Wikipedia. The phrase
"the one in the order formula" does not define $q$ at all.

Why it matters: the order formula is the closed form of every value here, and
`Formulas` is where the skill puts closed forms. The generator already holds
all seventeen, in `order()`, lines 91 to 140. The twisted formulas also settle
the $q$-versus-$q^2$ convention, which `comment-notation` currently explains
only for ${}^2A_n$.

Smallest change: one formula per family, transcribed from `order()`. A few
examples:

- $|A_n(q)|=\frac{1}{(n+1,q-1)}\,q^{n(n+1)/2}\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}(q^i-1)$
- $|{}^2A_n(q^2)|=\frac{1}{(n+1,q+1)}\,q^{n(n+1)/2}\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}(q^i-(-1)^i)$
- $|{}^2B_2(q)|=q^2(q^2+1)(q-1)$

With those in place, `comment-notation` can say "$q$ is the $q$ of formula
(n)" by `CITE` label. Worth doing. It is the largest change here and the one
that most improves the page.

### 4. $A_5$, $A_6$, $A_8$ and $|A_n|$ mean two different things on this page

What a reader sees:
- "$\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(4)$. This group is isomorphic to $A_5$."
- "$\operatorname{PSL}_{4}(2)$. This group is isomorphic to $A_8$."
- In Similar tables: "the order $|A_n|=n!/2$ of an alternating group".

The family column of the same table calls $A_n(q)$ "$A_n$". A reader who has
just read $A_2(2)$ in a neighbouring comment has good reason to read $A_5$ as
the Lie-type family of rank 5, which it is not.

Smallest change:
- Name the group in words at each of the four places: "isomorphic to the
  alternating group $\mathrm{Alt}_5$".
- In the Factorial relation, write $|\mathrm{Alt}_n|=n!/2$.

Generator lines 232, 236 and 238 cover the comments. Worth doing; it is cheap.

### 5. Entry labels that do not typeset as the family does

What a reader sees:

- **Two-digit subscripts.** Eight comments render $B_10(2)$ as $B_1$
  followed by a plain 0, which reads as "$B_1\,0(2)$". The same happens
  for $C_10(2)$, $B_11$, $C_11$, $B_12$ and $C_12$. The affected rows are
  $B_{10}(2)$, $B_{10}(3)$, $B_{11}(2)$, $B_{12}(2)$ and the four matching
  $C$ rows. Why: `"$%s_%d(%d)$"` writes the subscript without braces, and
  TeX subscripts one token (generator lines 226, 245 and 248). The fix
  is `_{%d}`, as the PSL/PSU/PSp names already do.
- **No subscript at all.** All 72 rows of $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, $F_4$ and
  $G_2$ have comments "$E6(2)$.", "$F4(3)$.", "$G2(5)$." with no
  subscript, directly under a family label that reads $E_6(q)$. Why:
  `"$%s(%d)$" % (family, q)` uses the key where it meant the display
  (line 215).

Smallest change: both are one-line fixes in `group_name` and `comment`,
followed by a refill. Worth doing.

Relatedly, and only noted: for the families with no second name ($B$ and the
exceptional and twisted families), the comment repeats the row label and adds
nothing. Dropping it there would also be fine.

### 6. The parameter list renders `family` as a product of variables, and does not say which ranks exist

What a reader sees (inferred): the first parameter line, as math italic
$family$ followed by "— family". The line is built as `"$%s$" % key` when a
parameter has no `display`. The column header shows the same $family$.

The `n` line reads "$n$ is the rank of the untwisted Dynkin diagram; for
fixed-rank families it is the displayed rank". It does not say that $n\geq1$
for $A$, $n\geq2$ for $B$ and ${}^2A$, $n\geq3$ for $C$, and $n\geq4$ for $D$
and ${}^2D$. Those bounds are why there is no $C_2(q)$ or $D_3(q)$ row, and
they are the family, not the run. A reader looking for $\operatorname{PSp}_4(5)$
under $C$ finds nothing, and nothing on the page sends them to $B_2(5)$.

Smallest change:
- Give `family` a `display` (plain `family` without dollars reads fine) and
  a title that says something, such as "Lie type".
- Rewrite the `n` constraint as "$n\geq1$ for $A_n$, $n\geq2$ for $B_n$ and
  ${}^2A_n$, $n\geq3$ for $C_n$, $n\geq4$ for $D_n$ and ${}^2D_n$, so that
  $C_2=B_2$ and $D_3=A_3$ are not repeated; for the other families $n$ is
  the subscript of the name".

Worth doing.

### 7. The definition does not define

What a reader sees: "This table lists the orders of finite simple groups of
Lie type, with the simple quotients and the Tits group taken in the notation
of [1]."

Why: "the simple quotients" leaves the reader asking: quotients of what, and by
what? "Taken in the notation of" defers the definition to Wikipedia. Two
people could still build different tables: one with $\mathrm{SL}_n(q)$, or
the adjoint group $\mathrm{PGL}_n(q)$, the other with $\mathrm{PSL}_n(q)$. Only
the entry comments show which was meant.

Smallest change, one sentence: "The order of each finite simple group of Lie
type: the quotient by its centre of the group of fixed points of a Steinberg
endomorphism of a simply connected simple algebraic group over
$\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, together with the Tits group ${}^2F_4(2)'$, named as
in CITE{Wiki}." Worth doing; the person with the literature at hand should
check the wording.

### 8. The two Comments say less than they could

- `comment-notation`, second sentence: "The table keeps isomorphic names as
  separate rows when the classification lists both names, because a reader
  may arrive with either name." "When the classification lists both" does not
  tell a reader which rows these are. "Because a reader may arrive with
  either name" is a remark about the website. Smallest change: list them.
  "$A_1(4)\cong A_1(5)$, $A_1(7)\cong A_2(2)$, $B_2(3)\cong{}^2A_3(2^2)$,
  and $B_n(q)\cong C_n(q)$ for even $q$ are each given as two rows."
- `comment-exclusions` omits $B_2(2)$, $G_2(2)$ and ${}^2G_2(3)$ and says only
  that they are not simple. Their derived subgroups are simple and already
  here: $B_2(2)'\cong A_1(9)$, $G_2(2)'\cong{}^2A_2(3^2)$ and
  ${}^2G_2(3)'\cong A_1(8)$. A reader looking for $G_2(2)'$ is not told where
  it is. Adding one sentence fixes that.

Worth doing, both cheap.

### 9. The `complete-note` gives a family an order

What a reader sees: "complete: no (it holds every listed Lie-type family with
$q\leq32$ and order below $10^{100}$, the Tits group, and every $A_1(q)$ with
prime-power $q\leq1000$)".

A family has no order; a group does. "Listed" is unclear: listed where? The
note also does not say why $A_1(q)$ runs to 1000 while everything else stops
at 32. The skill asks for that sentence, and the reason is presumably that
$\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)$ is the group people meet.

Smallest change: "it holds every group of the families above with $q\leq32$
and order below $10^{100}$, every $A_1(q)=\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)$ with
$q\leq1000$, and the Tits group". Add the reason for 1000 if there is one.
Cheap, and worth doing along with the rest.

## Noted only

- **Row labels.** They show the symbolic family display, so a row reads
  "$A_n(q)$: 1: 4: 60", and the reader substitutes. The entry comment names
  the group right under it, so this is tolerable. Labels without arguments
  ($A_n$, ${}^2A_n$, …) would read more cleanly, but only once formula 3
  carries the $q^2$ convention.
- **Keywords** carry PSL, PSU and PSp but not "projective special linear
  group", "unitary group", "symplectic group", "orthogonal group" or "PΩ".
  The group names live only in entry comments, which the index does not read.
- **Row order.** The 191 rows of $A_1(q)$ come first, so a reader looking for
  $B$ or $E_8$ scrolls past them. The order follows the family list, which is
  defensible.
- **The T77 relation** reads "lists the 26 sporadic finite simple groups, the
  other non-cyclic finite simple groups outside the alternating families and
  the Lie-type families". There is one alternating family, and the appositive
  is hard to parse. Better: "lists the 26 sporadic groups, which with the
  cyclic groups of prime order, the alternating groups and the groups of Lie
  type make up the finite simple groups". Some authors count the Tits group as
  a 27th sporadic group, and one clause would say that it is here and not
  there.
- **The Factorial relation** is related-not-equal, and says so. It is fine
  once finding 4 fixes its notation.
- **There is no `Programs` entry.** `PSL(3,4).order()` in Sage, or `Size(PSL(3,4))`
  in GAP, is what a reader wanting one more row would type.

## Checked and fine

Both `HREF` targets resolve to public tables with the titles the captions give.
Both Links answer. The entry comments contain no numbers. There is no `--`
anywhere in the prose. The B/C comments are symmetric and correct about
isomorphism for even $q$ and equal order for odd $q$. `rigour details` says
how the numbers were made and nothing else.
