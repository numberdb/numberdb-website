## Worth doing

### 1. The range is set by what the site can store, and the note gives only the symptom

The completeness note renders inside the site's own sentence:

> Table is complete: no (it holds every entry with $1\leq n\leq 7$, and
> $\mathrm{Td}_7$ is the last entry whose polynomial uses at most six
> variables)

Both halves are true. The second half is the argument for the range, and a
reader cannot use it, because nothing on the page says why six variables
matters. It reads as a curiosity about the Todd polynomials when it is in fact
a statement about this database: `MAX_VARIABLES_FOR_CANONICAL_FORM = 6` in
`utils/numbers/polynomial.py`, the guard on the renaming-invariant search key,
which `Number.save` computes for every polynomial entry. A seventh variable
raises `ParseError('too many variables for a renaming-invariant key')`, so
$\mathrm{Td}_8$, which needs eight, cannot be stored here at all. I confirmed
the refusal on the vendored parser locally; I did not attempt a live
submission, since that would mean writing to a table.

That matters to two different readers. Somebody holding $\mathrm{Td}_8$ wants
to know that it is absent and will stay absent, not that it has too many
variables for a reason they are left to guess. And the next builder, reading
"complete: no", will reasonably try to extend the table and find the site
refuses the row.

Smallest fix, one clause in `complete-note`:

> it holds every entry with $1\leq n\leq 7$; $\mathrm{Td}_8$ is the first
> component needing more than six variables, which is the most a stored
> polynomial may have

Say "more than six" rather than "at most six": the count goes from six
straight to eight, and no Todd component has seven variables at all.

### 2. Comment (7) ends in a remark about the website, where the mathematics was one clause away

It renders as

> For odd $n>1$, the coefficient of $c_n$ vanishes. Thus $\mathrm{Td}_7$ has
> no $c_7$ term and still fits in the six-variable polynomial search.

The first sentence is a fact about the Todd polynomials and a good one. The
second is about this site's search, and the skill is explicit that how well a
value will be found is a remark about the website rather than about the
mathematics. It also duplicates the range argument, which finding 1 puts in
`complete-note` where it belongs, and it leaves the interesting half
unexplained: *why* does the coefficient vanish?

Smallest fix, replacing the second sentence with the reason:

> The coefficient of $c_n$ in $\mathrm{Td}_n$ is $B_n^+/n!$, which vanishes
> for odd $n>1$; $\mathrm{Td}_7$ therefore has no $c_7$ term.

That is checkable, it is true for every $n\leq11$ I computed, and it uses the
$B_i^+$ the table has already defined in Formula (2), so the comment now
points back into the table instead of out at the search box.

### 3. The fold hides both outside checks, and leaves the visible half circular

`rigour details` renders as one paragraph and a disclosure. What a reader sees
with the page closed is

> The generator computes exact rational coefficients in
> $\mathbb Q[c_1,\dots,c_6]$ from Formula (1).

and behind **more** is

> It is checked against the displayed terms in [1] and [2], and by
> substituting the Chern classes of $T\mathbb P^m$ for $1\leq m\leq7$, which
> gives Todd genus $1$ for each complex projective space [2].

The visible sentence says the table was computed from the formula the table
itself states, which is not evidence of anything; the hidden sentence is the
whole of the case, two outside sources and an independent identity. This is
the fault the T332 critique reported as its finding 5 and the T331 critique
before it, so the batch is now three for three.

The measurement, for whoever fixes it: `FOLD_ABOVE = 400` in
`numberdb_app/prose.py` counts *rendered* characters, the note is 390
characters as written and **486** as rendered, and the four `CITE{}` markers
account for about 180 of those because each becomes a 45-character anchor to
show `(1)` or `[1]`. Eighty-six characters over, so either order or trimming
will clear it; leading with the check is the better of the two.

Smallest fix:

> Every component was checked against the terms printed in CITE{ToddWiki} and
> CITE{GenusWiki}, and by substituting the Chern classes of $T\mathbb P^m$ for
> $1\leq m\leq7$, which gives Todd genus $1$ CITE{GenusWiki}. The generator
> computes exact rational coefficients from Formula CITE{formula-component}.

The ring is the one phrase worth dropping: a reader can see the coefficients
are rational in the column.

### 4. Comment (6) reserves a notation in tables that do not exist, and will not name the sources that do use it

It renders as

> The variables are Chern classes. Some sources write the elementary symmetric
> functions of the Chern roots as $p_i$; this table writes them as $c_i$,
> reserving $p_i$ for Pontryagin classes in the characteristic-class tables.

Two problems, both in the second half.

"The characteristic-class tables" points instead of naming, and what it points
at is not there: `search_text('Pontryagin')` returns nothing, and no draft in
this batch holds Pontryagin classes either — T332 is the Chern classes of
complete intersections and T333 their Euler characteristics. A reader is told
about a convention across a set of tables they cannot inspect, and the promise
is one a reviewer cannot check.

"Some sources" is vague where naming costs nothing, and the sources in question
are the table's own two links. Wikipedia's Todd class article defines the
polynomials as $\operatorname{td}_j(p_1,\dots,p_j)$ "in the elementary
symmetric functions $p$ of the $\beta_i$s", and the genus article writes
$K(p_1,p_2,p_3,\dots)$ with the parenthesis "(The variables $p_k$ will often
in practice be Pontryagin classes.)"

Smallest fix:

> The variables are Chern classes. Both sources cited here write the
> elementary symmetric functions of the Chern roots as $p_i$
> CITE{ToddWiki} CITE{GenusWiki}, because those variables are Pontryagin
> classes as often as Chern classes; this table writes them as $c_i$.

### 5. There is no `Programs` block, and both siblings in the batch have one

T332 and T333 each carry a `program-sage` of about fifteen lines, which is the
standard incantation for a reader who wants one more value. T334 has none, so a
reader who wants $\mathrm{Td}_8$, or who wants to reproduce $\mathrm{Td}_4$
before trusting the row, has only the attached `generate.py`: 380 lines,
written to fill this table rather than to be read, and pinned at
`MAX_DEGREE = 7` behind an assertion that no $c_7$ term survives.

The skill asks for both where both apply, and both apply here: the whole
computation is a truncated product of power series, which is the same shape as
the two snippets the batch already ships. Smallest fix is a snippet in that
shape — `PolynomialRing(QQ, ['c1',...,'c6'])`, the $Q$ coefficients
$B_i^+/i!$, power sums by Newton, `exp` truncated by weighted degree — and it
has the side benefit of showing a reader how to go past the range this table
stores even though the database will not hold it.

### 6. $\mathrm{Td}_0=1$ is stated twice, in two sections

Formula (3) is

> $\mathrm{Td}_0=1$.

and comment (5) is

> The constant component is $\mathrm{Td}_0=1$ and is stated in Formula (3),
> rather than stored as a row.

One fact, two blocks, and the comment sends a reader four lines up the page to
read a formula they have just been told the content of. Excluding the row is
right — a stored value of `1` would match every constant polynomial anybody
ever searched for — and the skill puts "what the values mean at a special
point" in `Comments`, not in `Formulas`.

Smallest fix: delete `formula-zero` and let the comment stand alone.

> The constant component $\mathrm{Td}_0=1$ is not stored as a row; the table
> begins at $n=1$.

## Smaller, still worth a minute

### 7. Nothing on the page says the $x_i$ are the Chern roots

The Definition is about "the Todd class of a complex vector bundle", and
Formula (1) is

> $1+\sum_{n\geq1}\mathrm{Td}_n(c_1,c_2,\dots)=\prod_i Q(x_i)$, where
> $c_j=e_j(x_1,x_2,\dots)$.

The mathematics is pinned down: the $x_i$ are indeterminates, the $c_j$ are
their elementary symmetric functions, and the degree-$n$ part is the entry, so
two people would build the same table from this and both would be right. What
is missing is the bridge back to the bundle the Definition mentions — the
$x_i$ are its Chern roots, and the splitting principle is why a product over
them computes a characteristic class. Both cited sources name them ("If $E$
has the $\alpha_i$ as its Chern roots, then $\operatorname{td}(E)=\prod
Q(\alpha_i)$"), and the attached generator names them in its first paragraph.
Only the page does not.

Smallest fix, three words in Formula (1): "where the $x_i$ are the Chern roots
and $c_j=e_j(x_1,x_2,\dots)$".

### 8. The table writes $\mathrm{Td}$ and both of its sources write $\operatorname{td}$

Wikipedia's Todd class article writes the class $\operatorname{td}(E)$ and its
components $\operatorname{td}_j$; the genus article writes $K_j$. A reader who
arrives from either, holding what they call $\operatorname{td}_4$, has to
notice that $\mathrm{Td}_4$ is the same object. $\mathrm{Td}$ is a common
notation and I would not change the title over it, but comment (6) is already
the notation comment and one clause there would close the gap: "Wikipedia
writes the class $\operatorname{td}$ and its components $\operatorname{td}_j$."
A `Keywords` entry spelling "td" would do the same work for search.

## Noted only, because I noticed

### 10. Seven entries is the right size, and the order is right

A reader might expect a table to be bigger. Seven is what the family has
inside the cap, they share an index, and the skill's rule is one table however
few. They are enumerated $n=1$ to $7$, so the small cases a reader wants are
at the top, and there are no entry comments, correctly: one formula covers
every row and a per-row note would only repeat it.

### 11. What the audit cannot see

Findings 1 to 8 are all outside its rules: it has nothing that reads the
