## What reads well

It is one table: a single integer argument, the column headed
$\alpha_s(k)$, and no `param-latex`. The parameter describes the family
($k\geq3$), and the range sits in `complete-note` as a clause that finishes
"complete: no (...)". Somebody holding 4.2667 finds $k=3$ at the top of the
first screen, with its source and equation number. `comment-source-notation`
deals honestly with a real trap: the primary source uses $\alpha_s$ for a
different quantity. `rigour details` says what the $k=3$ radius is, says
that the other rows carry only last-digit intervals, and says that the
asymptotic comparison is not an error bound. `heuristic` is the right level.

The audit's clean result is right as far as its rules go. Both findings worth
doing are about what the prose means, which no rule checks.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The definition never says what a satisfiability threshold is (worth doing)

A reader sees:

> For $k\geq3$, random $k$-SAT [1] is the random $k$-CNF model at clause
> density $\alpha=m/n$. The number $\alpha_s(k)$ is the one-step
> replica-symmetry-breaking cavity-method prediction for its satisfiability
> threshold MMZ.

The first sentence says that random $k$-SAT is "the random $k$-CNF model",
which only renames it. The model itself is described two sections further
down, in `comment-conventions` (distinct variables in a clause, each literal
negated with probability $\frac12$). Nothing on the page defines the
threshold: the density at which the probability of satisfiability falls from
$1$ to $0$ as $n\to\infty$. Nothing says whether clauses are drawn
independently, or whether $m=\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor$. A reader who does not
already know the subject cannot tell from this page what the number measures.
T272, built in the same batch, does define its threshold ("the density such
that, as $n\to\infty$, satisfiability has limiting probability $1$ for
$\alpha<\alpha_k$ and $0$ for $\alpha>\alpha_k$"), which makes the gap here
more visible.

Smallest fix: rewrite the Definition as two sentences, one for the object and
one for what is stored. For example: "A random $k$-CNF formula on $n$
variables has $m=\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor$ clauses drawn independently, each
on $k$ distinct variables chosen uniformly, with each literal negated
independently with probability $\frac12$. Its satisfiability threshold is
the density at which, as $n\to\infty$, the probability of satisfiability
falls from $1$ to $0$, and $\alpha_s(k)$ is the one-step
replica-symmetry-breaking cavity-method prediction for it CITE{MMZ}." Then
`comment-conventions` keeps only the average literal degree $k\alpha$, or
can be dropped. Check the "drawn independently" and floor conventions
against MMZ before writing them in.

### 2. The relation to T273 hides a factor of two (worth doing)

`Similar tables` reads:

> core thresholds of random $k$-uniform hypergraphs — the $2$-core
> thresholds give the pure-literal obstruction thresholds for random $k$-SAT
> after the density convention is adjusted

"After the density convention is adjusted" leaves the reader to work out
how. T273 already states the answer in `comment-peeling`: Molloy's pure
literal threshold is clause density $2c_{k,2}$. A reader who is not told
this will compare $c_{3,2}\approx0.818$ with the known 1.636 and conclude
that one of the tables is wrong. "Pure-literal obstruction threshold" is
also not a term anyone uses; the literature says the threshold for the pure
literal rule.

Smallest fix: "twice the $2$-core threshold $c_{k,2}$ is the clause density
at which the pure literal rule stops solving random $k$-SAT CITE{...}". If
the relation should not carry a citation, drop the `CITE`: the sentence is
still the one to write.

### 3. Names are spelled without their accents (worth doing, cheap)

"Mezard" and "Zdeborova" appear in the prose of three comments, in four entry
comments and in both bib entries. The names are Mézard and Zdeborová, and
T273 in the same batch writes Erdős–Rényi with its accents. Search has no
`unaccent`, but these are names in running text and in a citation, not
search terms. Fix: restore the accents everywhere. If the unaccented forms
are wanted for search, put them in `Keywords`.

### 4. The entry comments repeat `rigour details` (noting)

Four of the five entry comments say only "Mertens, Mezard and Zecchina print
$x$ [1]; Krzakala, … round it to $y$ [2]". The row already shows $x$.
`rigour details` already says the values come from MMZ Table 1 and were
compared with KMRTSZ's rounded values. The skill says a per-row comment earns
its place by saying something the row cannot. The $k=3$ comment does,
because it gives Eq. (43) and the meaning of the $\pm$; the others do not.
This is harmless at five rows, so it is optional.

### 5. Two small gaps in the comments (noting)

- `comment-heuristic` says the Ding–Sly–Sun theorem "does not give an
  explicit $k_0$", but no $k_0$ has been introduced. Write "for all $k$
  above some $k_0$ CITE{DSS}, which the proof does not make explicit".
- `comment-k2` states $\alpha_s(2)=1$ without a citation. Its reason, "the
  cavity-method table in MMZ starts at $k=3$", is about the source rather
  than the mathematics. The mathematical reason is that the $2$-SAT
  threshold is a theorem (Chvátal–Reed, Goerdt, Fernandez de la Vega), and a
  1RSB prediction is not the right description of it. Keeping $1$ in a
  comment rather than as a row is correct, as the skill advises for small
  integers.

### 6. `Keywords` has no "3-SAT" (noting, unverified)

The number most readers hold is the random 3-SAT threshold, and "random
3-SAT threshold" is what they would type. I could not test whether the text
search matches "3-SAT" against "$k$-SAT", because a draft answers no search.
If it does not, adding "random 3-SAT" to `Keywords` costs nothing.

## Not checked
