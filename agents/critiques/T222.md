The table is a good one. The parameter describes the family, the range sits
in `complete-note`, and rows run from $\Delta(2,3,7)$ upwards, so Lehmer's
number is the first thing on the page. Every row carries the minimal
polynomial of its value, which is exactly what somebody holding a Salem
number wants next. The faults are one rendering fault on most rows, one
false formula, one wrong technical word, and a set of facts the table knows
and does not say.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The minimal polynomials render wrong on 288 of 345 rows, starting with the first

What a reader sees, under the first value on the page: "$\Delta(2,3,7)$,
with minimal polynomial $t^1 0 + t^9 - t^7 \ldots$". A superscript 1 is
followed by a full-size 0. On `[10,4]`: "$t^1 0 - 2 \ast t^9 + 2 \ast t^8
\ldots$".

Why it is wrong: the comments hold Sage's `str()` of the polynomial inside
`$...$`. In TeX, `t^10` raises only the `1`. `*` is the operator $\ast$, not
juxtaposition. 246 comments have a two-digit exponent, 249 have an asterisk,
and 288 have one or both. Lehmer's polynomial on `[7,3]` is the row a reader
most likely arrived for, and a reader copying it off the page gets
$t\cdot 10$ where $t^{10}$ was meant. The asterisk half of this is already
a pending lesson (T169, `agents/lessons/PROPOSALS.md`). The exponent half is
new.

Smallest change: in the generator, write `latex(P)` (or
`str(P).replace('*','')` with every exponent braced) and refill the
comments. While the comments are being regenerated anyway, write them as a
sentence in a variable that does not collide with the growth series' $t$,
for example "$\Delta(2,3,7)$. The minimal polynomial of $\tau$ is
$x^{10}+x^9-x^7-\cdots+1$." **Worth doing.**

### 2. `formula-rate` is false for every row with an $\infty$ label

What a reader sees, in Formulas (2): "The growth rate $\tau$ is the
reciprocal of the radius of convergence of $W(t)$, equivalently the largest
real pole of the reduced rational function $W(t)$."

Why it is wrong: $\tau$ is the reciprocal of the *smallest positive* pole.
For the 269 finite-label rows the denominator is reciprocal, so $\tau$
happens to be a pole as well and the "largest real pole" gives the right
number. For the 76 rows with an ideal vertex it does not. For
$\Delta(2,3,\infty)$, $W(t)=-(t^4+3t^3+4t^2+3t+1)/(t^3+t^2-1)$, whose only
real pole is $0.7549 = 1/\tau$. For $\Delta(2,\infty,\infty)$ the real poles
are $-1.618$ and $0.618$, and the largest of them is not $\tau$. A reader who
follows the formula gets a number below 1 for every Pisot row. (Checked in
Sage on $\Delta(2,3,\infty)$, $\Delta(2,\infty,\infty)$,
$\Delta(3,3,\infty)$, $\Delta(3,\infty,\infty)$ and $\Delta(\infty,\infty,\infty)$.)

Smallest change: "…is the reciprocal of the radius of convergence of
$W(t)$, which is the smallest positive pole of $W(t)$." Because the
coefficients are non-negative, Pringsheim's theorem puts that singularity on
the positive axis. **Worth doing.**

### 3. The definition says "reflection length", which is a different length

What a reader sees: "$a_n$ is the number of elements of reflection length
$n$ in the standard Coxeter generators".

Why it is wrong: in Coxeter groups, *reflection length* is a standard term
for length with respect to **all** reflections, meaning every conjugate of
a generator. That is a different function with a different growth. What
Steinberg's formula counts, what the generator computes, and what the cited
Wikipedia article calls it is **word length** with respect to the three
generators. A reader who knows the term will build a different table from
this definition, and the definition is the one field meant to prevent that.
The phrase "in the standard Coxeter generators" also repeats
`comment-generators` ("The generating set is the three reflections in the
sides").

Smallest change, which also shortens the definition, because $W(t)$ already
lives in Formulas:

    Listed is the growth rate $\tau=\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n^{1/n}$, where $a_n$
    is the number of elements of word length $n$ with respect to those three
    reflections CITE{WikiGrowth}.

Then open `formula-steinberg` with "The growth series
$W(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nt^n$ satisfies…". The limit exists here because
$1/\tau$ is the only pole of smallest modulus. The first sentence of
`comment-generators` can then go. **Worth doing.**

### 4. What the values *are* is in Keywords and nowhere on the page

What a reader sees: `Keywords` carries "Salem number" and "Pisot number",
and a reader never sees Keywords. The page names two values, Lehmer's
number and the plastic number, and leaves the rest unnamed. In particular:

- Every finite-label value is a Salem number, and every value with an
  $\infty$ label is a Pisot number. I checked this on all 345 minimal
  polynomials. It is the theorem that makes this table interesting to
  somebody holding one of these numbers, and it is also why the minimal
  polynomials look the way they do.
- `[inf,inf]` and `[(3,3,inf)]` are $1.6180339\ldots$, the golden ratio,
  which the corpus holds as T32 row `phi`. Neither comment says so.
- $\Delta(p,\infty,\infty)$ has minimal polynomial
  $x^p-x^{p-1}-\cdots-x-1$ for every $p$ here, so its value is the
  $p$-bonacci constant. The tribonacci constant $1.8392867\ldots$ is at
  `[(3,inf,inf)]`, and a reader holding it lands on a row that does not name
  it.
- $\Delta(2,3,7)$ is the smallest value in the table and Lehmer's number.
  $\Delta(2,3,\infty)$ is the smallest value with an ideal vertex and the
  plastic number, the smallest Pisot number. The comments name both numbers
  and say neither is smallest.

Smallest change: one Comment, and three words on some rows.

    For a triangle with finite labels $\tau$ is a Salem number, and for one
    with an ideal vertex it is a Pisot number CITE{Floyd}. The smallest
    values of the two kinds are Lehmer's number, at $\Delta(2,3,7)$, and the
    plastic number, at $\Delta(2,3,\infty)$.

Add a Reference for W. J. Floyd, "Growth of planar Coxeter groups, P.V.
numbers, and Salem numbers", *Math. Ann.* 293 (1992), 475–483. I have not
checked those details against the paper. In the comments on `[inf,inf]` and
`[(3,3,inf)]`, add
"This is HREF{Golden_ratio#phi}[the golden ratio]." In the comments on
`[(p,inf,inf)]`, add "This is the $p$-bonacci constant", naming tribonacci
at $p=3$. **Worth doing.**

### 5. 130 rows share a value with another group, and nothing says so

What a reader sees: a reader holding $1.2806381\ldots$ gets two rows,
$\Delta(2,3,10)$ and $\Delta(2,4,5)$. A reader holding $1.7220838\ldots$
gets three: $\Delta(3,4,8)$, $\Delta(3,5,5)$ and $\Delta(4,4,4)$. Neither
entry comment mentions the other.

Why it matters: the table holds 345 entries and only 271 distinct values.
56 values belong to two, three or, once, four groups. The growth *series*
differ. For $\Delta(2,3,10)$ the denominator is
$(t^4+1)(t^8-t^5-t^4-t^3+1)$, and for $\Delta(2,4,5)$ it is the second factor
alone. So this is a fact about the growth rate, not a transcription slip,
and a reader who sees a repeated value with no remark will suspect one.
Being told is what search by number is for.

Smallest change: a Comment such as "Different triangles often have the same
growth rate, though not the same growth series: $\Delta(2,3,10)$ and
$\Delta(2,4,5)$ share $\tau$, and so do $\Delta(3,4,8)$, $\Delta(3,5,5)$ and
$\Delta(4,4,4)$". Better, have the generator add "Also the growth rate of
$\Delta(\ldots)$." to each shared row. I did not find the reason for the
coincidences and would not guess one into the table. **Worth doing, the
comment at least.**

### 6. `formula-steinberg` uses $S$, $W_T$ and $W_T(t)$ without defining them, and narrates

What a reader sees: "The growth series is computed from Steinberg's formula
$1/W(t^{-1})=\sum_{T\subseteq S,\ W_T\text{ finite}}(-1)^{|T|}/W_T(t)$". $S$
and $W_T$ are never introduced. $W$ is used both for a series and, inside
$W_T$, for a group, and the group itself is called $\Delta$ in the
definition. "Is computed from" is a sentence about the build, and its home
is `rigour details`, which already says it.

Smallest change: state the formula as a relation, and give it in the form
this table uses, which is what the Programs snippet computes:

    Steinberg's formula CITE{Steinberg}: for $\Delta(p,q,r)$,
    $1/W(t^{-1}) = 1-\dfrac{3}{1+t}+\sum_{m\in\{p,q,r\},\ m<\infty}
    \dfrac{1}{(1+t)(1+t+\cdots+t^{m-1})}$.

The general form can follow, with "$W_T(t)$ the growth polynomial of the
finite subgroup generated by $T\subseteq S$, $S$ the three reflections".
**Worth doing.**

### 7. Similar tables relates the table to one number by what it is not

What a reader sees: one entry, Viswanath's constant, "another exponential
growth rate, from random Fibonacci recurrences rather than Coxeter groups".
T189 holds a single value. The relation says only that both are growth
rates of something. The table that holds two of this table's values, T32,
is not listed.

Smallest change: add HREF{Golden_ratio}[Golden ratio] with "$\varphi$, the
growth rate of $\Delta(2,\infty,\infty)$ and $\Delta(3,3,\infty)$". The
Viswanath entry can stay. **Worth doing; small.**

### 8. The completeness note is ambiguous and gives no reason

`complete-note`: "…and those with $r=\infty$ and $2\leq p\leq q\leq12$ or
$q=\infty$". Read literally, "or $q=\infty$" has no bound on $p$, so it
admits $\Delta(\infty,\infty,\infty)$. That group is in the family as
defined, has $\tau=2$ exactly, and is not in the table. The note also does
not say why 12.

Smallest change: "…and those with $r=\infty$ and either
$2\leq p\leq q\leq 12$ or $2\leq p\leq 12$, $q=\infty$". If the ideal
triangle was left out deliberately, add a Comment giving its value as
$\tau=2$, since it is an integer that would match everything. Add a clause
with the reason for 12, if there is one. **Worth doing.**

### 9. The parameter is titled "Coxeter diagram" and holds Coxeter symbols

What a reader sees, under Parameters: "diagram — Coxeter diagram (a Coxeter
diagram of a finite-area hyperbolic triangle reflection group)". In the
Numbers column the rows are labelled "[7, 3]", "[(3, 4, 4)]" and "[inf, 3]",
and `comment-addresses` calls them "ASCII Coxeter bracket symbols". A
bracket symbol is not a diagram. The `display` is the word "diagram" where
other tables print a symbol, so "Coxeter diagram" appears twice in one line.
"Entry addresses" is this website's word, not a reader's.

Smallest change, while the table is a draft: title the parameter "Coxeter
symbol", with constraint "the Coxeter symbol of a hyperbolic triangle
reflection group of finite area". Begin `comment-addresses` with "Rows are
labelled by Coxeter symbol: $[r,q]$ is $\Delta(2,q,r)$…". The key `diagram`
can stay. **Worth doing if the prose is being touched anyway.**

## Noticed, not worth an edit on their own

- Tags. `dynamical systems` (T189's subject) and `special values` (values of
  functions) do not describe growth rates of groups. `algebraic`, the tag on
  T32 and fourteen other tables, does describe every value here.
- `rigour details` says "Before the draft was filled", which is website
  workflow, and "four control groups" without naming them. "The first
  growth-series coefficients of $\Delta(\ldots)$ … were compared" would say
  which.
- `comment-generators`: the von Dyck subgroup's growth rate "is a different
  convention". It is a different group with different generators, so a
  different number. "is a different number, not held here" would be
  plainer.
- The Programs snippet prints every real root of the reversed denominator,
  so a reader gets both $0.850\ldots$ and $1.176\ldots$ for $\Delta(2,3,7)$
  and has to know to take the larger. It also passes $\infty$ as `None`,
  and nothing says so. Ending with `max(r for r, _ in …)` and a comment line
  `# pass None for an infinite label` would fix both.
- The WikiGrowth article states that growth *type* does not depend on the
  generating set. `comment-generators` rightly says the number does. The
  citation supports the notion and not that caveat, which is fine as it
  stands.
