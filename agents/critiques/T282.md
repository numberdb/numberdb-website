# Critique of T282, "Mahler measures of $x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}+k$"

Read on 2026-09-16. The table is a private draft owned by zeta3. It holds 101
real values $m_k$, $0\leq k\leq 100$, with `rigour: proven`.

## How it was read

- **Skill:** fetched from <https://numberdb.org/skill> once. The SOCKS proxy
  then refused every connection, so I reached the site directly for
  everything else (this is already recorded in `docs/agent-environment.md`).
- **Document:** `GET /api/table?id=T282` with the key.
- **Audit:** `GET /api/table/T282/audit` with the key. It returned no
  findings and `clean: true`. This runner has no Django, so I did not run
  `manage.py audit_table`.
- **Rendering:** `/T282` answers 404 without a session, because it is a draft.
  The whole document is too long for `/preview?table=...` (414, and 400 for
  anything over 4094 bytes). I rendered it in six pieces that together
  cover every section and, with the parameters, the entries for
  $k=0,1,2,4,5,100$. All six returned 200. None contained `Math input error`, `argument ()`,
  `CITE-broken` or `HREF-broken`. The column headings read $k$ and $m_k$,
  and each row label is the bare integer.
- **Corpus:** searches for Mahler, Lehmer, Catalan, spanning tree, Boyd and
  "Entropy constants" found T153, *Entropy constants of lattice models*, which
  is published and holds $4G/\pi$ at `spanning-tree,square,entropy`. They also
  found T145, *Values of Dirichlet L-functions at positive integers*, which
  holds Catalan's constant $G=L(2,\chi_{-4})$ at `4,3,2`. T182, *Mahler
  polynomials*, is a different object. No other table holds Mahler measures.
- **Values:** I did not recheck them. I did check three things by hand:
  $m_5$ from formula (2) to four places, the factorisation in the $k=0$
  comment, and the Jensen reduction in formula (1). All three are right.

## The audit

The audit is clean, and I agree that nothing it checks for is wrong here.
This is one table with one integer parameter, the headings say what the
columns hold, and every `HREF` and `CITE` resolves. The audit missed two
things that it cannot see. "Catalan's constant" should link to a table whose
title does not contain that phrase (finding 4). The $k=4$ row is the same
number as a published entry but carries no `equals` (finding 2).

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The page does not say what these numbers are for $k\neq 0,1,4$. Worth doing, and first.

Suppose a reader arrives holding $0.79471244797954\ldots$ from a
calculation. The page shows them the row $k=3$, with no comment, and a
definition that restates the integral. The only thing the Comments say about
the family is "Boyd used this family in his study of Mahler measures and
special values of $L$-functions". That sentence is history. It does not say
what Boyd found.

What Boyd found is the reason the table exists. For integer $k\neq 0,4$ he
conjectured, from numerical evidence, that $m_k=r_k\,L'(E_k,0)$. Here $E_k$
is the elliptic curve given by $x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}+k=0$ and $r_k$ is a
rational number. The conjecture has been proved for several small $k$, among
them $k=1$ (Rogers and Zudilin, already cited). A reader who computed an
elliptic-curve $L$-value is exactly the reader who holds one of these
numbers. The page never tells them that. Without it, the rows from $k=5$ to
$k=100$ look like values of $\log k$ minus a series, which nobody looks up.

The same fact is the argument that `complete-note` is missing. The skill asks
the note to say *why* the range was chosen. At the moment it says only "it
holds every integer $k$ with $0\leq k\leq100$".

Fix: replace the first sentence of `comment-family` with a statement of the
conjecture, citing `CITE{Boyd}`. Name the $k$ for which it is proved, taking
the list from a source rather than from memory. Brunault and Zudilin, *Many
Variations of Mahler Measures* (2020), collects them. I have not checked
which $k$ beyond $k=1$ are proved, so do not copy a list from this report.
Then say why the range stops at 100 in the complete-note, even if the reason
is only that it is a round bound on the family Boyd computed.

### 2. The $k=4$ row should carry `equals` to the published entry in T153. Worth doing, one line.

$m_4=4G/\pi$ is stored in T153 at `spanning-tree,square,entropy`, and its
digits agree with this row. T153 is published. At the moment the page makes
the connection three times in prose: in the $k=4$ comment, in formula (3),
and in Similar tables. None of those is the field that search reads. A search
for $1.16624361\ldots$ will return two independent hits instead of one answer
with the other named beside it. The skill describes this case exactly:
"`equals` says the two entries *are the same number* ... and search reads
it." The corpus already writes it this way, for example in the Conway and
Jones polynomial generators.

Fix: add `equals: HREF{Entropy_constants_of_lattice_models#spanning-tree,square,entropy}`
to the $k=4$ entry. The comment can stay.

### 3. The $k=1$ comment refers to an identity without stating it. Worth doing, small.

The comment reads: "Rogers and Zudilin proved an identity relating this value
to the $L$-series of a conductor 15 elliptic curve." A reader holding
$0.2513\ldots$ wants the identity, not the news that one exists. Rogers and
Zudilin prove Boyd's conjecture for $k=1$, and the formula I remember from
their paper is $m_1=L'(E_{15},0)=\frac{15}{4\pi^2}L(E_{15},2)$. Take it from
the paper before writing it down. The comment should also name the curve
precisely, for example by its LMFDB isogeny class 15.a.

`comment-family` says the same thing in the same vague words: "the polynomial
whose elliptic-curve $L$-value identity was proved". Once finding 1 is fixed,
that second sentence can go.

Fix: "$m_1=L'(E,0)$ for the elliptic curve $E$ of conductor 15 (LMFDB 15.a),
proved by Rogers and Zudilin CITE{RogersZudilin}", with the formula checked
against [3].

### 4. "Catalan's constant" is not linked, and the corpus holds it. Worth doing, one line.

Formula (3) reads "$m_4=4G/\pi$, where $G$ is Catalan's constant". $G$ is the
entry `4,3,2` of T145, *Values of Dirichlet L-functions at positive
integers*. The audit cannot find this, because the phrase does not occur in
that table's title.

Fix: `where $G$ is HREF{Values_of_Dirichlet_L-functions_at_positive_integers#4,3,2}[Catalan's constant]`.
The sentence means one number, so the link should point at the row. I read
the key from T145's `Numbers` (nested $q$, $n$, $s$) but did not resolve the
anchor in Django, so run `audit_table --links` after the edit.

### 5. The rigour note lost its paragraph break, and its last sentence is about the build. Worth doing, small.

In `generators/mahler-measures-square-lattice/table.yaml` the note has two
paragraphs separated by a blank line. It is written as a `>-` folded scalar,
and YAML folds a single blank line into a single `\n`. The stored text
therefore has no blank line, and the page shows one paragraph. The note is
longer than 400 characters, so the renderer folds it after a sentence. It
shows the method for $k=1,2,3$ and for $k=4$, then hides "For $k\geq5$ it
sums the absolutely convergent series ..." behind *more*. The method for 96
of the 101 rows is therefore the hidden part.

The last sentence reads: "The range $0\leq k\leq100$ measured 101 entries, a
longest written value of 146 characters at $k=4$, and a 17.9 KB entries
block." That sentence is about the size of the build, not about how well the
digits are known. A range also does not "measure" entries. "Before the draft
was filled" will also be stale once the table is published.

Fix: put two blank lines between the paragraphs, or use `|-`. Drop the
sizing sentence, and start the second paragraph with "Every entry was also
checked against a computation not used for that row: ...".

### 6. The parameter's title is the word "parameter". Worth doing, one word.

The Parameters section renders as "$k$ — parameter ($k\geq0$)". That tells a
reader nothing the symbol did not. "constant term" says what $k$ is in
$x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}+k$. The key `k` does not change, so no citation is
affected.

## Noted only

- The Similar tables gloss reads "holds the same number $4G/\pi$ as the row
  $k=4$, as a square-lattice spanning-tree entropy". The two uses of "as"
  mean different things. Also, the relation between the two tables is
  broader than one row: Guttmann and Rogers express lattice spanning-tree
  entropies as Mahler measures in general. For example: "holds
  spanning-tree entropies of planar lattices, which are Mahler measures; the
  square lattice's is $m_4$".
- The keyword "Lehmer problem" and the tag "dynamical systems" have no
  support in the prose. $e^{m_1}=1.285735\ldots$ may be one of the small
  known limit points of two-variable Mahler measures (Boyd and Mossinghoff),
  and that would justify the keyword. I have not checked this. If it is
  true, it belongs in the $k=1$ comment. If it is not, drop the keyword.
- T153's entry comment names "the Mahler measure of $4+x+x^{-1}+y+\dots$".
  Once T282 is published, that could become a link. This is a note for T153,
  not a fault in this table.

## What reads well

The definition defines the object and stops. It gives the polynomial and the
integral, so two people would build the same table. The parameter states the
family ($k\geq0$), and the range sits in `complete-note`, where it belongs.
Formulas (1) and (2) are stated as relations, with $\operatorname{arcosh}_+$
defined where it is used. The symmetry $m_{-k}=m_k$ explains why the
negative $k$ are missing, and the $k=0$ comment explains its zero with a
factorisation instead of an apology.
