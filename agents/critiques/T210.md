## What was checked hardest

* **The reader holding a number.** The numbers are the first thing on
  the page, headed $M(a;b;z)$ with the three parameters beside them,
  and the title names the function. That reader is served, with the
  exception in finding 1.
* **Parameters versus run.** The parameter lines say
  $a\in\mathbb Q$, $b\in\mathbb Q\setminus\mathbb Z_{\leq0}$,
  $z\in\mathbb Q$, which is the family; the grid actually computed is
  the complete-note, written as a clause that finishes "complete: false
  (…)". This is the split the skill asks for, done right.
* **The rigour note against the generator.** The note names a 64-bit
  guard and a widest radius under $3\cdot10^{-115}$; the generator's
  `WORKING_GUARD` is 64 and its comment records the same measurement
  over all 384 entries. The checks the note lists ((2) where both rows
  are stored, (3) at $(1,2)$, (4) at $x=1,2$, (5) at $b=2a$) are each
  possible on this grid, and two of them I repeated.
* **The two programs.** `z.hypergeometric([a], [b])` exists on a Sage
  complex ball, and the comment saying the method is called on the
  argument is the one thing a reader would otherwise get wrong.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Three rows print a bare 0 and nothing near them says why

Rows $(3/2,1/2,-1/2)$, $(2,1,-1)$ and $(3,2,-2)$ hold the exact
integer $0$, in the first screen of the table, with no entry comment.
The only explanation on the page is the second sentence of the rigour
note, "Rows whose Kummer transform is a terminating polynomial that
vanishes are returned as the exact integer $0$", which sits after the
"more" fold under Data properties, and which describes the generator's
test rather than the mathematics. A reader who has just found
$M(2;1;-1)$ to be zero to a hundred places wants the reason: Kummer's
transformation (2) at $b-a=-1$ gives $M(b+1;b;z)=e^z(1+z/b)$, which
vanishes at $z=-b$, and each of the three rows has $b=a-1$ and $z=-b$.

The same identity says more than that. Every row with $b-a$ a
non-positive integer is $e^z$ times a polynomial, and the grid has six
such pairs, $(3/2,1/2)$, $(2,1)$, $(5/2,3/2)$, $(3,2)$, $(5/2,1/2)$ and
$(3,1)$: 48 stored rows, verified above for the 32 with $b=a-1$. The
page omits the $a=b$ rows "because $M(a;a;z)=e^z$" and stores
$M(2;1;z)=e^z(1+z)$ without remark, so the rule a reader infers for
what is left out is not the rule that was applied. That is a decision
and a defensible one, since $e^z(1+z)$ is a value somebody might hold
and $e^z$ is in every reference; it only needs saying.

Fix: add a formula, "For $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}$,
$M(b+n;b;z)=e^z M(-n;b;-z)$ is $e^z$ times a polynomial of degree $n$
in $z$ by (2); in particular $M(b+1;b;z)=e^z(1+z/b)$", and end
comment (8) with "The three rows with $b=a-1$ and $z=-b$ are exactly
$0$ by that formula." An entry comment on each of the three rows saying
the same would do instead. Worth doing; it is the one thing on the page
a reader is left to guess.

### 2. Kummer's equation is named three times and written nowhere

The definition's second sentence is "The function is the standard
solution of Kummer's equation normalised by $M(a;b;0)=1$", and
Similar tables names the equation twice more. It is not on the page.
Somebody who does not already know it gets nothing from the sentence,
and somebody who does knows that it does not pin the function down:
for $0<b<1$ the second solution $z^{1-b}M(a-b+1;2-b;z)$ also vanishes
at $z=0$, so every $M+c\,z^{1-b}M(a-b+1;2-b;z)$ is a solution with
value $1$ there. What singles out $M$ is being analytic at $z=0$, or
simply the series (1), which the first sentence already gives by
naming ${}_1F_1$.

Fix: either drop the second sentence, since $M={}_1F_1$ with (1)
defines the table, or make it true and write the equation: a formula
"$M$ is the solution of Kummer's equation
$z\,w''+(b-z)\,w'-a\,w=0$ that is analytic at $z=0$, with $w(0)=1$",
and "analytic at $z=0$" in the definition in place of "standard".
T209 has the same equation in its definition and does write it as its
formula (1); this table should match it. Worth doing.

### 3. "Not listed separately" does not say the rows are absent, and one omission is unstated

Comment (8) opens "Rows with $z=0$ or $a=b$ are not listed
separately". A reader who found no $M(1;1;2)$ cannot tell from this
whether such rows are listed together with something, listed elsewhere,
or omitted; they are omitted, and the complete-note's "except the rows
with $a=b$" is where that is said. The same sentence was found on T209
and the fix is the same. The second half of the comment then says that
$M(-n;\alpha+1;z)$ is a Laguerre-type polynomial and does not say that
rows with $a$ a non-positive integer are omitted too, which they are:
the generator's `_is_elementary_row` refuses them, and the grid has
none. The page's account of what is left out is one omission short of
the generator's.

Fix: "Rows with $a=b$ are omitted, since $M(a;a;z)=e^z$ by (3), as are
rows with $z=0$, where $M(a;b;0)=1$, and rows with $a$ a non-positive
integer $-n$, where $M(-n;\alpha+1;z)$ is a Laguerre polynomial by (6);
the ordinary case $M(-n;1;z)=L_n(z)$ is in the table of Laguerre
polynomials." One sentence. Worth doing.

### 4. "Coulomb wave function" is a keyword and nothing on the page mentions it

`Keywords` carries "Coulomb wave function" at the title's weight, and
the page never uses the words. The relation is DLMF 33.2.4,
$F_L(\eta,\rho)=C_L(\eta)\rho^{L+1}e^{-i\rho}M(L+1-i\eta;2L+2;2i\rho)$,
checked numerically while reading; its first parameter and its argument
are complex unless $\eta=0$, where the function is a Bessel function,
so no row of a table with rational $a$ and $z$ is a value of a Coulomb
wave function. Somebody typing the phrase lands here first and is not
helped. Fix: drop the keyword. Adding the formula would keep it honest
but sends a reader to a table that cannot serve them.

Comment (9), on the Whittaker function, is the near miss: it says the
table does not store $M_{\kappa,\mu}(z)$ and stops, where one line
turns the disclaimer into a conversion a reader can use:
$M_{\kappa,\mu}(z)=e^{-z/2}z^{\mu+1/2}M(\tfrac12+\mu-\kappa;1+2\mu;z)$
(DLMF 13.14.2, checked). Worth doing, in the same edit.

### 5. Two notations for the same function in one sentence

The title and every formula write $M(a;b;z)$ with semicolons; comment
(7) writes Olver's $\mathbf{M}(a,b,z)$ with commas in the same sentence
as $M(a;b;z)$, and the sibling table is $U(a,b,z)$ with commas. DLMF
writes commas for all three. The T209 critique noted this while the
title was still cheap to change; it is now the slug of a public table,
so the fix is the smaller one: commas in comment (7) alone would leave
two conventions on the page, so leave it, and note that the two tables
of one equation spell their arguments differently. Noted only.

### 6. The sibling does not link back

This table's Similar tables names T209 as "the other standard solution
of Kummer's equation". T209's Similar tables, read from the API today,
has no row for this table, although both are public and the T209
critique asked for the pair. A reader arriving at $U$ has no way to
$M$. That is a finding about T209, written here because this is where
it was seen.

### 7. The pointer to $e$ serves one of the 48 omitted rows

The gloss on the table of $e$ says "the omitted rows with $a=b$ are
exponential values by (3)". Those rows are $e^{\pm1/2}$, $e^{\pm1}$,
$e^{\pm2}$ and $e^{\pm4}$; the table of $e$ holds $e$ alone, so a
reader holding $7.389\ldots$ finds it in neither place. The gloss is
true and the pointer is right for $z=1$; the other seven values are
simply not in the corpus, which is a decision somebody may want to
revisit and not a fault of this page. Noted only.

### 8. The Beta and Ramanujan glosses are two steps removed

"the moment generating function of the Beta distribution is
$M(\alpha;\alpha+\beta;t)$" is a fact about $M$, and the linked table
holds $B(a,b)$, the distribution's normalising constant, not its
generating function; the relation between the two tables is a
distribution neither of them holds. The Ramanujan gloss is the same
sentence T209 carries and was noted there: it is true of any ${}_2F_1$
table. Both are fair sideways pointers for a reader browsing
hypergeometric functions. Noted only.

### 9. Four groups of rows hold the same number

The three exact zeros of finding 1 are one group. $(2,1,-2)$ and
$(3,1,-2)$ are both $-e^{-2}$, from $e^z(1+z)$ and $e^z(1+2z+z^2/2)$
at $z=-2$. $(1,3/2,-4)$ and $(3,1/2,-4)$ agree to 160 places and are
a coincidence of the contiguous relations at $z=-4$, not at $z=-1,-2,-3$;
$(1/2,3/2,4)$ and $(-5/2,1/2,4)$ are their images under Kummer's
transformation. Search by number deduplicates exact repeats, so a
reader is answered once; nothing to change, written down so the next
person who counts distinct values is not surprised.

## What is right and should stay

The parameter lines, the complete-note and the rigour note divide the
work exactly as the skill asks, and the rigour note's guard and radius
are the generator's own measurement. Formulas (3) to (6) each name and
link the table the relation lands in. The generator's docstring opens
with the three commands and explains the one non-obvious call. The
order of the rows puts $a$ and $-a$ side by side. None of that should
be touched while fixing 1 to 4.
