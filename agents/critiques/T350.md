## What I checked hardest and found sound

* **The page renders, whole.** 552 entry anchors, all present and all unique,
  `1/24,0` through `1/2,11/12`; the three column headers `$\varphi/\pi$`,
  `$m$`, `$E(\varphi,m)$`; every section in the page's own order. No `Math
  input error`, no `argument ()`, no `(Unknown key)`, no surviving `HREF{` or
  `CITE{`, no raw `<` or `&`. The one place a rendering fault was plausible is
  comment_1's `$E(\varphi\backslash\alpha)$`, and the backslash reaches the
  page intact.
* **The `Programs` snippet is the table's own number.** Run verbatim
  (preparsed, `Integer` in the namespace) it returns
  `[0.74818650417766137745...8718992122387781152128 +/- 4.81e-119]`, and the
  stored row `1/4,1/2` is exactly that midpoint rounded to 100 places: the
  difference is $7.9\cdot10^{-103}$, inside the half-ulp $5\cdot10^{-101}$ that
  the written digits claim. A reader who runs the snippet gets the row.
* **An independent library agrees.** mpmath's `ellipe(pi/4, 1/2)` at 40 digits
  gives `0.74818650417766137745`, the same digits from code that is not arb.
* **Formula (2) holds where it applies.** The 12 rows with $m=0$ are $t\pi$:
  `1/2,0` is $1.5707963267948966\ldots$ and `1/6,0` is
  $0.5235987755982988\ldots$
* **It is one table.** Two parameters, both arguments of one function, neither
  a name. The good kind of answer to the audit's question.
* **`repeats:` is rightly absent.** The $m=0$ column agrees with T29 because
  of a theorem, not because it was transcribed, and the skill says both tables
  should answer in that case.
* **The order is the reader's.** The elliptic parameters run by denominator
  and then numerator, so $0$, $1/2$, $1/3$ are the first three rows of every
  block rather than $1/12$; the amplitudes run upwards.
* **The grid is a grid.** The twelve amplitudes are $k\pi/24$ for
  $1\leq k\leq12$, which is why $\pi/6$, $\pi/4$, $\pi/3$ and $\pi/2$ are all
  in it. 552 rows is $12\times46$ exactly, with 46 reduced $m=a/b\in[0,1)$ at
  $b\leq12$: the stated range is the range, with nothing missing and nothing
  extra.
* **The generator reads.** It opens with the commands to run it, names its
  rings rather than importing `sage.all`, divides only between `QQ`s, and
  refuses a non-finite or non-real ball instead of returning one.

## Findings

### Worth doing

**1. The nearest table in the corpus is not named. (`Similar tables`)**

What a reader sees: four similar tables, all three *complete* elliptic
integrals and the rational multiples of $\pi$. What is not there is T349,
*Values of the incomplete elliptic integral of the first kind
$F(\varphi,m)$* — the same twelve amplitudes, the same 46 elliptic parameters,
the same $m=k^2$ convention, the same 100 digits. A reader holding
$E(\varphi,m)$ and wanting $F(\varphi,m)$ beside it, which is the commonest
thing anybody wants next, is sent to the three complete integrals instead and
never learns the incomplete first kind is here.

Why it is wrong: this is the skill's "first mention of a family the corpus
holds", and the corpus does hold it. It is also the corpus's own habit — T25,
T26 and T59 each name the other two.

Smallest fix: one more `Similar tables` row naming T349, with the relation
saying what it is, for example "the first-kind integral $F(\varphi,m)$ over
the same amplitudes and the same elliptic parameters". Two caveats. T349 is
itself an unpublished draft, so the pair has to be published together or
first-kind first, as `docs/agent-environment.md` records for T142/T145. And
its slug is not in any search result while it is a draft, so it must be read
off the draft rather than guessed from the title — the skill is explicit that
a guessed slug points at nothing.

**2. Nothing says what to do with an amplitude outside $(0,\pi/2]$.**

What a reader sees: a parameter constrained to $0<\varphi/\pi\leq1/2$, three
formulas, and no way out. The reader who arrives here is usually holding an
arc length, and an arc length hands back whatever amplitude the geometry had.
With the table as it stands, a number computed at $\varphi=2.1$ cannot be
matched against anything in it, and a reader could reasonably conclude the
function is only defined on the first quadrant.

Why it is wrong: the cap is a convention the table chose and does not explain,
and the identity that makes the choice harmless is not stated anywhere. This
is `Formulas`' own business: identities and closed forms.

Smallest fix: one formula, $E(\varphi+k\pi,m)=E(\varphi,m)+2kE(m)$ for integer
$k$, together with $E(-\varphi,m)=-E(\varphi,m)$; with formula (1) already
naming $E(m)$ and linking T26, that reduces every real amplitude to a stored
row. I checked both in ball arithmetic before writing them here: 36 cases of
the first over $m\in\{0,1/4,1/2,5/7\}$, $t\in\{1/6,3/8,11/24\}$, $k\in\{1,2,3\}$
and 4 of the second, every ball narrower than $10^{-30}$ and every pair
overlapping. T349 has the same gap, so this is a remark about the pair.

**3. `complete-note` ends by excluding rows that were never there.**

What a reader sees, on the last line of the page:

> Table is complete: no (it holds the values with $\varphi=t\pi$ for
> $t\in\{1/24,\ldots,1/2\}$ and $m=a/b\in[0,1)$ in lowest terms with
> $b\leq12$; **rows whose value is rational would be omitted**)

Why it is wrong: no row was omitted. The table holds $12\times46=552$ entries,
which is the full grid the same sentence describes, and the attached generator
contains no such filter — `enumerate` yields every pair and `value` returns
every one of them. A reader who reads that clause goes looking for gaps in the
grid and finds none; if they take it as a live rule they will wonder which of
their own numbers this table would have refused. The clause is also in the
conditional mood in a field whose whole job is to state what is here, and it
hangs off a semicolon rather than being a sentence.

Smallest fix: delete everything after `b\leq12`. T349's note is the same
sentence without it and is the better of the two.

**4. The definition is not a sentence.**

What a reader sees:

> Real principal values of the incomplete elliptic integral of the second kind
> $E(\varphi,m)=\int_0^\varphi \sqrt{1-m\sin^2\theta}\,d\theta$ [1] [2] , with
> $\varphi=t\pi$ for rational $t$ and elliptic parameter $m=k^2$.

Why it is wrong: there is no finite verb in it. It is a noun phrase with two
trailing modifiers, and the reader has to supply "this table holds" for
themselves. Three smaller things ride along. $k$ appears in it and is defined
one section further down, in comment_1. "$\varphi=t\pi$ for rational $t$" is
the grid this run computed, which `complete-note` already states and which the
skill puts there and not here. And "Real principal values" caveats a branch
choice that does not arise: for $0\leq m<1$ and real $\varphi$ the integrand
$\sqrt{1-m\sin^2\theta}$ is real and positive, so there is nothing to take a
principal value of.

Smallest fix: T349's shape, two sentences — "The incomplete elliptic integral
of the second kind is $E(\varphi,m)=\int_0^\varphi\sqrt{1-m\sin^2\theta}\,
d\theta$ CITE{DLMFDefinitions} CITE{Wiki}, in the parameter convention
$m=k^2$. This table gives its values at $\varphi=t\pi$ for rational $t$ and
rational $m$."

**5. Two of the four similar-table relations state no relation.**

What a reader sees:

> Complete elliptic integral of the first kind $K(m)$ —   stores the
> corresponding complete first-kind integral in the same parameter convention

Corresponding to what? Nothing in T350 corresponds to $K(m)$; the sentence
names a convention the two share and calls that a relation. The third-kind row
is the same sentence again. The skill says the relation "is the valuable
part", and T349 does better in the same slot: "the third kind reduces to the
first kind when its characteristic is zero".

Smallest fix: name the quantity and the tie. For T25, "holds $K(m)$, the
first-kind integral, at the same elliptic parameter $m$"; for T59, "holds
$\Pi(n,m)$, the third-kind integral, at the same $m$, and $\Pi(0,m)=K(m)$".

**6. "the row with $\varphi=\pi/2$" is 46 rows.**

What a reader sees: "the row with $\varphi=\pi/2$ is the complete integral by
(1)". There are 46 such rows, one for each stored $m$, and the singular
invites a reader to look for one distinguished entry. T349 writes "the entries
with $\varphi=\pi/2$ are the complete integral", which is right.

Smallest fix: the plural.

**7. The parameter's constraint uses a symbol the column does not.**

What a reader sees, in the parameter list:

> $\varphi/\pi$ —   angle multiple ($0<t\leq1/2$, where $\varphi=t\pi$)

Three names for one quantity in one line: the column is $\varphi/\pi$, the
constraint is about $t$, and $t$ then has to be defined by a trailing clause
that introduces a third symbol, $\varphi$. A reader matching the constraint to
the column has to do the substitution themselves.

Smallest fix: T349's wording, `$0<\varphi/\pi\leq 1/2$`, which needs no
`where` at all.

**8. The amplitude is never called the amplitude.**

What a reader sees: a parameter titled "angle multiple" — a multiple of what,
and in what sense an angle? Every source this table cites calls $\varphi$ the
*amplitude*: Wikipedia's "Elliptic integral", which is `Links` [2], says "The
other argument can likewise be expressed as $\varphi$, the amplitude", and
DLMF 19.2 is the same. The word appears nowhere in T350 — not in the title,
the definition, the parameters, the comments or the `Keywords` — while T349
titles the same parameter "amplitude ratio" and carries "elliptic amplitude"
in its `Keywords`. When both are published, a reader searching for the
amplitude reaches one of the pair and not the other.

Smallest fix: title the parameter "amplitude, in multiples of $\pi$" and add
"elliptic amplitude" to `Keywords`.

**9. A book is named and not cited.**

What a reader sees: "Abramowitz and Stegun also use the modular angle notation
$E(\varphi\backslash\alpha)$ with $k=\sin\alpha$", with no reference and
nothing in `Links` or `References` to follow. The claim is right — Wikipedia's
article documents the backslash as the modular-angle form — but a reader who
wants to check it is given an author pair and no edition, page or link.

Smallest fix: either `CITE{Wiki}` at the end of that clause, since the article
is already declared and already says it, or a `References` entry for
Abramowitz and Stegun with its `bib` and identifiers.

### Noting only

**10.** The comment's key is `comment_1`, where every other key in the
document is descriptive and hyphenated — `formula-complete`,
`formula-zero-parameter`, `program-sage`. A reader never sees a key, so
nothing renders wrong; but keys are frozen addresses that citations resolve
on, and `comment_1` will still be the address of the parameter convention
after a second comment is added above it.

**11.** T349 carries the tag `hypergeometric functions` and T350 does not,
though the two are equally hypergeometric. I would not add the tag without the
formula behind it. If it is added, the identity is
$E(\varphi,m)=\sin\varphi\,F_1(1/2;1/2,-1/2;3/2;\sin^2\varphi,m\sin^2\varphi)$
with Appell's $F_1$ — the same shape as T349's `formula-appell` with its
second parameter negated. I derived it by the Euler integral and checked it
against mpmath's `appellf1` at 40 digits on twelve pairs, all agreeing to
$10^{-30}$; the Gauss ${}_2F_1$ form one might reach for first is the
*complete* integral's and is wrong here, which I found out by trying it.

**12.** Both tables carry `period` and `elliptic curves`. For an incomplete
integral neither is literally true away from $\varphi=\pi/2$ — 506 of the 552
values are not periods of anything. I would leave them: T25, T26, T59 and T349
all carry the same pair, and a tag whose job is to let a reader browse
sideways through the elliptic-integral tables does that job here.

**13.** The attached generator takes its table from the environment,
`table = os.environ.get("NUMBERDB_TABLE", "T350")`. Harmless — the default is
right — but the file outlives the run, and a reader who downloads it with
`NUMBERDB_TABLE` set for something else has a generator pointed somewhere
they did not choose.

## In short

The table is sound: the page renders whole, the numbers are the numbers the
snippet and mpmath return, the range is exactly what it says, and it is
unmistakably one table. What it needs is small and mostly one line each. The
two I would do first are the missing link to T349 (finding 1) and the missing
reduction formula (finding 2), because both are about a reader who cannot get
from where they are to what they want. Findings 3 and 4 are the two places
where the prose says something that is not so — a set of omitted rows that is
empty, and a definition with no verb in it. The rest is tidying, and findings
5 to 8 all have their answer already written in T349.
