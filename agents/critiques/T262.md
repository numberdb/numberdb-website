# T262 critique: $q,t$-Catalan numbers $C_n(q,t)$, with the Carlitz and MacMahon $q$-Catalan numbers

Read 2026-09-16. T262 is a private draft, so `/T262` answers 404 to a request
without a session. I read the document through `GET /api/table?id=T262` with
the key and rendered it through `/preview?table=` in eight pieces, each under
3 KB. The SOCKS proxy on 127.0.0.1:1080 was not listening, but numberdb.org
answered `curl` directly. The table holds 22 entries of type `Z[]` with rigour
`exact`. They are the `qt` rows for $3\leq n\leq6$ and the `carlitz` and
`macmahon` rows for $3\leq n\leq11$. The largest entry is 1158 characters.

`GET /api/table/T262/audit` reports `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. On
finding 1 I disagree with that result. The audit has both rules that should ask
about this table, and the table slips past each of them on a detail of form
(see below).

**Nothing is wrong with the values or the rendering.** Here is what I checked
hardest:

* *The page renders.* All 28 `$...$` fragments in the prose typeset in
  MathJax 3 with every package loaded, including `{2n\brack n}_q` and
  `\binom n2`. A control fragment with an unclosed brace did fail. The prose
  has no bare `<`. Every `CITE{formula-*}` resolves to a numbered formula, the
  parameter list shows titles and constraints, and the long `rigour details`
  folds after its first paragraph.
* *The parameters describe the family.* `n` is "a nonnegative integer", and
  the range actually computed is in `complete-note`, written as a clause that
  finishes "complete: no (...)". Leaving out $n\leq2$ is also right: $1$,
  $q+t$, $q+1$ and $q^2+1$ would match everything.
* *The programs give the stored values.* Sage's `q_catalan_number(11)` is,
  character for character, the stored `11,macmahon` row, and
  `qt_catalan_number(3).subs(t=1)` is the stored `3,carlitz` row. I checked
  both through `agents/sage.sh`.

The findings are ranked. 1 to 3 are worth doing. 4 and 5 are small and worth
doing. 6 is only a note.

---

## 1. Three tables under one title, and the column header shows it

**What a reader sees.** The title is "$q,t$-Catalan numbers $C_n(q,t)$, with
the Carlitz and MacMahon $q$-Catalan numbers". The value column is headed
$C_n$. Under that header, the row `3 carlitz:` holds $q^3+q^2+2q+1$ and the row
`3 macmahon:` holds $q^6+q^4+q^3+q^2+1$. Neither of those is $C_3$. The first
is $C_3(q,1)$. The second is $q^{3}C_3(q,q^{-1})$, which is not $C_3$ evaluated
anywhere. So the header is true of 4 of the 22 rows.

**Why it is wrong.** This is the case the skill describes, including its tell.
The parameter `specialisation` takes three *names*, `n` repeats under each of
them, and no one symbol fits every row. The header $C_n$ avoids the word
`value` only by being false for most rows. None of the three good answers
applies:

* The rows are not *parts of one object*: nobody needs $C_n(q,1)$ in order to
  read $C_n(q,t)$.
* They are not *one number in two conventions*. The Carlitz–Riordan and
  MacMahon polynomials are different polynomials that happen to share a name.
* `specialisation` is not an argument.

The skill lists what is left, "a polynomial and a different polynomial of the
same object ... is two tables". A specialisation of $C_n(q,t)$ is that. The
proposal (`agents/table-ideas/BATCH-2026-09-12T1922.md`, §4) chose one table
because "the three are one object and two specialisations of it". That argues
the objects are related, and the skill says relatedness belongs in `Similar
tables`. There, the relation can be written as a sentence: "the specialisation
$t=1$", or "$q^{\binom n2}C_n(q,q^{-1})$, the Gaussian binomial quotient".

**Why the audit passed it.** The grid rule skips parameters whose values do
not all appear under every value of the other parameters. The `qt` rows stop at
$n=6$ while the other two go on to $n=11$, so the grid is $9\times3=27$ cells
against $22\times1.05=23.1$ entries, and the rule stops. The header rule only
reacts to generic words like `value`, and $C_n$ is not one. Both checks were
built to catch this table, and both miss it on a technicality.

**Smallest fix.** This is a person's decision, so here are both ways:

* *Split* (my recommendation). Make three tables:
  * "$q,t$-Catalan numbers $C_n(q,t)$", 4 rows.
  * "Carlitz–Riordan $q$-Catalan numbers", 9 rows.
  * "MacMahon $q$-Catalan numbers", 9 rows.

  Each should have its own definition and header, the same tags, and `Similar
  tables` naming each specialisation. Both one-variable titles contain
  "$q$-Catalan", so a reader typing that still finds both, and the naming
  collision the proposal worried about is stated where it belongs.

  A two-table split, $q,t$ against the two $q$-Catalan conventions, is a
  defensible middle course because of the shared name. It still puts two
  different polynomials under one header, though.
* *Keep one table.* Then the audit's own advice applies: "say so in the
  definition". The Definition has to define all three kinds of row (see 3),
  the header has to be something true of every row, and the rows need labels
  (see 2). I could not find a header that fits every row, and that is the
  argument for splitting.

## 2. Rows are labelled with keys: `qt:`, `carlitz:`, `macmahon:`

**What a reader sees.** Every row prints the parameter key as its label, in
lower case and ending in a colon. The column heading, and the parameter list,
say "specialisation — specialisation (one of `qt`, `carlitz` and `macmahon`)".
The word appears twice because `display` and `title` are the same string. The
Comments then use the keys as nouns: "The `carlitz` rows store $C_n(q,1)$",
"the specialisation parameter records which convention a row uses".

**Why it is wrong.** A key is an address, not a word, and the skill says not to
name a parameter by its key in prose. A reader holding MacMahon's polynomial
sees `macmahon:` and has to scroll to the Comments to learn what it stands for.

**Smallest fix.** Give the parameter per-value labels. The renderer reads
`Parameters.specialisation.values`, and I checked that it works in preview:

    values:
      qt: $C_n(q,t)$
      carlitz: Carlitz–Riordan $C_n(q)$
      macmahon: MacMahon $\tilde C_n(q)$

The rows then read "3 / Carlitz–Riordan $C_n(q)$: q^3 + q^2 + 2*q + 1". Also
give `display` a word different from `title`, or a symbol, and in Comments name
the polynomials instead of the keys. If the table is split, this finding goes
away.

## 3. The Definition points at a formula whose statistics are defined nowhere

**What a reader sees.** "The $q,t$-Catalan polynomial $C_n(q,t)$ is the
Dyck-path generating function in (1)." Formula (1) is
$\sum_{D} q^{\operatorname{dinv}(D)}t^{\operatorname{area}(D)}$. Nothing in the
table says what $\operatorname{dinv}$ or $\operatorname{area}$ is.

**Why it is wrong.** The Definition should let two people build the same
table, and it cannot do that while it depends on a statistic it never defines.
Area has one obvious reading. Dinv is not a statistic a reader can guess: it is
defined differently in different sources, through arms and legs of cells or
through the area sequence, and without one of those definitions a reader cannot
check a row. The skill also says
to "define notation where you use it". The sentence also covers only 4 of the
22 rows. The Carlitz and MacMahon rows are defined only in Formulas (2) and (3)
and again in Comments. Finally, it sends the reader to "(1)" before they have
seen any formulas, since the Definition renders above Parameters and Formulas.

**Smallest fix.** Put the definition in the sentence itself, in the convention
the generator uses (`_dinv`, `_area_sequences`):

> For a Dyck path $D$ of semilength $n$ with area sequence
> $(a_1,\dots,a_n)$, $a_1=0$, $0\leq a_{i+1}\leq a_i+1$, let
> $\operatorname{area}(D)=\sum a_i$ and let $\operatorname{dinv}(D)$ be the
> number of pairs $i<j$ with $a_i-a_j\in\{0,1\}$. Then
> $C_n(q,t)=\sum_D q^{\operatorname{dinv}(D)}t^{\operatorname{area}(D)}$.

If the table stays whole, add one more sentence defining the two one-variable
rows. That makes Formulas (2) and (3) redundant; (3) can keep only the
$q$-binomial identity.

## 4. `GarsiaHaiman` is listed and never cited

**What a reader sees.** Reference [2], Garsia and Haiman 1996, sits at the
bottom of the page, and nothing in the text points to it.

**Why it is wrong.** It is the paper that introduced $C_n(q,t)$, and the table
treats it as furniture: "a bibliography nobody points at".

**Smallest fix.** Cite it where the object is introduced. Either add "introduced
by Garsia and Haiman CITE{GarsiaHaiman}" to a comment, or put it beside the
Haglund citation in Formula (1).

## 5. The `small-cases` comment is a remark about the website

**What a reader sees.** "Rows with $n\leq2$ are not stored as entries. Their
values are given by (6), and they are too small to identify this family on
their own."

**Why it is wrong.** The final clause is about how useful a search hit would
be, which is the kind of apology the skill tells authors to leave out. The rest
of the comment repeats `complete-note`, which already says "the omitted rows
with $n\leq2$ are the closed forms in (6)".

**Smallest fix.** Delete the comment. Formula (6) and `complete-note` already
say everything it says.

## 6. Noted only

* **Programs** gives only `qt_catalan_number(7)`. A reader who wants one more
  MacMahon row would be helped by `q_catalan_number(12)`, and a reader who wants
  a Carlitz row by `qt_catalan_number(12).subs(t=1)`. I checked both against
  stored rows. The comment "one row past the two-variable range here" goes
  stale when the range changes.
* **Formulas.** The proposal asked for Haglund's area/bounce form and the
  $\langle\nabla e_n,e_n\rangle$ definition next to the dinv/area form. Neither
  is in the table, and a reader who computed $C_n(q,t)$ either way would
  recognise the table by that formula.
* **Neighbours.** No published table is about Catalan numbers, Dyck paths or
  $q$-binomials: `api/lookup` for those words returns nothing related. The
  natural `Similar tables` entries are the Macdonald–Kostka and Hall–Littlewood
  drafts T260 and T261, through $\nabla e_n$, once they are published. The
  corpus tag `generating function` fits and is not used.
