# T256 critique: Dimensions of the irreducible representations of the exceptional simple Lie algebras

Read 2026-09-16. T256 is a private draft — `/T256` answers 404 both anonymously
and with the owner's bearer token, so it was read through
`GET /api/table?id=T256` and rendered in eight `/preview?table=` pieces. It
holds 599 entries, `dim V(λ)` for the nonzero dominant weights of $G_2$, $F_4$,
$E_6$, $E_7$ and $E_8$, two parameters (`algebra`, a named Dynkin type;
`weight`, a tuple), type `Z`, rigour `exact`, `complete: no` with a per-algebra
bound, and no entry comments. The audit reports nothing, and I agree with it.

**This table reads well.** The three things I checked hardest all held: it is
one table and not two wearing one title; every one of its 56 TeX fragments
typesets under the package set the site actually loads; and the `Programs`
snippet runs and returns the number its comment claims. Measurements are in
"How it was read" at the end.

What is left is four small things, three of them prose.

- **1 and 2 are worth doing.** 1 is a convention the page states, but one
  section away from the notation it governs — a reader who stops at the
  parameter block cannot tell which of $E_6$'s two 27s is which. 2 is a
  sentence in `rigour details` that lists eight numbers in an order the reader
  has just been told to read differently, and I confirmed the mismatch in Sage.
- **3 and 4 are worth doing while the file is open**: one clause of editorial
  advice in a comment, and a symbol the definition uses without binding.
- **5 and 6 are noted only because I noticed them.** 6 is not this table's
  fault at all.

## 1. The `weight` parameter never says what the tuple means

What a reader sees, in the *Parameters* block — the second thing on the page
after the numbers themselves:

> $\lambda$ —  highest weight (a nonzero tuple $(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ of
> nonnegative integers, where $r$ is the rank of $\mathfrak g$)

and, in the *Numbers* column beside it, rows reading `(0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1): 27`
and `(1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0): 27`.

Why it is wrong. Nothing in the definition or in the parameter block says the
tuple is the sequence of Dynkin labels — that $\lambda = \sum_{i=1}^r a_i
\varpi_i$ — nor in which order the nodes are numbered. Both facts are on the
page, in the first comment:

> Weights are written as Dynkin labels in Bourbaki node numbering, the
> numbering used by Sage's root-system data [1].

but a comment is a section below, and this is not a remark: it is the rule that
decides which row is which. Without it a reader looking at the two $E_6$ rows
above has no way to tell which 27 is the standard representation and which is
its dual, and a reader who wants to check one row in Sage does not know what to
feed it. The parameter block is where somebody looks to learn what a row's key
is, and there it says only "a tuple of nonnegative integers", which is true of
any labelling convention whatever.

The smallest change. One clause in `weight`'s `constraints`, leaving the
comment where it is:

> the Dynkin labels of $\lambda = \sum_{i=1}^r a_i\varpi_i$ in Bourbaki node
> numbering: a nonzero tuple $(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ of nonnegative integers, where
> $r$ is the rank of $\mathfrak g$

The comment then reads as the elaboration it is — it carries the worked $E_8$
example, which is the right thing for a comment to carry — rather than as the
only place the convention appears.

## 2. `rigour details` lists the fundamental dimensions in two different orders

What a reader sees, behind the "more" fold under *Data properties*:

> The fundamental representation dimensions are checked against the standard
> values $7,14$ for $G_2$, $26,52,273,1274$ for $F_4$, $27,78,351,2925,351,27$
> for $E_6$, $56,133,912,1539,8645,27664,365750$ for $E_7$, and
> $248,3875,30380,147250,2450240,6696000,146325270,6899079264$ for $E_8$.

Why it is wrong. The reader has just been told that this table numbers nodes
the Bourbaki way, and the $E_6$ list is palindromic — $27,78,351,2925,351,27$
— which is unmistakably a list *in node order*, not a list sorted by size. So
the natural reading is "these are $\dim V(\varpi_1),\ldots,\dim V(\varpi_r)$".
For $E_6$ that reading is right. For $F_4$, $E_7$ and $E_8$ it is wrong: those
three lists are sorted ascending. In Sage, on the exceptional types, with
`RootSystem(kind).ambient_space()` and Weyl's formula:

    F4   stated: [26, 52, 273, 1274]        Bourbaki: [52, 1274, 273, 26]
    E6   stated: [27, 78, 351, 2925, 351, 27]   Bourbaki: same
    E7   stated: [56, 133, 912, 1539, …]    Bourbaki: [133, 912, 8645, 365750, …]
    E8   stated: [248, 3875, 30380, …]      Bourbaki: [3875, 147250, 6696000, …]

Every list is the right multiset; only $E_6$'s is in the order the sentence
invites you to read it in. A reader who takes "$248$" as $\dim V(\varpi_1)$ for
$E_8$ gets the adjoint representation where the table's own Comment 1 says
$\dim V(1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)=3875$ — the page contradicts itself two screens apart.

The smallest change. Put all five lists in Bourbaki node order and say so, so
the sentence agrees with the table's own convention and with its $E_8$ example:

> … are checked, in Bourbaki node order, against the standard values $7,14$ for
> $G_2$, $52,1274,273,26$ for $F_4$, $27,78,351,2925,351,27$ for $E_6$, …

(Saying "as a set" instead is cheaper but weaker, and does not quite work: the
$E_6$ list has repeats.)

## 3. The last comment ends with advice rather than a fact

What a reader sees, as Comment 4:

> The classical simple Lie algebras are not included. Their familiar families
> of representations often have binomial-coefficient or short product
> dimensions, so their small values are better found through their own
> classical constructions.

Why it is wrong. The first sentence is a fact about this table's scope and
belongs. The second is a claim about where a reader would be better off
looking — how findable a number is, what a reader should conclude — which the
standard puts outside the mathematics and which reads as apology for a decision
that needed none. The hedge "often" gives it away: it is not asserting anything
checkable.

The smallest change. Stop after the first sentence.

## 4. The definition uses $V_{\mathfrak g}(\lambda)$ without saying what it is

What a reader sees:

> For an exceptional complex simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ [1], this table
> stores $\dim V_{\mathfrak g}(\lambda)$ for nonzero dominant integral highest
> weights $\lambda$.

Why it is wrong. Two things, both small. The sentence introduces
$V_{\mathfrak g}(\lambda)$ and never binds it: the words "irreducible
representation" appear in the title and in the keywords, but nowhere in the
definition, so the one sentence whose job is to define asks the reader to carry
the meaning down from the title. And the subscript is not used consistently —
the column header and the definition write $\dim V_{\mathfrak g}(\lambda)$
while the completeness note, Comment 1 and the Weyl formula all write
$\dim V(\lambda)$. A reader meeting "Table is complete: no (it holds every
nontrivial irreducible representation with $\dim V(\lambda)\leq10^6$ for
$G_2$…)" has to decide whether that is the same quantity as the column they are
looking at. It is; nothing says so.

The smallest change. Bind the symbol once and pick one form of it:

> For an exceptional complex simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ [1], let
> $V_{\mathfrak g}(\lambda)$ be the irreducible representation of $\mathfrak g$
> with highest weight $\lambda$. This table stores $\dim V_{\mathfrak
> g}(\lambda)$ for every nonzero dominant integral weight $\lambda$.

and drop the subscript in the other three places, or add it.

## 5. Two closer siblings than Schur polynomials are not in `Similar tables`

Noting only. The two entries that are there are both true — T220 does use the
same exceptional Dynkin types, and T122 does specialise to $GL_n$ dimensions —
but T233 (*Ehrhart polynomials of the root polytopes*) and T243 (*Ehrhart
$h^*$-polynomials of the root polytopes*) are indexed by a parameter whose
constraints read "$A_n$ with $n\geq1$, …, or one of $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, $F_4$,
$G_2$" — the same five objects, one number each, with a positive-root
computation behind them. That is a shorter reach than the type $A$ characters.
One line each if somebody is editing the field anyway.

## 6. The `Programs` snippet does not run under `sage -python`

Noting only, and this one is not T256's to fix. The snippet opens

    from sage.combinat.root_system.root_system import RootSystem
    from sage.rings.rational_field import QQ

which is correct in a Sage session — everything is preloaded there, and exec'd
in a Sage namespace the snippet ran and gave `dimension = 248`, exactly its
comment. Saved to a file and run as `sage -python prog.py` it fails on line 1
with `AttributeError: partially initialized module 'sage.rings.integer_ring'
has no attribute 'ZZ' (most likely due to a circular import)`. The `language`
field says `Sage`, so the snippet is right for the environment it names.

It is worth writing down because it is a corpus-wide habit, not one table's:
T233 and T243 open their Sage programs the same way (`from sage.arith.misc
import binomial`), and that pair fails under `sage -python` too, with a
different error. `from sage.all import RootSystem, QQ` works in both. I have
put this in `agents/lessons/proposals/20260916T024304Z-critique.md` rather than
proposing an edit to T256 alone.

## How it was read

- **Fetching.** The `curl -s --socks5-hostname 127.0.0.1:1080` of the critique
  prompt failed: nothing is listening on 1080 on this builder and `ALL_PROXY`
  is empty. `curl --noproxy '*' https://numberdb.org/…` answers 200. That is
  already in `docs/agent-environment.md` under the T221 note; a line has been
  added there because the prompt's own command is the one that fails.
- **The audit.** `GET /api/table/T256/audit` with the owner's key:
  `{"findings": [], "clean": true}`. There is no Django in this checkout, so
  `manage.py audit_table` was not available; the endpoint runs the same
  `findings_for`.
- **Rendering.** Eight `/preview?table=` pieces, each under the 4,094-byte
  request-line limit, each carrying `Parameters` and at least one real entry,
  all 200. Between them they cover Definition, Parameters, Keywords, Tags,
  Comments, Formulas, Programs, Similar tables, Links and Data properties. No
  `argument ()`, no swallowed section, no `Math input error`, no `CITE-broken`
  or `HREF-broken` in any piece that carried the links its `CITE`s name. The
  header row of the numbers block reads `$\mathfrak g$ | $\lambda$ |
  $\dim V_{\mathfrak g}(\lambda)$`, in that order, so the parameter order and
  the `Numbers` nesting agree and nothing is shifted a column. The trailing
  colon on `(1, 0):` is how the site renders the last parameter group on every
  table (T104 does the same), not a stray character.
- **Typesetting.** All 56 `$…$` fragments in the prose were converted with
  MathJax 3; 0 errors. A control, `$\frac{1}{2$`, failed with "Missing close
  brace". There is no bare `<` or `&` anywhere in the document, so the fault
  that ate a section on an earlier table cannot happen here — `\leq` is used
  throughout.
- **The program.** Exec'd in a Sage namespace under `agents/sage.sh`: ran, gave
  `dimension = 248`, which is what its comment says and what the stored
  `E8 (0,0,0,0,0,0,0,1)` row holds. The fundamental-dimension orders of finding
  2 came from the same run.
- **It is one table.** `algebra` has five values that are *names*, which is the
  shape the audit asks about, and it is the good kind: it names *what the
  number is of* — one dimension per (algebra, weight) pair, as T233 holds one
  Ehrhart polynomial per root system — not *which quantity is taken*. The tell
  agrees: the value column is headed `$\dim V_{\mathfrak g}(\lambda)$`, a symbol
  true of every row, not the word `value` a table of two quantities falls back
  on. (The audit's split check never ran here anyway: five values is past
  `MOST_LABELS = 4`. Had it run it would have exempted the table at the next
  test — "the same table twice" means every combination of the other
  parameters appears under every label, and no weight is shared between two
  algebras at all, since the tuples have different lengths.)
- **The parameters describe the family, not the run.** `algebra` is the five
  exceptional types; `weight` is *every* nonzero dominant weight. The finite
  range that was actually computed lives in `complete: no` and its note, per
  algebra. That is the commonest fault in this slot and it is absent.
- **A reader arriving with one of these numbers is served.** Of the 491
  distinct values, `GET /api/lookup` finds 16 anywhere in the public corpus
  today, and those are the small and accidental ones — 7, 14, 26, 27, 52, 56,
  64, 351, 378, 1728, 4096, 6480, 15625, 85293, 262144, 362880, mostly in T88's
  curve counts and T62's $abc$ qualities. 248, 3875, 27664 and 146325270 match
  nothing. Only 12 of the 599 entries are at most 100, so this is not the
  "handful of small integers that match everything" the standard warns about.
- **Links.** Both `Similar tables` slugs answer 200. The seven `Links` URLs are
  the two Wikipedia pages, the Sage reference and five OEIS A-numbers, all
  reached by `CITE` from `rigour details` or the definition.

## What the audit could not have seen

Nothing it reports is wrong. Of the six findings above, five are outside what a
rule could reach from the document alone:

- 1 and 4 are about where a true statement sits and whether a symbol was bound,
  which needs a reader, not a checker;
- 2 needed the fundamental weights computed in Sage and compared against a
  sentence's word order;
- 3 is a judgement about which of two sentences is a fact;
- 6 needed the snippet run twice, two ways, and two other tables' snippets read.

Finding 5 is the kind a rule could reach — a corpus table sharing a parameter's
value set and not cross-linked — but it would fire noisily on tables where the
omission is deliberate.
