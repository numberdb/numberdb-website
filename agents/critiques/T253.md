# T253 critique: Wigner $6j$ symbols

Read 2026-09-16. T253 is a private draft. It holds 485 real values at 485
parameter tuples $(j_1,\dots,j_6)$, one value each, of type `R`, rigour
`proven`, 100 significant digits, with the 147 rational values written exactly.
No entry has a comment. The audit reports nothing.

**The row set is exactly right, the page renders, and every fragment of TeX
typesets.** Those are the three things I checked hardest and all three held:

- Enumerating every admissible tetrahedral class with all $j_i\leq7/2$ in
  independent Python gives 490 classes, of which four vanish and one is the
  all-zero symbol. The remaining 485 are **set-equal** to the stored keys — no
  missing row, no extra row. The page's own description of its range is
  therefore true of the data.
- Every prose section rendered through `/preview` at 200 with no
  `Math input error`, no `argument ()`, no `CITE-broken`, no `HREF-broken` and
  no empty parameter line. The numbers block renders as a flat seven-column
  table, six narrow parameter columns and the value, which is the right shape
  for six numeric parameters.
- All 36 `$...$` fragments typeset under MathJax 3, both with `AllPackages`
  and with the package set the site actually loads; a control with an unclosed
  brace failed.

What is wrong is prose. One sentence on the page is false of this table, one
`rigour details` claim is wider than what was done, and the page never gives a
reader a way to compute a single value.

- **1 and 2 are the reason to open the file.** A comment states something
  that three of 485 rows satisfy, and `rigour details` names a source no
  reader can reach and that covers under half the table.
- **3 to 7 are worth doing**: no formula evaluates a $6j$ symbol, the
  canonicalisation is stated without the fact that makes it work, and three
  fields in the omission story each say half of it.
- **8 is for the reviewer rather than the author**: T253 must not be
  published before T252, and the audit will only say so afterwards.
- **9 to 12 are small** and worth doing while the file is open.
- **The notes at the end are there only because I noticed them.**
- **I agree with the audit, which found nothing, and there is one thing it
  cannot see.** See "What `audit_table` says".

## 1. `comment-algebraic` says many of these numbers are also in T35. Three of the 485 are

What a reader sees, in Comments:

> Every nonzero Wigner $6j$ symbol is a rational number or a rational multiple
> of the square root of a rational number. Many of the simplest radicals are
> also in the table of algebraic numbers of degree 2.

The first sentence is true and is the useful one. The second is a claim about
how much of this table a reader will find next door, and it is testable.

Why it is wrong. The 485 rows carry 158 distinct irrational magnitudes. Every
one of them is $\sqrt{1/N}$ for an integer $N$, so its minimal polynomial is
$Nx^2-1$. T35 holds the roots of $a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0$ with **every coefficient in
$[-5,5]$** — I fetched the whole table and read the key set — so the only
values of this table it can possibly hold are $N\le5$:

| value | minimal polynomial | in T35 |
|---|---|---|
| $1/\sqrt2 = 0.70710678\ldots$ | $2x^2-1$ | yes |
| $1/\sqrt3 = 0.57735026\ldots$ | $3x^2-1$ | yes |
| $1/\sqrt5 = 0.44721359\ldots$ | $5x^2-1$ | yes |
| $1/\sqrt6 = 0.40824829\ldots$ | $6x^2-1$ | **no** |
| $1/\sqrt7,\ 1/\sqrt8,\ 1/\sqrt{10},\ 1/\sqrt{12},\ \dots$ | | **no** |

Three magnitudes, on **three rows of 485**. Independently: `/api/lookup` on
`0.4082482904638630163` and `0.2236067977499789696` returns nothing from the
whole corpus, and on `0.7071067811865475244` and `0.5773502691896257645`
returns T35 among the hits. "Many" is not what three of 485 is, and the cutoff
is not soft — it is T35's coefficient bound, and every value past it is absent.

The sentence is inherited from T252, where it *is* true: the T252 critique
spot-checked six of its radicals and found four in T35. It was copied to a
table whose radicals are mostly $\sqrt{1/N}$ for large $N$, and it stopped
being true in the copying.

Smallest change: drop the second sentence. The `Similar tables` entry already
carries the relation, correctly and without a quantity —
"the non-rational Wigner $6j$ symbols are quadratic algebraic numbers" — and
that is a theorem rather than a count, so it stays true however T35 grows. If
a sentence is wanted in Comments, the honest one names the three:

> Every nonzero Wigner $6j$ symbol is a rational number or a rational multiple
> of the square root of a rational number. Three of them, $-1/\sqrt2$,
> $1/\sqrt3$ and $1/\sqrt5$, are also in
> HREF{Algebraic_numbers_of_degree_2}[the table of algebraic numbers of degree 2].

Worth doing. It is the one sentence on the page a reader could act on and be
wrong about.

## 2. `rigour details` names a source a reader cannot reach, over a range it does not cover, and omits the check the generator actually makes

What a reader sees, under "How they were obtained", after the fold:

> The values are checked against Richard Mathar's exact `6jSymb` table,
> against the zero-entry formula (2), and against the finite sum of Wigner
> $3j$ symbols in (3).

Three things.

**The source is not citable from the page.** "Richard Mathar's exact `6jSymb`
table" appears in no `Link`, no `Reference` and nowhere else in the document.
There is no `References` section at all. A reader who wants to repeat the
check has a person's name and a filename. It took me a guess at
`github.com/rmathar/Clebsch-Gordan` to find it. The skill is explicit that a
reference is for citing, and this is the one claim on the page that rests on
somebody else's numbers.

**The check is real, and it covers 232 of the 485 rows.** I parsed `6jSymb`
(2267 lines, 2264 of them symbols, 1759 distinct tetrahedral classes, each
value written as $\pm[p/q]^{1/2}$, or as `0` on eleven lines), canonicalised
both sides the same way, and compared:

    rows with a 6jSymb value:  232 of 485
    agree:                     232
    disagree:                    0

So the claim is true where it applies and the sentence does not say where that
is. 253 rows — 52% of the table — have no counterpart in that file. As a
bonus, three of the four non-trivial zeros this table omits are listed there
explicitly as `= 0`:

    {2 2 2 ; 3/2 3/2 3/2} = 0
    {3 2 2 ; 1 2 2} = 0
    {7/2 7/2 3 ; 5/2 3/2 3} = 0

which is worth more than the agreement, and is not mentioned either.

**The check the generator makes is not listed.** `self_check()` in the
attached `generate.py` does three things before any entry is returned, and
runs them over the whole range:

1. every row is recomputed at all 24 of its tetrahedral images and required to
   overlap (not mentioned in `rigour details`);
2. the zero-entry closed form, on the rows where it applies (mentioned);
3. the finite sum over four $3j$ symbols (mentioned).

There is no Mathar comparison anywhere in the file. Whether it was done in a
scratch script or not, the file attached to the table is what a reader
downloads, and it does not contain the check the table advertises. Two of the
three checks it does contain share the file's own `factorial_ratio`,
`triangle` and `phase_from_sum` helpers, so **the attached generator makes no
comparison against an independent implementation at all**.

Meanwhile `Programs` and `Links[3]` both point at Sage's `wigner_6j`, which is
independent, is on the page, and is what T252's `rigour details` uses ("the
Wigner rows are checked against Sage's exact `wigner_3j` function on every
row"). I ran it on five rows spanning the range, in the table's own column
order:

    (1,   1,   0,  0,   0,   1  ) -> sqrt(1/3)   = 0.5773502691896257   stored 0.57735026918962576450...
    (1/2, 1/2, 0,  1/2, 1/2, 0  ) -> -1/2                               stored -1/2
    (7/2, 7/2, 3,  7/2, 7/2, 3  ) -> 31/616                             stored 31/616
    (1,   1/2, 1/2, 0,  1/2, 1/2) -> 1/2                                stored 1/2
    (1/2, 1/2, 0,  0,   0,   1/2) -> -sqrt(1/2)  = -0.7071067811865476  stored -0.70710678118654752440...
    (2,   2,   2,  3/2, 3/2, 3/2) -> 0                                  omitted, as a zero

All five agree and the omitted zero is confirmed.

Smallest change: add the source to `Links` and cite it, say what it covers,
and name the symmetry check —

> `Links: Mathar: {title: "R. J. Mathar, SO(3) 6j vector coupling
> coefficients", url: https://github.com/rmathar/Clebsch-Gordan}`

> ... Every row is recomputed at all 24 of its tetrahedral images and the
> results are required to agree. The 232 rows that appear in Richard Mathar's
> exact table CITE{Mathar} agree with it exactly. The rows with a zero entry
> are checked against (2), and every row against the finite sum of Wigner $3j$
> symbols in (3).

Worth doing. `rigour details` is the field that says how much to trust the
numbers, and this is the only claim on the page that a reader is invited to
check and cannot.

## 3. Nothing on the page evaluates a $6j$ symbol

A reader holding $0.2236067977\ldots$ arrives, finds it is
$\begin{Bmatrix}2&2&0\\3/2&3/2&1/2\end{Bmatrix}$ — and eight other rows
besides, which is the database working — and wants to know what that means and
how it was got. The page offers:

- a Definition naming the object;
- `formula-racah`, which converts it to a Racah $W$ that is never defined;
- `formula-zero`, a special case that applies to 89 of the 485 rows;
- `formula-3j-sum`, a sentence saying it is a sum of products of four $3j$
  symbols, without the sum.

Nothing computes anything. `rigour details` says the generator "evaluates
Racah's finite sum" and Racah's finite sum is nowhere on the page. This is the
same gap the T252 critique named in its finding 3, carried into the sibling
table.

It is not even a new source: DLMF 34.4, which the Definition already cites as
`[1]`, is headed "Definition: $6j$ Symbol" and consists of exactly the sum
`formula-3j-sum` describes in words (34.4.1), with the Racah single sum at
34.4.2 beside it.

Smallest change: give `formula-3j-sum` the formula instead of a description of
it, or add Racah's single sum as a fourth entry, citing `[1]` where the table
already points. Either makes the table reproducible from its own page.

Worth doing.

## 4. `comment-symmetry` gives the canonical form without saying what the symmetries are, or that the value is unchanged by them

What a reader sees, in Comments:

> Entries use the lexicographically largest $(j_1,j_2,j_3,j_4,j_5,j_6)$ among
> the 24 tetrahedral symmetries of the Wigner $6j$ symbol.

Why it is wrong. This is a statement about the key set, and it leaves out the
fact that makes the key set complete. Two things a reader has to supply:

- **What the 24 are.** Permuting the three columns, and swapping the upper and
  lower entries of any two columns. Nothing on the page says this, so a reader
  who has $\begin{Bmatrix}1&2&1\\1&1&2\end{Bmatrix}$ and wants its row cannot
  work out which of the 485 keys to ask for. The entry address
  `/T253?entry=j1,...,j6` is the table's own citation format, and it is
  unusable without the rule.
- **That the value is the same at all 24.** "the 24 tetrahedral symmetries of
  the Wigner $6j$ symbol" implies it to somebody who already knows; to anybody
  else it reads as a tie-breaking convention, leaving open whether the other
  23 arrangements are different numbers this table does not hold. They are not
  — that invariance is *why* storing one of each is lossless, and it is what
  the generator asserts on every row.

Smallest change: two clauses.

> A Wigner $6j$ symbol is unchanged by the 24 tetrahedral symmetries:
> permuting the three columns, and swapping the upper and lower entries of any
> two columns. Each class is stored once, under the lexicographically largest
> $(j_1,j_2,j_3,j_4,j_5,j_6)$, so a symbol in another arrangement is found by
> applying those symmetries first.

Worth doing.

## 5. "The all-zero symbol is also omitted, since its value is $1$" gives a reason that is a reason to keep it

What a reader sees, in Comments:

> Rows whose Wigner $6j$ symbol is zero are omitted. The all-zero symbol is
> also omitted, since its value is $1$.

Why it is wrong. A value of $1$ is not a ground for leaving a row out; it is a
value. As written the second sentence is a non sequitur, and a reader is left
guessing what the real reason was. There is a good reason and it is a
different one: $1$ matches every search there is, and the skill's rule about
values that are a handful of small integers is exactly this case.

And "nonzero" is doing two jobs within two lines of itself. The neighbouring
`complete-note` renders as

> Table is complete: no (it holds every nonzero canonical tetrahedral class
> with all $j_i\leq 7/2$, except the all-zero symbol)

where "nonzero" is about the *value* and "all-zero" is about the *arguments*.
The exception clause is needed precisely because the all-zero symbol is
nonzero, and a reader meeting the two words in one sentence has to work that
out.

Smallest change: say the real reason, and use two different words.

> Rows whose Wigner $6j$ symbol is zero are omitted, as is
> $\begin{Bmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{Bmatrix}$, whose value is $1$ and would
> match every search.

and in `complete-note`, "it holds every canonical tetrahedral class with all
$j_i\leq7/2$ whose value is not zero, other than the symbol with every
$j_i=0$".

Worth doing.

## 6. The triangle conditions are necessary and not sufficient, and nothing says so

What a reader sees, in Comments:

> (1) The four triads $(j_1,j_2,j_3)$, $(j_1,j_5,j_6)$, $(j_4,j_2,j_6)$ and
> $(j_4,j_5,j_3)$ satisfy the triangle inequalities and have integer sums.
> (3) Rows whose Wigner $6j$ symbol is zero are omitted.

A reader with a tuple that is not here reads (1), checks the four triads, finds
they hold, and concludes the tuple is out of range or that something is wrong.

Why it is wrong. Admissibility does not imply the symbol is nonzero. In this
table's own range there are 490 admissible canonical classes and **four** of
them vanish:

    2,2,2,3/2,3/2,3/2
    3,2,2,1,2,2
    7/2,3,3/2,1,3/2,3
    7/2,7/2,3,5/2,3/2,3

(computed in exact rational arithmetic from Racah's sum; three of the four are
listed as `= 0` in Mathar's file, and Sage returns `0` for the first). So the
490 split as 485 rows, 4 non-trivial zeros, and the all-zero symbol. Nothing on
the page tells a reader that the second group exists, and comment (1) as
phrased invites the wrong inference. T252's repaired `comment-zeros` says
exactly this for its own six vanishing tuples; T253 has the same situation and
does not.

Comment (1) has a second, smaller problem: it names no subject. "The four
triads ... satisfy the triangle inequalities" — of what? Of every entry here.
A reader will guess right, but the sentence does not say it, and it does not
say that a symbol failing any of them is zero, which is the useful direction.

Smallest change: fold the two comments together.

> A Wigner $6j$ symbol is zero unless each of the four triads
> $(j_1,j_2,j_3)$, $(j_1,j_5,j_6)$, $(j_4,j_2,j_6)$ and $(j_4,j_5,j_3)$
> satisfies the triangle inequalities and has an integer sum. That condition is
> necessary and not sufficient: of the 490 admissible classes in range, four
> vanish anyway, the smallest being
> $\begin{Bmatrix}2&2&2\\3/2&3/2&3/2\end{Bmatrix}$. Rows whose value is zero
> are omitted.

Worth doing. Findings 5 and 6 are the same two comments read twice and should
be fixed together.

## 7. `formula-zero`'s "If one entry is zero" does not describe the identity under it

What a reader sees, in Formulas:

> (2) If one entry is zero, then
> $\begin{Bmatrix}a&b&c\\b&a&0\end{Bmatrix}=(-1)^{a+b+c}/\sqrt{(2a+1)(2b+1)}$.

Two things a reader has to guess.

**The antecedent does not match the consequent.** "If one entry is zero" would
admit $\begin{Bmatrix}0&j_2&j_3\\j_4&j_5&j_6\end{Bmatrix}$, and the formula
below it applies only when the zero is in the last position *and* the other
four have already been forced into the pattern $a,b\,/\,b,a$. That forcing is
the content — a $6j$ symbol with an entry zero is nonzero only in that shape —
and the sentence states it as a hypothesis rather than as the conclusion it is.

**The letters.** The table's six parameters are $j_1,\dots,j_6$ and this is the
only place $a$, $b$, $c$ appear. They are never defined. A reader checking a
row has $j$'s in front of them in six columns and has to map them onto three
letters whose positions in the array are the only clue.

The identity is right — I checked it against three rows, including
$\begin{Bmatrix}1/2&1/2&0\\0&0&1/2\end{Bmatrix}=-1/\sqrt2$, which needs a
rearrangement before the formula applies, and that is exactly the reader's
difficulty.

Smallest change: state it as a consequence, in the table's own letters.

> A Wigner $6j$ symbol with an entry $0$ is zero unless the two columns beside
> it are a transposed pair, and then
> $\begin{Bmatrix}j_1&j_2&j_3\\j_2&j_1&0\end{Bmatrix}
> =(-1)^{j_1+j_2+j_3}/\sqrt{(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1)}$.

Worth doing.

## 8. `HREF{T252}` answers 404 today, and the audit will only complain after publication

The Definition's companion, `formula-3j-sum` and the first `Similar tables`
row all point at `T252`, which is a private draft: `curl https://numberdb.org/T252`
returns 404. On the rendered page the link is
`<a class="HREF" href="T252">Wigner $3j$ symbols</a>` — a live-looking link to
nothing.

This is not a fault in the draft. `audit_table` has a check for it and the
check says nothing here, correctly, because it is gated on
`table.published`: a draft linking a draft is fine. But the gate means the
finding

> HREF{T252} points at a draft, which answers 404 to everybody; a published
> table must not link to one

can only appear *after* somebody has published T253. So it is a fact for
whoever reviews the two, not for the author: **T253 must be published after
T252, or at the same time.** The tables were built together and both are
offered, so this is a sequencing note rather than a repair.

## 9. `formula-3j-sum` is prose in the section for formulas, and cites the wrong one of its own two links

What a reader sees, in Formulas:

> (3) A Wigner $6j$ symbol is a finite signed sum of products of four Wigner
> $3j$ symbols [2].

The skill gives `Formulas` "identities, closed forms, generating functions,
recurrences", and "prose about them" as what it is not. This is a description
of a formula with no formula in it, and `rigour details` then cites it as
though it were one ("against the finite sum of Wigner $3j$ symbols in (3)") —
a reader following that citation to see what was checked finds a sentence.

The citation is also the further of the two available. `[2]` is Wikipedia;
`[1]` is DLMF 34.4, which is *headed* "Definition: $6j$ Symbol" and whose
34.4.1 is this sum. The table's own Definition already cites `[1]` for the
object; the formula that defines it should too.

Smallest change: write 34.4.1 out, or at minimum cite `[1]`. Overlaps with
finding 3 and should be fixed with it.

## 10. $W$ is used and never defined; `Links[3]` is never cited

Two half-findings of the same kind.

`formula-racah` reads "The Racah $W$ coefficient satisfies
$\{\dots\}=(-1)^{j_1+j_2+j_4+j_5}W(j_1j_2j_5j_4;j_3j_6)$". The identity is
right — it is Wikipedia's, at the link it cites. But nothing on the page says
what $W$ is, and the skill asks that a symbol appearing in a formula be
defined in the same table. A reader also has to read $W(j_1j_2j_5j_4;j_3j_6)$
as six juxtaposed arguments rather than two products; that is standard Racah
notation and still worth a comma or a thin space.

`Links[3]`, the Sage `wigner` reference page, carries no `CITE{Sage}` anywhere
in the document, so it renders as a numbered entry in the Links list that
nothing on the page points at. T252 cites the same link from `formula-cg`. The
natural home here is `rigour details` (finding 2) or the `Programs` block,
whose snippet is the one thing on the page a reader can run.

Small, both.

## 11. `complete-note` says what the range is and not why it is that range

It renders as "complete: no (it holds every nonzero canonical tetrahedral
class with all $j_i\leq 7/2$, except the all-zero symbol)". That is what; the
skill asks for the argument as well, because it is the sentence a reader
checking whether their own symbol belongs here actually reads. $7/2$ is a
defensible bound — it is where the $f$ shell stops, and it gives 485 entries,
about half the house target — and one clause saying so is the difference
between a range and a choice.

Also, "canonical tetrahedral class" is a phrase that appears nowhere else:
`comment-symmetry` says "the 24 tetrahedral symmetries" and never "class". If
finding 4 is done, this can lean on it.

Small.

## 12. The Parameters block says one thing six times, three times each

What a reader sees:

    $j_1$  —  first angular momentum ($j_1$ is a nonnegative integer or half-integer)
    $j_2$  —  second angular momentum ($j_2$ is a nonnegative integer or half-integer)
    ...
    $j_6$  —  sixth angular momentum ($j_6$ is a nonnegative integer or half-integer)

Each line names its own subscript twice — "first" is what the $1$ in $j_1$
says, and the constraint repeats the display symbol — and the six lines differ
only in the ordinal. Eighteen restatements of "a nonnegative integer or
half-integer at position $i$", and nothing about which entries are coupled to
which.

I am half in favour of leaving it. Unlike T252, where $j_3$ is "coupled
angular momentum" and $m$ "coupled magnetic quantum number", the six entries
of a $6j$ symbol really are interchangeable — that is the content of the 24
symmetries — so "first" through "sixth" is the honest answer and any role a
title gave one of them would be false. But the block earns that only if the
page says so, which is finding 4 again. With `comment-symmetry` repaired, a
reader reads the six flat lines and understands why they are flat.

The one thing the block could add cheaply is the joint condition, which
currently lives only in `comment-admissible`: a pointer from $j_6$'s
constraint, or the four triads stated once under $j_6$, would put it where a
reader looks for what the entries satisfy.

Small.

## What `audit_table` says

Nothing:

    {"tid": "T253", "title": "Wigner $6j$ symbols", "findings": [], "clean": true}

**I agree with all of it, and with one silence in particular.** The check the
prompt asks about — a parameter whose values are a handful of names, every
other parameter repeating under it — does not fire, and should not. All six
parameters are numbers, there is one quantity, and the value column is headed
with that quantity's own symbol,
$\begin{Bmatrix}j_1&j_2&j_3\\j_4&j_5&j_6\end{Bmatrix}$, rather than falling
back on the word `value`. This is one table. The header is also doing real
work: the six columns are headed $j_1$ to $j_6$ and the header tells a reader
how those six fit into the two rows of the array, which is the one thing the
flat layout otherwise loses. It typesets 13.7 ex wide by 5.4 ex tall and
appears once, in the header row, not on 485 rows — the fault T252 had with
`param-latex` is not repeated here.

Three things the audit cannot see, in descending order of how much they matter:

- **Findings 1 and 2.** `_values_also_in_another_table` samples this table's
  rows and asks whether another table holds the same numbers; it fires when
  the overlap is *large* and unacknowledged. `comment-algebraic` makes the
  opposite error — it asserts an overlap that is not there — and no check
  reads a claim about another table and tests it.
- **Finding 8.** The published-table-links-to-a-draft check is gated on
  `table.published`, so it is structurally unable to warn before the moment it
  is too late to warn.
- **Finding 10.** The `audit_table` docstring lists "notation used in a
  formula and defined nowhere in the table" among its checks and no code makes
  it, which `docs/agent-environment.md` already records from the T221 run.
  $W$ is a live instance.

## Noted only because I noticed

- **Two keywords name subjects the page never mentions.** `Ponzano-Regge
  model` and `Turaev-Viro invariant` are in `Keywords`, which is title weight
  in the index, and neither phrase appears in the Definition, the Comments,
  the Formulas or the Links. Somebody typing "Turaev-Viro invariant" lands
  here and reads nothing about it. Both are genuine homes for $6j$ symbols, so
  the fix is a clause in a comment rather than dropping the keywords.
- **The link title is less than the section's own.** `Links[1]` is titled
  "DLMF 34.4: Wigner 6j symbols"; the section is headed "Definition: $6j$
  Symbol". T252's equivalent says "DLMF 34.2: definition of the Wigner 3j
  symbol", which tells a reader what they will find. Not wrong, just quieter.
- **`generate.py` reaches into the client's private modules.** `fill_draft_once`
  imports `numberdb._generate._check_precision`, `._check_rigour`, `._producer`,
  `._run_name`, `._source_files` and `numberdb._write.Entries`, `.attach`,
  `.submit_entries`, `.to_text` to avoid "the client's empty upsert probe". The
  file is attached to the table and is what a later contributor runs to extend
  it; it will break on a client release that renames any of those. A comment
  saying which client version it was written against would cost one line.
- **65 of the 147 exact rows are rationals with denominator at most 12** —
  $1/6$ appears 8 times, $-1/10$ 11 times, $-1/4$, $-1/3$, $1/2$ and so on.
  These match a great deal and identify little. It is the design T252 already
  carries and was reviewed with, and the rationals are the true values of the
  quantity, so this is not a change I would make; it is the cost of the
  convention and worth knowing the size of.
- **The `&` inside `\begin{Bmatrix}` reaches the HTML unescaped**, in the
  Definition, both matrix formulas and the value-column header. Harmless here
  for the same reason as in T252: `&j`, `&b`, `&c`, `&a` and `&0` are not
  prefixes of any legacy named character reference, so a browser leaves them
  as text and MathJax sees the array. An `&` before `amp`, `lt`, `gt`, `copy`,
  `not` or `times` would not survive.
- **The `Programs` snippet is a row the table already holds.**
  `wigner_6j(1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1)` returns `1/6`, which is exactly what the
  comment says and exactly what the table stores at $(1,1,1,1,1,1)$ — I ran
  it. `Programs` exists for the reader who wants *one more* value, so an
  argument outside $j_i\leq7/2$ would demonstrate more, but there is nothing
  wrong with this one and it is the check a reader can make in five seconds.
