## Findings, ranked

### 1. Formula (3) and the rigour note use `\Sha`, which the page cannot typeset

What a reader sees: formula (3) is stored as
`\Omega_A R_A |\Sha(A)|\prod_p c_p`, and the rigour note has "analytic
order of $\Sha$". `\Sha` is the LMFDB's own macro for Ш, defined in the
LMFDB's MathJax configuration, not in MathJax. The bundle this site
serves contains no such macro (the string `Sha` does not occur in
`tex-svg.js`), `load-mathjax.js` defines no macros, and the bundle's
`noundefined` extension prints an unknown control sequence as its own
name in red. So the BSD formula, the one formula a reader of this table
most wants, shows a red `\Sha` in the middle of it, and the rigour note
shows another.

Why it is wrong: a formula is read as mathematics, and this one is not
rendered as such. The stored YAML is valid, which is why the audit is
silent; the fault lives only in the rendering.

Fix: write `|\text{Sha}(A)|` in formula (3) and `$\text{Sha}$` in the
rigour note, which is how the corpus already writes it in T69's BSD
comment (`|\text{Sha}(E)|`). Worth doing.

### 2. Ten entry comments print `(1)y`, `(x)y` or `(x^3)y`

What a reader sees, on the first screen: 597.a reads
"$y^2 + (1)y = x^5 + 2x^4 + \dots$", 709.a reads "$y^2 + (x)y = \dots$",
461.a reads "$y^2 + (x^3)y = \dots$". Five rows have `(1)y` (597.a,
797.a, 847.d, 971.a, 997.b), four have `(x)y` (709.a, 909.a, 925.a,
997.a) and one has `(x^3)y`.

Why it is wrong: `equation_text` in `generate.py` wraps $h(x)$ in
parentheses whatever it is. The parentheses are right for a sum and
wrong for a single term, and "$(1)y$" is not how anybody writes $y$. It
is the first mathematics a reader meets in a quarter of the rows.

Fix: in `equation_text`, parenthesise $h$ only when it has more than one
term, and write `y^2 + y` when $h=1$. One function, then republish.
Worth doing.

### 3. "The LMFDB class has 2 curves in this range." points at a range the reader was never given

What a reader sees: 13 of the 41 comments end with this sentence
("4 curves" for 961.a).

Why it is wrong: "this range" has no antecedent on the page. It means
the LMFDB's search range for genus 2 curves, which is a bound on the
discriminant that the table nowhere states, and an isogeny class may
contain curves outside it. The sentence is a fact about a database's
coverage, not about the class, and the skill asks a comment to state a
fact about the mathematics.

Fix: drop the sentence, since the table has one row per class and comment
(4) already says why. If the count is wanted, "The LMFDB lists 2 curves
in this class." says it without the pointer. One line in `entry_comment`.
Worth doing.

### 4. Formula (3) uses four symbols the table does not define

What a reader sees: "$\Omega_A R_A |\Sha(A)| \prod_p c_p /
|A(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}|^2$, with the factors named as in the
LMFDB BSD invariants [3]".

Why it is wrong: $\Omega_A$, $R_A$, $c_p$ and Ш are named only by a link
out. The rigour note, three sections down and behind a fold, does name
them in words ("period, regulator, Tamagawa product, analytic order of
Sha, torsion order"), so the table knows what they are and does not say
so where they are used. The skill's rule is that notation is defined in
the same table as the formula that uses it.

Fix: replace "with the factors named as in" by a clause: "where
$\Omega_A$ is the real period, $R_A$ the regulator, $c_p$ the Tamagawa
numbers and Ш$(A)$ the Tate-Shafarevich group CITE{LMFDBBSD}". One
sentence. Worth doing.

### 5. The model is written three ways

What a reader sees: comment (4) says each entry gives "its model
$y^2+h(x)y=f(x)$"; the rigour note says "the representative curve
$y^2+Q(x)y=P(x)$"; program (P1) uses `P` and `Q`; and formula (2) uses
$P_p$ for the local Euler polynomial, so $P$ is two things on one page.

Fix: use $f$ and $h$ throughout the prose, as the LMFDB does, and let
the program's `P` and `Q` stand, since PARI's `lfungenus2([P,Q])` is
what the reader will type; or say once in comment (4) that PARI's
`[P,Q]` is $[f,h]$. A word swap. Small, worth doing while finding 1 is
being fixed.

### 6. Comment (7) explains the method where a reader wanted the scope

What a reader sees: "Only odd conductors are listed. PARI's
documentation for `lfungenus2` says that when the conductor is even,
its valuation at $2$ may be incorrect [5]; those classes need the local
Euler factor at $2$ supplied separately before their values can be
computed by this method."

Why it matters: the first sentence is the caveat and belongs here. The
rest is about how the generator works, and "by this method" points at a
generator the page never names. The scope itself is understated: the
LMFDB has 68 even-conductor classes with $N\leq1000$ against the 41
listed, so a reader browsing for a class of conductor 196 or 256 is
looking at the smaller half of the family and is not told so.

Fix: keep "Only odd conductors are listed" with the reason in one
clause ("because PARI's `lfungenus2` does not yet compute the Euler
factor at $2$ reliably CITE{PARILfun}"), and move the sentence about
supplying the factor separately to the rigour note. Small.

## Noted only

* **The fold on the rigour note** shows one sentence before "more", and
  the sentence is "The generator uses the 41 odd-conductor LMFDB genus 2
  isogeny classes with conductor $N\leq1000$, grouped from 56 curve
  rows." That is the least informative sentence of the note; the
  method sentence that follows it is the one a reader wants first. Also
  "agreed with the LMFDB `leading_coeff` fields, including [4]" reads
  oddly, a citation being "included" in a set of fields. Reordering
  would fix both.
* **The complete-note** says the table holds every LMFDB class "whose
  analytic rank and leading coefficient are recorded in the LMFDB genus
  2 curve data". All 41 have both, so the clause excludes nothing and
  makes a reader wonder what was left out. It also says LMFDB three
  times in one clause.
* **The long url** is `/Special-values_of_genus_2_curves_over`: the slug
  generator drops `$L$` and `$\mathbb{Q}$` and leaves a leading hyphen
  and a dangling "over". That is the site's slugging, not the table's,
  and the skill already warns that mathematics is dropped from a slug;
  anybody writing `HREF{...}` to this table must copy the slug rather
  than guess it.
* **Similar tables** glosses "Zeros of Dirichlet L-series" as "stores
  zeros of another family of arithmetic $L$-functions", which is the
  weakest of the six relations; the other five are apt, and the three
  elliptic-curve $L$-value tables are exactly what this table should
  point at. There is no rank-0 elliptic table in the corpus to add.
* **Comment (6)**, "The factor $1/r!$ is part of the definition for every
  rank", is true and idle here, since every row has $r\leq1$ and the
  factor is 1 on all of them. A reader may take it as a hint that
  higher ranks are present.
