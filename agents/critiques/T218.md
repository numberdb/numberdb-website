## Findings, ranked

### 1. The definition does not define $R$ or the expansion

What a reader sees: "Let $f$ be a Maass cusp form of weight $0$ for
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, ordered by its positive spectral parameter
$R$ and normalized by $a_1=1$. This table gives the Fourier coefficient
$a_p$ at a prime $p$ in the expansion described by the LMFDB coefficient
knowl [1] for Maass forms."

Why it is wrong: three things a reader needs are not on the page or are
below the fold. Nothing says what $R$ is; T84 says it, the Laplace
eigenvalue is $1/4+R^2$, but T84 is linked only from the parameter
line, and "spectral parameter" in the definition is a plain phrase.
The expansion that fixes what $a_p$ means is formula (1), two sections
down, yet the definition sends the reader to an external knowl for it.
And nothing in the definition says $f$ is a Hecke eigenform, which is
what makes "the" form with a given $R$ a well-defined object up to
scalar and what makes formula (3) true; formula (3) has to supply the
word itself ("the normalized level-one Hecke eigenforms here"). A
single $f$ is also not "ordered": the family is.

Smallest change: "Let $f$ be a Maass cusp form of weight $0$ for
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ with Laplace eigenvalue $1/4+R^2$, $R>0$
(HREF to T84 on "spectral parameter"), which is a Hecke eigenform
normalized by $a_1=1$. This table gives its Fourier coefficient $a_p$
at the prime $p$ in the expansion CITE{formula-expansion}." The knowl
citation can move to comment (5), which is about the expansion's
symmetry, or stay in `Links` uncited.

### 2. Zeros of the Riemann zeta function is not similar to this table

What a reader sees: under `Similar tables`, "Zeros of the Riemann zeta
function, stores another numerical spectrum attached to an
$L$-function".

Why it is wrong: the rows here are Hecke eigenvalues, not a spectrum.
The spectrum attached to these forms is T84, and it is T84 that the
zeta zeros resemble, if anything does. The gloss says "another
spectrum" about a table that holds none.

Smallest change: drop the line. If a relation to zeta zeros is wanted
it belongs on T84.

### 3. `modular form` is the corpus's tag and this table does not carry it

What a reader sees: tags `number theory` and `L-function`. T84, the
table this one is built on, is tagged `modular form`; a reader browsing
that tag finds the spectral parameters of these forms and not their
coefficients.

Smallest change: add `modular form`. Whether `L-function` stays is a
judgement; formula (2) earns it.

## Noted only

- Comment (4) opens "The first parameter is a label". Name it: "$R'$ is
  a label, not an approximation to be used in a computation". The
  parameter line says the same thing and could carry the whole of
  comment (4) instead, leaving the comment to say only that the label
  is the first five characters of the LMFDB spectral parameter.
- The parameter line's link is captioned "T84", so a reader sees "the
  corresponding row of T84". The skill has the link carry the name;
  "the corresponding row of the table of spectral parameters" reads
  without the address.
- $K_{iR}$ in formula (1) is not named. "where $K_{iR}$ is the modified
  Bessel function of the second kind" with `HREF{Values_of_modified_
  Bessel_functions_of_the_second_kind}` costs one clause and links a
  family the corpus holds. Optional: T199 holds real orders and these
  are imaginary, so the link is to the function, not to any row.
- The first sentence of comment (5) repeats formula (1). The second
  sentence is the useful one.
- "Fricke sign $+1$" on all ten per-form comments is content-free at
  level $1$: the Fricke involution is in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$,
  so the sign is $+1$ for every form. Harmless, and true.
- The per-form comment's link text is the full label `1.0.1.1.1` while
  the address is `Maass/1.1`; both resolve on LMFDB and the page under
  `1.0.1.1.1` is titled "Maass form 1.1". A reader copying the address
  gets a different string from the one they read. Point the link at
  `Maass/1.0.1.1.1` and the two agree.
- The LMFDB download carries about 74 digits with a radius near
  $7\times10^{-68}$, and the table writes 60 digits with radius
  $10^{-60}$. The `rigour details` say "It writes $60$ significant
  digits" without saying why fewer than the source supports. A clause
  ("the source supports about 67, and 60 is the house precision") would
  make the sentence an argument. Not a fault.
- `rigour: proven` here means the intervals are LMFDB's certified ones,
  parsed exactly and widened. The `rigour details` say so plainly, and
  the reader who cares reads them. I would not change it.
- The `R` parameter's document type is `Symbolic`; T84 keys the same
  strings under `R'` with type `Z`. Nothing of this reaches the page.

a reader holding $a_3$ and $a_9$ wants, and comment (6) explains why
only the primes are stored by pointing at it. The `complete-note`
