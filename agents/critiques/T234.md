type `Q[]` and rigour `exact`: both forms for every $\Delta(k,n)$ with
$4\leq n\leq20$ and $2\leq k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. No entry carries a
comment. The longest entry is $L_{\Delta(9,20)}(t)$ at 682 characters, and the
entries block is about 52 KB.

**Summary.** The table reads well and renders cleanly. Nothing below stops it
being published. Findings 1 to 3 are worth doing, and each is a sentence or a
clause. Finding 4 is a small improvement. Section 5 is noted only.

## Findings, ranked

The full page numbers Formulas (1)–(4). Comments are named below by their keys.

### 1. Five keywords name things the page never mentions (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** `Keywords` carries "uniform matroid", "Grassmannian",
"Veronese type", "dice sums" and "delta polynomial". Keywords have the title's
weight, so a search for "uniform matroid" or "Grassmannian" lands here first.
The reader then finds none of those words anywhere on the page, and is left
to guess why the table answered.

Each keyword is a true connection:
- $\Delta(k,n)$ is the base polytope of the uniform matroid $U_{k,n}$, which
  the linked Wikipedia article says.
- It is the moment polytope of the Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)$.
- $L_{\Delta(k,n)}$ is the Hilbert function of the algebra of Veronese type
  generated by the squarefree monomials of degree $k$ in $n$ variables.
- Formula (1) counts the ways $n$ dice with faces $0,\dots,t$ sum to $kt$.
- "$\delta$-polynomial" is another name for the $h^*$-polynomial.

The page states none of this.

**Smallest fix.** Add one comment that makes the connections, for instance:

> $\Delta(k,n)$ is the base polytope of the uniform matroid $U_{k,n}$
> CITE{WikiHypersimplex} and the moment polytope of the Grassmannian
> $\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)$. Formula (1) says that $L_{\Delta(k,n)}(t)$ counts the
> ways $n$ dice with faces $0,1,\dots,t$ can sum to $kt$, and it is the
> Hilbert function of the algebra of Veronese type generated by the
> squarefree monomials of degree $k$ in $n$ variables. The $h^*$-polynomial
> is also called the $\delta$-polynomial.

Otherwise, drop the keywords the page does not support. I checked the matroid
statement against the Wikipedia article and the dice statement against the
rows. The Grassmannian and Veronese statements are standard, but I did not
check them against a source.

### 2. `comment-lattice` names a hyperplane the dilates do not lie in (worth doing)

**What a reader sees.** "The lattice is the affine lattice cut out by
$x_1+\cdots+x_n=k$ inside $\mathbb{Z}^n$." The dilate $t\Delta(k,n)$ lies in
$x_1+\cdots+x_n=kt$. Read literally, the sentence counts the points of
$t\Delta(k,n)$ in a hyperplane that contains none of them once $t\geq2$.
Nothing says which quantity depends on the lattice. $L(t)$ does not, since
counting in $\mathbb{Z}^n$ gives the same answer. The "normalized volume" in
Formula (4) does.

**Smallest fix.**

> Lattice points are counted in $\mathbb{Z}^n$. Since $t\Delta(k,n)$ lies in
> the hyperplane $x_1+\cdots+x_n=kt$, the normalized volume in Formula (4) is
> taken with respect to the lattice $\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^n:x_1+\cdots+x_n=0\}$.

### 3. Formula (4) does not say which Eulerian numbers (worth doing; one clause)

**What a reader sees.** "$h^*_{\Delta(k,n)}(1)$ is the Eulerian number
$A(n-1,k-1)$." Eulerian numbers are indexed at least three ways: by descents
from 0, by ascents from 1 as OEIS A008292 does, and with $n$ shifted. A reader
checking $\Delta(2,5)$ against a table written the other way gets 1 or 26
instead of 11. Nothing in the corpus defines $A$, and the skill asks that
notation be defined where it is used.

**Smallest fix.** "…is the Eulerian number $A(n-1,k-1)$, the number of
permutations of $n-1$ letters with $k-1$ descents, and it is the normalized
volume of $\Delta(k,n)$." That convention matches all 81 `h-star` rows.

### 4. `Programs` is a piece of the generator, not an incantation (small)

**What a reader sees.** Twenty lines: `import numberdb.sage`, `from math import
factorial`, three named Sage imports, a hand-written polynomial binomial and a
function. The code runs and is correct. But the skill asks `Programs` for the
standard incantation for a reader in Sage who wants one more value. That
reader needs none of the imports, Sage's `binomial` already accepts a
polynomial, and the program gives no $h^*$-polynomial, which is half of what
the table holds.

**Smallest fix.** Use this instead. I ran it through `agents/sage.sh`, with
`**` in place of `^` since the script was not preparsed. It printed the
stored $L_{\Delta(2,6)}$ and $h^*_{\Delta(2,6)}$:

    n, k = 6, 2
    t = polygen(QQ, 't'); z = polygen(QQ, 'z')
    L = sum((-1)^i*binomial(n, i)*binomial((k-i)*t - i + n - 1, n - 1) for i in range(k))
    h = (sum(L(j)*z^j for j in range(n)) * (1 - z)^n).truncate(n)

If wanted, add `polytopes.hypersimplex(n, k).ehrhart_polynomial()  # needs
LattE`. I could not run that line.

### 5. Noted only

- **The Similar-tables relation for T232** is "the transportation-polytope
  analogue with all row and column sums fixed". A reader cannot tell what is
  analogous to what, or what the sums are fixed to. An exact version is
  "$n\times n$ matrices with entries in $[0,1]$ and every row and column sum
  equal to $1$, where $\Delta(k,n)$ fixes one sum". T233, the root polytopes,
  is the third table in the same series and is not listed. None of the three
  drafts is published, so T232 should be published no later than T234 (see
  the audit note above).
- **`rigour details`** opens its second paragraph with "Before the draft was
  filled, the rows were checked…". That describes this run's timeline, and
  after publication it reads as a stage nobody sees. The T232 and T233
  critiques noted the same wording. "The rows were checked…" is enough.
- **`complete-note`** gives the range but not the reason for $n\leq20$. The
  skill asks for the reason there. A clause about the largest entry would do:
  $L_{\Delta(9,20)}(t)$ is 682 characters.
- **The palindromes are visible and unexplained.** $h^*_{\Delta(k,n)}$ is
  palindromic on exactly the nine rows with $n=2k$, and on no other stored
  row. A reader scanning the column will see it. The likely source is De Negri
  and Hibi, *Gorenstein algebras of Veronese type*, J. Algebra 193 (1997),
  where $\Delta(k,n)$ is Gorenstein exactly when $n=2k$ for
  $2\leq k\leq n-2$. **I have not checked that reference**; the pattern is
  checked on the rows.
- **`comment-symmetry`** says "this table stores one representative of each
  pair". A reader holding $\Delta(3,5)$ is better served by "so
  $\Delta(n-k,n)$ has the same two polynomials; for $k>n/2$ look up
  $\Delta(n-k,n)$".
- **Notation order.** Link [1] writes $\Delta_{d,k}$ with $d$ first, and
  Sage's `polytopes.hypersimplex(n, k)` puts $n$ first. This page writes
  $\Delta(k,n)$, although its parameters run $n$, $k$. Since
  $\Delta(k,n)\cong\Delta(n-k,n)$, a reader who swaps the two still gets the
  right polynomials. One comment sentence would still help: "Some authors,
  Wikipedia among them CITE{WikiHypersimplex}, write $\Delta_{n,k}$".
- **OEIS A275514** answered 403 with a Cloudflare challenge from this runner.
  That is already recorded in `docs/agent-environment.md` and says nothing
  about the link.

## What reads well

- **It renders.** Every section, every CITE, the program block and all 190
  mathematics fragments come through without an error.
- **The Definition defines, and the parameters describe the family.** The
  polytope is given two equivalent ways. The range actually computed,
  $n\leq20$, is in `complete-note` and not in a constraint. The lower bounds
  are explained on the page: `comment-standard-simplex` gives the omitted
  $k=1$ case in closed form, and `comment-symmetry` justifies
  $k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.
- **The formulas are right on every row**, and nothing apologises or points
  backwards. Each row label, $L_{\Delta(2,6)}(t)$ or $h^*_{\Delta(2,6)}(z)$,
  tells a reader holding a polynomial which polytope and which form it is, in
  the first screen.
