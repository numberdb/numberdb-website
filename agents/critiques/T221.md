## Findings, ranked

### 1. The Kronecker symbol is typeset as a fraction

What a reader sees, in Formulas (2): "where $\chi_D(n)=(D/n)$ is the
Kronecker character". It renders as $D$ divided by $n$.

Why it is wrong: a slash in parentheses is how every reader writes a
quotient, and $D/n$ is a meaningful number here. The only thing telling a
reader it is a symbol is the word "Kronecker" after it. The table this one
points to for the same convention, T128, writes it
`$\chi_D=\left(\frac{D}{\cdot}\right)$ is the Kronecker symbol`.

Smallest change, in `formula-dirichlet`: replace `$\chi_D(n)=(D/n)$ is the
Kronecker character` with `$\chi_D=\left(\frac{D}{\cdot}\right)$ is the
Kronecker symbol`. It also lines up with T128 and T145. **Worth doing.**

### 2. $\zeta_K$ is never defined, and both declared sources that would define it are never cited

What a reader sees: Formulas (1) uses $\zeta_K(2)$ with no word saying what
$\zeta_K$ is. Formulas (2) uses $L(s,\chi_D)$ in the same way. Under Links,
"Wikipedia: Dedekind zeta function" is listed and nothing on the page points
to it. Under References, Maclachlan and Reid is listed and nothing points to
it either. The formula is also never named. "Humbert volume formula" is in
`Keywords`, and the generator's docstring says "Humbert's formula", but the
page does not, so a reader who knows the result by name cannot see that this
is the one.

Why it is wrong: the skill's rule is that a symbol used in a formula has its
definition in the same table, and that a reference is for citing, not for
listing. Here the page is one clause away from both: the Link that defines
$\zeta_K$ is already declared, and so is the book that proves the formula.

Smallest change, in `formula-humbert`:

    $\mathrm{Vol}\bigl(\ldots\bigr) = |D|^{3/2}\zeta_K(2)/(4\pi^2)$, Humbert's
    formula CITE{WikiBianchi} CITE{MaclachlanReid}, where $\zeta_K$ is the
    Dedekind zeta function of $K$ CITE{WikiDedekindZeta}.

In `formula-dirichlet`, add `HREF{Values_of_Dirichlet_L-functions_at_positive_integers}[the Dirichlet $L$-function]`
before `$L(s,\chi_D)$`. That link is the first mention of T145 in Formulas.
Maclachlan and Reid state Humbert's formula in their chapter on volumes. Read
the theorem number off the book before writing one in, because I have not
checked it. If the book is not going to be cited, delete the Reference
instead. **Worth doing.**

### 3. The definition never says which group is the Bianchi group, or what a covolume is

What a reader sees: the title says "Covolumes of the Bianchi groups". The
definition says "Listed is the hyperbolic volume of the Bianchi orbifold
$\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$". Neither "Bianchi
group" nor "covolume" appears in it. A reader has to infer that
$\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ is the group in the title, and that its
covolume is this volume. The first Comment then begins "The group is
$\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$", which points back to a group the definition
never introduced.

Why it is wrong: the definition is the one place that should connect the
title's words to the object, precisely enough that two people build the same
table. Some authors call $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ or
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ the Bianchi group, so the definition is where
this table's choice belongs.

Smallest change: make the second sentence "The Bianchi group is
$\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$, and listed is its covolume: the hyperbolic
volume of the orbifold $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$
for the metric of curvature $-1$ CITE{WikiBianchi}." The definition grows to
about 330 characters, a little over the audit's 320. That is the kind of
overrule its docstring allows. Alternatively, drop "Let … with ring of
integers $\mathcal{O}_K$" to a shorter "$\mathcal{O}_K$ its ring of integers".
Then `comment-convention` can begin "$\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ acts by
…". **Worth doing.**

### 4. Two Similar-tables relations do not state a relation, and the closest sibling is missing

What a reader sees:

- Residues of Dedekind zeta functions of quadratic fields: "the same
  fundamental discriminant convention for quadratic fields". A shared
  indexing convention is a reason to trust the join, not a relation. The
  relation is that T128 holds the same $\zeta_K$ at its pole, under the same
  $D$.
- Values of Dirichlet L-functions at positive integers: "for the conductors it
  stores". A reader cannot tell whether that means five rows or three hundred.
  T145 holds every primitive character with $q\leq 30$ at $s=1,2,3$, so it
  covers the $D$ here with $|D|\leq 30$.
- **Values of Dedekind zeta functions of cubic fields at 2 (T162)** is not
  listed. It holds the same quantity, $\zeta_K(2)$, one degree up. Its
  `comment-volume` gives Borel's formula, which makes $\zeta_K(2)$ a
  hyperbolic volume for a complex cubic field, as Humbert's formula does
  here. T219, which this table does list, reaches its volume through T162.

Smallest change:

- T128: "$\kappa_D$, the residue at $s=1$ of the same $\zeta_K$, under the
  same $D$".
- T145: "$L(2,\chi_D)$ from CITE{formula-dirichlet} for $|D|\leq 30$".
- Add T162: "$\zeta_K(2)$ for cubic fields, which Borel's formula makes a
  hyperbolic volume when $D<0$, as Humbert's formula CITE{formula-humbert}
  does here".

The T219 relation, "closed hyperbolic 3-manifold volumes rather than
arithmetic cusped orbifold covolumes", says what T219 is not. It could name the
Weeks manifold, but it is not wrong. **Worth doing for the first three.**

### 5. The figure-eight sentence gives the fact without its reason, and points at a row

What a reader sees, in Comments (3): "Twelve times the $D=-3$ entry is the
figure-eight knot complement volume."

- "Twelve" is the surprising part, and nothing says where it comes from. The
  figure-eight knot group is a subgroup of index 12 in
  $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})})$ (Riley, 1975), so its
  complement is a 12-sheeted cover of this orbifold. The skill asks for
  exactly that: where a value looks surprising, the fact is the explanation.
- "the figure-eight knot complement volume" is four nouns stacked in a row.
- "the $D=-3$ entry" is a row the page can link.

Smallest change: "The figure-eight knot group is a subgroup of index $12$ in
$\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$ for $D=-3$ CITE{…}, so twelve times
HREF{#-3}[the $D=-3$ entry] is
HREF{Hyperbolic_volumes_of_the_prime_knots_with_at_most_ten_crossings#4,1}[the volume of the figure-eight knot complement]."
I checked only that the stored numbers agree (12 × 0.16915693… =
2.02988321…). Source the index-12 statement before writing it in. Maclachlan
and Reid treat this example, and citing them there would also help with
finding 2. **Worth doing; small.**

### 6. The completeness note says what range, not why

`complete-note`: "it holds every negative fundamental discriminant with
$|D|\leq 1000$". The clause is well formed. The skill asks this note to also
say why that range was chosen, since that is what a reader checking whether
their own number belongs here reads. A reason would do, for example that it is
the range of T128, which holds $|D|\leq 1000$ for the same fields. But only if
that is in fact the reason. **Worth doing if there is a reason; otherwise
leave it.**

## Noticed, not worth an edit on their own

- `rigour details` says "Before the draft was filled, all $305$ rows were
  compared…". "Draft" is this website's workflow and means nothing once the
  table is public. "All $305$ values were also compared…" says the same thing.
- A reader holding a covolume computed for $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathcal{O}_K)$
  holds half of this table's value. The index is
  $[\mathcal{O}_K^\times:\mathcal{O}_K^{\times2}]=2$ for every imaginary
  quadratic field. `comment-convention` already handles
  $\mathrm{SL}_2$, and one more sentence there would handle
  $\mathrm{PGL}_2$. It is optional. That half is a value somebody could
  plausibly hold, and it is not a factor a reader would guess.
- The keyword "PSL2 of imaginary quadratic integers" is not a phrase anybody
  types. "Bianchi orbifold volume" or "covolume" would do more.
